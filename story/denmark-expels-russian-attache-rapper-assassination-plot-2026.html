Denmark Expels Russian Defence Attaché Over Alleged Plot to Kill Ukrainian Rapper

Danish intelligence said Russian services ordered a foiled attempt to assassinate the Ukrainian-American rapper Kyivstoner at a concert near Copenhagen in September, prompting Copenhagen to expel Moscow's defence attaché.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Denmark said on Friday that it would expel Russia’s defence attaché, after accusing Moscow of ordering the attempted killing of a Ukrainian-American rapper on Danish soil. The Danish security services said the plot to kill the rapper, known by his stage name Kyivstoner, was thwarted in September, and two Danish men appeared in court on Friday charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen called the alleged plot an unacceptable transgression and said Denmark was sending Moscow a very strong signal.

Danish police averted the attempt when they arrested three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before Kyivstoner was due to give a concert north of Copenhagen on September 25, police and security officials told a press conference on Friday. Prosecutors said the men were driving towards the venue with two loaded pistols and two knives when police arrested them at around 9.55 p.m., five minutes before the concert was due to begin. One of the suspects had bought a ticket to the concert, according to the charges. The concert was cancelled.

“It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services,” Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, head of Denmark’s national security and intelligence service, PET, said at the press conference. “A Russian assassination attempt is not something we’ve seen in Denmark before, and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia,” he said. “We believe that the case is important for understanding the threat that Denmark faces from Russia.”

The two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday, charged under Section 108 of Denmark’s Criminal Code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder, court documents showed. Both men denied wrongdoing, broadcaster DR reported. Danish police and PET suspect the banned criminal network Loyal to Familia, a Danish street gang, helped plan the attack. PET said the operation was stopped minutes before the scheduled concert, when officers intercepted a vehicle carrying men linked to the gang and found the weapons inside.

The intended target was the Ukrainian-American rapper known as Kyivstoner, whose legal name is Albert Viktorovych Vasyliev, a Ukrainian-born artist who holds both Ukrainian and American citizenship. Danish media identified him after the announcement on Friday. The rapper had been due to perform at a venue north of Copenhagen when police moved in. No motive for why Russian intelligence would target a musician has been publicly explained by Danish officials, and the Russian Embassy in Copenhagen called the idea absurd.

“We know the Russian intelligence services are behind this. We are absolutely certain,” Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said. Rasmussen said he had summoned the Russian ambassador to the Foreign Ministry and notified him of the decision to expel the defence attaché within seven days. Rasmussen said Denmark was also considering suspending Schengen visas for Russian citizens. “We don’t want to break off diplomatic relations with Russia in this particular situation that Denmark and Europe are in,” he added. “But on the other hand, we want to send a very, very strong signal.”

Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen issued a forceful warning on Friday, describing the alleged Russian operation as evidence of an increasingly dangerous security environment across Europe. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the alleged plot in a statement on Saturday, saying: “I strongly condemn the assassination attempt against a US-Ukrainian citizen in Denmark, orchestrated by Russian intelligence services.” The Danish justice minister, Nicolai Wammen, appeared alongside Rasmussen at the press conference announcing the charges and the expulsion.

Russia has denied organising the attack or other hybrid operations in Europe, calling the Danish claims unsubstantiated. The Russian Embassy in Copenhagen called the Danish allegations baseless, saying the notion that Russia would assassinate “a musician with limited political influence and questionable fame” was “absurd.” Moscow has regularly denied accusations of stepping up hybrid attacks — incidents of aggression that fall short of acts of war — in the region following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The embassy did not immediately reply to a request for comment from Reuters on Friday.

The case fits a wider pattern Danish officials have been warning about for months. PET said in September that Russia was recruiting Danish citizens to help plan sabotage against Danish defence firms with ties to Ukraine. Denmark has been one of Ukraine’s most outspoken supporters in Europe, and Rasmussen framed the alleged assassination plot as Russian “intimidation” aimed at making Denmark “waver” in its support for Kyiv. The two charged men remain in custody, and Danish prosecutors are expected to set out more of their case as the investigation continues. For now, the expulsion of the defence attaché is Copenhagen’s answer — a public attribution, a diplomatic punishment, and a warning that Denmark believes Moscow’s covert campaign has reached its soil.

Why it matters: An assassination plot against a rapper may look like a strange target, but Denmark is treating it as a test case for something much bigger: whether Russian intelligence is now willing to order killings inside a NATO country that has been one of Ukraine’s loudest backers. The expulsion of a defence attaché is a calibrated punishment — severe enough to be noticed in Moscow, short of breaking relations — and the possible suspension of Schengen visas for Russians would widen it further. The case also lands days after Russian missiles fell in Poland, and European capitals will read the two events together as evidence that Moscow’s campaign of intimidation is escalating from sabotage and airspace violations towards direct violence on allied soil.

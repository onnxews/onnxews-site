Strike on Nasser Hospital in Gaza Kills Six, Including a Surgeon

An Israeli airstrike hit the surgery department of Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis early on Thursday, killing at least six people including a general surgeon and wrecking the surgery department of one of the few functioning hospitals left in Gaza.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

An Israeli airstrike targeting Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza killed six people, including a general surgeon, and wounded at least 20 more, Gaza's health authorities said on Thursday, November 12. The strike was carried out early Thursday, just after midnight, and hit the hospital's surgical wing in Khan Younis, one of the few functioning medical facilities left in Gaza. Gaza's Health Ministry said at least 28 more people were wounded; the civil defence service put the figure at 20.

The dead surgeon was identified by medics as Rizk Abu Shaira, a general surgeon at the hospital. British surgeon Goher Rahbour, who is volunteering at Nasser Hospital, said he had been operating alongside Abu Shaira just hours before the strike. 'Lovely guy, happy and smiling,' Rahbour said. Rahbour was on the fourth floor of the surgical department when the bomb hit. 'It was extremely smoky,' he said. The recovery area on the same floor was reduced to rubble.

The strike hit the hospital's surgery department, causing extensive damage. Inside the building, parts of several floors were either severely damaged or destroyed, with debris scattered across hospital beds and equipment and rebar dangling from the ceiling where the munition had penetrated. Video from inside the hospital showed rubble and furniture strewn across rooms and wires dangling from damaged ceilings, with people carrying injured patients on stretchers through debris-covered corridors. Several bodies were also seen being carried away as firefighters worked outside.

The Israeli military said it struck a Hamas commander and other militants in the attack, without providing evidence. In a statement on Thursday, the military said Hamas had 'established a presence within the hospital complex' and that the commander had turned it 'into a headquarters.' A Hamas spokesperson described the strike as a 'fully fledged war crime.'

Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis is one of the few functioning medical facilities left in Gaza, whose healthcare infrastructure has been decimated by a yearslong war. Hospitals and their staff are protected under international humanitarian law, though a hospital can lose its protected status if it is used for military purposes. The Israeli military has raided or laid siege to hospitals across Gaza, accusing Hamas of using them as command centers and to hide fighters, and has destroyed medical equipment and supplies and killed or detained hundreds of healthcare workers.

Israeli forces have attacked Nasser Hospital before. In 2025, the military killed five journalists, including AP contributor Mariam Dagga, when it struck a gathering point for local reporters at the complex. The latest strike wrecked the surgery department of a hospital that was already operating under severe strain, at a moment when the territory's remaining medical facilities are stretched past breaking point.

The strike came more than a year into a U.S.-backed ceasefire that took effect in October 2025. Israel has continued to strike Gaza despite the truce and controls at least 60% of the enclave. Since the truce went into effect, Israeli strikes and gunfire have killed 1,622 people, health authorities say, and five Israeli soldiers have also been killed during that period.

The wider war began on October 7, 2023, when Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel, killing around 1,200 people and taking 251 others hostage. Since then, Israel's retaliatory offensive has killed more than 74,600 people, according to the Gaza Health Ministry, which is part of the Hamas-run government but whose figures are generally considered reliable by the international community. At least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza since the ceasefire deal was agreed, according to the territory's health officials.

The casualty figures from Thursday's strike come from Gaza's health authorities and its civil defence service, and there has been no independent count. The two sides' accounts of what was hit and why differ sharply: Israel says it struck a Hamas command center, while Hamas calls the attack a war crime. What is not in dispute is that the surgery department of one of the few functioning hospitals left in Gaza has been wrecked, and the patients who needed operations there now have fewer places to turn.

Why it matters: The Nasser Hospital strike shows how threadbare the October 2025 ceasefire has become: more than a year after the truce took effect, a major hospital can still be hit in the middle of the night, and more than 1,600 people have been killed since the deal was agreed. The two sides' accounts cannot both be true in full, and with independent reporters barred from Gaza, the facts will be argued over long after the rubble is cleared. What is certain is the practical cost: one of the few functioning hospitals left in Gaza has lost its surgical ward, and the patients who needed operations there now have nowhere to go.

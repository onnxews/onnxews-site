CoreWeave Shares Jump After Revenue Surges 168% and Guidance Rises

The AI cloud provider's $3.66 billion quarter and record $132.5 billion backlog steadied a stock that had lost 40% in a month.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

CoreWeave, the artificial intelligence cloud company at the center of the market's debate over AI spending, reported third-quarter revenue of 3.66 billion dollars on Tuesday, up 168 percent from a year earlier and ahead of the roughly 3.56 billion dollars Wall Street expected. The company also raised its full-year guidance and reported a record 132.5 billion dollar revenue backlog, sending its shares sharply higher in after-hours trading and steadying a stock that had lost more than 40 percent of its value in the past month.

The results arrived at a sensitive moment for AI investors. CoreWeave is one of the so-called neoclouds, smaller AI infrastructure firms that have come under intense pressure this month amid concerns about their capacity to raise funds under tighter market conditions. The company's shares had been sliding for weeks, and Tuesday's report was widely seen as a make-or-break test for the entire sector.

Revenue grew 42 percent from the second quarter alone, with quarterly net new revenue exceeding 1 billion dollars for the first time, and the company said its exit annualized revenue run-rate forecast had risen to between 20 and 21 billion dollars. Chief Executive Michael Intrator described the quarter as a breakout quarter, highlighting revenue increasing by more than 1 billion dollars sequentially.

The company also raised its outlook for the full year. CoreWeave now expects 2026 revenue of between 13.0 and 13.3 billion dollars, up from a previous range of 12.4 to 13.2 billion, and lifted its adjusted operating income guidance by 26 percent at the midpoint, to between 1.275 and 1.375 billion dollars. Adjusted operating income reached 379 million dollars in the quarter, well above the 200 to 260 million dollar range the company had guided, and the adjusted operating margin rose to 10 percent from 5 percent in the second quarter.

The backlog figure drew particular attention. Signed contracts not yet booked as revenue grew to 132.5 billion dollars at the end of September, up from about 104 billion dollars at the end of June and 138 percent higher than a year earlier. That pipeline of committed customer business gives the company visibility into future revenue that few of its peers can match, and it was the centerpiece of management's case that demand for AI computing remains exceptionally strong.

Profitability remains a work in progress. CoreWeave reported a net loss of 709 million dollars in the quarter, up from a 110 million dollar loss a year earlier, and an adjusted loss of 1.27 dollars per share. The company expects adjusted net income to turn positive in the first half of 2028, a timeline that will test investors' patience.

The market's reaction was dramatic and volatile. CoreWeave closed the regular session on Tuesday at 48.73 dollars, then surged about 5.6 percent in late trading to 51.49 dollars after the results, and climbed further overnight to around 54.87 dollars, roughly 12.6 percent above the regular-session close. At one point in the overnight session, sellers pushed the stock below its pre-earnings close to about 46.50 dollars before buyers stepped back in.

The rally came with a reminder of the risks. CoreWeave's total debt had soared to 46.87 billion dollars as of September 30, and yields on its bonds maturing in 2031 and 2032 have climbed to nearly 15 percent, reflecting the sizeable risk premium some lenders are demanding. The company expects to spend up to 39 billion dollars on capital expenditure during 2026 as it accelerates data center construction and graphics processor deployment.

Management laid out a funding plan aimed directly at that fear. Intrator told analysts that most of the company's capital requirements in fiscal 2027 will be sourced from clients through prepayments on infrastructure services and from hardware partners such as Nvidia, and the company pointed to about 9 billion dollars in customer prepayments and deposits expected over the coming three months. It also said it would not use the unsecured debt market to fund growth at current yields.

The report steadied more than CoreWeave's own shares. Nasdaq futures jumped on Wednesday morning, led by a rebound in AI sentiment, after CoreWeave's raised outlook revived the trade. Rival neocloud Nebius reports its own earnings on Thursday, and Nvidia, whose chips power CoreWeave's data centers, reports next week. For now, CoreWeave has bought the AI infrastructure story some breathing room, but the debt question that drove its 40 percent slide has not gone away.

Anthropic Turns Claude Into an Office Suite With Meetings, Sheets and Whiteboards

Anthropic unveiled Claude Meetings, Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards on November 10, pushing its assistant from the engineer's desk into the meeting room — on the same day it kicked off its IPO roadshow.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Anthropic unveiled a set of workplace tools on Tuesday, November 10, that turn its Claude assistant into something much closer to a full office suite, pushing the company beyond its base of software engineers and into the daily work of everyone else. The launch came at a San Francisco event and includes Claude Meetings, Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards, plus the general availability of Claude Data.

The headline act is Claude Meetings, which lets a team add Claude to a video call as a visible participant. While people talk, Claude follows the conversation and starts working on tasks in real time: it can draft the plan being discussed, map out a timeline on a whiteboard, answer questions in the meeting chat and pull information from connected work apps, such as last year's figures. After the call, the meeting notes, action items and other materials Claude created are available in claude.ai.

Anthropic has built in guardrails for the meeting room. Claude identifies itself as a participant and posts a notice in the meeting chat. It will not join calls with external guests unless an administrator authorizes it, and it will automatically leave if an external guest joins. The feature is launching in beta on Team and Enterprise plans and works with Google Meet at launch, with support for additional platforms expected later.

There is one wrinkle in the rollout. Meetings is launching in beta and is limited to Team and Enterprise plans, while Sheets and Whiteboards are in beta on all paid plans, on web, desktop and mobile — a sign that the feature may reach different customers on different timetables. At launch, Meetings works only with Google Meet, with support for additional platforms expected later.

Claude Sheets lets teams ask Claude to build a working spreadsheet — a campaign tracker or a hiring budget, for example — with multiple tabs, formulas, formatting and drop-down menus. Claude makes edits directly in cells alongside the team and outlines each change so users can see what it did. Sheets export to Excel or CSV and can continue in Airtable, Asana, monday.com or Smartsheet.

Claude Whiteboards is a shared board where a team can talk through a plan while Claude turns it into an organized visual workspace with timelines and deadlines. When the brainstorm is done, Claude can turn the board into a document or a slide deck, and boards can also be moved into Lucid, Miro or Zoho's Vani. Whiteboards also work inside Claude Meetings, so the board builds while people talk.

Claude Data, formerly known as Claude Dashboards when it launched in beta in October, is now generally available on all paid plans. It connects Claude to the systems companies use to keep their numbers, such as a data warehouse or customer relationship platform, and answers questions with dashboards, written data stories or narrated videos. Its launch partners include Amplitude, Mixpanel, Omni, PostHog and Sigma.

Anthropic also announced interactive connectors that open the full version of apps from partners such as Adobe, Atlassian and HubSpot inside Claude, so users can work in those tools without switching tabs. The company's marketplace of tools has grown to more than 9,000 plugin options. “We've seen developers work at the speed of thought,” said Nicholas Lin, Anthropic's product lead for knowledge work, adding that the company wants other knowledge workers “to be able to do the same thing.” Lin said Anthropic has tested Claude Meetings internally for about two months, and the assistant now joins nearly all of its meetings.

The timing is no accident. The launch landed the same day Anthropic kicked off the roadshow for its initial public offering, which aims to raise as much as $64.4 billion at a $1.02 trillion valuation. The suite is a pitch to investors as much as to customers: proof that Claude can expand from coding into the much larger market for office work. It also puts Anthropic directly against Microsoft and Google, whose own suites already bundle meeting agents and productivity tools, and against OpenAI, which unveiled its own workplace tools a few weeks earlier. On Enterprise plans, Whiteboards and Sheets start switched off, while Claude Data turns on by default on November 19.

Why it matters: This launch matters because it is Anthropic's biggest strategic shift since Claude Code took off: from a tool for engineers to a product for everyone who works in an office. The company is asking businesses to let an AI sit in their meetings, edit their spreadsheets and read their company data — a much deeper level of trust than a chat window requires. The bet is that the same assistant that won developers can win the far larger market of knowledge workers, and that investors will pay a trillion-dollar valuation for it. The risk is equally plain: these tools are mostly betas, they run into Microsoft and Google on their home turf, and Meetings only works on Google Meet for now. Watch the IPO pricing and whether enterprises actually switch the tools on.

Senate Confirms Two Trump Judicial Picks for Texas in Lame-Duck Push

Richard Wesley Bennett was confirmed 49-43 for the Southern District of Texas and Courtney Coker 49-41 for the Northern District, as Republicans race to fill lifetime judgeships before January.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Senate on Friday confirmed two of President Donald Trump's judicial nominees for Texas, pressing ahead with a lame-duck push to fill lifetime federal judgeships before Democrats take control of the chamber in January. By a vote of 49 to 43, the Senate confirmed Richard Wesley Bennett to be a United States District Judge for the Southern District of Texas, and by a party-line vote of 49 to 41 it confirmed Courtney Leigh Coker to be a District Judge for the Northern District of Texas.

Bennett will serve on the Houston-based Southern District court. The confirmation vote was held at noon on Friday, and it was a party-line vote, with Senators Booker, Britt, Cassidy, Coons, Cornyn, Klobuchar, Murphy and Tuberville not voting. The Senate had invoked cloture on the Bennett nomination the night before, on a party-line vote of 49 to 46, clearing the final procedural hurdle and setting up Friday's confirmation.

Coker will serve on the Dallas-based Northern District court. His confirmation came later in the day, at 3:35 p.m., also on a party-line vote of 49 to 41, with Senators Booker, Britt, Cassidy, Coons, Cornyn, Durbin, Klobuchar, Murphy, Tuberville and Warner not voting. Earlier in the day, the Senate had invoked cloture on the Coker nomination by a party-line vote of 49 to 43.

The two confirmations were part of a much busier day of judicial votes. The Senate also invoked cloture on four other district-judge nominees on Friday: Daniel Lewis Ballou for the Eastern District of Kentucky, by a party-line vote of 48 to 36; Kyle Reardon for the District of Alaska, by a vote of 55 to 31, with Democrats Cortez Masto, Fetterman, Hirono, Kaine, Rosen and Shaheen voting in favor; Robert E. Long Jr. for the Middle District of Florida, by a party-line vote of 47 to 35; and Peter M. Mansfield for the Eastern District of Louisiana, by a vote of 48 to 31, with Democrats Fetterman and Hirono voting in favor. The Senate then invoked cloture on Jesus A. Osete for the Western District of Missouri by a vote of 45 to 33.

The pace reflects the calendar. With Democrats set to take control of both chambers of Congress in January after big wins in this month's midterm election, Republicans are using the remaining weeks of the lame-duck session to confirm as many of the president's judicial picks as possible. District judges hold lifetime appointments, so each confirmation locks in a judge who will serve long after the current Senate majority is gone.

The votes also showed how thin the margins have become. With several senators absent on Friday, 49 votes were enough to confirm both Texas nominees, and every Republican who cast a vote backed them while every Democrat who voted opposed them. The pattern held across the day's cloture votes, with only the Reardon and Mansfield nominations drawing any Democratic support.

After the votes, the Senate adjourned until 3 p.m. on Monday, November 30, with only pro forma sessions scheduled for Tuesday, November 24, and Friday, November 27. The adjournment means the next round of confirmation votes will come after the Thanksgiving break, leaving Republicans a narrow window in December to work through the remaining nominees before the new Congress is seated.

The Texas confirmations fill two of the most closely watched vacancies on the federal bench, in districts that handle a heavy load of immigration, energy and commercial cases. Whether the lame-duck push can clear the rest of the queue, including the four nominees who won cloture on Friday, will be decided in the short December session that remains.

Why it matters: The two confirmations matter less for Texas alone than for what they signal about the lame-duck session: with Democrats taking the Senate in January, every lifetime judgeship confirmed now is one the new majority cannot block. Friday's votes show Republicans are moving nominees in batches, clearing cloture on six district judges in a single day, and the 49-vote margins show they can do it even with several of their own members absent. The December session will decide how many more lifetime appointments get through before the gavel changes hands.

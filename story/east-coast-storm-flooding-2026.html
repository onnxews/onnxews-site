Cross-Country Storm Shifts to the East Coast After Catastrophic Flooding in Georgia and Tennessee

The same system that swamped towns on the Georgia-Tennessee border is now bringing the Northeast its first significant rain in weeks, with flash flooding possible from Virginia to New England.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

A powerful cross-country storm that has affected more than 35 states is now bearing down on the East Coast, bringing heavy rain and a flash flood threat from the Carolinas to New England on Friday. The system is the same one that produced historic and catastrophic flooding on the Georgia-Tennessee border earlier this week.

More than 100 people had to be rescued as feet of floodwater overwhelmed homes and businesses in McCaysville, Georgia, and Copperhill, Tennessee, after downpours swelled their shared river to a new record high. A few spots in North Georgia picked up 6 to 11 inches of rain, with the heaviest totals reaching a one-in-1,000-year rainfall rate, according to the National Weather Service. Flash flood emergencies, the highest level of flood warning, were issued for both towns.

Heavy rain is now falling along coastal portions of the Northeast and mid-Atlantic and is expected to last through Friday night or into early Saturday. A Level 2 of 4 risk of flooding rain is in place for southern New England, including Boston and Providence, Rhode Island, where 2 to 4 inches of rain are expected and a few locations could see up to 6 inches. A Level 1 risk covers the coast from the New York City area to southeastern Virginia and northeastern North Carolina.

The rain ends a dry stretch of more than two weeks for the region. New York City, Philadelphia and Washington, D.C., have not recorded measurable rainfall since October 17. Flash flood warnings were issued Friday morning for three New Jersey counties, and a flood watch is in effect for Rhode Island and southeastern Massachusetts through Saturday morning.

Severe storms are also possible Friday afternoon across the Delmarva Peninsula and eastern North Carolina and northeastern South Carolina, with damaging wind gusts and even an isolated tornado or two. Most of the East Coast should dry out by Saturday afternoon.

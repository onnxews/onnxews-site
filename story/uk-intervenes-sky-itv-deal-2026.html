U.K. Government Intervenes in Sky's $2.15 Billion ITV Deal, Ordering Regulators to Review It

Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy issued a public interest intervention notice, directing the competition watchdog and Ofcom to report by January 27 on media plurality and free expression.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

The U.K. government has intervened in Comcast-owned Sky's plan to buy ITV's media and entertainment business, directing the country's competition regulators to review the deal in a preliminary step that could trigger an in-depth probe.

Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy issued a public interest intervention notice on Wednesday, requiring the Competition and Markets Authority and media watchdog Ofcom to complete a preliminary investigation by January 27. She could then refer the deal for a full competition investigation.

Sky agreed in July to buy the ITV unit in a 1.6 billion pound deal, equivalent to $2.15 billion. The transaction would bring ITV's television channels and ITVX streaming service under Sky, while ITV Studios would be separated and listed in London.

Nandy said she was minded to intervene last month, citing concerns about the deal's potential impact on news plurality, Sky's influence over the media landscape and the availability of a broad range of programming. The government's concerns center on free expression in news media, the diversity of viewpoints and the number of independently controlled media businesses.

Sky said it believes the transaction is in the public interest, with commitments already made to protect ITV's identity, public service broadcasting obligations and the distinct editorial voices of Sky News and ITV News. ITV called the update a major milestone in the regulatory review process toward creating a U.K. champion.

Reporting: Morningstar

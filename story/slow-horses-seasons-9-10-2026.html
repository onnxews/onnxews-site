Slow Horses Renewed for Seasons 9 and 10 in Apple's Longest Commitment Yet

Apple TV has ordered two more seasons of the spy drama, a day after Olivia Cooke said the cast thought the show was ending.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Apple TV has renewed Slow Horses for two more seasons, ordering seasons nine and ten of the spy drama in the longest commitment the streamer has ever made to a series. The renewal was confirmed on Monday, November 16, and it makes Slow Horses Apple's longest-running series — and the first Apple TV show to reach a ten-season commitment.

Only one cast member is confirmed to return. Gary Oldman, who plays the slovenly, brilliant spymaster Jackson Lamb, is signed for the new seasons. No other cast has been confirmed, and Apple has announced no storylines, dates or episode counts for seasons nine and ten. The renewal is, for now, a statement of intent rather than a production plan.

The timing is striking. The renewal came one day after Olivia Cooke, who plays Sid Baker, told The Times that she was "shooting the last season" and that it "definitely feels like we are ending." The cast, it seems, believed the show was winding down. Apple had other plans, and the announcement landed as a genuine surprise — even to the people making the show. Composer Daniel Pemberton, speaking to Far Out on November 17, said he was shocked: "Oh my God, there's two more series."

The schedule for the seasons already in the pipeline is clearer. Season seven, which adapts Mick Herron's eighth novel, Bad Actors, has finished filming and is expected in 2027. Season eight, adapting the ninth novel, Clown Town, is currently filming in London and is expected in 2028. Seasons nine and ten would go beyond the books that have been published so far: Herron has said a tenth novel is in the works, though it is not clear whether it will be published before filming begins.

Slow Horses has earned its longevity the hard way. Six seasons and 36 episodes have aired since 2022, and the sixth season, which ended on October 21, 2026, earned a near-perfect Rotten Tomatoes score. The season six finale left Jack Lowden's River Cartwright seemingly poisoned, a cliffhanger that will need resolving in season seven. The show's mix of grubby realism, black comedy and genuine suspense has made it one of the most acclaimed dramas on television, and one of the few streaming shows that seems to get better as it goes on.

The renewal raises an obvious question: how do you keep a show fresh for ten seasons when the source material runs out at nine books? Herron's novels have provided the spine of every season so far, and the show's writers have stayed close to them. Seasons nine and ten will either wait for the tenth novel, adapt material Herron has not yet published, or move beyond the books entirely. Any of those options carries risk, and the show's producers will know it.

There is also the question of Oldman. Jackson Lamb is the centre of the show, and his return is the one certainty in the announcement. At 68, Oldman has played Lamb for four years, and the role has become one of the defining performances of his career. Keeping him is the renewal's most important decision; everything else can be negotiated.

For Apple, the renewal is a statement about what kind of streamer it wants to be. In an era when shows are cancelled after two seasons and libraries are pruned for tax reasons, committing to ten seasons of a single drama is an act of faith in quality over volume. Slow Horses is not Apple's most-watched show, but it is arguably its best — and Apple has just bet that it will still be its best in 2029.

Why it matters: Slow Horses is the show that proved streaming could do television properly — slowly, carefully, and for the long term. In an industry that cancels shows after two seasons and measures success in opening-weekend numbers, Apple has just committed to a decade of a spy drama about failing, farting, middle-aged intelligence officers. That is not a business decision; it is a statement of taste. The risk is real — the books run out, the cast ages, the formula could tire — but the reward is a show that will be remembered as one of the greats. Apple is betting that quality, given time, is its own business model.

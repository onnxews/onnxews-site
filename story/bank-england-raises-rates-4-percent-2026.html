Bank of England Raises Rates to 4% for First Time Since 2023, Says More May Come

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 to lift Bank Rate to 4 percent, warning inflation could reach 5 percent next year and that more hikes may follow if the Iran conflict drags on.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

The Bank of England raised interest rates for the first time in over three years on Thursday and warned that at least one more increase was likely if the U.S.-Iran conflict continues, Reuters reported.

The central bank's Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 to raise rates to 4 percent from 3.75 percent, the first rate hike since August 2023. British inflation is likely to reach around 5 percent in the middle of next year, the Bank said, more than double the central bank's target. UK consumer price inflation increased to 3.5 percent in September.

In contrast to expectations in a Reuters poll, the dissent came not from a policymaker seeking to keep rates on hold but from one seeking a bigger hike. Catherine Mann, who said last month that the Bank had been slow to reassure markets that it had a grip on inflation, voted for a half-point interest rate increase to 4.25 percent.

Governor Andrew Bailey and the majority said current circumstances warranted a 25 basis point increase. Chief economist Huw Pill said the move was a significant step toward the monetary policy stance needed to address threats to price stability, including uncertainty from events in the Middle East and their influence over energy prices.

The Bank's move puts it on a path its main peers have already embarked on: the U.S. Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate last month to the 3.75 to 4.00 percent range, its first hike in three years, and economists expect another increase in December.

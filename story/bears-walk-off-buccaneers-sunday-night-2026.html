Bears Walk Off Buccaneers 27-24 on Santos' 38-Yard Field Goal

Cairo Santos kicked a 38-yard field goal as time expired to beat Tampa Bay 27-24 at Soldier Field, extending Chicago's winning streak to four games.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

The Chicago Bears, substantial favorites over the visiting Tampa Bay Buccaneers, escaped Soldier Field with a 27-24 win on Sunday Night Football, thanks to a 38-yard walk-off field goal from kicker Cairo Santos. It was the kind of win that says more about a team than a comfortable victory ever could.

Chicago improved to 7-2 with the win, extending its winning streak to four games. The Bears were expected to win comfortably at home, but the Buccaneers refused to go away, and the game came down to the final kick. Santos, calm under the lights, split the uprights from 38 yards as time expired to send the home crowd into a frenzy.

The defining defensive play came in the third quarter, when cornerback Jaylon Johnson intercepted a Tampa Bay pass and ran it back into the end zone for a touchdown. The pick-six swung the momentum Chicago's way at a moment when the game was threatening to slip from its grasp, and it was the kind of play that has defined the Bears' season.

Quarterback Caleb Williams had a strange night. He threw for 279 passing yards, but he also threw four interceptions — a stat line that would normally spell defeat. That the Bears won anyway says a lot about the rest of the team: the defense scored, the running game kept the chains moving, and Santos delivered when it mattered most.

The Buccaneers, for their part, deserved better than a loss. They came into Soldier Field as heavy underdogs and pushed the Bears to the final seconds, answering every Chicago score and refusing to be blown out. A road win against a 7-2 team would have been a statement; instead, they leave with a narrow defeat and the knowledge that they were one kick from a famous result.

The win keeps Chicago firmly in the playoff picture and extends a winning run that has become the story of the team's season. Four straight wins, a 7-2 record and a habit of finding ways to win — even on a night when the quarterback throws four interceptions — is the profile of a team that believes in itself.

For Tampa Bay, the loss stings precisely because it was so close. The Buccaneers played well enough to win for long stretches, and they will look back at the missed chances that left the game in Santos's hands at the end. In a league of fine margins, a 38-yard field goal is the difference between a season-defining win and a frustrating loss.

The Bears' next challenge is to clean up the mistakes. Four interceptions from the quarterback is not a formula for sustained success, however good the defense is. But winning while playing badly is a skill in itself, and Chicago has now done it four weeks in a row.

Sunday night at Soldier Field ended with the home crowd roaring and the Bears at 7-2. The walk-off kick was the headline, but the real story was a team that keeps finding ways to win — even when it tries its hardest not to.

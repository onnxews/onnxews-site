Johnson's All-Around Night Powers Hawks Past Hornets 111-97

Jalen Johnson had 26 points, six rebounds, six assists and four steals as Atlanta shook off a 14-point first-half deficit to beat Charlotte for its third straight win.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Jalen Johnson compiled 26 points, six rebounds and six assists on Friday to help the Atlanta Hawks post a 111-97 victory over the visiting Charlotte Hornets in NBA Cup East Group C play. Johnson also had four steals in his most complete performance of the season, and the Hawks overcame a slow start to win their third straight game.

Atlanta's start was anything but smooth. The Hawks jumped out to an 11-5 lead on consecutive baskets by Nickeil Alexander-Walker, but Charlotte answered with 14 straight points and went ahead by eight on a Brandon Miller three-pointer midway through the first quarter.

Liam McNeeley gave Charlotte a 28-17 lead before a late Atlanta spurt, and Royce O'Neale closed the first quarter with a triple to give the Hornets a 31-23 advantage entering the second. The Hornets pushed their first-half lead to 14 points before Johnson took over.

Johnson scored eight points during a 12-0 Atlanta run that trimmed the deficit to 50-48 with 2:47 left in the first half. Charlotte still led 57-56 at halftime, with Alexander-Walker leading all scorers with 14 first-half points and 10 Hornets reaching the scoresheet in the opening half.

Atlanta seized control after the break. The Hawks pulled within a point by halftime, then took a 76-69 lead on Johnson's three-point play with 4:35 left in the third quarter. The cushion ballooned to 86-74 on Aaron Wiggins' triple, and Atlanta led 87-76 after three quarters.

Charlotte pulled within nine early in the fourth when Coby White found Moussa Diabate for a dunk, but Johnson's steal and dunk stamped a 10-0 Atlanta run that pushed the lead to 99-80. Okongwu dunked and Wiggins drilled a triple to give Atlanta a 104-86 edge with 5:17 remaining, icing the win.

Wiggins bailed out Atlanta's depleted backcourt with 19 points, and Alexander-Walker added 17. Onyeka Okongwu had 16 points and Dyson Daniels tallied 11 points, 10 assists and eight rebounds for the Hawks, who won their seventh game in nine tries.

Daniels, who leads the NBA in steals and deflections, had four steals — matching Johnson's total — as the Hawks recorded 12 steals and took a 36-16 advantage in points off turnovers. That defensive pressure was the difference once Atlanta's offense found its rhythm.

The contrast between the halves told the story. Ten Hornets reached the scoresheet in the opening half, when Charlotte moved the ball freely and built a 14-point lead. After the break, Atlanta's pressure changed the game entirely: the Hawks turned Charlotte's mistakes into fast-break points, with Johnson's steal and dunk the signature moment of a 10-0 run that broke the game open. Once the turnovers started, the Hornets never found a way to settle the game back down.

Diabate led Charlotte with 21 points, while Miller scored 19 for the Hornets, who lost their fourth straight game and their 10th in 11. Naz Reid collected 12 points and 10 rebounds, and White finished with 11 points.

Charlotte actually won the battle on the glass 49-37 and had 29 second-chance points compared with 11 for Atlanta, but the Hornets could not protect the ball or slow the Hawks once the game turned. Atlanta improved to 2-1 in Cup action, while Charlotte fell to 0-3. The Hawks' next Cup test comes Tuesday against Boston, a game with major group implications.

Why it matters: The Hawks' third straight win matters because of how it was built: Atlanta trailed by 14 in the first half, lost the rebounding battle by 12 and gave up 29 second-chance points — and still won by 14. The 36-16 edge in points off turnovers explains it. Johnson's 26 points, six rebounds, six assists and four steals, paired with Daniels' near triple-double and four more steals, show a team that can defend its way out of a bad shooting start, which is exactly the identity Atlanta needs with a depleted backcourt. For Charlotte, the pattern is now familiar: a hot start, a lead, and then a collapse once the turnovers mount. Losers of four straight and 10 of 11 as of Nov. 20, the Hornets are 0-3 in Cup play and running out of answers for the second half of games.

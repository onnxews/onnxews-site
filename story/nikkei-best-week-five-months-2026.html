Japan's Nikkei Posts Its Best Week in Five Months as AI Optimism Returns

The Nikkei 225 gained 6.46 percent on the week, its largest rise since June, as chip stocks rebounded on renewed artificial intelligence optimism.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Japan's Nikkei 225 stock average closed the week at 61,142.35 on Friday, up 6.46 percent for the week in its best weekly performance in five months, as renewed optimism about artificial intelligence lifted chip stocks and strong corporate earnings drew buyers back to Tokyo. The index rose 322.99 points, or 0.53 percent, on Friday alone, capping a week in which it gained 3,707.58 points, the largest one-week point and percentage gain since the week ending June 19, 2026, according to Dow Jones Market Data. The Nikkei's 6.46 percent weekly gain was its biggest since mid-June, and the broader Topix index rose 6.3 percent on the week to 3,941.57, its best weekly performance since mid-August 2024.

Semiconductor and artificial intelligence stocks led the rally. Advantest, which makes testing equipment for chipmakers, rose 2.08 percent on Friday, and Tokyo Electron gained 1.69 percent, as investors who had sold AI-related shares in recent weeks returned to the sector. "The rise in semiconductor- and AI-related shares, which had been under pressure for a relatively long period, appears to have been a factor behind this week's gains in the Nikkei average and Topix," said Maki Sawada, a strategist at Nomura Securities. SoftBank was the notable exception, falling 5.11 percent on Friday, a reminder that the rally was driven by chipmakers rather than the broader technology sector. Breadth was positive but not overwhelming: 132 stocks on the Nikkei rose on Friday, 91 fell and two were unchanged.

The week's biggest individual movers told the story of a market rewarding earnings and punishing disappointment. Sapporo Holdings rose 8.56 percent, the largest gain on the index, followed by Trend Micro at 8.32 percent and Amada at 7.59 percent. On the downside, Mercari fell 8.88 percent, Japan Steel Works dropped 8.46 percent and Kawasaki Heavy Industries slid 8.29 percent. The dispersion reflects a market in which corporate results, rather than a single theme, are driving individual stocks, even as the AI narrative sets the direction for the index as a whole.

Friday's gain was also a recovery from a wobble earlier in the week. The index had weakened on Thursday amid concerns about Nvidia, after the U.S. chipmaker's chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned, raising questions about American semiconductor export policy and access to the Chinese market. But gains in Broadcom and Alphabet suggested that broader confidence in artificial intelligence spending remained intact, and Japanese chip stocks followed their U.S. counterparts higher on Friday. The rebound capped a week in which the Nikkei rose on four of the past five trading days, a sign that buyers were willing to step in on weakness rather than wait for confirmation.

The rally leaves the Nikkei up 21.46 percent for the year, a gain of 10,802.87 points since the start of January, and up 21.37 percent from where it stood 52 weeks ago.

The index remains 15.51 percent below its record closing high of 72,366.34, set on June 25, 2026, a reminder of how far it fell during the summer sell-off, and it is down 3.39 percent for the month of November despite this week's surge. Its 52-week low was 48,537.70 on November 19, 2025, so the market has climbed more than a quarter from its trough in the past year, a recovery driven more recently by the global AI spending boom.

The Topix's 6.3 percent weekly gain, its best since mid-August 2024, shows the rally was not confined to the Nikkei's large-cap exporters. The broader index, which covers more than 2,000 companies, rose 0.11 percent on Friday to close at 3,941.57, and its performance suggests domestic-facing companies also benefited from the week's optimism. That breadth matters, because the Nikkei's earlier gains this year were concentrated in a handful of chip and export names, while the Topix lagged. A rally that lifts both indexes is a healthier one, and it suggests investors are buying Japan rather than just buying AI.

Whether the rally holds depends on the same forces that drove it: U.S. technology earnings, the yen, and the Federal Reserve's December decision, which will set the tone for global risk appetite. Japanese chipmakers are tied to the American AI supply chain, so any further disruption at Nvidia or a shift in U.S. export policy toward China would hit Tokyo quickly. For now, though, the week's numbers speak for themselves: the best week in five months, the biggest point gain since June, and a market that has recovered more than a fifth from its November 2025 low. The Nikkei's next test is whether it can hold above 61,000 and take aim at its June record, or whether this week proves to be a bounce inside a longer correction.

Why it matters: Japan's best week in five months matters because it shows the global AI trade is alive even after a week that began with the resignation of Nvidia's chief executive. The Nikkei's 6.46 percent gain, led by chipmakers Advantest and Tokyo Electron, came alongside a 6.3 percent rise in the broader Topix, which means the rally reached beyond the usual large-cap exporters into the wider market. With the index still 15.51 percent below its June record and down for November despite the surge, the week looks less like a new high and more like a powerful rebound inside a correction. What matters next is whether Japanese chip stocks can hold their gains if U.S. export policy toward China tightens, and whether the Fed's December decision keeps global risk appetite intact.

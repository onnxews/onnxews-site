Doncic Returns With 32 Points as Lakers Beat Hawks 124-114 for Sixth Straight Win

Luka Doncic nearly posted a triple-double in his return from back tightness, and even reminded Atlanta fans of the 2018 draft trade that sent him away.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Luka Doncic did not miss a beat. The Los Angeles Lakers star nearly notched a triple-double on Monday night in his return from injury, tallying 32 points, 10 assists and eight rebounds in a 124-114 win against the Atlanta Hawks at State Farm Arena. The victory extended the Lakers' winning streak to six games and improved them to 10-2, one of the best records in the NBA.

Doncic missed one game with back tightness, a win over the Detroit Pistons on Sunday, but he returned to the starting lineup a day later and picked up exactly where he left off. He shot 11 of 22 from the field and made all seven of his free throws, and he added five steals and two blocked shots in a night when he stuffed the stat sheet. His run of scoring 40 or more points ended at four games, but the Lakers will happily trade that streak for a sixth straight win.

The Lakers did it without Austin Reaves, who was out with an illness one night after scoring a season-high 43 points in the win at Detroit. In his absence, the supporting cast stepped up. Sandro Mamukelashvili scored a season-high 23 points off the bench on 7-of-13 shooting, including five of eight from three-point range, Walker Kessler added 21 points and 10 rebounds, and Collin Sexton contributed 18 points and six assists. It was the kind of deep, balanced performance that wins games when a star is missing.

Atlanta, for its part, had its four-game winning streak snapped, along with a three-game home winning streak against the Lakers. CJ McCollum led the Hawks with 21 points off the bench, and Nickeil Alexander-Walker added 20, but the Hawks could not keep up with a Lakers team that had too many weapons. The Hawks took an early 15-7 lead in the first quarter, but the Lakers responded with a 23-5 run to take a 41-27 lead, and they did not trail again for the rest of the game.

The Hawks made it interesting late. They shot 12 of 24 in the third quarter and closed the gap to three points, and when Dyson Daniels cut the Lakers' lead to 115-111 with three minutes and 30 seconds to play, the game was suddenly in the balance. But a 6-0 burst by Los Angeles put it out of reach, with Doncic driving for a layup and assisting on another basket during the run. It was a reminder that the Lakers, for all their star power, know how to close a game.

Doncic also gave the Atlanta crowd something to remember. During the game, he engaged in a courtside exchange with Hawks fans, reminding them that Atlanta once held the keys to his basketball future before trading them away. The exchange reopened a wound from the 2018 NBA Draft, when Atlanta selected Doncic third overall before trading him to the Dallas Mavericks in a deal involving Trae Young. Doncic torched the Hawks for 32 points, 10 assists, eight rebounds, five steals and two blocks, becoming the first Lakers player to record multiple games with at least 30 points, five assists, five steals and two blocks.

The numbers behind Doncic's season are staggering. He came into the game averaging a league-high 39.3 points per game, and his last outing before the back injury was a 53-point effort in a win over the Portland Trail Blazers on Friday, November 6. He is a six-time All-Star playing the best basketball of his career, and the Lakers, at 10-2, are reaping the rewards. The team came into the game ranked 21st in the league in defensive efficiency, a weakness that has not mattered much while the offence is scoring at this rate.

For the Hawks, the loss is a setback, but not a disaster. They had won four straight before running into the Lakers, and they pushed the game into the final minutes despite playing against one of the best players in the world. The challenge for Atlanta is to find a way to beat the elite teams, not just the teams around them in the standings, and nights like this one show how far they still have to go.

What happens next for the Lakers is a trip to Milwaukee to play the Bucks on Wednesday, and a chance to extend the winning streak to seven. For Doncic, the back tightness appears to be behind him, and the only question is how long he can keep up this level of play. If the answer is all season, the Lakers will be very hard to stop, and the rest of the league knows it. Atlanta, meanwhile, will have to live with the reminder that they once had him, and let him go.

Duckett's 133 Powers England to First ODI Series Win Over Australia in Eight Years

Ben Duckett hit a match-winning century in Hobart as England won the third one-day international by 97 runs to take the series 2-1.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ben Duckett hit a match-winning 133 as England beat Australia by 97 runs in the third one-day international in Hobart on Wednesday, November 18, to win the series 2-1. It was England's first one-day series win against Australia in eight years, after defeats in 2020, 2022, and 2024.

England posted 328 all out in 48.3 overs at Bellerive Oval, and Australia were bowled out for 231 in 46.5 overs in reply, chasing 329 to win. The margin, 97 runs, reflected a game that England controlled for most of the day.

Duckett was the difference. The left-hander hit 15 fours and three sixes in his 133 from 115 balls, his seventh one-day international hundred and his third in five games. He reached his fifty from 36 balls and his hundred from 94, and he held the innings together for more than two and a half hours, even as wickets fell around him.

He had his luck. Alex Carey dropped him on 29 at square leg, and he survived another chance when a drive went through the hands of Spencer Johnson. After that, he made Australia pay, picking up the pace with three consecutive boundaries off Xavier Bartlett and clearing the ropes twice more with a pair of slog sweeps before he was finally caught in the 39th over.

Tom Banton gave England a fast start, hitting 44 from 35 balls in an opening stand of 74, and Will Jacks added a handy 59 from 48 balls late in the innings to make sure England had enough runs. Jack Edwards took four for 31 for Australia, and Matt Renshaw took three for 51.

Australia's chase never got going. Travis Head and Mitch Marsh were both dismissed in single figures, and the hosts were 34 for three before Renshaw, who top-scored with 55, and Carey, who made 37 before being run out, steadied the innings. Cameron Green was hit on the helmet by a bouncer from Gus Atkinson and had to leave the field, with Ollie Peake coming on as a concussion substitute and making five.

Sonny Baker was the pick of England's bowlers, taking four for 45, and Liam Dawson took three for 42. Baker removed Peake as well as Bartlett, and came close to a first five-wicket haul for England, before Harry Brook took the final wicket, tempting Spencer Johnson out for his first one-day international scalp.

The series had started badly for England. They were bowled out for 131 in the first match in Perth and lost by seven wickets, but they bounced back in Adelaide, winning by 135 runs, and then won the decider in Hobart. It was also England's first one-day international win in Hobart.

Captain Harry Brook said after the game: "It wasn't an ideal start to the series, notoriously we've been a bit slow getting into series." "But the way we bounced back in Adelaide and the performance we put in today was outstanding. The boys should be really proud." Australia's captain, Mitch Marsh, was honest about the defeat, saying: "England outplayed us in the last couple of games and there is lots of room for improvement for our team. We have a bit of work to do." The two teams now move on to a five-match Twenty20 series, starting on Saturday, November 21, in Melbourne.

Why it matters: This is a significant result for England's one-day team, and not just because it ended an eight-year wait. England had lost three straight series to Australia in this format, and they started this one by being bowled out for 131 in Perth. To bounce back with two big wins, built on Duckett's third hundred in five games and a young bowling attack led by Baker, suggests the team is finding a new identity. The result also came on a day when Australian cricket was in turmoil off the field, with Cameron Bancroft's latest revelations about the 2018 sandpaper scandal dominating the news. England's win will not fix Australian cricket's problems, but it will give their own team real confidence going into the Twenty20 series.

Baidu's AI Cloud Nearly Doubles as Search Ads Slide 22%

The Chinese search giant reported third-quarter results on Monday showing its AI business now makes up more than half of its core revenue — but heavy spending on data centers pushed free cash flow into negative territory.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Baidu reported third-quarter results on Monday showing two very different businesses inside one company: an AI cloud unit that nearly doubled, and the search advertising business that built the company, which fell 22%. Total revenue slipped 1% and profit missed expectations, but the AI growth was strong enough to lift the shares slightly in premarket trading.

Baidu reported third-quarter revenue of 30.8 billion yuan, or about $4.60 billion, down 1% from a year earlier, according to the company's results release. Net income attributable to Baidu was 1.4 billion yuan, equivalent to about $209 million, worse than market expectations for 1.65 billion yuan. The company recorded a loss in the year-ago period due to an impairment charge for long-lived assets. Revenue was roughly in line with analysts' estimate in a FactSet consensus.

The headline numbers hide the split underneath. Online marketing, the search-ad business that built Baidu, fell 22% to 11.9 billion yuan, from a year earlier. Strip out iQIYI, the streaming arm, and the picture changes: Baidu General Business revenue was 25.0 billion yuan, up 1% year on year. iQIYI is what dragged the group total negative, bringing in 6.0 billion yuan, down 10%.

The reason General Business grew at all is the AI-powered segment, which made 13.2 billion yuan, up 37% and now 53% of General Business revenue. AI Cloud Infra revenue reached 8.2 billion yuan, up 94%, and GPU cloud alone rose 191% from a year earlier. Revenue from external customers' token usage on the Qianfan model-as-a-service platform increased 1,159%. Baidu said AI cloud infrastructure had “emerged as a major driver” of the business.

Chief executive Robin Li said in the release that the company's full-stack AI investments are generating meaningful growth, adding that AI Cloud Infra had become a major revenue driver. The company expects its inference-focused Kunlunxin M100 chip to be ready for volume production by the end of 2026.

The profit figures show the cost of the shift. Operating income was 626 million yuan, a 2% margin. Non-GAAP operating income was 1.7 billion yuan, a 6% margin. On standard accounting, Baidu made 1.4 billion yuan, against a loss a year ago, when it wrote down long-lived assets. Non-GAAP earnings per American depositary share came in at $1.01, beating the roughly $0.92 consensus tracked by GuruFocus.

Cash is the bigger concern. Operating cash flow was 2.8 billion yuan, but heavy spending on AI data centres left free cash flow, the cash left after running the business and paying for new equipment, at about negative 4.6 billion yuan, or about negative $682 million. Baidu spent 7.35 billion yuan on capital expenditures in the quarter. Chief financial officer Haijian He said Baidu would focus on investment returns and disciplined capital allocation while continuing to invest in AI.

Baidu still has 276.6 billion yuan, or about $41.25 billion, in cash and investments at the end of September, so it can fund the shift for some time. It has returned $373 million through buybacks since the start of 2026, and expects to declare its first-ever dividend before the end of 2026, subject to board approval and market conditions. Shares of the Beijing-based company have dropped more than 40% this year amid weak sentiment toward tech stocks, and its American depositary receipts were 0.4% higher in premarket trading after the results.

Analysts said ahead of the earnings that Baidu's profit could be pressured in the near term. “A revenue-mix shift to [the] lower-margin cloud business could pressure margins,” Deutsche Bank said in a note, maintaining its revenue forecasts but cutting adjusted net profit projections for this year and next by 15% and 14%, respectively. Baidu faces fierce competition in the AI sector, with peers including Alibaba and ByteDance all racing to build frontier models, and its Ernie Bot chatbot — the first real Chinese contender to ChatGPT — has seen its popularity fall in recent quarters.

The quarter leaves Baidu in the same place as many of its peers: growing fast in AI infrastructure, shrinking in its legacy business, and spending heavily to make the transition. The difference is that Baidu's AI business is now more than half of its core revenue. Whether that is enough to return the company to growth — and to profit — depends on whether the cloud margins improve faster than the search ads keep falling.

Why it matters: Baidu matters as a read on the Chinese AI market and on the economics of the AI boom itself: the company is growing its cloud business 94% a year and its GPU cloud revenue 191%, yet it is burning cash and its profit is falling, because the new business carries lower margins than the search ads it replaces. That is the same tension facing the American AI giants, and it is why Baidu's quarter is worth more than its $4.6 billion of revenue suggests. Orbit covered the AI spending debate in the Nvidia story; Baidu shows the other side of it — a company that has already made the pivot and is paying for it.

Nicki Minaj's a Dumb Ass

She built a career off Black fans, then spent years cashing that love in for a seat at somebody else's table. The press secretary rumors just made it official.

By Peter Osibajo · Orbit News Network · Oct 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Let's just say it plain: Nicki Minaj spent years letting Black people carry her to the top, then turned around and sold that same love right back to the people who never loved her in the first place.

Because let's not rewrite history. Nicki did not get here by herself. She came up through hip-hop, built the Barbz into one of the most loyal fanbases music has ever seen, and became the biggest female rapper alive off the backs of Black fans — Black women especially — who streamed every album, learned every bar, defended her in every stan war, and made her wigs, her slang, her whole persona part of the culture. We made her. That's not debatable, that's the receipt.

And what did we get for it? A slow-motion pivot that's been running for years now. The cozying up to Trump. The rally stages. The “forever and ever” pledge on a Michigan stage on Sunday, campaigning for Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers and gubernatorial candidate John James. The speech telling a crowd: “We don't want our children to be bombarded with sex changes, right? We want people to leave the children alone. Leave them out of it.” The years-long grudge against California Gov. Gavin Newsom — she calls him “Newscum” — because, in her telling, he ignored her after swatting incidents at her Beverly Hills home. Every time she needed a new audience, she found one further away from the people who built her.

Then came Monday, and the mask fully slipped. White House director of communications Stephen Cheung posted on X: “Ladies and gentlemen, I think we may have found the new White House Press Secretary!” — attaching a video of Minaj praising Trump's work ethic. White House adviser Alex Bruesewitz chimed in: “Hey Barbz, what do we think about this idea??” And Nicki? She didn't shut it down. She quote-tweeted it with American flag and nervous laughter emoji, playing with the idea like it was a cute little game. The job, by the way, has been vacant since Katie Zacharia quit after just five days. Nobody serious has confirmed anything — a White House spokesperson just referred reporters back to Cheung's post — but Nicki didn't need it to be real. The attention was the point.

Here's the trick, and honestly it's been running for years: she figured out she could take Black love, Black culture, Black defense — all of it — and trade it for proximity to power that never had Black people's interests at heart. The Barbz defended her through every beef, every lawsuit, every messy era, and the payoff for that loyalty was watching her stand on a rally stage telling a crowd she'll support Trump “forever and ever,” while Trump himself introduced her by telling the crowd he'd “looked at one hell of a beautiful a— before: Nicki Minaj” and “That's hard to miss. Stand up! That's hard to miss.” That's not respect. That's a prop. And she smiled through it.

So yeah — Nicki Minaj's a dumb ass. Not because she has opinions. Because she spent years making Black people believe she was for us, then auctioned that belief off the second a bigger stage called. Newsom's office, which has sparred with her all year, answered the press-secretary chatter with four words: “We can only pray.” On this one, hard to argue. The Barbz deserved better. And the briefing room? It deserves better than a meme candidacy too.

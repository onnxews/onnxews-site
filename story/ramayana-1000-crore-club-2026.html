'Ramayana' Crosses ₹1,000 Crore Worldwide in Eight Days

Nitesh Tiwari's epic, starring Ranbir Kapoor as Lord Rama, is the 11th Indian film to join the ₹1,000-crore club — and it passed the lifetime domestic totals of 'Pathaan' and 'Animal' in just over a week.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

"Ramayana" crossed ₹1,000 crore at the worldwide box office in eight days, becoming the 11th Indian film to reach the milestone, according to trade tracker Sacnilk.

The film, directed by Nitesh Tiwari and released on Thursday, November 5, took ₹53.50 crore nett in India on its eighth day, Thursday, November 12, lifting its India nett total to ₹564.60 crore. Its India gross stands at ₹675.05 crore, with ₹358.50 crore from overseas, for a worldwide gross of ₹1,033.55 crore.

The numbers add up: the domestic gross of ₹675.05 crore plus overseas earnings of ₹358.50 crore equals the worldwide total of ₹1,033.55 crore. Trade estimates vary slightly — Pinkvilla puts the worldwide figure closer to ₹1,005 crore — but both trackers agree the film has passed the ₹1,000-crore mark.

The pace is striking. In eight days, "Ramayana" has already passed the lifetime domestic collections of "Pathaan" (₹544.28 crore) and "Animal" (₹556.36 crore), two of the biggest Hindi hits of recent years.

The film opened to ₹64.85 crore nett in India on its first day, then surged over the Diwali holiday, taking ₹107.50 crore on day five and ₹94.10 crore on day six. The Hindi version accounts for ₹437 crore of the domestic total.

Ranbir Kapoor stars as Lord Rama, with Sai Pallavi as Sita, Ravi Dubey as Lakshmana and Yash — who also produced the film with Namit Malhotra — as Ravana. The cast and the scale of the production, built around the ancient epic, have made it one of the most anticipated Indian releases of the year.

The film's run is not finished. A sequel is planned for Diwali 2028, with Sunny Deol set to play Hanuman, and the producers will be hoping the first part's momentum carries through the coming weeks as it chases the all-time records.

The ₹1,000-crore club remains a small one — only 11 Indian films have reached it — and "Ramayana" joined it in its second weekend, a sign of how big event releases have become in the Indian market, where a festival opening can now rival the biggest global launches.

For the industry, the film is also a test of the two-part epic model: the first part must earn enough to justify the second, and early signs suggest it has cleared that bar with room to spare.

Trade trackers will now watch the second weekend closely. The Diwali surge is over, and how the film holds once the holiday crowds fade will decide whether it settles as a big hit or pushes on toward the top of the all-time list, according to Madhyamam.

The production itself is one of the biggest undertakings in Indian cinema, built around the Ramayana, the ancient Sanskrit epic that remains one of the most retold stories in the world. Casting Ranbir Kapoor as Rama and Yash as Ravana put two of Indian cinema's biggest stars on opposite sides of the same story, and the film's scale — from its battle sequences to its music — was designed to be seen on the biggest screens available.

The Diwali release date was no accident. The festival is the biggest moviegoing period of the year in India, and the film's day-five and day-six numbers — ₹107.50 crore and ₹94.10 crore — show how much of the opening week was driven by holiday audiences. The question now is whether the film can hold once the festival crowds thin out, and whether its second weekend confirms it as a genuine phenomenon rather than a holiday spike.

Why it matters: The film's run matters because it shows how large the Indian theatrical market has become when a festival release lands: a ₹1,000-crore total in eight days puts 'Ramayana' in a club of only 11 Indian films, and passing the lifetime totals of 'Pathaan' and 'Animal' in just over a week underlines the scale. The Diwali timing was clearly part of the plan, and the sequel — already dated for Diwali 2028 — means the first part's success is also a down payment on the next one. What to watch is the second-weekend hold, which will show whether the film has legs beyond the holiday rush.

Google's November Pixel Drop Brings Gemini Intelligence to the Pixel 10

Google's latest Pixel Drop pushes the Gemini Intelligence features from the Pixel 11 series onto last year's Pixel 10 phones — plus a new item tracker, smarter call screening and more.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Google's November Pixel Drop is rolling out, and its headline change brings the Gemini Intelligence features that debuted on the Pixel 11 series to last year's Pixel 10 phones. Updates started rolling out on November 10 and will continue over the next several weeks, with availability varying by device and carrier.

Gemini Intelligence is now available on the Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL and Pixel 10 Pro Fold — but not the Pixel 10a. It brings Rambler, personalized suggestions and multi-step task handling to phones that launched a year ago. Rambler is a voice-typing upgrade in Gboard that removes filler words like “um,” tidies up the parts where you correct yourself mid-sentence and formats spoken thoughts into clean, polished text. Unlike some of the other Gemini Intelligence features, Rambler is available in all regions and languages.

Gemini can now take care of multi-step tasks such as ordering groceries, booking a ride, reordering takeout or finding a restaurant reservation, working with your apps in the background while you can view or end the task at any time. That automation is now available in Japan, the UK and India, alongside the United States. Google also says the Pixel will offer proactive suggestions, such as sharing an ETA with a tap when someone asks when you will arrive, with that feature coming soon to the Pixel Watch 4.

Google is also expanding an early experiment that lets Gemini make routine phone calls on your behalf. It is now open to all Gemini users in public beta on the Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 series, letting the assistant check whether a shop has an item in stock or reschedule an appointment, with a live transcript the user can follow and take over at any point.

On the Pixel 11 series in the United States, Call Screen has been upgraded, with Gemini now asking unknown callers why they are reaching out in a much more conversational manner until the user decides to jump in or end the call. A live transcript appears on screen throughout. Notification summaries now support German, French, Spanish, Italian, Hindi and Traditional Chinese, while Notification Organizer, which groups and silences lower-priority notifications, now works in Japanese and German. Both features need a Pixel 9 or newer, excluding the Pixel 9a and Pixel 10a.

The drop also adds a piece of hardware. Pixel Tag, Google's small item tracker, is available starting November 11 at $29.99 for one or $99.99 for a four-pack. It is slim enough to fit in a wallet and durable enough to attach to belongings, with a battery that lasts more than a year. The Find Hub network of more than 1 billion Android devices helps locate belongings even miles away, and ultra-wideband gives visual direction and distance guidance when the item is nearby.

Smaller additions fill out the update. Custom widgets can now be built from a plain-language description, such as a tracker for monthly spending or a countdown that automatically pulls in flight and reservation details, and they are coming soon to the Pixel Watch 4 and Pixel Watch 5. There are new custom icon styles for the home screen, Pixel search now works across photos, emails, messages, calendar events and settings on Pixel 6 and newer, and Extreme Charging Mode — which pauses background activity to speed up charging — is expanding to the Pixel 10 series.

Watches get attention too. Step-by-step workouts are rolling out to Pixel Watch 3 and newer models, with rep and weight logging, and in the next few weeks a Pixel Watch will automatically unlock when it is on the wrist and the nearby phone has been unlocked. Google Fi is also launching a new super network that automatically switches between two of America's top 5G networks and millions of secure Wi-Fi networks, with 50 percent off select unlimited plans for 12 months for new Fi users who sign up by December 1.

The timing matters because the Pixel 10 line is barely a year old, and Google is using the drop to close the gap between its two most recent phone generations rather than making owners wait for new hardware. As of November 11, the rollout is under way in phases, so some features may take days or weeks to reach every device — and the Pixel 10a, the budget model in the family, sits outside the Gemini Intelligence upgrade entirely.

Why it matters: The drop matters because it shows how Google now treats its phones: the hardware is the platform, and the AI features are the product. Bringing Gemini Intelligence to the Pixel 10 a year after launch keeps last year's buyers in the fold and gives Google a much larger base of phones demonstrating its assistant — including the phone-call experiment, which is the most ambitious and the most likely to draw scrutiny. The test from here is whether owners actually hand routine calls and errands to Gemini, and whether the staged rollout reaches them before the novelty wears off. Watch for usage numbers and for the next Pixel Drop to see which of these experiments graduate and which quietly disappear.

James Gunn and Peter Safran Are Out at DC Studios as Skydance Hands the Keys to Marvel Veteran Jeremy Latcham

Barely a month after Skydance took over Warner Bros. Discovery, the studio's DC chiefs are stepping down, and the man who helped build the Marvel Cinematic Universe will run DC's film, TV and animation slate from December 1.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Skydance on Monday named former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham to lead DC Studios, replacing co-heads James Gunn and Peter Safran in one of the most surprising executive shake-ups Hollywood has seen in years. The change, announced on November 9, comes barely a month after Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery closed, and it ends a four-year run in which Gunn and Safran rebuilt DC's screen universe from scratch.

Latcham will take over on December 1 as president and chief creative officer of DC Studios, leading its film, television and animation slate and overseeing the creative pipeline, production, talent and strategy. He will report to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, the co-chairs of Skydance Motion Picture Group. It is a consolidation of power: where Gunn and Safran shared leadership as co-chairs and co-chief executives, Latcham will run the studio alone.

Gunn and Safran took over DC Studios on November 1, 2022, and in January 2023 they announced a ten-project slate for the first chapter of the new DC Universe, titled Gods and Monsters. In their four years in charge, they delivered three films, Superman, Supergirl and Clayface, and a handful of shows, including Creature Commandos. The results were mixed: Superman earned 618 million dollars worldwide, a solid success, while Supergirl stumbled badly at the box office, leaving the new universe on uncertain footing.

Latcham is one of Marvel Studios' earliest employees. He joined the company in 2004 and rose to senior vice president of production and development, producing foundational Marvel Cinematic Universe hits including Iron Man and The Avengers. That resume is exactly what Skydance is buying: a veteran who knows how to build a connected universe of films that audiences will follow from one instalment to the next. His appointment also carries a certain irony, since DC is now being run by a man who helped build its biggest rival.

The question everyone in Hollywood is asking is whether Gunn and Safran were pushed out or walked away. David Ellison, Skydance's chairman and chief executive, had assured the public and shareholders that the pair would retain their positions after the acquisition, and the sudden reversal has observers picking over the wording of the announcements. Variety's early framing described the pair as fired, while later reports described them as stepping down, and the full picture of who initiated the exit has not been established.

Gunn himself sounded relieved rather than bitter. In a lengthy farewell message posted to his Threads account, he said he would miss the people and miss being a co-caretaker of the great DC characters, but that he was incredibly relieved and excited to get back to pure filmmaking. He stressed that he and Safran are not immediately done with DC or superheroes: they have Man of Tomorrow, the Superman sequel, coming out next summer, and Gunn has been engaged on another DC project which he is writing. He will direct Man of Tomorrow, which is due in theatres on July 9, 2027.

The pair will also remain involved as producers on The Batman Part II, the sequel to Matt Reeves' The Batman, which is due in 2028, along with the 2027 miniseries The People v Gorilla Grodd. But continuing to make a film is not the same as controlling the studio that commissions the next one, and no comprehensive plan has been announced establishing which parts of their wider universe Latcham will preserve, change or approve. The third chapter of what Gunn called his Super-Family saga is in production, but everything beyond it is now an open question.

Skydance's leadership backed Latcham warmly. Ellison and co-chief executive Ynon Kreiz said in a joint statement that Jeremy has been a longtime creative partner to both, and that they have complete confidence James and Peter will ensure a smooth, collaborative transition. Ellison also posted on social media that he was thrilled to welcome Jeremy to DC Studios. The warmth of the welcome, set against the suddenness of the exit, has only deepened the sense that this was a decision made at the top, quickly, and with little room for negotiation.

For fans, the shake-up raises three immediate questions. Will the DC Universe get another reboot, or will Latcham build on what Gunn and Safran started? Is Gunn done with the DC Universe completely, or will he return for more films after Man of Tomorrow? And what happens to the many projects in development that never had a release date? None of those questions has an answer yet, and the answers will shape the next decade of superhero cinema.

What happens next begins on December 1, when Latcham formally takes charge. His first decisions will be closely watched: which films move forward, which quietly disappear, and whether the tone and identity Gunn and Safran gave the DC Universe survive under new management. The superhero era has always been a story of studios as much as of heroes, and this week the studio changed hands in the most dramatic way possible.

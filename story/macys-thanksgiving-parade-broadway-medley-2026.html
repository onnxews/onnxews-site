Macy's 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade Swaps Solo Broadway Numbers for One Big Medley

For its landmark 100th march, the parade will feature a single ten-minute Broadway medley with performers from nine productions, introduced by Julianne Hough, plus a tribute to Dolly Parton.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will mark its 100th edition with a different kind of Broadway moment. Rather than presenting multiple numbers from current shows, as it has in past years, the parade on Thursday, November 26, will feature a single ten-minute medley taking viewers on a musical journey through Broadway past and present, filled with beloved songs and Easter eggs for theatre fans to discover along the way.

The medley will bring together performers from nine Broadway productions: The Book of Mormon, Chicago, Aladdin, Hamilton, The Lost Boys, The Rocky Horror Show, Schmigadoon! and Wicked, all currently running, plus Rent, celebrating its 30th anniversary as the only musical in the number not currently running on Broadway. Julianne Hough, who is readying a Broadway return in Damn Yankees, will introduce the segment, which has been arranged by Tony and Pulitzer-winning Next to Normal songwriter Tom Kitt and creative producer Sarah O'Gleby.

The event airs live on NBC and Peacock on November 26, beginning at 8:30 a.m. in all United States time zones and also airing live on Telemundo. Today hosts Savannah Guthrie, Hoda Kotb and Al Roker will return as hosts of the annual broadcast, which will have an encore on NBC at 2 p.m. The parade begins at 8:30 a.m., traveling two and a half miles from Manhattan's Upper West Side to Macy's Herald Square, with public viewing at designated locations along the route.

The 100th celebration will include performers from across entertainment, including a tribute to the late Grammy-winning Dolly Parton led by Megan Hilty, the two-time Tony nominee who starred in the musical stage adaptation of Parton's 9 to 5 and currently appears in It's a Wonderful Life: A Live Radio Play. Country singer Megan Moroney and Grammy nominee Teddy Swims are also set to perform.

Float performances will include Natasha Bedingfield, Baha Men, Blues Traveler, DEVO, Jimmy Fallon and The Roots, Feid, EJAE, Mckenna Grace, Indigo Girls, Icona Pop, JoJo, Wyclef Jean, Queen Latifah, Il Volo, Natalie Merchant, Jamie Miller, Ryan Peter Murphy, Leslie Odom Jr., Keke Palmer, Anthony Ramos, RIIZE and Smash Mouth. Special appearances will come from Jack McBrayer, Janet McTeer, John Stamos, Melissa Joan Hart, Megan Hilty, Holly Robinson Peete and Rodney Peete, along with Paddington the Musical, which is also listed among the expected parade float appearances, separate from the Broadway medley.

New floats in the 2026 lineup include Dashing All the Way with DoorDash, Hogwarts Holiday with HBO, Mr. Peanut Power Plant with Planters, Purina Dog Chow Salute to Service Dogs with Purina Dog Chow, Road Trip to Parade with GEICO, and Macy's Tony Sarg's Amazing Marionettes. The new Macy's All Stars float will feature iconic characters from past parades, including Angry Birds' Red, Arthur, Bugs Bunny, Bullwinkle J. Moose, Curious George, Adventure Time's Finn and Jake, Jimmy Neutron, Popeye and Olive Oyl and Swee'Pea, Pink Panther, Poppy from Trolls, Scooby-Doo, Sonic the Hedgehog, the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles and Wild Thing.

Viewers can also expect a record-breaking 100 inflatable elements, and a selection of Macy's Blue Sky Gallery balloons will return to the New York City skyline to showcase designs from world-renowned artists, including Tim Burton, Keith Haring, Jeff Koons and Yayoi Kusama. More than 11,000 volunteers and participants will bring the spectacle to life before the parade reaches its climactic finale: the arrival of Santa Claus and the official start of the holiday season.

A new documentary, America's Parade: 100 Years of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, is set to air November 20 at 9 p.m. Eastern Time on NBC and begin streaming the next day on Peacock. The film will pull back the curtain on the landmark 100th parade, celebrating the people, artistry and imagination behind the tradition, blending archival moments with an inside look at preparations for the march. NBC's annual Countdown to Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade special will return for a fifth year and air November 25 at 8 p.m. Eastern Time on NBC, simulcast on Peacock.

Founded by Macy's colleagues, their friends and their families, the parade has grown from its 1924 debut into a national tradition, according to Playbill's report on the 100th parade plans. With the exception of a three-year break during World War II, it has delivered a century of Thanksgiving mornings. The decision to mark the centenary with a single shared Broadway number, rather than a string of separate performances, fits the occasion: a parade that began as a local celebration now treats Broadway itself as the headliner.

Why it matters: The medley is a smart choice for a centenary parade. In past years the Broadway segment has been a relay of separate numbers, each show getting its moment; this year the parade is treating Broadway as one shared story, from Hamilton and Wicked to Rent's 30th anniversary, introduced by a star about to return to the stage herself. That framing suits a 100th edition, which is less about any single show than about the tradition itself. The Dolly Parton tribute adds an emotional anchor, and the record 100 inflatables give the spectacle its scale. The real test will be whether ten minutes can hold nine shows without feeling rushed, but if Tom Kitt's arrangement lands, the medley could become the template for how the parade handles Broadway in the years ahead.

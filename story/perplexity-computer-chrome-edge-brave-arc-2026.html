Perplexity Brings Its Computer Agent to Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc

Perplexity's AI agent Computer can now work inside the browser users already have open, carrying out multi-step tasks across tabs and logged-in sites without installing Comet.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Perplexity's AI agent Computer can now work inside Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc, the company announced on November 20, in a move that frees the agent from Perplexity's own Comet browser. A new extension lets users hand the agent a task from a side panel and watch it work across their tabs and tools in the browser they already use.

Until now, Computer's browser abilities were tied to Comet, the AI-native browser Perplexity launched in July 2025. The extension removes the need to download and migrate to a new browser, which matters because most people do their work, shopping, research and account management in a browser they chose long ago. Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc are all supported, and other Chromium-based desktop browsers that accept Chrome extensions work too.

Computer is built for multi-step work rather than single answers. Users can ask it to carry out workflows that move between tabs and fill out forms, and it can operate inside sites where the user is already logged in. Representative jobs include downloading receipts from travel accounts and organising them in a spreadsheet, comparing prices across retailer sites, triaging an inbox and drafting replies, and entering data into long forms and administrative portals.

The tasks are user-initiated. Computer does not act between requests, and it only handles authentication information and website content while a task is running, according to the company's materials. It does not collect data in the background, Perplexity says, a point the company stresses because the extension necessarily works inside a user's signed-in sessions.

Users also get per-site controls for deciding where Computer may operate. A domain can be allowed or blocked, and the lists can be updated as workflows change, so a sensitive service such as a bank can be kept out of the agent's reach while routine tools stay open to it. That control matters because an agent that can click through logged-in accounts is only as safe as the boundaries its owner sets.

The release caps a year of steady expansion for Computer. Perplexity launched the agent in February 2026, followed with a Mac app in April and a Windows app in July, and the extension went live around October 30, 2026, listed in the Chrome Web Store as version 0.0.6. The announcement itself came through posts on X from chief executive Aravind Srinivas, with a follow-up posted on November 20.

There are practical limits. CAPTCHAs, passkeys, multi-factor prompts, anti-bot systems, expired sessions and browser security policies can pause or block a run, and computer-use agents remain slower than people on many simple tasks and can misread ambiguous instructions during long runs. Computer is best suited to jobs spread across several websites, particularly where those sites lack useful public interfaces.

The move also puts Perplexity closer to its rivals. Google's Gemini has its own side panel in Chrome, and other browser-based assistants are racing to add agents that do things rather than just answer questions. Computer's distinguishing feature is direct execution across tabs, combined with Perplexity's citation-focused search and research tools. Whether users trust an agent inside their main browser, with their logins, will decide how far the extension spreads.

Why it matters: This is the moment Perplexity's agent strategy stops being a browser pitch and becomes a feature of the browsers people already use. Tying Computer to Comet was a distribution problem: asking users to switch browsers is a far bigger request than asking them to install an extension. By meeting users in Chrome and Edge, Perplexity gains access to a much larger audience and a much more sensitive test — an agent loose inside signed-in email, banking and work accounts. The per-site controls and the promise of no background collection are the company's answer to that trust problem, but the real test will be the first time the agent mishandles a logged-in session. If it earns that trust, the browser becomes the agent's home; if not, the extension will be the first thing users uninstall.

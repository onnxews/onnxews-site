Grand Theft Auto VI Launches Worldwide With a 99 on Metacritic and $1.65 Billion in Presales

Rockstar's long-awaited sequel went on sale at midnight around the world on Thursday, 13 years after Grand Theft Auto V, to near-perfect reviews and millions of people watching it being played.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Grand Theft Auto VI launched on Thursday, November 19, going on sale at midnight local time around the world, with New Zealand the first country to get it. In the United States, the game went live seconds after midnight on the East Coast, ending a wait of 13 years since Grand Theft Auto V arrived in 2013.

The game is available on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S. Players on computer will have to wait a little longer, as Rockstar has not announced a date for a PC version. The game is a large download, with the preload running to about 210 gigabytes on the Xbox Series S.

The early reviews have been almost perfect. The game holds a score of 99 out of 100 on Metacritic, based on the first wave of reviews, matching the record held by The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time from 1998. IGN's Luke Reilly wrote: "I see no real limit to the amount of time I could spend here, endlessly exploring and interacting with Vice City and the massive area surrounding it." Other critics have praised the game's scale, its story, and its detail.

The New York Times focused on the game's love story, while the Washington Post argued that GTA VI is "more than a typical video game. It is a great gathering of artforms and talent, an unruly, comprehensive cultural atlas of American life and manners". The French site Jeuxvideo.com, with the headline "a knockout like we only get once every ten years", warned that players expecting a wild free-for-all without consequences may be disappointed. Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, one of the most closely watched games writers, also reviewed it as part of the first wave.

The commercial numbers are just as striking. The game took in $1.65 billion in presales, with 17.07 million units sold before launch, according to the analytics firm Sensor Tower. Preorders opened in June, and the split was heavily toward Sony's console, with 74% of presales on PlayStation 5 and 26% on Xbox.

Millions of people watched the launch as it happened. By 12:15 p.m. Paris time, 2.3 million people were watching streams of the game on Twitch, according to Le Monde. As of 8:15 a.m. Eastern time, about 2.12 million people were watching livestreams across platforms, including 1.31 million on Twitch, 233,600 on YouTube, and 574,400 on Kick, beating the previous record of 1.74 million set by the Valorant beta in 2020.

The launch also disrupted the working day. One in five salaried workers in the United States planned to take Thursday off to play the game, according to the human-resources firm BambooHR, and the remote-work platform Remote found that 32% more time off was booked for Thursday than on the same day last year, the biggest non-holiday leave spike of 2026.

The game is set in Leonida, a fictional version of Florida, and its main city is Vice City, a fictional Miami. It follows two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, with Lucia the first female lead in the history of the series. Rockstar has warned players about spoilers, posting a message to its Discord community of about a million followers asking them not to ruin the story for others.

Grand Theft Auto V sold more than 200 million copies and became one of the best-selling entertainment products of all time, so the bar for its sequel was always going to be high. On the evidence of the first day, with a 99 on Metacritic and $1.65 billion already in the bank, the sequel has cleared it.

Why it matters: This is the biggest entertainment launch of the year, and possibly of the decade. A video game more than a decade in the making is now being judged by millions of players at once, and the early verdict is near-unanimous praise. The presales alone, $1.65 billion before a single copy was sold in a store, show how the games industry has changed: this is no longer a niche hobby but the biggest release window in entertainment, and the fact that one in five American office workers planned to take the day off to play it says as much about the culture as the sales figures do.

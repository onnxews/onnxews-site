Hong Kong Activist Joshua Wong Sentenced to 10 Years in Second National Security Case

The 30-year-old, already serving a term for the Hong Kong 47 case, pleaded guilty to conspiring to seek foreign sanctions. He has been in custody since November 2020.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Prominent Hong Kong activist Joshua Wong was sentenced to 10 years in prison on Thursday for his second conviction under a Beijing-imposed national security law, in a case critics say reflects the crackdown on the city's once-thriving pro-democracy movement.

Wong, 30, was charged in 2025 with conspiracy to collude with foreign forces to endanger national security. He pleaded guilty in September. He was accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others between July and November 2020 to ask foreign countries to impose sanctions or blockades, or engage in other hostile activities against Hong Kong and China.

High Court Judge William Tam said during the sentencing hearing that Wong's offense was of a grave nature and rejected that his role was limited. He said he considered a 13-year prison sentence as a starting point but reduced it because of Wong's guilty plea.

Wong is already serving a four-year, eight-month prison term for the city's biggest national security case, linked to an unofficial primary, after entering a guilty plea in that case. He had been due for release in January 2027; Human Rights Watch said he now faces imprisonment until late 2036, when he will be 40.

Wong has been in detention since November 2020, when he pleaded guilty to inciting and organizing an unauthorized anti-government assembly during the 2019 protests. He is the only activist in Hong Kong prosecuted twice under the security law. Amnesty International said the law has become a tool to punish dissent.

Disney+ Brings More Than 60 Movies in 3D to Meta Quest Headsets

Disney+ subscribers with a Meta Quest 3 or 3S can now stream more than 60 films in 3D, from Avengers: Endgame to Avatar: Fire and Ash, inside themed virtual viewing rooms — at no extra charge.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Disney+ subscribers with a Meta Quest headset can now watch more than 60 films in 3D, after Disney and Meta pushed a refreshed version of the app to the Meta Horizon Store. The update, dated November 10, 2026, also adds themed virtual viewing spaces, and both features are included in a standard Disney+ subscription at no extra charge.

The 3D catalog spans the big Disney libraries. As for Star Wars, it currently includes five of the six most recent films: Episodes VII through IX, Rogue One and The Mandalorian and Grogu. Beyond Star Wars, most of the Marvel movies are available in 3D, including all the Avengers films and the Guardians of the Galaxy trilogy. Other standouts include the Avatar trilogy, Frozen 2, multiple Pixar films such as Toy Story 5 and Incredibles 2, and the most recent Tron movies.

The films are presented in “immersive themed environments” — virtual rooms drawn from the worlds on screen, such as Tatooine, Avengers Tower and the Scare Floor from Monsters, Inc. The environments work on the older Quest 2 as well, but the 3D films themselves are available only on the Quest 3 and Quest 3S at this time, according to Disney's help page.

Premium subscribers can enjoy select content on Meta Quest in Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos, according to the announcement. But the rollout comes with caveats: Disney says only select titles are offered in 3D and that availability varies by region and device, while Meta says 3D, Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos playback varies by region and subscription plan. Neither company has published a list of the countries where the 3D library is live.

The launch follows a promise Meta made earlier this year. At Meta Connect 2026, the company said Disney+ subscribers in select countries would be able to stream 3D movies — such as Marvel's The Avengers and Black Panther — with custom themed environments starting in November. In a new post from Meta, the company confirmed that as of now, more than 60 films are available to view in 3D as part of the upgraded app.

Star Wars fans get a small piece of future news tucked into the announcement. Star Wars: A New Hope is eventually getting the 3D treatment as well, which could end up being timed around the movie's 50th anniversary on February 19, 2027. Disney previously announced that A New Hope is getting an IMAX release next year, including its first-ever digital IMAX and 70mm showings.

The same 3D experience is headed to Meta's next device. Meta's upcoming VR Glasses, priced at $1,299.99 and set to release sometime in spring 2027, will also support 3D films, with IMAX Enhanced content. For now, Quest owners need the Disney+ app from the Meta Horizon Store, an active subscription and the latest headset software.

The move is part of a wider push to make headsets a place where people actually watch things, not just play games. The 3D update gives owners a reason to put the headset on for a two-hour film rather than a short demo — and the themed rooms are the kind of thing a flat television cannot offer. Whether that habit sticks will depend on how much of the catalog each subscriber can actually see, given the regional limits neither company has spelled out.

What happens next is a test of the catalog's depth. The 60-plus films cover the obvious blockbusters, but the value of the update will be measured by how quickly Disney adds the rest of its library — and by whether the spring 2027 glasses launch, with its IMAX Enhanced pitch, turns 3D streaming from a headset novelty into a selling point for the hardware itself. As of November 11, the app is live on Quest 3 and 3S in select countries, with the rest of the world waiting to find out if they are included.

Why it matters: This launch matters because it is the clearest sign yet that Disney and Meta want the headset to be a cinema, not just a games console. More than 60 films in 3D, included in the subscription, is a real catalog — not a demo — and the themed rooms are the kind of thing a flat TV cannot offer. But the uneven rollout is the catch: if subscribers in some countries get the rooms and not the films, the launch will feel half-finished. The bigger test comes in spring 2027, when the $1,299.99 VR Glasses arrive with IMAX Enhanced content. If Disney's 3D library is the reason people buy the glasses, this week's quiet app update will turn out to have been the start of something much larger.

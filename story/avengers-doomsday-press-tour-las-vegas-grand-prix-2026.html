Avengers: Doomsday Stars Kick Off Global Press Tour at the Las Vegas Grand Prix

Chris Evans, Anthony Mackie and Simu Liu reunited on the Las Vegas Strip on Saturday as Marvel launched the global tour for Avengers: Doomsday, with Doctor Doom taking over the Sphere and a branded air taxi in the sky.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Chris Evans, Anthony Mackie and Simu Liu are back together to start promotion for their upcoming movie, Avengers: Doomsday. The superhero actors reunited on Saturday, November 21, at the 2026 Formula 1 Heineken Las Vegas Grand Prix in Nevada, marking the first stop on the global press tour for the film, which hits theaters in just less than a month, on December 18.

Avengers: Doomsday took over the Las Vegas Strip and the F1 race. A special Doomsday-wrapped Formula 1 show car sat trackside, Doctor Doom took over the Sphere, and there was a custom-branded Archer Aviation air taxi. Marvel Studios marked the moment on social media, posting: "Off we go. Chris Evans, Anthony Mackie, and Simu Liu kick off the Avengers: Doomsday Global Tour at @F1LasVegas Grand Prix 2026."

The actors are set to reprise their roles as Steve Rogers, Captain America and Shang-Chi as the heroes face a new villain, Doctor Doom, played by Robert Downey Jr. In the film, beloved heroes from three distinct universes will be set on a deadly collision course and face an existential threat unlike anything they have ever encountered. Evans returns as Steve Rogers in the film, while Mackie carries the Captain America mantle as Sam Wilson.

Another Marvel star, Chris Pratt, was also in attendance at the F1 race, and he waved the checkered flag as Max Verstappen crossed the finish line in first place. Earlier in the night, Pratt and Mackie were seen meeting up as they both took part in hot laps on the track, and a clip shared online showed the pair greeting each other warmly near the track. The night before, Simu Liu and his wife, Allison Hsu, were in attendance at Race Night at the Bellagio Fountain Club during the F1 race weekend.

The Las Vegas Grand Prix has quickly become one of Hollywood's favorite places to be seen. The fourth edition of the event brought Formula 1 back to the Las Vegas Strip from November 19 to 21, and the race was the 21st round of the F1 World Championship. Kim Kardashian, Mark Wahlberg and Keanu Reeves were among the other celebrities at the Las Vegas Strip Circuit, and Hannah Waddingham was also spotted at the event.

The press tour arrives at a key moment in the film's campaign. Avengers: Doomsday hits theaters on December 18, and Marvel's final trailer is reportedly set to arrive on Monday. Disney will also air new footage during The Wonderful World of Disney: Holiday Spectacular on November 30. Expectations for the film are huge: Box Office Theory currently has Avengers: Doomsday tracking for a domestic opening between $375 million and $424 million.

The Las Vegas takeover was designed to be unmissable. The Sphere, the giant venue on the Strip, glowed with the Doomsday title and the insignias of the Avengers, the X-Men and the Fantastic Four, before showing a menacing look at Robert Downey Jr.'s Victor von Doom. The Archer Aviation air taxi, an electric vertical takeoff aircraft in custom Avengers: Doomsday branding, added a futuristic note to a weekend that was already a showcase for speed.

Marvel's global tour will continue from Las Vegas to cities around the world in the weeks before the film opens. The studio announced the tour earlier in November with a cryptic image of Robert Downey Jr. portrayed as Doctor Doom, and the Las Vegas launch set the tone: big, loud and built for cameras. Expect more cast appearances as the tour continues, with the full ensemble fanning out across the globe.

For Marvel, the stakes could hardly be higher. Avengers: Doomsday is the studio's biggest swing since the last Avengers film, reuniting heroes from across the Marvel universe against a villain played by the actor who built the franchise. The Las Vegas launch was a statement of intent: Marvel is treating this as an event, not just a movie, and the next four weeks will be a countdown played out in public.

Why it matters: Marvel chose the Las Vegas Grand Prix for a reason: it is the rare event where Hollywood, sport and spectacle already collide, and the studio turned it into a launchpad. The Sphere takeover and the branded air taxi show how much of a modern blockbuster campaign now happens outside cinemas, in the physical world, before a single ticket is sold. With tracking already pointing to a $375 million-plus domestic opening, the tour is less about building awareness than about protecting a number that big from slipping.

Aaron Paul and Giovanni Ribisi Join Snoop Dogg Biopic as Jimmy Iovine and David Kenner

Universal's film about the rapper's rise through the music industry adds two big names, with Craig Brewer directing and a theatrical release set for August 2027.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Universal Pictures has added Aaron Paul and Giovanni Ribisi to its upcoming Snoop Dogg biopic, Snoop, in the latest casting news for one of the most anticipated music films on Hollywood's slate. The film, directed by Craig Brewer, will explore Snoop Dogg's rise through the music industry, and the two new additions will play key figures from the rapper's early career.

According to Deadline, Paul will play music executive Jimmy Iovine, the co-founder of Interscope Records, the label that helped launch Snoop's career. Ribisi will take on the role of attorney David Kenner, the lawyer who represented Snoop during the most turbulent period of his early fame. Both roles are central to the story of how a young rapper from Long Beach became one of the most recognisable figures in popular music.

Iovine's part in the story is hard to overstate. As the head of Interscope, he was one of the most powerful figures in the music business in the early 1990s, and his backing gave Snoop's debut album, Doggystyle, the platform it needed to become a phenomenon. The film's portrayal of the relationship between a young artist and the executive who bet on him is likely to be one of its most important threads, and Paul, best known for his Emmy-winning turn as Jesse Pinkman in Breaking Bad, brings a track record of playing complicated, sympathetic characters under pressure.

Ribisi's character, David Kenner, enters the story at its darkest moment. Kenner was Snoop's lawyer during the murder trial that dominated headlines in the mid-1990s, a case that ended in acquittal but defined the rapper's public image for years. Ribisi, a veteran character actor with a long list of film and television credits, is a natural fit for a role that demands quiet intensity rather than showmanship.

Craig Brewer, the director, is no stranger to music stories. He directed Hustle and Flow, the 2005 film about a Memphis pimp trying to become a rapper that won an Academy Award for its song It's Hard Out Here for a Pimp, and later directed Dolemite Is My Name, the Eddie Murphy comeback film about the comedian and filmmaker Rudy Ray Moore. Brewer's feel for music, hustle and the American South made him a logical choice for a film about an artist whose career was built on exactly those things.

The project is the first film under Death Row Pictures' overall deal with NBCUniversal, a partnership that gives Snoop's own production company a home at the studio that will release the film. Death Row, the label that released Snoop's early records, is now as much a brand as a record company, and the biopic is the centrepiece of its move into film. Universal has scheduled Snoop for a theatrical release on August 6, 2027.

Music biopics have become one of Hollywood's most reliable genres, and the bar has risen steadily. Bohemian Rhapsody, Rocketman, Elvis and the recent Michael Jackson biopic, which earned three Hollywood Music in Media Awards nominations, have all shown that audiences will turn out for a well-made film about a musical life, especially when the music is woven into the story rather than pasted on top of it. Snoop's catalogue, from Gin and Juice to Drop It Like It's Hot, gives Brewer a soundtrack most directors would envy.

The casting of Paul and Ribisi also suggests the film is aiming for prestige rather than parody. Both actors are known for serious, grounded work, and their presence signals that Universal wants Snoop to be treated as a real story about ambition, loyalty and survival, not as a nostalgia exercise. The rapper himself has spent the past decade reinventing his public image, from Olympic torchbearer to cooking show host, and the film arrives at a moment when his career is being reassessed as something far bigger than a single era of hip-hop.

What happens next is the long wait for the first footage. With a release date set for August 2027, the film is still in the casting and pre-production phase, and the actor who will play Snoop himself remains the biggest unanswered question. Whoever takes the role will carry the film, and the choice will be scrutinised by fans who have spent thirty years watching the real thing. For now, the additions of Paul and Ribisi are a strong start, and a sign that Universal is building this film to last.

mSecure Password Manager Hack Prompts Password Reset Warnings

The maker of the mSecure password manager emailed customers on November 3 about "suspicious activity" but has not said what was taken or how many people are affected.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

mSeven Software is warning current and former mSecure password manager customers to change passwords, reset two-factor authentication and remove passkeys following a cyberattack in which a hacker reportedly stole an encrypted customer database.

In a security notification sent to customers on November 3 and shared with BleepingComputer, the company said it recently discovered suspicious activity involving its mSecure password management application. It said it had launched an investigation, taken steps to mitigate the issue and engaged external cybersecurity experts.

The notice did not provide any details on what systems were compromised, whether customer data was stolen, or how many users were affected. Customers have since shared responses reportedly received from mSeven's support team indicating that a hacker stole an encrypted database from mSecure Cloud, containing information belonging to current and former users.

The same support responses say mSeven has no evidence that the attacker actually got into the confidential information stored inside that encrypted database, and that the issue only affects people who were syncing their vault with mSecure Cloud. The company reportedly said it had implemented additional security measures and had no evidence the app is currently unsafe to use.

mSecure uses AES-256 encryption and a zero-knowledge architecture that encrypts data locally on users' devices before transmitting it to the company's servers. But stolen password vaults can still pose risks if attackers can crack weak master passwords through offline attacks, as happened in the 2022 LastPass breach. BleepingComputer said mSeven had not yet responded to its questions about the incident.

Reporting: BleepingComputer

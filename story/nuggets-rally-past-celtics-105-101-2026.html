Nuggets Rally From 17 Down to Edge Celtics 105-101

Denver erased a 17-point first-half deficit behind Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray, then survived a frantic finish to hand Boston its second straight loss.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Denver Nuggets rallied from a 17-point deficit to beat the Boston Celtics 105-101 on Wednesday night at Ball Arena, in a game that swung from a blowout to a nail-biter and back again. Denver trailed 61-44 late in the second quarter and was down 61-49 at halftime before turning the game around in the third.

Nikola Jokic led the comeback with 24 points, nine assists, and eight rebounds, falling just short of a triple-double. Jamal Murray added 21 points, seven rebounds, and four steals, and DeMar DeRozan scored 18 points, 13 of them in the fourth quarter, when Denver needed him most.

The turning point came in the third quarter. Denver went on a 20-4 run that wiped out the deficit, and a Cameron Johnson jumper gave the Nuggets their first lead of the night at 69-67. Boston went scoreless for four minutes and 35 seconds during the stretch, and the game that had looked like a rout became a contest.

The finish was frantic. There were 15 lead changes in the final 16 minutes and 10 seconds, and neither team led by more than a few points down the stretch. DeRozan hit two free throws with 49.1 seconds left to put Denver ahead 102-101, and after Derrick White missed a three-pointer for Boston, Murray hit a fadeaway with 6.4 seconds left to make it 104-101.

The final seconds were decided by a technical foul. Jayson Tatum, who had 24 points for Boston, threw the ball toward an official after a call and was called for a technical, and Murray hit the free throw to seal the 105-101 win. It was a sour end to a night in which Tatum scored only four points in the second half and committed a season-high six turnovers.

Boston's other contributors kept the Celtics in the game. Neemias Queta had a season-high 17 points and 10 rebounds, Payton Pritchard scored 15 points off the bench, Derrick White had 12 points and eight assists, and Paul George added 10 points before fouling out. But the Celtics shot just 11 of 44 from three-point range, a poor night for a team that had been making 38.2% of its threes this season.

Denver's bench also played a part. Christian Braun scored 15 points and Aaron Gordon added 13, giving the Nuggets the depth they needed to survive a night when their starters had to work hard for every basket.

The result changes the shape of both teams' seasons. Denver has now won five of its last seven games, climbing back into the thick of the Western Conference race. Boston has lost two in a row after winning 11 of its previous 12, and suffered back-to-back regular-season losses for the first time since March 10-12.

For Denver, the win is a statement. Coming back from 17 down against one of the best teams in the league, on a night when Jokic was one rebound short of a triple-double and Murray hit the shot that decided it, is the kind of win that builds belief. For Boston, it is a reminder that even a team that has won 11 of 12 can lose its grip on a game it once led by 17.

Why it matters: This game matters because it shows how quickly a season can turn. Boston had won 11 of 12 and looked like the best team in the league, but two straight losses, including a blown 17-point lead, have exposed a vulnerability, particularly from three-point range, where the Celtics went cold at the worst time. Denver, meanwhile, has now won five of seven and is finding its rhythm, with Jokic doing what he always does and Murray hitting the shot that mattered. The Nuggets are not the favorites in the West, but wins like this one are how teams become contenders.

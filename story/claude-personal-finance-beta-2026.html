Claude Can Now Read Your Bank Account — But It Cannot Touch Your Money

Anthropic's Personal Finance beta links bank and card accounts through Plaid, giving the AI assistant read-only access to answer money questions in plain language.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Anthropic has launched Personal Finance in Claude, a new feature that lets the AI assistant connect to users' bank and card accounts and answer questions about their money in everyday language. The beta, announced on November 5, 2026, is rolling out gradually to paying subscribers in the United States, and it comes with a hard boundary: the access is read-only, and Claude cannot move, manage or invest a user's money.

The feature connects accounts through Plaid, the service that links apps to banks and financial institutions. Users sign in on Plaid's screens, so Claude never asks for or sees a bank password. Once linked, Claude can read balances, credit limits and up to 24 months of transactions,, and answer questions about spending, bills and unusual charges.

Availability is deliberately narrow for now. Personal Finance is open to Claude Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers in the United States, on plans that cost $20, $100 and $200 a month respectively. Free, Team and Enterprise accounts are excluded, and even eligible subscribers may not see the feature immediately because of the phased rollout. Only US banks can be linked, and the feature does not appear outside the United States.

Anthropic has also drawn clear lines around what the feature will not do. Personal Finance carries no ads or sponsored recommendations, and Anthropic is not pairing it with its own credit cards, loans or savings accounts. The company says the feature is not designed to provide investment advice, and it warns that figures can lag or double-count linked accounts, so users should treat answers as a starting point rather than a ledger.

The launch puts Anthropic in direct competition with OpenAI, which introduced its own personal finance feature in ChatGPT in May 2026, nearly six months earlier. The race to become the AI that knows your finances is now fully joined, and both companies are betting that people will trust an assistant with their spending data if the guardrails are clear enough.

That trust is the real question. Handing an AI chatbot read-only access to bank transactions is a bigger ask than letting it draft an email, and Anthropic knows it. The company is inviting financial services professionals and firms to help shape how the feature works alongside them, a sign that it wants the banking industry's input rather than a fight with it.

The feature is part of a much larger moment for Anthropic. The company is preparing for a public listing and racing OpenAI and Google for the role of the AI assistant people trust with their daily lives. Personal finance is a natural place for that contest to play out, because it is one of the few areas where an assistant needs real, personal data to be useful, and where getting it wrong has real consequences for the person asking.

For users, the practical appeal is simple: ask Claude where the money went last month, which subscriptions are still charging, or whether a bill looks unusual, and get an answer in plain language instead of a spreadsheet. The feature works on Claude's web, desktop, iOS and Android versions, so the same questions can be asked from anywhere.

What happens next will depend on whether early users find the answers useful enough to keep their accounts linked, and whether Anthropic expands the beta beyond the United States. For now, the company is moving carefully, and the read-only promise is the whole pitch: Claude can look at your money, but it cannot touch it.

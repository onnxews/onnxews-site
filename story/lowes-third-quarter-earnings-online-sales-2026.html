Lowe's Beats Earnings Estimates as Online Sales Jump 17.4%

The home improvement retailer raised its full-year outlook, but a $250 million tariff refund did much of the heavy lifting on the bottom line.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Lowe's reported third-quarter sales of $22.756 billion on Wednesday, November 18, up 9.3% from $20.813 billion a year earlier, as online sales surged and the home improvement retailer raised its full-year outlook. The quarter ended October 30, 2026, and the results beat Wall Street's expectations on both the top and bottom lines.

Net earnings rose to $1.673 billion from $1.616 billion a year earlier, a gain of 3.5%. Diluted earnings per share were $2.98, up from $2.88, while adjusted diluted earnings per share were $3.10, up 1.3% from $3.06 a year earlier and beating the $2.88 consensus by $0.22. Comparable sales rose 1.8%, the sixth consecutive quarter of growth, driven by online sales, which jumped 17.4%, and by strength in the Pro business and home services.

As with Target, a tariff refund flattered the bottom line. Lowe's received a pretax benefit of about $250 million from tariff refunds under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, worth $0.34 per share on both a GAAP and an adjusted basis. Excluding the refunds, adjusted earnings per share would have been about $2.76, below expectations, a reminder that the underlying profit picture is softer than the headline.

Gross margin fell to 32.95% from 34.19% a year earlier, a decline of 1.24 percentage points, and operating income was $2.575 billion, a margin of 11.32% versus 11.92% a year earlier. For the first nine months of the year, net sales were $71.790 billion, up 9.3% from $65.701 billion, and net earnings were $5.699 billion. The quarter also included $96 million of pretax expenses for amortization of intangible assets related to the acquisitions of Foundation Building Materials and Artisan Design Group.

Lowe's operated 1,762 stores with 197.0 million square feet of retail selling space as of October 30, 2026. The company paid $701 million in dividends during the quarter, continuing a long record of returning cash to shareholders.

The company raised its outlook for fiscal 2026. Total sales are now expected to be about $92.5 billion, up from about $92.0 billion, and comparable sales are expected to rise about 0.5%, up from flat. The operating margin is expected to be about 11.1%, down slightly from 11.2%, and the adjusted operating margin about 11.5%, down from 11.6%. Diluted earnings per share are expected to be about $11.75, and adjusted diluted earnings per share about $12.25. Capital spending is expected to be up to $2.5 billion, net interest expense about $1.5 billion, down from $1.6 billion, and the tax rate about 24.5%.

"We just delivered our sixth consecutive quarter of positive comp sales driven by continued growth across Pro, Online and Home Services. These results reflect the resiliency of our Total Home strategy," said Marvin R. Ellison, Lowe's chairman, president and CEO. The Pro business and home services, along with the online surge, are doing the work that the broader housing market is not, with existing-home sales still soft and big-ticket remodeling projects delayed.

Shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading on Wednesday, a sign that investors were focused on the tariff-refund boost and the margin decline rather than the raised outlook. The holiday season and the housing market will decide whether Lowe's can keep the comparable-sales streak alive into next year, and whether the online momentum can offset a consumer who is still cautious about big home projects.

Why it matters: Lowe's quarter is a study in reading past the headline. Sales rose 9.3% and the company beat estimates, but the tariff refund added $0.34 per share, without which adjusted earnings would have missed, and gross margin fell by more than a percentage point. The real story is online: a 17.4% jump in e-commerce and strength with professional contractors are carrying the business while the broader housing market stays soft. The raised outlook is modest, a half-point of comparable growth, which suggests management is not betting on a housing recovery. Investors noticed, sending the shares down before the open, and the question for the holiday season is whether the online and Pro momentum can keep the streak alive without another refund to lean on.

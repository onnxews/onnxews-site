Nasdaq Plunges 5.5% Into Correction as AI Selloff Wipes Out $2 Trillion

Chip stocks suffered their second-biggest drop on record as investors reassessed the AI data-center boom after the midterms.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

U.S. stocks plunged on Thursday in their worst session since April 2025, led by a ferocious selloff in technology and artificial intelligence shares. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5 percent, or 1,422 points, to 24,315, its largest single-session point loss on record. The S&P 500 dropped 4.4 percent and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 1,833 points, or 3.4 percent, more than wiping out Wednesday's post-election rally.

The Nasdaq is now firmly in correction territory, down as much as 14 percent from the all-time high it reached only a month ago. About $2 trillion was wiped off the Nasdaq's market value in a single day. All 11 sectors of the S&P 500 fell, with technology down 7.3 percent.

Chip stocks took the worst of it. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 12 percent, its second-biggest fall on record, with every member lower. Micron and AMD each fell about 17 percent, Nvidia dropped 9 percent and SanDisk tumbled 22 percent. Qualcomm's weak smartphone-chip sales added to the gloom after its handset revenue fell 26 percent from a year earlier.

Investors reassessed the AI data-center buildout after Democrats swept the midterm elections, fearing the new Congress could slow approvals and tighten rules for the massive facilities that drive demand for AI chips. Voters also rejected data-center projects in most local ballot measures, and the Democratic governor-elect of Texas has pledged to halt new approvals until the state passes laws on electricity, water and property rights.

The rout also pushed back expectations for the Federal Reserve's next rate increase, with interest-rate traders now not fully pricing in another hike until March. Gold rose as investors sought safety, while oil rebounded. The next test for markets comes Friday with the October jobs report, which economists expected to show about 65,000 new jobs.

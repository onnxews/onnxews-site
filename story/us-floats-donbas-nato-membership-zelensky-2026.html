U.S. Floated Trading Donbas for NATO Membership, Zelensky Says

President Volodymyr Zelensky said American negotiators asked for the first time whether Ukraine would withdraw from the Donbas in exchange for joining NATO, and that he would put any such deal to a national referendum.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The United States has floated the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from its eastern Donbas region in exchange for joining NATO, President Volodymyr Zelensky said, in the first time the question of linking NATO membership to territorial concessions has been raised during peace talks with Russia. Zelensky told journalists on Friday that U.S. negotiators had put the question during the latest round of U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi, in remarks his office released on Saturday.

"The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?' This was the first time this had been raised," Zelensky said. "That is why I believe NATO is back on the agenda. But you have also heard (Russian President Vladimir) Putin's signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation." Putin has long said Ukrainian membership of NATO is a red line for Moscow, citing the issue as one reason for launching Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.

Zelensky stressed that Ukraine would not give up territory, but said that if he received a comprehensive security package - including security guarantees, the right to a strong and well-funded Ukrainian army and funds to rebuild - he would put the question to a national referendum. "If the American side confirms precisely this to us, together with other NATO partners, then I will ask our Ukrainian society what it thinks: do we accept it or not? I have no right to make that decision on my own. But we need specifics, and we don't have them yet."

The offer is not Washington's alone to make. Bringing a new country into NATO requires the unanimous consent of all member states, former U.S. Ambassador to Ukraine William Taylor told CNN. "Every NATO member state has a say. Most would agree. There may be, however, a couple of nation member states in NATO who would not agree," Taylor said. "So it's not a firm offer at this point." Taylor added that finally entering NATO could be the security guarantee Ukraine needs to accept a ceasefire.

The two days of U.S.-brokered talks between Ukraine and Russia took place in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday and concluded without a major breakthrough, though the sides agreed to a prisoner exchange that will involve more than 1,000 people, Zelensky said. The final numbers for the swap were still under discussion. Much of the Donbas is controlled by Russia, although Moscow's grinding ground offensive in the region has largely stalled.

Zelensky also dismissed Russia's call for a broad agreement on a new European security architecture to be reached before the specifics of ending the war are agreed. "We cannot turn everything upside down and start by talking about changing the security framework in the world or across the continent overall, and only then talk about ending this war. This war must be ended first," he said. He also dismissed Putin's suggestion of a local humanitarian ceasefire in Kostiantynivka, a hotly contested city in Ukraine's eastern Donetsk region, saying it reflected Moscow's inability to take the city by force. "Putin simply wants us to leave Kostiantynivka so that they can move in and occupy the entire city that way," he said.

Ukraine has given a list of seven or eight other frontline settlements where civilians, including children, are trapped, for a potential humanitarian truce, but Russia prefers to use them as military targets, Zelensky said. Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians in the war. U.S. peace envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner had pledged to raise an energy ceasefire with Putin at a meeting in Moscow on Thursday, Zelensky said, but there was no news of any progress after their talks. "Putin personally does not support a ceasefire," he said.

The talks did produce one procedural breakthrough: Europe will have a seat at the negotiating table in forthcoming peace talks. Zelensky met Witkoff and Kushner on Thursday in Ireland after their talks in Moscow, and said the U.S. negotiators had told him all sides had agreed to European participation. At a summit in Ireland, EU leaders agreed that Europe's team for any Ukraine peace talks would comprise five major military powers - non-EU member Britain plus France, Germany, Italy and Poland - as well as EU officials.

Kushner said on Saturday that "several new ideas emerged from these discussions and are now being explored," without referring directly to the NATO proposal. "NATO will remain a critical pillar of European security," he said, adding that Trump would "only support arrangements that strengthen Ukraine's and Europe's security." Zelensky welcomed the agreement on European participation, noting that Europe - now Ukraine's largest financial and military backer - had been effectively sidelined from the peace talks for nearly two years.

Zelensky also used his remarks to journalists to question whether Elon Musk's SpaceX was being honest about restrictions on Ukraine's use of its Starshield system, saying restrictions had been in place for five days along the front line and in Russian-occupied territories, affecting the operation of Ukrainian drones. SpaceX has denied imposing any new restrictions. In an update on Sunday morning, Zelensky warned that many Russian strikes using Shahed-type drones were evading Ukraine's defences because of the restrictions, and said that over the past week Russia had launched more than 2,300 drones, 1,590 aerial bombs and 15 missiles against Ukraine.

Why it matters: The Donbas-for-NATO question matters because it is the first time the United States has linked the two issues that have defined the war: Ukraine's territory and its future security. Zelensky's answer - no surrender of land, but a referendum if a full security package is confirmed - sets the terms for the next round of talks, while the agreement to give Europe a seat at the table changes who negotiates. The proposal also exposes the gap between Washington and Moscow: Putin has called NATO membership a red line, and no deal can move without his agreement or the unanimous consent of NATO's members.

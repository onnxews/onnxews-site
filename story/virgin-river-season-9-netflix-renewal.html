Netflix Renews Virgin River for Season 9, Its Longest-Running Scripted Series

Netflix has renewed Virgin River for a ninth season months before Season 8 premieres in 2027, making the romantic drama the streamer's longest-running original scripted series.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Netflix has renewed Virgin River for a ninth season, making the romantic drama the longest-running original scripted series in the streamer's history by number of seasons. The renewal was announced on Friday, November 13, 2026, months before the show's eighth season has even premiered, in an unusually early vote of confidence from the streaming service. Season 9 will consist of 10 one-hour episodes, taking the series to a total of 94 episodes once the eighth and ninth seasons have aired.

The renewal cements Virgin River's place at the top of Netflix's longevity chart. With a ninth season confirmed, the small-town romance stands alone as the streamer's longest-running original scripted series by number of seasons, and its episode total will reach 94 once the eighth and ninth seasons have aired. Netflix paired the announcement with a viewing milestone: the series has now passed half a billion views across its first seven seasons.

Those numbers explain why Netflix moved so early. Virgin River has spent 28 weeks in Netflix's Global Top 10 and has reached the Top 10 in more than 80 countries, an unusually broad international footprint for a quiet, character-driven drama about a remote Northern California town. In a streaming era where shows are routinely cancelled after two or three seasons, a series that keeps returning viewers year after year has become one of the most valuable assets a platform can hold.

The series, based on Robyn Carr's bestselling Harlequin book series, centres on nurse practitioner Melinda Monroe, played by Alexandra Breckenridge, who moves to the remote town of Virgin River seeking a fresh start, only to find that small-town living is not quite as simple as she expected. Martin Henderson plays Jack Sheridan, who becomes the centre of her new life. The book collection features more than 20 novels that together have sold more than 13 million copies.

Season 9 will see showrunner Patrick Sean Smith return as creator and executive producer, alongside a producing team that includes Erin Cardillo, Richard Keith, Jackson Sinder, Mary Page Keller, Thomas Ian Griffith, Norman Buckley, Ian Hay, Sue Tenney, Roma Roth, Christopher E. Perry and author Robyn Carr.

Smith greeted the renewal with gratitude for the partnership with Netflix and pride in what the team has built. “We're all so grateful for our partnership with Netflix and incredibly proud to be a part of their legacy,” he said in a statement. “In a world that can sometimes feel increasingly disconnected, there's something meaningful about having a place people want to return to. I'm incredibly proud of what we've built and continue to grow, and deeply grateful to the cast, crew, writers, and fans who have made Virgin River feel like home the world over.”

Netflix's head of scripted series for the United States and Canada, Jinny Howe, echoed that language, calling the town a place that feels like coming home for viewers around the world. “Reaching Season 9 as our longest-running original scripted series, with over half a billion views, is a milestone that speaks to how deeply fans continue to love being a part of this community,” Howe said. The show's two leads agreed: Breckenridge called continuing into Season 9 truly a gift, while Henderson said it was staggering to think the show was still being made and that he was delighted to play Jack for a ninth straight season.

Season 9 picks up a story that left viewers on a cliffhanger. The seventh season ended with Mel and Jack welcoming the newborn they had agreed to adopt from Marley, only to discover that the baby had a congenital heart condition. That revelation is expected to shape the couple's journey in Season 8, with Smith previously indicating that the story would explore the challenges of raising a child with special medical needs and how parenthood affects their marriage. Season 8 is set to arrive in 2027, though Netflix has not announced a premiere date for either the eighth or ninth season.

The early renewal says something about how streaming economics have changed. Virgin River is not the kind of show that dominates awards conversation or generates viral moments, but it is the kind that subscribers return to reliably, season after season, in more than 80 countries. By ordering Season 9 before Season 8 has even aired, Netflix has effectively told its audience that this town is not going anywhere, and that the slow, steady drama that built a half-billion-view audience will keep coming for as long as viewers want it. Cinema Express likewise reports the renewal as a record for the platform.

Why it matters: The renewal matters because it shows what streaming platforms actually value in 2026: not buzz, but retention. Virgin River rarely tops cultural conversation, yet it has spent 28 weeks in the Global Top 10 and reached the Top 10 in more than 80 countries, making it one of Netflix's most reliable subscriber-keeping assets. Renewing a ninth season before the eighth has aired is a statement that the streamer trusts the audience to keep returning, and it sets a new longevity benchmark that every other Netflix drama will now be measured against. For fans, the practical effect is certainty: the Season 7 cliffhanger about Mel and Jack's adopted baby will play out across two more confirmed seasons rather than one, and the show's creative team can plan long arcs instead of writing each season as though it might be the last.

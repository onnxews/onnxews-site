Glenn Maxwell Retires From International Cricket After 14-Year Career

The 38-year-old all-rounder, known as "The Big Show," ends a career of 149 ODIs, 130 T20Is and seven Tests, with three World Cup titles. He will keep playing in the Big Bash League.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Glenn Maxwell has retired from international cricket, bringing the curtain down on a 14-year career representing Australia.

The 38-year-old Australian announced the decision on The Final Word podcast on November 2. He is also walking away from first-class cricket and the List A game, but will be available to play for the Melbourne Stars in the Big Bash League, having had his contract extended to the 2027-28 season.

Maxwell had already quit ODIs in June last year but had kept himself available for T20Is and played the short-format World Cup in 2026, where his team was unable to progress past the group stages. Australia's final match of that tournament, against Oman in February, becomes the last game he ever played for his country. He picked up two wickets with the ball and did not bat.

Maxwell played 130 T20Is for Australia and scored 2,897 runs at a strike rate of 154.42, along with 51 wickets. Across his international career he played seven Tests, 149 ODIs and 130 T20Is, scoring 10 centuries and taking 136 wickets.

He won two ICC Men's Cricket World Cups, in 2015 and 2023, and the ICC Men's T20 World Cup in 2021. Maxwell said there had been a small hope of a send-off game at the MCG, but he understood the direction the selectors were going with the squad. Cricket Australia had omitted him from its national central contract list for the 2026-27 season, a decision announced in April.

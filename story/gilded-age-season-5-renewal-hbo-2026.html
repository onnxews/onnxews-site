HBO Renews The Gilded Age for Season 5 as Its Audience Keeps Growing

The Julian Fellowes period drama earned an early fifth season midway through its fourth, with Season 4 averaging 5.9 million viewers an episode and tracking as the show's most-watched season yet.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

HBO has renewed The Gilded Age for a fifth season, announcing the early pickup on Thursday, November 19, midway through the show's fourth season. The renewal comes as the Julian Fellowes period drama posts its strongest ratings yet, with audiences growing week after week instead of drifting away.

Season 4 is averaging 5.9 million United States cross-platform viewers per episode so far, outpacing last season by nearly 20 percent and tracking as the series' most-watched season. The season premiere was the show's most-watched season opener to date with nearly 4 million viewers, and viewership has grown week over week, with the November 15 episode hitting a season high of 4.3 million viewers in the first three days after it aired, according to multiplatform live-plus-three-day viewing data.

The early renewal is a vote of confidence from a network in the middle of a corporate transition. HBO's parent company, Warner Bros., is being absorbed by Paramount under Skydance, and the swift pickup signals that the new owners have no interest in disrupting one of the network's most reliable successes. The show is co-produced by HBO and Universal Television, and both sides sounded eager to keep going.

Francesca Orsi, president of HBO Drama Series and Films, said in a statement that Julian Fellowes and the cast have once again captured the zeitgeist this fall, delivering another captivating, unmissable season, and that she loves how the growing audience has continued to embrace the series year after year. She added that HBO cannot wait to return to 61st Street, as the Russells, van Rhijns and Kirklands continue to wrestle with the cost of ascent, while the network expands the canvas and deepens the operatic stakes for the characters.

Erin Underhill, president of Universal Television, said it was very exciting and deeply satisfying to see audiences fall more in love with The Gilded Age every season. She praised the cast, the writers led by Fellowes, and the entire creative team for making the world feel both timeless and resonant while keeping viewers on the edge of their seats, and said she cannot wait for audiences to see what is in store for Season 5.

The series follows the new-money Russell family and the old-money van Rhijn family in 1880s New York, with the action also reaching the summer playground of Newport, Rhode Island. The show is partly set and partly filmed in Newport, where several mansions owned by the Preservation Society of Newport County portray both Newport and Manhattan scenes. Two other mansions, Salve Regina University's Ochre Court and Belmont, have been combined to portray the English estate of the Duke and Duchess of Buckingham.

The Season 4 cast includes Carrie Coon, Christine Baranski, Cynthia Nixon, Morgan Spector, Louisa Jacobson, Denée Benton, Taissa Farmiga, Harry Richardson, Blake Ritson, Ben Ahlers, Ashlie Atkinson, Dylan Baker, Kelley Curran, Jordan Donica, Jessica Frances Dukes, Amy Forsyth and Jack Gilpin. The renewal arrived just three episodes into the current season, and only a few weeks after the season premiere featured a widely noticed cameo, with David Letterman and Matt Damon strolling through as background performers.

Created, written and executive produced by Fellowes, the series is a rare example of a period drama that keeps adding viewers in its fourth season. Most shows fade by this point in their run, but The Gilded Age has grown in each of its seasons, and Season 4 is on pace to be its best ever. New episodes debut on Sundays on HBO and stream on HBO Max, with the Season 4 finale slated to air on December 20.

HBO has not announced when Season 5 will premiere or which cast members will return, according to The Wrap's report on the renewal. The early renewal means the writers' room can form sooner, which could shorten the wait between seasons. For a show that took its time finding its audience, the fifth season is a reward for patience, and a sign that HBO sees the Gilded Age world as one of its long-term assets rather than a show winding down.

Why it matters: The renewal is notable less for the fact of it than for the timing. HBO picked up Season 5 only three episodes into Season 4, while the show is still climbing in the ratings, which is the kind of confidence networks usually reserve for their biggest hits. The Gilded Age was never the loudest show on television, but it has grown steadily season after season, and its audience is now large enough that the new Paramount-era owners clearly see it as an asset worth protecting. The open question is how the story evolves: the Russell family's rise has always been the engine of the show, and each season has raised the stakes of that ascent. With a fifth season guaranteed, Fellowes has room to let the consequences of the characters' ambitions play out over a longer arc.

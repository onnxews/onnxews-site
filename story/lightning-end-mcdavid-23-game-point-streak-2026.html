Lightning End Connor McDavid's 23-Game Point Streak in 3-1 Win Over Oilers

Ilya Mikheyev's third-period goal proved the winner as Tampa Bay snapped Edmonton's six-game winning run and held the NHL's leading scorer off the scoresheet for the first time this season.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ilya Mikheyev scored the go-ahead goal in the third period as the Tampa Bay Lightning beat the Edmonton Oilers 3-1 on Thursday night, ending two of the NHL's most impressive streaks in a single evening at Benchmark International Arena in Tampa.

The defeat ended Edmonton's six-game winning streak and, more significantly, ended Connor McDavid's season-opening point streak at 23 games. McDavid had recorded at least one point in every game the Oilers had played this season, and the run was tied with Wayne Gretzky's 1988-89 streak as the fourth-longest season-opening point streak in NHL history.

McDavid finished the night with two shots on goal and no points, the first time this season that any team had kept him off the scoresheet. He had 55 points during the streak, with 13 goals and 42 assists, and he leads the NHL in scoring as of Thursday night.

Tampa Bay opened the scoring in the second period when John Carlson blasted home a power-play goal at 13:29, his seventh goal of the season, after Jake Guentzel slipped a pass into the slot. The goal gave the Lightning a 1-0 lead after a scoreless first period in which Edmonton had produced the better chances.

Mikheyev doubled the lead at 10:46 of the third period, one-timing a long rebound past Edmonton goaltender Devon Levi for his fifth goal of the season. Less than four minutes later, Nikita Kucherov was credited with Tampa Bay's third goal when his shot trickled behind Levi and was banked into the net by Edmonton's Mattias Ekholm as he tried to clear it.

Colton Dach spoiled Andrei Vasilevskiy's shutout bid with 1:36 left in the game, tipping a shot home for his fourth goal of the season. Vasilevskiy still finished with 23 saves, while Levi stopped 16 shots for Edmonton.

Edmonton's power play, usually one of the league's most dangerous weapons, went 0-for-3 on the night, and the Oilers were limited to 24 shots. Leon Draisaitl also saw his own 12-game point streak come to an end in the defeat, a further sign of how thoroughly Tampa Bay's defensive plan worked.

Correction: An earlier version of this article said Edmonton was limited to 23 shots; the Oilers had 24 shots on goal (23 saves by Vasilevskiy plus Dach's goal).

The result moved Tampa Bay to 15-5-2 on the season and 10-1-0 at home, while Edmonton fell to 17-4-3 and 10-1-1 on the road, suffering its first regulation road loss of the season. The Lightning have now won four of their past five games as they continue a homestand that runs through Sunday against Buffalo.

The game was billed as the first meeting of the season between two of the top four clubs in the NHL in winning percentage, and it lived up to that billing for two periods before Tampa Bay's third-period burst settled it. Edmonton had arrived in Tampa on a six-game winning streak and with the league's best road record, while the Lightning had lost just once at home all season, so something had to give — and in the end it was the Oilers' perfect road record in regulation that went first.

For Edmonton, the challenge now is to start a new run. The Oilers return to action on Saturday afternoon against the Carolina Hurricanes, the first game of a weekend back-to-back, and McDavid will be chasing his first point of a new streak rather than the history that ended in Tampa.

Why it matters: This was more than a regular-season win: it was the night the NHL's most remarkable individual run of the season came to an end, and it happened because Tampa Bay executed a defensive plan built specifically for McDavid. The Lightning matched their checking lines against him, kept him to two shots and gave him no room on the power play, showing the rest of the league a workable blueprint. For Edmonton, the loss is a reminder that even a team as talented as the Oilers can be shut down on a given night, and the response on Saturday against Carolina will say a lot about whether this was a blip or the start of a wobble.

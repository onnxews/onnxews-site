Midterms Diminished Trump at Home, but His Foreign Policy Power Stays Intact

A Democratic Congress can block Trump's domestic agenda, but analysts say it has little power to stop him as commander-in-chief.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

The midterm elections left President Donald Trump diminished in Washington, facing a Democratic Congress ready to investigate his administration and block his domestic agenda. But on foreign policy, analysts say, he could remain largely unbowed for the final two years of his presidency.

Even as Democratic lawmakers seek to rein in the president when they take control of Congress in January, Trump will retain broad latitude to pursue his objectives abroad, from Iran and Israel to Ukraine and China. The central question, analysts say, is whether he will feel chastened by his party's poor showing or emboldened to take more dramatic steps to shape his legacy.

The most urgent issue is the war with Iran, which Trump acknowledged on Wednesday helped cost his party both chambers of Congress. He insisted he had no regrets about attacking Iran and said stopping Tehran from acquiring a nuclear weapon was essential. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer told reporters his party's mandate is to put a check on "Trump's chaos at home and abroad" and end the Iran war, but Democrats' main tools, investigations, confirmation hearings and funding authority, offer limited leverage over a president's war powers.

"A Democratic Congress has the ability to stop Trump's domestic legislative agenda, but it doesn't have much ability to stop a president from exercising his powers as commander-in-chief," said William Wechsler of the Atlantic Council. Some experts warn that a cornered Trump could even escalate abroad, possibly targeting Cuba, which he has placed under an oil blockade and repeatedly threatened.

Democrats also plan to use their new majority to revive support for Ukraine, where Trump has halted direct assistance, and to push back on his lifting of sanctions on Russian diesel exports. Some winning Democratic candidates campaigned against military aid to Israel and plan to push for an end to billions of dollars in annual assistance, though Trump himself announced on Election Day that such aid would stop at the end of the year. In the end, analysts say, Trump's veto power and his authority as commander-in-chief will limit how far Congress can force major changes in foreign policy.

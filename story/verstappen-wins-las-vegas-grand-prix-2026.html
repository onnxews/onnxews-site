Verstappen Holds Off the Field to Win Wild Las Vegas Grand Prix

Max Verstappen survived 50 laps of constant lead changes under the lights in Las Vegas, beating Lando Norris by less than a second for his third win on the Strip in four years.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix on Saturday night, holding off a race-long challenge from Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri to take his third victory on the streets of Nevada in four years. The Red Bull driver crossed the line 0.639 seconds ahead of Norris after 50 laps in which the lead changed hands again and again, a race that will be remembered as one of the most frenetic in recent Formula 1 history.

Verstappen completed the 50-lap race in 1 hour, 27 minutes and 39.624 seconds. Norris finished second for McLaren, 0.639 seconds back, while his team mate Piastri completed the podium in third, 1.529 seconds behind the winner, after climbing all the way from 17th on the grid. Kimi Antonelli, the newly crowned world champion, finished fourth for Mercedes, ahead of Alpine pair Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto in fifth and sixth. Verstappen simply would not be beaten on a night when the lead rarely stayed in one place for long.

The four-time world champion started from pole position after Antonelli's five-place grid penalty for a power unit change wiped out the fastest time in qualifying and promoted Verstappen to the front of the grid. George Russell joined him on the front row, and the Mercedes driver spent much of the evening trying to take the lead, repeatedly passing Verstappen on the straights only for the Red Bull to sweep back past at the final corner moments later. The pattern repeated so often that it became the defining image of the race, with different cars taking turns at the front for a corner or two before Verstappen reclaimed the place.

The chaos was not limited to the lead battle. A safety car was deployed in the middle of the race after Sergio Perez collided with Rafael Camara, ending the Mexican's race, and Arvid Lindblad spun his Racing Bulls car behind the safety car and could not get going again. Isack Hadjar, who had run as high as second early on, suffered a broken front wing and dropped to the back of the field, while Alex Albon never even started the race after his Williams was wheeled off the grid with damage from overheating brakes.

The decisive drama came in the closing laps, when the Mercedes challenge finally collapsed. Russell, who had been Verstappen's most persistent rival all evening, locked up while trying to pass Antonelli on lap 48 and ran into the barriers, dropping to ninth by the finish. Antonelli then appeared to suffer a problem of his own and slipped from second to fourth in the final laps, which handed the McLaren pair a double podium they had scarcely looked likely to claim a few hours earlier.

Ferrari endured a miserable evening of its own. Lewis Hamilton finished seventh after collecting a 10-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage in an early battle with Norris, and his race unravelled further when he made contact with team mate Charles Leclerc, who was himself handed a 10-second penalty and finished 10th. Carlos Sainz scored points for Williams in eighth in his 250th Grand Prix, while Russell and Leclerc rounded out the top 10.

The result changes little at the top of the championship, which is already settled. Mercedes have already won both titles, and 20-year-old Antonelli became the youngest ever Formula One world champion earlier this month. But Verstappen and Russell are scrapping to finish the season as overall runner-up, and the Dutchman's victory was his fourth in the past six races, a late-season surge that underlines how strongly he is ending the year even without a title to chase.

There are two races left in the season, and the fight for second in the standings will carry into both of them. Norris, the 2025 series champion, will take encouragement from a podium that looked unlikely after he sank as low as 11th in the opening stint, while Piastri's recovery from a penalised 17th on the grid to third was his first podium since May. For Verstappen, the win was a reminder that on a night when holding position was almost impossible, the driver who kept retaking the lead was the one who took the chequered flag.

Why it matters: The championship may already be decided, but Las Vegas delivered a reminder of why the 2026 regulations have produced such unpredictable racing. With the new power units splitting output almost evenly between electrical power and combustion, slipstreaming turned the Strip into something close to an oval race, with the lead changing hands dozens of times and no driver able to break away. For a sport that spent years worrying about processional races, a night when the winner had to retake the lead on almost every lap is the best advertisement the new rules have had, and it keeps the battle for second place alive into the final two rounds.

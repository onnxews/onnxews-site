Samsung in Talks to Buy Modine in $7.5 Billion AI Cooling Bet

Modine shares soared 28% after Bloomberg reported the talks; a deal would be the biggest acquisition in Samsung's history.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire Modine Manufacturing, the Wisconsin maker of cooling systems for data centers, in a deal that could come together in the coming weeks and would mark the biggest acquisition in Samsung's history. Modine's shares soared to more than $135 in late trading on Thursday, a gain of 28 percent, after Bloomberg first reported the negotiations.

Modine closed regular trading on Thursday in New York at $106.10, giving the company a market value of about $5.6 billion. The shares had fallen about 15 percent since November 3, when Modine reported quarterly results, before the report sent them surging. Based on that market capitalization plus a control premium of roughly 33 to 42 percent, a purchase of the whole company would be expected to cost $7.5 billion to $8 billion, according to the Seoul Economic Daily. Samsung rose as much as 3.9 percent in Seoul on Friday, and its stock has surged 72 percent this year, giving the company a market value of about $910 billion.

Modine, founded in 1916 and based in Racine, Wisconsin, makes heavy-duty cooling and heating systems for large commercial buildings and industrial machines, and its fastest-growing business serves the hyperscalers building data centers. Its data center division posted revenue of $439.7 million in the third quarter of this year, up 95 percent from a year earlier.

The company only recently became a pure-play thermal management business, spinning off its automotive business in October to focus on cooling. Its data center cooling unit has grown 86 percent annually for the past three fiscal years, according to Bloomberg Intelligence, which called Modine 'a small peer relative to the larger HVAC OEM market, making it an attractive target for vertical integration in AI infrastructure.'

For Samsung, the deal would be a big step beyond its core memory chip business and into the infrastructure behind the AI boom. 'An acquisition of Modine would help Samsung diversify into other areas, given the group is too dependent on its memory business,' Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Masahiro Wakasugi said. 'While it can generate a lot of cash from chips, it might need to find other growth businesses such as data center-related assets.'

Samsung has been building its cooling business through acquisitions. It purchased Germany-based FlaktGroup for 1.5 billion euros last year, and this year decided to invest 240 billion won to build a production line for data center cooling equipment at its Gwangju plant. It lost out to Germany's Bosch in the 2024 bidding for Johnson Controls' air-conditioning business.

The talks underscore how dealmaking is picking up around the companies that cater to the booming AI market. U.S. companies and their overseas rivals have stepped up efforts to capture the growth coming from data centers through bigger investments and acquisitions, with Samsung's Asian rivals Daikin Industries and LG Electronics, as well as U.S.-based Vertiv Holdings, among those chasing the same opportunity.

Nothing is signed, and the talks could still fall apart. No final decision has been made, according to people familiar with the matter, and another suitor could also emerge. Representatives for Samsung and Modine declined to comment.

Why it matters: Cooling is the unglamorous bottleneck of the AI boom: every new data center needs to shed enormous amounts of heat, and the companies that do it have become takeover targets. Modine's data center revenue nearly doubled in a single quarter, which is why a 110-year-old Wisconsin industrial firm is suddenly worth $8 billion to the world's biggest memory chipmaker. If Samsung closes the deal, it would be its largest acquisition ever and a signal that the AI infrastructure race is now being fought in heating and cooling as much as in chips. If the talks collapse, Modine's surge will fade just as fast — the shares had fallen about 15 percent since November 3 before the report lifted them.

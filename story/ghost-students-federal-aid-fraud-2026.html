'Ghost Students' Stole Millions in Federal Aid, Justice Department Charges in Nationwide Crackdown

The Justice Department unveiled the first charges in 'Operation Ghostbusters,' a nationwide probe into fake college students created with stolen identities to siphon federal student aid.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 2, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

The Justice Department announced October 29 the first charges in 'Operation Ghostbusters,' a nationwide investigation into fraud in which stolen American identities were used to create fake college students and siphon off federal student aid. Five defendants were charged in cases in Nebraska, Virginia, and New Jersey, and more than $750,000 in cryptocurrency linked to a network associated with Kenyan nationals was seized.

According to the department, the scheme worked by filing federal student aid applications in the names of real people, often high school graduates who never attended college, enrolling the invented 'ghost students,' and then diverting loan and grant refunds after tuition was paid. Officials said artificial intelligence in some cases helped sustain the appearance of enrollment by completing coursework and simulating attendance.

The FBI said it had identified more than $32 million in losses in related schemes, while the wider fraud under investigation is estimated at well over $200 million. Separately, investigators flagged 830,840 potentially fraudulent federal student-aid IDs associated with nearly $130 million, a figure that reflects suspected accounts recommended for disabling, not a proven loss total on its own.

The charged cases include a Nebraska indictment of Francis Mogambi on theft of government money and aggravated identity theft counts, a sealed Virginia case involving more than 700 refund deposits totaling about $1.44 million, and a New Jersey complaint alleging three defendants acted as money mules moving aid refunds onward to associates in Kenya. The charges are allegations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proved guilty.

The investigation grew out of a referral from the Education Department's inspector general to the FBI's Richmond field office, and officials said more than 40 cases are now open across 26 FBI field offices, with Kenyan law enforcement partners involved in recovery efforts. The department described the Kenya cryptocurrency seizure as the first international seizure of its kind in the effort.

Reporting: U.S. Department of Justice

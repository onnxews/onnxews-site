Dow Surges 1,104 Points in Best Day Since April as Oil Slide Fuels Relief Rally

The Dow jumped 1,104 points and the S&P 500 had its best day since April as Brent crude fell below $90 on hopes the Iran war is winding down.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Wall Street staged a powerful rebound on Monday, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average surging 1,104 points and the S&P 500 posting its best day since April, as a steep drop in oil prices and a broad recovery in technology shares pulled the market out of last week's sell-off.

The Dow rose 2.15 percent to close at 52,389.75, the S&P 500 gained 2.35 percent to 7,549.94, and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 2.74 percent to 25,253.14. The small-cap Russell 2000 did best of all, up 3.15 percent, and all eleven sectors of the S&P 500 finished higher. The CBOE Volatility Index, Wall Street's fear gauge, fell 22.3 percent to 19.92.

The catalyst was oil. Brent crude, the international benchmark, fell 5.8 percent on Monday and dropped below $90 a barrel for the first time since August, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude settled at $85.93, down 1.1 percent. The slide followed President Donald Trump's meeting with Qatar's prime minister at the White House and his signal that the war with Iran would end 'fairly soon' and that new U.S. strikes were highly unlikely.

Cheaper oil means lower inflation expectations, and that is exactly the relief risk assets had been waiting for. The rally came one day before Tuesday's October consumer price report, the last inflation reading before the Federal Reserve's December meeting, and traders treated the drop in energy prices as a reason to buy back the shares they had sold in last week's rout.

That rout had been brutal. The Nasdaq plunged 5.5 percent into correction territory last week as the artificial-intelligence trade unraveled, wiping out about $2 trillion in market value, and the selling spread from chip stocks into crypto and beyond. Monday's session took back much of the damage in a single day, and it did so without its leader: Nvidia, the market's most important stock, fell about 4 percent as its board opened an independent investigation into reports about chief executive Jensen Huang.

The breadth of the rebound was striking. The S&P 500 posted 16 new 52-week highs against just three new lows, and the Nasdaq recorded 103 new highs. But the advance was not universal: the Nasdaq still logged 164 new lows on the day, a reminder that beneath the headline gains, plenty of stocks are still struggling.

Futures started Tuesday nearly flat, with the market's verdict now resting on the inflation data due at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Dow futures were up about 0.3 percent, S&P 500 futures were little changed, and Nasdaq 100 futures were down about 0.3 percent in early trading, as investors waited to see whether October's consumer prices would confirm the relief that cheaper oil promised.

The stakes are high. Economists expected headline consumer prices to rise 0.3 percent in October, slowing from September's 0.6 percent, with core prices up 0.2 percent. A number in line with those forecasts would keep the rally alive; a hotter reading would revive the rate-hike fears that have haunted the market since the Fed signaled another increase could come in December.

For now, the market has its bounce. The S&P 500 reclaimed the 7,500 level it lost last week, the Dow is back above 52,000, and the fear gauge has fallen back below 20. Whether the relief lasts will depend on what the inflation data says on Tuesday, and on whether the diplomatic hopes that sank oil prices turn into something real.

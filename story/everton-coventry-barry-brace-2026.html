Barry's Five-Minute Brace Fires Everton Into Fifth as Moyes Equals a Club Record

Thierno Barry scored twice in five minutes to turn a 3-1 win over Coventry, on the night David Moyes took charge of Everton for a record-equaling 592nd time.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Thierno Barry extended his red-hot streak with a double as Everton beat Coventry City 3-1 to climb into fifth place in the Premier League on Friday night. David Moyes's side trailed to Yann Gboho's goal early in the second half at the Hill Dickinson Stadium, but Barry proved too hot for Coventry to handle, netting twice in the space of just five minutes to turn the game in Everton's favor.

Gboho had given the visitors the lead with a neat exchange with Tatsuhiro Sakamoto, finishing from close range past Jordan Pickford. The goal silenced the home crowd and left Everton chasing the game, but the response was swift and decisive. Four minutes after falling behind, Barry rose to head home a cross from Vitalii Mykolenko and level the scores.

Just past the hour mark, the French striker bundled in a center from Tyrique George to complete the turnaround and take his goal tally for the season to 10. It was the kind of ruthless five-minute spell that changes a match and a season, and it left Coventry, managed by Frank Lampard, with no answer to Barry's movement and power in the box.

Barry was not finished. In the 81st minute he turned provider, clipping a pass through to Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall, who lashed past Rushworth from a tight angle to wrap up Everton's third successive victory in all competitions. A pair of wins over Newcastle in the League Cup and Premier League had already built momentum, and this result keeps it rolling.

The night belonged to Moyes as much as to Barry. The victory came on a landmark evening for the Everton manager, who took charge of the club for the 592nd time to equal the record held by Harry Catterick. Moyes has now matched one of the great names in the club's history, and he did it with his team playing some of its best football of the season.

Everton now head into the international break in buoyant mood, sitting above their city rivals in the table and firmly in the European conversation. Fifth place at this stage of the season is no accident: three wins in a row, a striker in the form of his life, and a manager who knows exactly how to organize a team have turned the Toffees into one of the league's form sides.

Coventry remain in 16th place, and Lampard was left frustrated by several refereeing decisions, including a rejected penalty appeal after a hair pull on Caleb Yirenkyi in the first half. The visitors had their moments, and Gboho's goal showed they could hurt Everton, but Barry's brace and Dewsbury-Hall's late strike punished every lapse.

For Barry, the season keeps getting better. Ten goals already, a match-winning brace on a landmark night for his manager, and an assist for the third goal for good measure. The Frenchman arrived with a reputation as a finisher, and he is living up to it in the most direct way possible: by deciding games in the space of five minutes.

Everton's next test comes after the international break, but the mood around the club has changed. A team that spent recent seasons looking over its shoulder is now looking up the table, and with Barry scoring at this rate, there is no telling how far up it can go.

Alcaraz and Medvedev Win Openers as the ATP Finals Get Under Way in Turin

Carlos Alcaraz needed 69 minutes to dismiss Frances Tiafoe, while Daniil Medvedev edged debutant Flavio Cobolli in straight sets.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Carlos Alcaraz and Daniil Medvedev both won their opening matches at the Nitto ATP Finals on Monday, as the season-ending championship got under way in Turin. Alcaraz, the second seed, beat Frances Tiafoe of the United States 6-2, 6-2 in 69 minutes, while Medvedev, the fourth seed, defeated Italian debutant Flavio Cobolli 7-6(4), 6-3. The two winners will meet on Wednesday in a match that could decide the Ivan Lendl Group.

Alcaraz was ruthless from the first ball. He led 4-0 in the opening set, which lasted 27 minutes, and won all 12 points on Tiafoe's second serve across the match. He hit 21 winners to Tiafoe's 12, won 93 percent of points on his first serve in the first set and all of them on his second. Tiafoe, making his ATP Finals debut and ranked eighth in the world, had no answer to the pace and precision of the Spaniard's game.

The win extended Alcaraz's winning run to 10 matches. The 23-year-old, who was the runner-up at last year's Finals, has won back-to-back titles in Vienna and at the Paris Masters, and earlier in 2026 he became the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam when he won his seventh major title at the Australian Open. He then missed four months with a wrist injury, returning at the US Open in August, and has won three titles since. "I would say this is the best I have felt so far in my career at this part of the season," Alcaraz said. "I have a lot of energy and ambition and I am hungry for more."

Medvedev's win was harder work. The 30-year-old former world No. 1 and 2020 champion faced no break points and won all 10 of his service games, hitting nine aces and winning 83 percent of points on his first serve — 34 of 41. He hit 17 winners against 18 unforced errors, converted two of eight break points and outpointed Cobolli 70-55. The match lasted one hour and 25 minutes, and it gave Medvedev his 42nd hard-court win of the season — the most on the ATP Tour this year, after he entered the match with 41.

Cobolli, 24, from Florence, was making his ATP Finals debut and is the only Italian singles player in the field, with Jannik Sinner — who won the event last year — absent. He competed well, pushing the first set to a tie-break, but Medvedev's serving was too strong. The Italian will need to beat Tiafoe on Wednesday to keep his hopes of reaching the semi-finals alive.

The doubles also began on Monday, in the Mark Woodforde Group. Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz beat Harrison and Skupski 6-1, 6-3, and Marcel Granollers and Horacio Zeballos beat Simone Bolelli and Andrea Vavassori 6-4, 6-4. The Finals, which run from November 15 to 22 at the Inalpi Arena, feature the eight best singles players and eight best doubles teams of the season, split into two round-robin groups.

Sunday's opening matches in the John McEnroe Group set the tone. World No. 1 Alexander Zverev beat Rafael Jódar 6-4, 6-4, and Ben Shelton beat Arthur Fils 6-2, 3-6, 6-3. On Tuesday, Fils plays Jódar and Zverev faces Shelton, a match Zverev leads 6-0 in their head-to-head. Wednesday brings the two group deciders: Medvedev against Alcaraz, and Cobolli against Tiafoe.

For Alcaraz, the tournament is a chance to finish a remarkable season with the one title that has eluded him. He has won seven majors, completed the career Grand Slam at 23 and returned from a four-month injury layoff to win three titles — but he has never won the ATP Finals. A victory in Turin would be the final piece of a season that has already made him the youngest man to hold all four majors. Medvedev, meanwhile, is chasing a second title at an event he first won six years ago, and his serving on Monday suggested he is in the form to do it.

Why it matters: The ATP Finals are where seasons are judged, and Monday's results suggest this year's edition will be decided by the two men who have defined the autumn. Alcaraz is playing the best tennis of his life at the end of a season in which he completed the career Grand Slam and came back from four months out; Medvedev is serving as well as he ever has and leads the tour in hard-court wins. Their Wednesday meeting is effectively a semi-final a round early. The winner will be the favourite to lift the trophy on Sunday — and for Alcaraz, that would complete the one gap left in a season that is already among the greats.

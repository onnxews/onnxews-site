Chargers End 11-Game Losing Streak, Beat Ravens 23-20 on Monday Night

Los Angeles won for the first time in 2026, running for 160 yards while Baltimore's ground game managed just 16.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Los Angeles Chargers finally won a football game. The Chargers beat the Baltimore Ravens 23-20 on Monday Night Football at M&T Bank Stadium, ending an 11-game losing streak that dated back to last season and giving the franchise its first win of 2026. The Chargers, who entered the game at 1-8, led from start to finish against a Ravens team that has now lost five in a row.

The two teams arrived in Baltimore carrying a combined 15 straight losses, a Monday-night record, and the game played out like a contest between two teams desperate not to be the one that blinked first. The Chargers blinked less. Justin Herbert scored on a 22-yard rushing touchdown on Los Angeles' first possession, and Omarion Hampton powered in from a yard out on fourth-and-goal — initially stopped by Roquan Smith, then driven across with help — to make it 17-7 with 1:56 left in the first half. The Chargers led by double digits twice and never trailed.

Baltimore's ground game, normally the foundation of its offence, was nowhere to be found. The Ravens managed 16 rushing yards on 15 carries, a season low and the third-fewest in franchise history, at an average of 1.1 yards per attempt. Derrick Henry, the most feared running back of his generation, gained 10 yards on 10 carries — the second-fewest of his career in a game with 10 or more attempts — although he still scored twice, on runs of two yards and one yard, the second of which pulled Baltimore within three at 23-20 with 10:23 left in the fourth quarter. Los Angeles outrushed Baltimore 160-16.

Herbert was the difference. He completed 12 of 18 passes for 129 yards with no interceptions — his first game this season without a pick — and added 51 rushing yards on seven carries with a touchdown. He was sacked only twice, after entering the game having been sacked nearly four times a contest on average. Hampton finished with 105 yards and a touchdown on 22 carries, and Cameron Dicker kicked three field goals. The Chargers entered the night ranked last in the NFL at 15.1 points per game; they scored 23.

Baltimore's offence, for all its struggles on the ground, moved the ball through the air. Jacoby Brissett, making his Ravens debut with Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley both on injured reserve after being traded from the Cardinals last week, completed 33 of 49 passes for 340 yards and one interception — his most passing yards since Week 11 last season, when he threw for 452 against the 49ers. Mark Andrews caught eight passes for 82 yards, Zay Flowers six for 74 and Tre' Harris five for 86. Baltimore outgained Los Angeles 347-287 overall, and still lost.

The decisive play came with 2:03 left. Flowers fumbled after a catch near the Los Angeles 45, Tarheeb Still forced it loose and Elijah Molden recovered at the LA 46. Herbert then completed an 11-yard pass to Charlie Kolar for a first down with 1:52 remaining, and the Chargers punted with 18 seconds left. Brissett's Hail Mary, intended for Ja'Kobi Lane, was knocked down by R.J. Mickens, and the losing streak was over.

"Let's do it every week," head coach Jim Harbaugh said afterwards. "It was good to see the smiles back on the faces." Herbert was more measured: "It's really hard to win a football game in the NFL." For the Ravens, the defeat was their fifth straight and their seventh loss in eight home games, a run that has left their season in ruins. Ravens coach Jesse Minter spoke before the game of the two-game stretch before the bye as a chance to stay alive; the first of those games is now gone.

The Chargers host the New York Jets on Sunday, while the Ravens travel to Carolina. The injuries piled up on Monday night as well: Chargers cornerback Rodney Shelley left with an ankle injury, and the Ravens lost guard Vega Ioane to a shoulder injury and defensive tackle Nnamdi Madubuike to a hamstring injury. For one night, though, the Chargers could enjoy something they had not felt in eleven games — a win.

Why it matters: This was not a good football game, and that is exactly why it mattered. Two teams on historic losing streaks, a combined 15 straight defeats, a backup quarterback making his debut and a running game that managed 16 yards — and still, the Chargers found a way to win, because they ran the ball, protected their quarterback and did not turn it over. The Ravens, for all their passing yards, could not run and could not hold the ball when it mattered. The result does not save the Chargers' season, and it does not end the Ravens' crisis, but it is a reminder that in the NFL, the teams that do the simple things usually beat the teams that do not.

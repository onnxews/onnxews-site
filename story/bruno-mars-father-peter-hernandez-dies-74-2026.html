Bruno Mars Announces Death of His Father Peter Hernandez at 74

The singer said he got to hug his father one last time, calling him the man who taught him everything he knows about music.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Bruno Mars announced on Thursday, Nov. 19, that his father, Peter Hernandez, has died at the age of 74. The 41-year-old singer shared the news in an emotional post on Instagram, writing that he got to hug his father one last time the previous week. The post included a black-and-white photograph of Mars embracing his father in a hospital bed, alongside throwback pictures of the two of them together over the years.

Mars, whose legal name is Peter Gene Hernandez, the same name as his father, wrote that his father was the man who taught him everything he knows, especially when it comes to music. He wrote that his father raised his four sisters, his brother and him, and that in his eyes he was the greatest dad in the world, adding that he will miss him every day until they meet again. He closed the tribute with the words, I love you Pops. Kiss mom up there for me, a reference to his late mother.

Peter Hernandez was a musician in his own right, and his influence on his son's career was direct and deep. Born and raised in Brooklyn, New York, he grew up surrounded by music and became a percussionist and doo-wop bandleader. He began calling himself Dr. Doo-Wop at the age of 13, and he later formed a band called the Love Notes, which at one point numbered as many as 18 people in Hawaii. The band was inducted into the Doo-Wop Hall of Fame in 2014.

The family's life in music shaped Mars from his earliest years. In the 1990s, Hernandez led a show at the Sheraton Waikiki Hotel in Hawaii, bringing his children on stage with the band. A young Mars developed a mini Elvis Presley act as part of the show, and the appearances eventually landed the six-year-old a cameo as Little Elvis in the 1992 film Honeymoon in Vegas. By the time Mars was 12, his parents had divorced, and he and his brother Eric spent time moving from place to place with their father.

Mars has often credited his father with shaping not only his musicianship but his whole sense of style. He told Latina magazine in 2017 that his sense of rhythm came from his father teaching him the bongos as a child, and that the pinky rings, patent-leather shoes, suits and pompadour all stemmed from watching his father perform. Hernandez was, in his son's telling, an old-school working musician, and the showmanship that defines Mars's stage act was learned at his father's side.

The loss comes more than a decade after the death of Mars's mother, Bernadette, who died in 2013 at the age of 55 from a brain aneurysm. Mars's reference to his mother in his tribute to his father underlined how central both parents were to his life and career. Hernandez was candid about his pride in his children, telling the Honolulu publication Midweek in 2014 that he was the proudest man in the world of Bruno Mars and his brother and sisters.

Tributes also came from Mars's siblings. His brother Eric Hernandez is a drummer who performs with him, and his sisters Tiara, Tahiti, Presley and Jaime all share the family's musical background. Presley Mahone described her father as truly one of one, writing that the nurses in the hospital called him an anomaly and that it had been an absolute privilege to be raised by him. Mars's cousin Bayot, who was adopted by his parents, wrote that Hernandez had saved her and taken her in as his own daughter.

Mars did not give a cause of death in his announcement, and no cause has been reported by the outlets covering the news. The focus of the tributes instead has been on Hernandez's life as a performer and a father, and on the career he helped launch when he put his children on stage with him in Waikiki. For Mars, who has spoken about his father at awards shows and in interviews for more than a decade, the loss closes a chapter that began with those early performances, when a father who loved doo-wop taught his son to sing, dance and command a stage.

Why it matters: Peter Hernandez mattered to popular music well beyond his role as a famous singer's father, because he was the working musician who built the act that became Bruno Mars. The doo-wop harmonies, the Elvis impersonation, the tailored suits and the stagecraft that made Mars one of the most successful live performers of his generation were all learned in his father's band, first in Brooklyn and then on stage in Hawaii. His death at 74 is a personal loss for Mars and his family, and it also marks the passing of a link to an older tradition of American popular music, the doo-wop and lounge acts that Hernandez carried from New York to Waikiki and passed on to his children.

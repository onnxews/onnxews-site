Trump Moves to Name Ousted Texas Justice Sullivan to Federal Bench

Days after voters removed him from the Texas Supreme Court, James Sullivan is in line for a lifetime federal judgeship.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump is moving to nominate a Texas Supreme Court justice whom voters ousted on Tuesday to a lifetime seat on the federal bench, according to Senate records made public on Thursday. The White House has not formally announced the nomination, but the Senate Judiciary Committee has received paperwork to begin considering outgoing Justice James Sullivan for a judgeship in Austin.

Sullivan, 45, was appointed to the Texas Supreme Court in January 2025 by Governor Greg Abbott, who lost his own reelection bid on Tuesday to Democrat Gina Hinojosa. Sullivan lost his race to Democrat Chari Kelly, and in a questionnaire submitted to the Judiciary Committee he described his candidacy to retain his seat as "unsuccessful." Democrats won all four seats on the state's nine-member high court that were on the ballot, shrinking the Republican majority to 5-4.

His nomination to the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas is expected to be taken up during the lame-duck session, in the final weeks before Democrats take control of the Senate in January. Republicans are pushing to confirm nearly two dozen pending judicial nominees and add to the 56 confirmed so far during Trump's second term before they hand over the chamber.

Over Democrats' objections, Senator Chuck Grassley of Iowa, the committee's outgoing chairman, has scheduled judicial nomination hearings in consecutive weeks starting November 10, departing from the panel's usual practice of holding them every other week. The accelerated pace reflects the narrow window Republicans have left to shape the federal bench.

Trump made 234 judicial appointments in his first term, including three Supreme Court justices, helping shift the federal judiciary's ideological balance to the right. The Sullivan nomination shows the administration is still moving quickly to fill vacancies, even after an election that cost the party control of Congress.

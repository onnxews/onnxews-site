Beijing Confirms Talks on a First-Ever Meeting of Xi, Trump and Putin at APEC Summit

China said it is in communication with the United States and Russia about a three-way gathering in Shenzhen, though no date or agenda has been agreed.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Beijing has confirmed for the first time that it is discussing a historic meeting between Chinese leader Xi Jinping, U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, raising the prospect of the three leaders sitting down together later this month.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told a press briefing in Beijing this week that China is in communication with relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state. She stopped short of confirming that the meeting will take place, giving no date, agenda or format.

If it happens, the gathering would take place on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19. China is hosting this year's leaders' summit of the 21-member grouping, which includes both the United States and Russia.

It would be the first time the three leaders have met together. Trump is already expected to travel to Shenzhen to meet Xi for the third time this year, with a fragile U.S.-China trade truce set to expire in January. A separate meeting between Xi and Putin is also being considered, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on November 3 that work was under way to organise a meeting involving all three leaders.

The Financial Times reported that officials from the three countries had been holding secret talks for two weeks about arranging the meeting. Neither Washington nor Moscow has confirmed that Putin will attend the APEC summit at all, and Beijing has announced no final decision.

The three leaders would come to the table with very different aims. Trump has said he would like to discuss nuclear arms control, while Putin is focused on the war in Ukraine, and Xi is positioning China as a potential mediator while managing its own economic and strategic interests. Ukraine would not be at the table, a fact that has already drawn concern in Kyiv and in European capitals.

Experts say the gathering, if it goes ahead, is unlikely to produce substantive agreements, because the three leaders' interests are at odds on trade, on Ukraine and on the future of the international order. The likeliest outcome, they say, is a carefully choreographed photograph, with China staging the moment to show itself at the centre of great-power diplomacy. Still, the symbolism alone would be significant: the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear-armed powers have never sat down together, and even a brief meeting would signal that Beijing sees itself as a broker between Washington and Moscow.

The talks about talks come at a crowded moment in diplomacy. The United States, Ukraine and Russia are due to hold trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, a week before the APEC summit, and the U.S.-China trade truce expires in January, giving Trump and Xi a deadline of their own.

For now, Beijing's confirmation establishes only that discussions are under way, not that the summit has been secured. Whether the meeting becomes one joint session or a series of bilateral talks on the sidelines, and whether Putin travels to Shenzhen at all, will be decided in the days before leaders gather in China.

Sen. Chris Murphy Leaves Hospital After Stroke, Heads to Rehab and Will Miss Weeks of Senate Votes

The Connecticut Democrat was discharged from Johns Hopkins on Friday after a small hemorrhagic stroke. His doctor expects a full recovery, but Murphy will spend several weeks at a rehabilitation center.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Sen. Chris Murphy of Connecticut was discharged from the hospital on Friday after suffering a stroke last weekend, and will spend several weeks at a rehabilitation center while he recovers, his office said.

Murphy, 53, said in a written statement that he will miss the next several weeks of votes in the Senate as he heals. His doctor expects him to make a full recovery, according to the statement from his office.

Murphy said he called 911 on Saturday, October 31, after feeling that something was not right with his body while attending his son's hockey game. He felt numbness in his arm and was taken to a local hospital before being transferred to The Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore, where doctors diagnosed a small bleed in the thalamus region of his brain.

"Senator Murphy experienced a small hemorrhagic stroke," Dr. Jose Suarez, chief of neurosciences critical care at Johns Hopkins, said in a statement released by Murphy's office. "He has shown tremendous progress, and at the current rate of improvement, we expect that he will make a full recovery following rehabilitation."

Murphy's hospitalization drew well-wishes from across the aisle. Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont and Lt. Gov. Susan Bysiewicz issued statements on Sunday wishing him a speedy recovery, joining colleagues from both parties who sent messages after his office announced the stroke. Murphy, who has served in the Senate since 2013, is one of the chamber's most prominent voices on gun safety.

Murphy's office announced on Sunday that he had been hospitalized, and said on November 5 that he was improving while remaining in the hospital. Friday's discharge marks a clear step forward from that update, though his recovery is not finished.

"In order to take my recovery seriously and get back to work on the quickest timeline possible, I will be spending several weeks at a rehabilitation center after being discharged from the hospital today," Murphy said in his statement. He said doctors were "very encouraged by the progress I've made so far."

The timing matters for the Senate. Congress returns on Monday after its recess following the midterm elections, and Murphy will be absent for the first weeks of the lame-duck session. Democrats hold a 54-44 majority in the chamber, with two races — in Alaska and Mississippi — still uncalled, so his absence narrows the party's working margin while the new majority plans its first moves. With a 60-vote filibuster still applying in the Senate, every vote counts for the new majority's plans, including the House's planned first-100-hours package, which would still have to clear the Senate.

Murphy is a prominent voice in his party, particularly on gun safety, and has been a prolific campaign surrogate through the midterm cycle. He has also been mentioned as a potential contender for the Democratic presidential nomination in 2028.

No date has been announced for Murphy's return to the Senate. Murphy said he will remain engaged with his office remotely while his staff continue legislative and constituent work.

The discharge closes the most worrying chapter of the week for Murphy and his family. What remains is the slower work of rehabilitation — several weeks of it, by his own account — before the senator is back casting votes in Washington.

Google's New Gemini Video Model Generates 15-Second Clips for 7 Cents a Second

DeepMind's Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash lets creators draft video at 360p or 720p and upscale to 4K, with multi-turn editing built in.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Google DeepMind has introduced Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, its latest video generation model, and the pitch is as much about price as it is about picture quality. The model lets users draft video content at 360p or 720p for $0.07 per second, with the flexibility to upscale final outputs to 1080p or 4K, a pricing structure aimed squarely at creators and developers who have found AI video expensive to experiment with.

The model supports generating and editing continuous video clips up to 15 seconds long. That length covers the short-form clips that dominate social feeds, advertising tests and rapid prototyping, and the multi-turn conversational editing means users can refine a clip through back-and-forth prompts rather than starting over with each change.

Developers can access the model through Google's developer tools, putting it directly into the hands of the app builders and studios that are racing to add AI video to their products. The launch comes as video generation has become one of the most hotly contested corners of the AI race, with every major lab shipping new models at a rapid pace.

The economics matter because video is far more expensive to generate than text or still images. A model that produces a second of video for seven cents changes the calculus for anyone building a product around AI clips, from marketing teams testing ad concepts to independent creators storyboarding short films. The draft-then-upscale workflow is designed to keep costs down during iteration and spend the expensive compute only on the final version.

Google's video push sits alongside a much bigger week for the company's AI business. Alphabet, Google's parent, has been under investor scrutiny over AI cash burn, and its shares have been caught in the same AI selloff that hit the sector this week. Shipping cheaper, faster video tools is one way to show that the spending is producing products people actually use.

The model is available through the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Google Vids and Flow, and it is included in the Gemini app for Google AI Ultra subscribers. Google is pitching it as a general-purpose tool for creators and developers who want to produce video without a traditional production pipeline, from ads and product demonstrations to social clips and prototypes.

Video generation is also a crowded field, and Google is not alone. OpenAI has been shipping image and video updates of its own, including animated GIF support in its latest image model, and a string of startups is chasing the same creators with cheaper and faster tools. Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash will be judged on whether its output is good enough to win repeat users, not just on its price tag.

For creators, the practical question is quality at the draft stage. A 360p or 720p preview is fine for testing an idea, but the final upscale to 1080p or 4K has to hold up on a big screen or a client's feed. Google is betting that the workflow, cheap drafts plus a polished final render, is what working creators actually want.

If the model delivers, it could make AI video a routine part of the creative toolkit rather than a pricey experiment. The next test will be what developers build with it in the coming weeks, and whether audiences can tell the difference between a clip that cost seven cents a second and one that cost far more.

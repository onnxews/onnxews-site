Trump Orders Justice Department to Investigate Democrats Over Alleged Threats

Four days after losing Congress, the president accused Democrats of threatening officials and Republican donors with impeachment and imprisonment — and said he was ordering the Justice Department to look into it immediately.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump said on Saturday that he is ordering the Justice Department to investigate Democrats he accused of threatening administration officials and Republican donors, opening a new confrontation over congressional oversight four days after his party lost control of both chambers of Congress.

Trump announced the order in a series of posts on Truth Social on Saturday afternoon. He accused Democrats of threatening the attorney general and other officials with impeachment and other forms of punishment, and of threatening Republican donors with imprisonment because of their political support. He called the conduct extortion at the highest level and said he was ordering the department to look into what he called a criminal act, immediately.

The president singled out Senator Elissa Slotkin, a Michigan Democrat, accusing her of seeking to intimidate young people who support Republican values. He did not name any other Democrat he accused of making threats, did not identify the threatening letters he cited, and provided no evidence for his claims.

In a second post, Trump urged Republicans to report any alleged threats to the Justice Department at once, or to send details directly to him at the White House. He wrote that the threats being made by the Democratic Party against innocent Republican donors and party members were absolutely unprecedented and totally illegal, and he called for those responsible to be jailed.

The directive is a request for executive-branch action, not an indictment and not a finding that any lawmaker broke the law. The Justice Department had not immediately responded to requests for comment, and it has not announced whether it will open a formal investigation or what its scope would be.

One concrete example of the congressional activity behind the dispute is a set of letters sent on November 5 by Senator Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, to donors to the White House ballroom project. The letters renewed earlier requests for records, directed recipients to begin production by November 30 and asked them to confirm receipt by November 9. Blumenthal described a broader effort covering cryptocurrency firms, immigration enforcement and the Federal Communications Commission as well as ballroom donors.

The confrontation comes as Democrats prepare to take control of Congress in January with the full investigatory powers of the legislature, including the ability to subpoena records and compel testimony. Incoming House Oversight chairman Robert Garcia has said Democrats intend to investigate the administration's immigration policies, its handling of the Epstein files, government contracting and the Trump family's business dealings.

Trump's order is the latest escalation in a dispute that has been building since Election Day. The president has already said he would be open to pardoning allies and staff if Democrats go after them, and his allies in Congress have warned that the coming investigations will be met with resistance, including legal challenges that could delay any public findings well past the news cycle that produced them.

For now, the documented development is the president's instruction itself. Whether prosecutors open an inquiry, which conduct they examine, and whether any of the alleged threats amount to a crime all remain unconfirmed. What is certain is that access to records and the limits of congressional oversight are set to become an early and bitter point of conflict between the incoming Democratic majorities and a president who has just lost Congress and is showing no appetite for cooperation.

Verstappen Inherits Las Vegas Pole as Antonelli's Penalty Wipes Out Fastest Lap

Kimi Antonelli was quickest in qualifying at 1:33.893, but a five-place grid penalty for power unit changes drops the new world champion to sixth — handing pole to Max Verstappen.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Kimi Antonelli finished fastest in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, but Max Verstappen will inherit pole position as the Mercedes driver serves a grid penalty. Antonelli jumped to the top of the times with a 1m 33.893s on his final effort, leaving the Italian 0.258s clear of Verstappen, but a five-place grid penalty for power unit changes means he will start sixth for Saturday's race.

Verstappen, who had set the early pace in the final session, will start from the front of the grid. The Red Bull driver will be joined on the front row by Mercedes driver George Russell, with Lando Norris of McLaren and Isack Hadjar of Red Bull on the second row.

The session came down to the final runs in Q3, and the slipstream down the Las Vegas Strip decided it. After the initial runs, Verstappen sat top of the times with a 1m 34.151s, benefitting from a tow down the back straight that left him nearly two-tenths clear of Norris.

Russell sat third from Hadjar, telling his race engineer he needed better track position after failing to benefit from a tow. Such was the value of the slipstream that the Mercedes, Alpine and Racing Bulls drivers jostled for position on their out laps, with Antonelli only just making it onto his final flying lap.

It proved crucial. The world champion posted a 1m 33.893s, leaving the Italian 0.258s clear of Verstappen, who had failed to improve with his final effort but will start first for Saturday's Grand Prix. Antonelli admitted the lap surprised even him, saying he had struggled for feel under braking but that the tow down the Strip was so powerful he was able to gain from it and put the car on pole — a pole he will not keep.

Russell jumped up to third, which becomes second with Antonelli's penalty, as Norris and Hadjar line up on the second row. Arvid Lindblad will start fifth ahead of Antonelli, with Oscar Piastri finishing seventh in qualifying but set to start 17th because of his own 10-place grid penalty, meaning Liam Lawson, Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto all advance one further position up the grid.

Verstappen had already shown his pace earlier in the session, finishing fastest in Q2 with a 1m 34.394s, just over one-tenth ahead of Gasly. Russell had topped both a wet second practice and a dry final practice before qualifying, and he was fastest in Q1 with a 1m 34.664s.

Ferrari suffered a difficult day, as both Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton failed to reach Q3 after finishing 11th and 12th respectively in the second part of qualifying. They were joined on the sidelines by Nico Hulkenberg's Audi, the Williams of Carlos Sainz — who will serve a 10-place grid penalty for power unit changes — Gabriel Bortoleto's Audi and the second Williams of Alex Albon.

The session was run in challenging conditions, with only one dry session under the teams' belts after a washout on Thursday and a track temperature of just 15 degrees Celsius for the opening 18-minute segment. Drivers conducted several laps simply to generate heat in the soft Pirelli tyres, and Formula 1 rookie Rafael Camara just missed out on reaching Q2 by less than one-tenth after a spin at Turn 1.

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix is set to begin at 8 p.m. local time on Saturday. Verstappen, a four-time world champion, starts from the front with a clear track ahead of him — while Antonelli, the newly crowned world champion who was fastest when it mattered, must fight through the field from sixth to turn the quickest car in qualifying into a race win.

Why it matters: Qualifying in Las Vegas exposed how much of Formula 1 is decided away from the stopwatch. Antonelli was genuinely the fastest driver on Friday night — 0.258 seconds clear on a lap he barely started in time — yet he will line up sixth because of a power unit change, and Verstappen inherits a pole he did not earn on pure pace. That reshapes Saturday's race entirely: Verstappen gets clean air and control of the start, Russell gets a front-row shot at his home team's rival, and the newly crowned champion must carve through traffic on a street circuit where overtaking is possible but punishing. Ferrari's double Q2 exit, with both cars missing the top 10, is the other story — on a weekend when their rivals found speed, the sport's most famous team will start from the midfield. As of Nov. 20, the grid is set by penalties as much as by pace.

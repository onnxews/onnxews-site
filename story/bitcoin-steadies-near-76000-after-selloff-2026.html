Bitcoin Steadies Near $76,000 After Selloff Wipes Out $1.27 Billion in Bets

The cryptocurrency bounced off its lows after a brutal day for risk assets, but traders are braced for more volatility.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Bitcoin steadied near $76,000 on Friday morning after a heavy selloff that swept through crypto markets alongside Thursday's plunge in U.S. stocks. The largest cryptocurrency edged up about 0.2 percent to around $76,150 after dipping to roughly $75,200 late Thursday, erasing the rebound above $80,000 that followed the midterm elections.

The slide spread from equities, where the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5 percent on investor fears that Democratic control of Congress will slow the AI data-center buildout. More than $1 billion in leveraged crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, according to CoinGlass, with the total reaching $1.27 billion and about $1.1 billion of that in long bets. Bitcoin has fallen 5.3 percent over the period and ether 7.3 percent, with ether trading near $2,241.

U.S. spot bitcoin funds also saw money leave on Thursday. Fidelity's FBTC lost around $200 million and BlackRock's IBIT about $105 million, extending IBIT's outflow streak to five sessions, according to Farside Investors. The selling pushed the crypto fear and greed index down into fear territory.

Traders were looking ahead to the U.S. jobs report on Friday morning, with economists expecting payrolls to rise by about 65,000 in October. The report could shift expectations for the Federal Reserve's next move on interest rates, which have been a major driver of crypto prices all year.

Not every corner of the market fell. Privacy coins held up better than the broader market, with monero down only 3.2 percent over 24 hours against bitcoin's 5.3 percent decline. Zcash rose about 1 percent after activating an upgrade that cuts the time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25. But with bitcoin still about 40 percent below its all-time high near $126,000, the mood in crypto remains cautious.

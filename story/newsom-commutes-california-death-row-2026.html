Newsom Commutes All 548 California Death Sentences in Largest Mass Clemency in U.S. History

California's governor is moving every person on the state's death row to life in prison without parole, though most cases still need the state Supreme Court's approval.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

California Gov. Gavin Newsom announced Thursday that he will commute the death sentences of all 548 people on the state's death row to life in prison without the possibility of parole. The Death Penalty Information Center described the move as the largest mass commutation in American history.

In an open letter to Californians, Newsom wrote that he firmly believes state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California's values and undermines the foundations of the legal system. The decision comes in his final weeks as governor after eight years in office, and follows the moratorium on executions he imposed shortly after taking office in 2019.

The commutations will not all take effect at once. Under the state constitution, the California Supreme Court must approve clemency for anyone convicted of more than one felony, which means the court will have to review 382 of the 548 cases, or roughly 70 percent, according to the governor's office. The remaining cases take effect immediately.

California has the largest death row population in the United States, holding more than a quarter of the nation's condemned prisoners. Among them are serial killers Randy Kraft, Charles Ng and Richard Allen Davis, who kidnapped and killed 12-year-old Polly Klaas in 1993. The state's last execution took place in 2006, and only 13 people have been executed in California since 1976.

The move does not repeal the death penalty, which California voters chose to keep in 2012 and again in 2016. Prosecutors can still seek death sentences in new cases. Victims' advocates criticised the decision and said they would challenge it in court, while supporters of the move pointed to figures showing that 68 percent of the people on California's death row are defendants of color.

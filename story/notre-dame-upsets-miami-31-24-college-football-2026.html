No. 11 Notre Dame Knocks Off No. 1 Miami 31-24 and Shakes Up the Playoff Race

CJ Carr threw for 319 yards and three touchdowns as the Fighting Irish built a 24-3 lead and held off a late Hurricanes rally in South Bend.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

CJ Carr passed for 319 yards and three touchdowns as No. 11 Notre Dame knocked off No. 1 Miami 31-24 on Saturday night in South Bend, ending the Hurricanes' unbeaten season and shaking up the College Football Playoff race. The Fighting Irish led 24-3 in the third quarter before Miami rallied, but Notre Dame held on for one of the biggest wins of the season.

Micah Gilbert made nine catches for 98 yards and two touchdowns for the Fighting Irish, who improved to 8-1. Jordan Faison added a touchdown reception, and Nolan James Jr. rushed for a touchdown. Carr completed 29 of 40 passes in a composed performance against a Miami defense that had been one of the best in the country.

Miami quarterback Darian Mensah recorded 276 yards, three touchdowns and one interception on 24-of-43 passing for the Hurricanes, who fell to 8-1. Miami's comeback made the final minutes tense, but the early deficit proved too much to overcome, and the Hurricanes suffered their first loss of the season.

The Hurricanes played without star receiver and Heisman Trophy hopeful Malachi Toney, who was out with an ankle injury. His absence was felt throughout the night, as Miami's passing game lacked its usual explosive edge and Notre Dame's defense was able to focus its attention elsewhere.

Notre Dame jumped ahead 17-3 at halftime and stretched the advantage to 24-3 in the third quarter before Miami came roaring back. The Irish had to withstand a furious rally in the second half, but their early dominance and their ability to answer Miami's scores when it mattered most carried them through.

The result changes the shape of the playoff race. Miami entered the weekend as the nation's top-ranked team and the favorite to claim the No. 1 seed, and a loss to a fellow playoff contender does not end its hopes, but it removes the margin for error. Notre Dame, meanwhile, has strengthened its own case enormously, with a win over the No. 1 team now sitting at the top of its resume.

Georgia also made its case on Saturday, routing No. 10 Ole Miss 42-7 in Oxford behind four touchdown passes from Gunner Stockton. With previously unbeaten Miami losing on Saturday night, Georgia has a strong argument for the top spot when the new rankings appear, and the Bulldogs' dominant performance against a top-ten opponent will be hard for the committee to ignore.

For Notre Dame, the win is a validation of a season that has been building toward this moment. The Irish have now won eight games, and beating the No. 1 team at home in front of a national audience is the kind of result that defines a season. Carr's performance, in particular, will be remembered, as the quarterback outplayed his counterpart on the biggest stage of the year so far.

Miami's response will be closely watched. The Hurricanes still control their path to the playoff if they win out, but they will need Toney back healthy and will need to prove that the second-half rally was the real Miami rather than the team that fell behind by three touchdowns. The margin for error is gone, and every game from here is effectively an elimination game.

The new rankings will bring the picture into focus, but Saturday night in South Bend already changed it. Notre Dame is now firmly in the playoff conversation as a genuine contender, Miami is no longer unbeaten, and Georgia is knocking on the door of No. 1. With three weeks left in the regular season, the race for the top four is wide open.

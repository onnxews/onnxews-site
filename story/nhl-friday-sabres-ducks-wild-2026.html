Sabres, Ducks and Wild All Win on a Busy Friday Night in the NHL

Buffalo beat Columbus 5-2 behind two goals from Josh Norris, Anaheim edged Calgary 5-3, and Minnesota downed San Jose 4-1.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

The Buffalo Sabres defeated the Columbus Blue Jackets 5-2 on Friday night on the strength of a 37-shot attack at KeyBank Center, leading a three-game NHL slate that also saw wins for the Anaheim Ducks and the Minnesota Wild. Josh Norris scored twice and Owen Power added three assists to lead Buffalo, which improved to 8-5-3 on the season.

Jack Quinn opened the scoring for Buffalo at 6:50 of the first period, assisted by Power and Conor Timmins, before Columbus countered at 16:00 when Kent Johnson scored from Sean Monahan and Denton Mateychuk. Norris restored Buffalo's lead at 18:10 with help from Power, making it 2-1 after one period, and the Sabres never trailed again.

Rasmus Dahlin scored the game-winner at 4:30 of the second period, and Buffalo's pressure told as the night wore on. Columbus claimed the lone third-period goal when Zach Werenski scored on the power play at 18:21, assisted by Matthew Knies and Johnson, but it was only a consolation. Columbus, which fell to 7-8-0, was outshot 37-26 overall.

In Calgary, the Anaheim Ducks beat the Flames 5-3, with Cutter Gauthier scoring the game-winner late in the third period at 18:26. The Ducks improved to 9-7-1, while Calgary dropped to 7-9-1 in a game that stayed close until Gauthier's late strike settled it.

Minnesota was the night's most convincing winner. The Wild beat the San Jose Sharks 4-1 at Grand Casino Arena in St. Paul, with the game-winner coming from Shabanov at 14:15 of the second period. Minnesota improved to 12-3-3, one of the best records in the league, while San Jose fell to 7-10-1.

The Friday results followed a strong Thursday for one of the league's hottest teams. Pavel Zacha scored twice as the Boston Bruins won 6-2 against the Pittsburgh Penguins at PPG Paints Arena, with Morgan Geekie adding a goal and an assist and David Pastrnak and JJ Peterka each recording two assists. The Bruins, now 12-4-1, have won three straight and five of seven.

Friday's three games were the only action on the night, but they covered plenty of ground in the standings. Minnesota's win kept the Wild near the top of the Western Conference, Buffalo's victory kept the Sabres in the thick of the Eastern playoff race, and Anaheim's road win in Calgary was the kind of result that builds belief in a young team.

The Sabres' performance was the most complete of the night. Five goals, 37 shots, three assists from Power and two goals from Norris added up to a win that never really looked in doubt after the second period, and the home crowd at KeyBank Center got a full evening's entertainment for its trouble.

Saturday brings a fuller slate, but Friday's winners will take their momentum into the weekend. The Wild look like genuine contenders, the Bruins are rolling, and the Sabres are scoring goals in bunches. The NHL season is still young, but the form lines are starting to take shape.

The road ahead will test whether these early trends hold. Buffalo's attack is producing, yet the Sabres must prove they can defend a lead against the conference's best teams, while Minnesota's challenge is to keep its pace through the long winter grind. Anaheim, meanwhile, is learning how to win tight games on the road, a skill that matters far more in April than it does in November.

Pistons Snap Spurs' 12-Game Winning Streak With 105–93 Win in San Antonio

Cade Cunningham and Daniss Jenkins combined for 52 points as Detroit rallied from 14 down to end San Antonio's 12-game winning streak.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Detroit Pistons snapped the San Antonio Spurs' 12-game winning streak on Tuesday night, beating the league's hottest team 105–93 at the Frost Bank Center in San Antonio behind 52 combined points from Cade Cunningham and Daniss Jenkins. The win ended a run that had carried San Antonio from a 0–2 start to the best record in the Western Conference.

Cunningham scored 27 points and Jenkins added 25, including 21 in the second half, as Detroit rallied from 14 points down early in the second quarter. Ausar Thompson was a rebound short of a triple-double with 14 points, 12 assists, nine rebounds and five steals, while John Collins scored 11 and Paul Reed added 10 off the bench.

The Spurs had not lost since October 23, and the defeat was their first after 12 straight wins. They had also not lost a game all season when leading by double digits, and their 93 points were their lowest total of the season, the first time they had been held under 100. The loss dropped San Antonio to 12–3, while Detroit improved to 9–4 and 6–2 on the road.

Detroit's comeback was built in the middle two quarters. Trailing by 14 early in the second, the Pistons went on a 21–7 run and took their first lead of the game at 55–54 on a Cunningham dunk after a Paul Reed steal with 11.4 seconds left in the half. They led 78–72 after three quarters, outscoring the Spurs 52–39 across the second and third quarters while shooting 51% from the field.

Victor Wembanyama led San Antonio with 25 points and 18 rebounds, but Detroit closed the game with a 13–5 burst while he sat. Dylan Harper added 20 points for the Spurs before leaving with 53 seconds remaining after his left foot slid out from under him and he tumbled to the floor. The team said the injury appeared to be to his groin, and his status was unknown after the game.

The win was notable for who Detroit did without. The Pistons were missing Jalen Duren, out with a strained right hamstring, Duncan Robinson, out with a strained left quadriceps, and Isaiah Joe, out with a right hip contusion. The shorthanded roster still found a way to beat a team that had swept them last season, and the result was a measure of how far Detroit has come in a year.

The Spurs' streak had been the story of the early season. After opening 0–2, they had won 12 in a row, the longest winning streak in the league this season, and Wembanyama's two-way dominance had made them one of the most talked-about teams in basketball. The Pistons, who had been beaten in all of their meetings with San Antonio last season, treated the game as a measuring stick and passed it.

Thompson's near triple-double summed up Detroit's night. His 12 assists and five steals set the tone on both ends, and his nine rebounds were one short of a triple-double that would have capped a complete performance. Cunningham's 27 points were the game high, but it was the balance — four Pistons in double figures, led by two guards combining for 52 — that wore San Antonio down.

The Pistons do not have long to enjoy the win. They travel to New Orleans to play the Pelicans on Wednesday, while the Spurs host the LA Clippers on Thursday. Reuters and NBC Sports both covered the game on Tuesday.

Why it matters: This was the most significant regular-season win of Detroit's young season, and not just because it ended a 12-game winning streak. The Pistons did it shorthanded, on the road, against a team that had swept them last season and had not lost in nearly a month, and they did it by outscoring San Antonio by 13 points across the middle two quarters. For a franchise trying to prove it belongs in the playoff conversation, beating the league's hottest team in its own building is the kind of result that changes how opponents prepare for them — and how the Pistons see themselves.

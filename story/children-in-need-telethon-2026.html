BBC Children in Need 2026 Telethon Airs Tonight With a Star-Studded Line-Up

The 47th annual appeal, hosted from MediaCityUK in Salford, runs from 7 p.m. on BBC One and iPlayer, with the year's fundraising challenges — including a 400-mile cycle ride — reaching their finish lines live on air.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The BBC's Children in Need telethon returns on Friday, November 13, airing live from 7 p.m. to around 10 p.m. on BBC One and iPlayer, from MediaCityUK in Salford.

It is the 47th money-raising telethon for the charity, which supports disadvantaged children and young people across the United Kingdom. This year's campaign is called "Challenge Yourself 2026," built around challenges inspired by the number 26.

The presenting team is Alex Jones, Mel Giedroyc, Rylan, Sara Cox and Tyler West, who will host the three-hour live show, introducing performances, appeal films and the results of the year's fundraising challenges.

One of the night's biggest moments will be the finish of Owain Wyn Evans' 400-mile cycle ride across the four nations, from Glasgow to Cardiff, which began on November 9 and concludes on the show. His ride had raised nearly £3 million as of Friday morning.

Other challenges reach their conclusions on air too. Michelle Ackerley and Gethin Jones will perform live with a band after spending weeks learning instruments as part of Pudsey's Build-a-Band Challenge, and the presenters' efforts, combined with The One Show's Challenge Squad, raised £1,503,391, announced on BBC Morning Live on Friday.

The celebrity segments include a turn from the stars of the new "Dune" film — Timothée Chalamet, Zendaya, Jason Momoa and Anya Taylor-Joy — in a segment with Rylan described as a dream assignment descending into chaos.

The cast of the new "High School Musical" stage show will perform, the Children in Need Choir returns — typically more than 1,000 children singing from the studio and eight to ten locations around the UK — and the hosts will be paired with professional dancers from "Strictly Come Dancing."

Last year's appeal raised £45.5 million, a figure this year's show will be measured against as the night's running total climbs.

Donations can be made online, by phone on 0345 7 33 22 33, or by texting FIVE, TEN, TWENTY, THIRTY or FORTY to 70701.

The telethon remains one of the BBC's biggest live events of the year, and the challenge-led format — ordinary presenters doing extraordinary things — is the reason it keeps working: viewers donate to people they have watched struggle, not just to a cause, according to Radio Times.

The telethon's staying power is unusual. Most charity broadcasts fade after a decade or two, but Children in Need has run for 47 years, and its format has barely changed: live television, famous faces, and ordinary people doing difficult things for money. The 'Challenge Yourself' theme is the latest version of that formula, and the presenters' willingness to look foolish — learning instruments in public, cycling 400 miles — is what makes viewers reach for their phones.

The money goes to projects across the United Kingdom that support children and young people facing disadvantage, from mental health services to youth clubs and food projects. The appeal films shown during the night are the other half of the format: short, direct portraits of the children the money helps, and the reason the telethon still raises tens of millions in a single evening.

Why it matters: Children in Need matters because it is one of the last big shared live television events in Britain, and the charity's challenge-led format has kept it relevant for nearly five decades. The fundraising is the point — last year's £45.5 million is the benchmark — but the show's real engine is the stories of the children it helps and the presenters who put themselves through the challenges. What to watch tonight is the final total, and whether the challenge totals announced this morning — £1.5 million from the presenters' challenges and nearly £3 million from Owain Wyn Evans' ride — push the night past last year's figure.

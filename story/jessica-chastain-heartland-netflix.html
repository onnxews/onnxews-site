Jessica Chastain Finally Works With Country Strong Director Shana Feste on Netflix Thriller Heartland

Sixteen years after auditioning for Feste's country music drama, Chastain stars as a former country megastar investigating her missing niece in a film that arrives on Netflix on November 13.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Jessica Chastain has revealed that she auditioned for the 2010 country music drama Country Strong, and that she is finally working with its director, Shana Feste, sixteen years later, on a new Netflix thriller called Heartland. The actress made the revelation in an interview on The Drew Barrymore Show airing on Tuesday, November 10, and the story of a near-miss that became a collaboration is the kind of Hollywood tale that rarely gets told.

Chastain said she went in to audition for Country Strong in 2010, and remembered the director being so sweet to her, an encounter she never forgot. The role eventually went to Gwyneth Paltrow, who played a troubled country singer in the film, and Chastain went on to build one of the most acclaimed careers of her generation, winning an Academy Award for The Eyes of Tammy Faye. But the audition stayed with her, and when Feste came calling for Heartland, the answer was easy.

Heartland is a country music thriller, and it is a long way from the world of Country Strong. Chastain plays Misty Jones, a former country megastar who comes out of seclusion to investigate the disappearance of her missing niece, a rising country musician. The search forces her to grapple with her own past and with Nashville's seedy underbelly, and the film promises a mix of mystery, music and the kind of character work Chastain is known for. Garrett Hedlund and Carter Faith co-star.

The film is directed by Shana Feste, whose career has been built on stories about women under pressure. Country Strong, her best-known film, followed a country singer trying to rebuild her career after a stint in rehab, and Heartland returns her to the world of Nashville music with a darker, more thriller-driven story. The pairing of Feste's feel for the country music world with Chastain's intensity is the film's most promising combination, and the sixteen-year wait only adds to the sense that this is a collaboration both women wanted.

Heartland arrives on Netflix on Friday, November 13, and it is part of a busy month for the streamer. Netflix has been leaning heavily into star-driven films in the run-up to the holidays, and a thriller with an Oscar winner in the lead role is exactly the kind of title it uses to anchor a weekend. The film will also be competing for attention with a crowded field of new releases, but Chastain's name and the Nashville setting give it a distinct identity.

For Chastain, the role is another in a run of characters who refuse to stay quiet. Her recent work has been defined by women who confront the past rather than running from it, from Tammy Faye Bakker to the characters in her stage and television work. Misty Jones fits that pattern: a star who walked away from fame, pulled back into the world she left behind by a family crisis. The role gives her the chance to play both a performer and a detective, and to sing, which she has rarely been asked to do on screen.

The story of the audition also says something about how Hollywood careers actually work. An audition that leads nowhere is usually forgotten, but Chastain remembered the kindness of the director, and the director remembered the actress. Sixteen years later, that small moment of decency became a film. It is a reminder that in an industry built on rejection, the relationships that survive are often the ones that matter most.

For audiences, Heartland offers a familiar pleasure: a mystery set in a world that most viewers only know from the outside. Nashville has been the setting for countless films and television shows, but few of them have looked at the darker side of the music business with the seriousness Feste brings to it. The film's portrait of a former star navigating a world that has moved on without her is likely to resonate with anyone who has ever tried to go home again.

What happens next is the release itself. Heartland arrives on Netflix on November 13, and the early response will tell whether the film finds the audience its premise deserves. For Chastain and Feste, though, the film is already a success in one sense: sixteen years after an audition that did not work out, they finally got to make a movie together. Some collaborations just take a little longer than others.

NASA Opens Astronaut Applications for a New Moon-Bound Class

NASA is accepting applications for its next class of astronauts, the first open call in two years, as the agency prepares to return crews to the lunar surface and build a permanent Moon base.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

NASA opened applications on Saturday for its next class of astronauts, launching the first open recruitment for the astronaut corps in two years as the agency prepares to send crews back to the Moon and lay the groundwork for the first crewed missions to Mars. Officials announced the call during an air show at the Kennedy Space Center in Florida, where the agency is promoting lunar exploration alongside advances in aviation and space technology.

Americans interested in joining the astronaut corps have until 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on Thursday, December 3, 2026, to submit their applications. NASA expects to make its final selections in late 2027, with the new candidates beginning their training in early 2028. The agency has not announced how many candidates it will select in this round.

The basic requirements are U.S. citizenship and a master's degree in a qualifying science, technology, engineering or mathematics field from an accredited institution, along with at least three years of related professional experience after completing the degree. NASA's selection guidance makes clear that neither military service nor flying experience is necessary to apply, a point the agency has stressed as it tries to widen the pool of candidates.

This will be NASA's 25th astronaut class since the original Mercury Seven were selected in 1959. The agency currently has just 32 active astronauts, a number officials say is too small for the workload ahead. The last open call, in 2024, drew more than 8,000 applications, from which 10 candidates were selected.

The new class is being framed squarely around the Moon rather than only low Earth orbit. NASA says the selected candidates will train to operate on the lunar surface and to live and work beyond Earth, with training covering spacewalking, lunar geology and flying high-performance aircraft. The agency links the class to its Artemis program, which aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface near the Moon's south pole and establish an enduring human presence there.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the moment made it an extraordinary time to join the astronaut corps, pointing to the return to the Moon, the planned Moon Base and the groundwork for Mars. Artemis III commander Randy Bresnik told the Associated Press on Saturday that the current workload far exceeds the number of available crew members, a sign of how thinly the existing corps is stretched.

The selection process will run for more than a year. NASA plans to review applications from December through April 2027, with initial interviews and assessments at the Johnson Space Center in Houston scheduled for the spring. The agency anticipates announcing its next candidate class in the fall of 2027, with recruits reporting to Houston in the winter of 2027-28, though NASA cautions that the schedule is tentative and could shift depending on how many applications arrive.

The recruitment drive comes as the Artemis program moves toward its most demanding phase. NASA intends to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028, and the crews who fly those missions will need to be trained and ready well in advance. The new class is meant to grow the corps so the agency can staff lunar missions, the planned Moon Base and, eventually, the first crewed flights to Mars without burning out the small group of astronauts it has today.

For prospective applicants, the message from NASA is that the door is open to a wider range of backgrounds than the popular image of the test pilot suggests. Engineers, scientists, doctors and other professionals with advanced degrees and relevant experience are all eligible, and the agency's own guidance stresses that a military background is not a requirement. Full details and the application link are on NASA's astronaut recruitment page.

The announcement also underscores how much the character of NASA's human spaceflight program has changed. For decades, new astronauts were trained mainly for the International Space Station. This class is being recruited for a different destination: the lunar surface, a permanent base there, and the long road to Mars. Whoever is selected in late 2027 will spend the first years of their NASA career preparing for missions that no American has flown in more than half a century.

Crusoe Hands Its Troubled Wyoming Data Center Project to Google as Tech Giant Plans $17 Billion Buildout

Google is taking over the unfinished southeastern Wyoming campus after reportedly raising concerns about cost and construction delays, and plans to invest $17 billion developing it.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Crusoe, the Denver-based data center developer, has sold an unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming to Google, the company confirmed on Friday. Google is taking over development of the campus after reportedly raising concerns about the project's cost and construction timeline under Crusoe's management, according to TechCrunch.

Google did not disclose how much it paid Crusoe for the unfinished project, but it announced plans to invest $17 billion to develop the data center campus. The investment was announced a day after Google unveiled its first Wyoming data center, a campus in Laramie County near Cheyenne, powered mainly by a 702 megawatt fuel cell plant.

Crusoe initially announced the Wyoming project last year together with Tallgrass, a Blackstone-backed infrastructure company, planning to eventually scale it to 10 gigawatts of power capacity. Crusoe said its pre-development work covered site selection, government approvals and a hybrid power plan using fuel cells, grid power and gas turbines, with carbon capture planned later through Tallgrass's regional sequestration hub.

The sale carries a sting for Crusoe. The company had tried and failed to land Google as a customer for the project before selling it outright. Bloomberg first reported the plan to sell the site to Google in June, and Crusoe paused work on the campus that same month. Whether the 10 gigawatt target, Tallgrass's role or the original schedule survives the handover is not yet public.

Crusoe is an eight-year-old company recently valued at $30.9 billion, and the Wyoming setback did not stop it raising a $3.9 billion funding round in September. The company said it had more than 6 gigawatts of gross contracted capacity, with 1 gigawatt delivered and running, and more than $140 billion in total contracted value. It is also building a campus in Abilene for Oracle and OpenAI, and a separate 900 megawatt campus next to it for Microsoft, with a first building expected in mid-2027.

Google has not walked away from working with Crusoe entirely. The developer is still building another data center campus for Google near Amarillo, Texas, in Armstrong County. The Wyoming sale ends the partnership at one location while the other carries on, a sign that Google's concerns were about this specific project rather than about Crusoe as a partner.

The deal is the latest evidence that the bottleneck in the AI buildout has moved. A year ago the constraint was chips, with companies racing to secure graphics processors. Now the constraint is power and construction, with data center developers scrambling to find sites, grid connections and generation capacity fast enough to keep up with demand. Ten gigawatts does not fit in a server room, and the companies that can deliver power are now as important as the companies that make the chips.

That shift explains why Google is willing to spend $17 billion on an unfinished site. The company needs the capacity for its cloud and AI businesses, and taking over the project directly gives it control of the schedule. Google's Wyoming announcement names Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy and Related Digital as partners, and describes power from a 702 megawatt fuel cell plant built by American Electric Power with Bloom Energy fuel cells, gas generators and carbon storage.

The sale also fits a wider pattern of consolidation in AI infrastructure. Smaller developers that raised money on ambitious plans are finding that building at gigawatt scale is harder and more expensive than expected, while the largest technology companies have the balance sheets to take over projects and finish them. Crusoe's September fundraising shows investors still believe in the company, but the Wyoming handover shows the limits of that belief.

What happens next will be watched closely in Wyoming and across the industry. Google has given no construction start date or completion target for the campus, and the first phase is slated to run on the 702 megawatt fuel cell project under a 20-year take-or-pay contract. If Google can deliver the campus on a schedule Crusoe could not, it will confirm that scale and control matter more than speed in the race to build AI infrastructure.

Becton Dickinson Beats Earnings, Raises Dividend and Authorises Buyback

The medical technology giant beat earnings forecasts with $5.27 billion in quarterly revenue, then raised its dividend and authorised a buyback of up to 10 million shares — though softer 2027 guidance kept the shares in check.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Becton, Dickinson and Company, the medical technology giant known as BD, has reported quarterly earnings that beat Wall Street expectations, raised its dividend and authorised a share buyback, closing out its fiscal year with a show of confidence even as it warned that growth will slow in the year ahead.

The company reported adjusted earnings per share of 4.15 dollars for the quarter, beating the consensus estimate of 4.07 dollars by 8 cents and rising 27.3 percent from a year earlier. Revenue reached 5.27 billion dollars, ahead of analyst estimates of 5.11 billion dollars and up 5.4 percent compared with the same quarter last year.

For the full fiscal year 2026, revenue increased 3.9 percent to 19.5 billion dollars, while adjusted earnings per share rose 6.7 percent to 12.70 dollars. BD, which supplies medical instruments, syringes, diagnostic systems and laboratory equipment to hospitals and clinics around the world, has now beaten consensus earnings estimates in recent quarters, a run of consistency that investors have come to expect from the healthcare bellwether.

The board backed the result with cash returns to shareholders. It raised the quarterly dividend 1 percent to 1.06 dollars per share, payable on December 31 to investors of record on December 10, and on November 5 it authorised a share repurchase programme allowing the company to buy back up to 10 million shares, or about 3.6 percent of shares outstanding.

The outlook, however, tempered the celebration. Fiscal 2027 guidance calls for low-single-digit revenue growth and adjusted earnings per share of 13.10 to 13.30 dollars, with operating margins roughly flat. Coverage of the results noted that the revenue-growth guidance came in just below expectations, and that caution weighed on the shares.

The market's response was muted. BD shares traded down 1.28 dollars during trading hours on Friday, reaching 212.27 dollars, a fall of 0.6 percent, as investors weighed the strong quarter against the softer outlook. The company reported a net margin of 4.52 percent and a return on equity of 14.79 percent for the quarter.

BD's business sits at the centre of the global healthcare system. Its products are used in hospitals, laboratories and clinics in almost every country, which makes its results a useful gauge of healthcare spending and of the cost pressures facing the medical supply chain, from raw materials to logistics to hospital budgets.

The company also faces the same forces shaping the wider economy. Tariffs, higher input costs and cautious hospital spending have squeezed margins across the medical technology sector this year, and BD's flat margin guidance for 2027 suggests those pressures are not going away quickly.

What comes next will depend on execution. If BD can deliver the low-single-digit growth it has promised while returning cash through dividends and buybacks, investors are likely to stay patient. But with the shares already trading lower on the guidance, the company will need to show in the coming quarters that the earnings beat was the start of a stronger year, not the end of a good one. Analysts will be watching hospital capital spending, procedure volumes and pricing trends closely, because those are the forces that will decide whether BD's cautious forecast proves too pessimistic or just about right.

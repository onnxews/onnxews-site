Ramayana Storms Past Rs. 1,576 Crore Worldwide as India Gross Crosses Rs. 1,000 Crore

The Ranbir Kapoor and Yash epic added Rs. 16.30 crore in India on its 16th day, taking its worldwide gross to about Rs. 1,575 crore in a run that shows no sign of slowing in its third week.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ramayana, starring Ranbir Kapoor and Yash, continues its phenomenal run at the worldwide box office. The Nitesh Tiwari-directed mythological epic has now collected Rs. 1,576 crore gross worldwide, comfortably crossing the Rs. 1,500 crore mark globally, with its India gross breaching Rs. 1,000 crore and overseas collections surpassing Rs. 500 crore.

According to trade tracker Sacnilk, the film collected Rs. 16.30 crore net in India on Friday, November 20, its 16th day in cinemas. That takes its total domestic net collection to Rs. 880.05 crore, while the India gross collection stands at Rs. 1,051.60 crore. Overseas, the film has added Rs. 523 crore, taking its worldwide gross collection to Rs. 1,574.60 crore. The figures add up: Rs. 1,051.60 crore from India plus Rs. 523 crore from overseas equals Rs. 1,574.60 crore worldwide.

Bollywood Hungama reported a near-identical worldwide tally of Rs. 1,576 crore, with India gross at Rs. 1,040.01 crore and overseas at Rs. 535.99 crore. The small differences between the trackers reflect different reporting cutoffs, but both agree on the story: the film has passed Rs. 1,500 crore worldwide, crossed Rs. 1,000 crore in India and taken more than Rs. 500 crore overseas. International markets account for roughly a third of the film's worldwide collections.

Friday's collection of Rs. 16.30 crore was 15.8 percent lower than Thursday's Rs. 19.35 crore, a weekday dip that is normal for a film in its third week. The Hindi version continued to lead the domestic collections, contributing Rs. 12 crore on Friday. The Malayalam version earned Rs. 1.75 crore, followed by Tamil with Rs. 1.10 crore, Kannada with Rs. 1 crore and Telugu with Rs. 45 lakh. The language figures add up exactly to the day's Rs. 16.30 crore total, and across all five languages the film registered 15,497 shows on Friday.

The film's staying power is the story. Ramayana was released worldwide on November 5, 2026, and it is still posting double-digit daily collections in India more than two weeks later, even as it enters its third week in cinemas. The Hindi version has driven the business throughout, and the film has held its screens across the country while newer releases have come and gone.

Directed by Nitesh Tiwari, Ramayana has been made in two parts. It stars Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi and Yash, with Ravi Dubey, Anupam Kher, Kajal Aggarwal, Rakul Preet Singh, Vivek Oberoi and Adinath Kothare among the supporting cast. The second part of the film is scheduled for release in 2028. The scale of the production, an adaptation of the ancient epic mounted as a two-part mythological spectacle, was a gamble, and the box office has now answered it emphatically.

The film's international performance is particularly noteworthy for a mythological spectacle rooted in Indian culture, traditions and storytelling. More than a third of its business has come from territories outside India, showing that the epic travels beyond its home audience. That overseas strength is what has pushed the worldwide total past Rs. 1,500 crore while the domestic run alone was already historic.

Ramayana's run is now a matter of how far it can go rather than whether it is a hit. The film's India net stood at Rs. 880.05 crore after 16 days, per Sacnilk, and its worldwide gross at Rs. 1,574.60 crore on the same tally, while Bollywood Hungama's count puts the worldwide total at Rs. 1,576 crore. Its third weekend will determine how quickly those totals grow. With the second part not due until 2028, the first film has two years to live in the audience's memory, and its box office has given the sequel one of the biggest launches any Indian film has ever had.

The milestones keep coming, but the numbers tell a simpler story: a film rooted in one of the world's oldest stories has become one of the biggest box office events of the year. As of Saturday, November 21, the film had collected Rs. 880.05 crore net in India and Rs. 1,574.60 crore worldwide, and it is still adding to both totals every day.

Why it matters: Ramayana's run shows that Indian cinema's biggest stories no longer need to choose between the domestic market and the world: more than a third of its gross has come from overseas, and it has crossed Rs. 1,000 crore in India while still playing strongly in its third week. For the industry, the film is proof that a two-part mythological epic can work at a scale once reserved for superhero franchises. The question now is not whether the first part is a hit but how high the second part can aim when it arrives in 2028.

Parker-Hannifin Posts Record Quarter and Raises Its Outlook

The industrial bellwether posted record fiscal first-quarter sales of $6.0 billion, with adjusted earnings up 23 percent and a record $13.7 billion backlog — and it lifted its full-year outlook across the board.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Parker-Hannifin, the industrial giant that makes motion and control systems for everything from aircraft to factories, has reported record fiscal first-quarter results and raised its full-year outlook, in a sign that industrial demand is accelerating despite the market turmoil of the past week.

Sales for the quarter ended September 30 rose 18 percent year over year to a record 5.992 billion dollars, with organic sales growth of 11.5 percent, the first time the company has delivered double-digit organic growth in more than three years, and acquisitions contributing a further 7 percent. Net income increased 9 percent to 884 million dollars.

The bottom line was even stronger. Reported diluted earnings per share rose 10 percent to 6.92 dollars, while adjusted earnings per share, which exclude acquisition, integration and realignment costs, increased 23 percent to a record 8.90 dollars, comfortably beating the 8.21 dollars analysts had expected. Adjusted segment operating margin widened 120 basis points to a record 28.6 percent.

The strength was broad-based. Diversified Industrial sales increased to 4.152 billion dollars, while Aerospace Systems sales reached 1.867 billion dollars, and demand increased across all market verticals. International industrial sales rose 22 percent, led by 23.4 percent organic growth in Asia Pacific, while North American industrial sales grew 20 percent with record performance across the board.

The order book tells the same story. Rolling 12-month order rates increased 13 percent, and the backlog reached a record 13.7 billion dollars, giving the company visibility well into next year. That backlog, built on aerospace, industrial and filtration orders, is the clearest evidence that customers are committing to new equipment rather than merely restocking.

The quarter also marked the completion of a major strategic deal. Parker closed its acquisition of Filtration Group, and the business is now included in the company's full-year outlook, with forecast sales of 1.85 billion dollars for the remaining ten and a half months of fiscal 2027. The acquisition adds filtration technology that complements Parker's existing industrial portfolio.

Management raised the outlook across the board. Reported sales growth for fiscal 2027 is now expected at 16 to 19 percent, with organic growth of 7.5 to 10.5 percent, adjusted segment operating margin of 27.7 to 28.1 percent, and adjusted earnings per share of 36.45 to 37.25 dollars. Revenue is forecast at 24.9 to 25.6 billion dollars, and free cash flow guidance stands at 3.5 to 4.0 billion dollars.

Parker team members delivered an exceptional quarter of record performance and an impressive start to our fiscal year, said Jenny Parmentier, the company's chairman and chief executive officer, in the earnings release. Investors agreed: the shares rose sharply on the results, trading up more than 22 dollars on Friday to 915.21 dollars.

For the wider economy, Parker is a bellwether worth watching. Its products go into aircraft, factories, farms and construction sites, so a record quarter with double-digit organic growth and a record backlog suggests that industrial America is spending again, even as technology stocks wobble and interest rates stay high. Parker is scheduled to present at the Robert W. Baird Global Industrial Conference on November 10, where investors will be listening for whether that momentum can hold through the winter.

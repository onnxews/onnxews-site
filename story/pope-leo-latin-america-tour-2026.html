Pope Leo XIV Begins 12-Day Latin America Tour Three Weeks After Lung Cancer Surgery

The 71-year-old pontiff's longest and farthest trip takes him to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, returning to the continent where he spent two decades as a missionary priest.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Pope Leo XIV begins a 12-day tour of Latin America on Friday, travelling to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru in the longest and farthest trip of his pontificate. The journey comes just three weeks after the 71-year-old underwent surgery on October 13 to remove a cancerous tumour from his right lung, though doctors gave him the green light to travel.

The trip begins in Uruguay, where Leo will be welcomed at a ceremony in Montevideo's Plaza Independencia before meeting the country's authorities and visiting the president. On Saturday he will celebrate Mass in Paysandú, and on Sunday he will mark the feast of the Virgin of the Thirty-three, Uruguay's patron, at the national shrine in the city of Florida.

Leo will also visit a parish in Casavalle, a poor neighbourhood on the outskirts of Montevideo hit hard by drug trafficking and gang violence, where the church builds homes, promotes job creation and supports addicts in recovery. Uruguay is one of Latin America's most secular countries, where Christmas is officially called Family Day and Holy Week is Tourism Week.

From November 8 to 11, Leo will be in Argentina, the first papal visit to the country in 39 years, since John Paul II in 1987. He will visit Buenos Aires, Córdoba and Luján. The final and longest leg, from November 11 to 17, takes him to Peru, where he will return to Chiclayo, the diocese he led as bishop from 2015 to 2023, as well as Lima, Cusco and Pucallpa in the Amazon.

Leo, the first U.S.-born pontiff, is also a citizen of Peru, where he spent two decades as a missionary priest and learned to be a pastor. The gruelling itinerary includes 36 speeches and 14 flights, including two helicopter rides, across the three countries.

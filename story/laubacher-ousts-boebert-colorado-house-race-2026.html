Boebert Ousted: Laubacher Flips Colorado's Reddest Seat

Democrat Eileen Laubacher defeated three-term Rep. Lauren Boebert 48.8% to 47.9% in a district Donald Trump carried by 18 points, capping a brutal midterm for House Republicans.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Democrat Eileen Laubacher has defeated Republican Rep. Lauren Boebert in Colorado's 4th Congressional District, ousting one of President Donald Trump's most outspoken allies in Congress. The Associated Press called the race on Friday afternoon, with Laubacher leading Boebert 48.8 percent to 47.9 percent and an estimated 99 percent of votes counted. CNN and NBC News had also projected Laubacher as the winner.

The margin was less than a percentage point — 48.8 percent to 47.9 percent — in a district that Trump carried by 18 points in 2024. Under Colorado law, an automatic recount is required only when the difference between the two leading candidates is no more than 0.5 percent of the winner's vote total, so the result stands without one.

Laubacher, a retired Navy rear admiral who spent 34 years in the service, had declared victory on Monday, November 9, before the race was called — and was so confident she headed to Washington to attend orientation for incoming members of Congress that same day. 'I spent 34 years in the Navy putting mission before politics. That's exactly what I'll do in Congress,' she said in a post on X. 'I don't answer to a political party. I answer to the people of the 4th District.'

She cast the result as a verdict on Washington, not on one candidate. 'The message was clear: Coloradans are tired of political games, broken promises, and being ignored by Washington,' Laubacher said. 'They want someone who will fight for them and deliver results.' Her campaign focused on lowering costs, protecting health care and strengthening rural communities.

Money told much of the story. Laubacher outraised Boebert by more than ten to one, $13.7 million to $1.2 million, according to Reuters, and Democrats poured advertising into a seat they had long written off as unwinnable.

Boebert's defeat ends a six-year tenure that began with an upset of her own. First elected in 2020, she became one of the most polarizing figures in the MAGA faction, loudly heckling President Joe Biden during his 2022 State of the Union address. She barely survived her 2022 reelection in Colorado's 3rd District, winning by just 546 votes over Democrat Adam Frisch, then switched to the more Republican-heavy 4th District in 2024 and won.

Controversy followed her throughout the campaign. In September 2023 she was thrown out of Denver's Buell Theatre during a performance of the musical 'Beetlejuice' after complaints about vaping and groping, an episode she later apologized for. In September of this year, an activist filed a House ethics complaint accusing her of sexual relationships with three congressional staffers and arranging a $200,000 payment to keep one former aide quiet. Boebert denied the allegations, calling them 'utterly false.'

Her loss was one of a handful of House races that remained undecided more than a week after the November 3 election, in which Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress. Reuters reported that Democrats will control at least 256 House seats and at least 55 Senate seats for the final two years of Trump's presidency.

Laubacher will join the new Congress in January, and she has said she will work with anyone, including Trump, to get things done for the district. 'I'll work with anyone and everyone, including President Trump, Republicans, and Democrats to get things done,' she said. 'The work starts now.'

Why it matters: Boebert's loss is the clearest single measure of how far the midterm wave ran: she lost a seat Republicans carried by 18 points two years ago, and she lost it while being outraised more than ten to one. The money gap explains part of it, but the deeper story is that even a district this red punished an incumbent voters saw as a national celebrity rather than a local representative. For Democrats, the flip pads a House majority already above 256 seats; for Republicans, it is a warning that no seat is safe when the national mood turns.

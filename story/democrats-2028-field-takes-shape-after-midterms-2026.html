The 2028 Race Has Already Started: Democrats' New Stars Shake Up the Field

Barely days after a resounding midterm victory, Democrats are pivoting to a presidential primary that could be the most wide open in a generation — and the prediction markets have already picked a new favourite.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The 2026 midterm elections have barely finished, but the 2028 presidential race is already informally under way. Democrats emerged from a resounding midterm victory with a deep bench and a crop of rising stars who have reinvigorated the party, and the contest to succeed Donald Trump is now taking shape in donor meetings, policy conferences and prediction markets across the country.

The midterm stars are the ones drawing the most attention. In Texas, James Talarico and Gina Hinojosa finally did what Democrats have been trying to do for decades, becoming the state's next senator and governor respectively. In Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed is poised to become the nation's first Muslim senator. And in Iowa, Rob Sand won the governor's race by 11 percentage points in a state President Trump won by 13 points in 2024, giving Democrats a model for winning Midwestern territory they had mostly written off.

Senator Jon Ossoff of Georgia has become the most talked-about name of all. His massive re-election victory this week, winning by 18 points in battleground Georgia, has spurred speculation about a presidential bid in 2028, and prediction market traders have responded. On Friday, Ossoff was trading at 25 percent for the Democratic nomination on Kalshi and 28 percent on Polymarket, ahead of California Governor Gavin Newsom at 13 and 12 percent and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez at 12 and 17 percent.

There is a catch. Ossoff has repeatedly said he has no interest in running for president and has pledged to serve his full Senate term. That has not stopped the speculation, or the trading, and it underlines how unsettled the Democratic field remains. Newsom, who once traded near 40 percent on Kalshi, has seen his probability fall sharply, while the rest of the field remains fragmented.

On the Republican side, Vice President JD Vance remains the favourite for the nomination, trading at 34 percent on Kalshi and 38 percent on Polymarket, but his position looks considerably less secure after the midterms. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Florida Governor Ron DeSantis are trading as substantial alternatives, at 22 and 18 percent respectively on Kalshi, and neither has formally entered the race.

The established figures are also in motion. Former Vice President Kamala Harris still leads early 2028 polling, and her decision about a third presidential campaign, after her abbreviated 2024 race against Trump, will be closely watched. Former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg was among the most in-demand surrogates of 2026, travelling to 38 states and campaigning for 102 candidates, of whom 90 have won so far, and early polling has shown him well-positioned in New Hampshire, slated to be the third state on the Democratic calendar.

Others are building their cases more quietly. Rahm Emanuel, who travelled to 20 states in the past 14 months, is set to deliver a major domestic policy speech on November 16 at Carnegie Mellon University, the latest step in his effort to lead the party's so-called ideas primary. Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, who would not have to aggressively court big-donor money if he runs, is also in the mix, as is Senator Cory Booker of New Jersey, a 2020 presidential candidate who has just won re-election.

With such a sprawling field expected, the nomination race threatens to expose divisions that have unsettled Democrats for years: establishment versus outsider, moderate versus progressive, pro-Israel versus Israel skeptics, and youth versus experience. The party's midterm victory papered over those tensions for a week. A primary will test them in the open.

The machinery of the race is already moving. There were two conferences in Washington this past week, and an additional gathering will be held in Denver next week, where Buttigieg and Representative Ro Khanna are among the prospective contenders scheduled to attend. Some candidates are expected to announce their bids in the coming months. Election Day 2028 is still two years away, but for the Democrats' new stars, and for the old hands eyeing one more run, the campaign has already begun.

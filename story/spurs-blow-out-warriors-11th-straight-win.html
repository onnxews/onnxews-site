Spurs Rout Warriors 123-85 for 11th Straight Win

Stephon Castle scored a season-high 32 points as San Antonio opened the game on an 18-0 run and never looked back.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The San Antonio Spurs ran their winning streak to 11 games on Friday night, blowing out the Golden State Warriors 123-85 in a game that was effectively over by halftime.

Stephon Castle led the way with a season-high 32 points on 12-of-18 shooting, including 5-of-8 from three-point range. His five three-pointers tied a career high, and he added six assists and five rebounds in just 25 minutes of action.

Victor Wembanyama added 12 points and 12 rebounds in 21 minutes, his 121st career double-double. Keldon Johnson scored 18 points and Harrison Barnes added 11 as the Spurs' depth overwhelmed a short-handed Golden State side.

The game was decided in the opening minutes. San Antonio opened with an 18-0 run over the first four minutes and 29 seconds, the most points to open a game since the NBA began tracking play-by-play in the 1996-97 season. The Spurs led 38-14 after the first quarter and 63-33 at halftime.

The 33 first-half points were the fewest by a Spurs opponent in a half since Phoenix scored 31 on February 7, 2018. San Antonio's lead grew to 43 points in the third quarter, and the final minutes were played with the benches emptied.

The Spurs improved to 11-2 and are now 2-0 in West Group C of the NBA Cup, a remarkable turnaround for a team that started the season 0-2. The 11-game winning streak is the longest in the league this season and has turned San Antonio into one of the early surprises of the year.

Golden State, which fell to 8-5, was without Stephen Curry, who is out for at least two weeks with a right oblique tear suffered in Wednesday's 115-108 loss in Houston. Without him, the Warriors managed only two double-figure scorers: L.J. Cryer with 20 points and Charles Bassey with 11.

The loss was a reminder of how much the Warriors depend on Curry. Their offense never found a rhythm against a Spurs defense that has been one of the league's best during the winning streak, and the 38-point margin was their worst defeat of the season.

The streak has changed how the league talks about San Antonio. At the start of the season, the Spurs were seen as a young team on the rise; now they are a team other coaches study. The 18-0 opening on Friday was the kind of statement that gets noticed around the league, a sign that this team does not need to warm up before it starts playing defense.

Castle's emergence is the other half of the story. Wembanyama draws the attention, but Castle's 32 points showed that the Spurs have a second scorer who can take over a game. If he keeps producing at this level, San Antonio will be harder to defend than any team that relies on one star. The rest of the league is starting to notice.

San Antonio now heads to Utah on Sunday, while Golden State travels to New Orleans on Monday. The Spurs will look to extend the streak to 12, and the Warriors will hope Curry's absence does not turn one bad night into a slide. Read the report from Reuters

Why it matters: Eleven straight wins has turned San Antonio from a promising young team into the league's hottest story, and the 18-0 start on Friday showed a team that no longer needs to ease into games. The streak is built on defense and depth, not just Wembanyama, which is why it looks sustainable. For Golden State, the blowout is a warning about life without Curry: two weeks is a long time to survive on this roster, and the schedule will not wait for him to heal.

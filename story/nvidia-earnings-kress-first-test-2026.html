Nvidia Reports Its First Results Without Huang on Tuesday. Kress Faces the Credibility Test.

Interim chief Colette Kress will present numbers she set as chief financial officer, with analysts expecting about $109 billion in revenue — but the market wants to know whether the outlook for next year survives the leadership change.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Nvidia will report its first quarterly results without Jensen Huang on Tuesday, and the person presenting them is also the one who set the targets. Colette Kress, the chipmaker's chief financial officer since 2013, became interim chief executive on Thursday after Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. She keeps the chief financial officer job while the board searches for a permanent successor.

Results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 are due at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific time on November 17, with the earnings call at 2 p.m. Pacific, according to Nvidia's investor relations site. In August, Nvidia guided third-quarter revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%. Hitting the midpoint would mean a 12% gain on the record $96.2 billion booked in the second quarter.

Analysts expect slightly more than the company's own guide. The LSEG consensus is about $109.2 billion in revenue and adjusted earnings of $2.48 a share, while Zacks puts the figures at $108.99 billion and $2.49. Barchart puts adjusted earnings at $2.49 a share, up from $1.24 a year ago. Adjusted earnings per share were $2.22 in the second quarter. Nvidia has beaten Wall Street's earnings expectations in each of the past four quarters, according to Barchart.

Gross margin is the softer line in the report. Nvidia guided 74.0%, plus or minus half a point, down from 75.0% in the second quarter, with non-GAAP operating expenses of about $9.0 billion. On the August call, Kress blamed extreme pricing conditions in the memory market. She said margins should bottom in the fourth quarter at 71% to 72% before settling at 72% to 73% in fiscal 2028.

Four things are likely to matter more than a narrow beat or miss. The first is the fourth-quarter outlook: it will be Nvidia's first forecast since Huang left, and investors will treat it as a test of whether demand is holding. In August, Kress said hyperscale growth should re-accelerate in the fourth quarter as supply of the new Vera Rubin platform grows, and that Rubin would make up about 20% of data centre revenue in the third quarter. She also gave a preliminary forecast of about 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028. Whether that number survives the leadership change will be closely watched.

The second is supply. Nvidia said in August that supply would remain a bottleneck at least through the end of fiscal 2028. The third is customers' budgets: Evercore ISI said the four largest cloud companies raised their 2026 capital spending forecasts by just 1% this earnings season, the smallest increase in at least 12 quarters. The fourth is China, where Nvidia's revenue from data centres is near zero and lawmakers from both parties are pushing for tougher chip-export curbs after the Huang reports.

Kress's first test is credibility. She will defend guidance she set as chief financial officer while also speaking as chief executive, and investors will look for signs that a caretaker team is guiding more cautiously. Michael Feldman of ClearBridge Investments told the Guardian that a new chief executive would likely want to establish credibility by setting a more conservative bar. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon, who cut his rating to Market Perform and slashed his price target to $200 from $400, questioned whether any successor could truly fill Jensen's shoes, given how tightly Huang managed relationships with customers, suppliers and staff.

The market has already rendered a harsh verdict on the transition. Nvidia closed at $162.85 on Friday, down 2% on the day and 4.3% on Thursday, and down about 12.5% for the week — more than 30% below its October 6 closing high of $239.24. Its market value stood at about $3.9 trillion on Friday. At least nine firms trimmed their price targets on Thursday, lowering the average to about $217 from roughly $343, and Evercore downgraded the stock to In Line and cut its target to $186 from $465. Options imply a share move of about 7% either way the day after Tuesday's results, according to Reuters.

Washington is watching too. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for a “dramatic shakeup” at Nvidia and urged an outside chief executive, CNBC reported, and the Financial Times has reported that Huang has been discussing a return, with Silicon Valley allies assembling a 'war room' to build support. Harvey C. Jones is now the board's first-ever chair, and the board's independent review into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort has not published findings. House committee chairs have asked Huang for a closed-door interview on December 1. Kress has already been on calls to reassure customers and suppliers, a person familiar with the matter told Reuters, and she had pushed back on some of Huang's financing deals with customers.

Tuesday's numbers may well be strong — the quarter closed before the scandal broke, so they will describe the business as it was under Huang. The harder test for Kress is whether her outlook, and her answers on the call, convince investors that Nvidia can keep delivering without him.

Why it matters: This preview matters because Tuesday is the first hard data point since the most important company in the AI boom lost its founder, and because the market has already priced in a great deal of doubt: the stock is down sharply from its October high and analysts have cut their price targets. The quarter itself is almost beside the point — it ended before the scandal broke. What investors are really judging is Kress: whether she holds the guidance she set as CFO, whether she keeps the 70% growth forecast for next year, and whether customers still believe in the company without Huang. Orbit has covered the resignation, the board probe and the market fallout; Tuesday's call is where those threads meet.

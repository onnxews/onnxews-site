'The Rings of Power' Season 3 Arrives With Four Episodes — and a Major Character Death

Prime Video dropped the first four episodes of The Rings of Power season three on November 11, jumping five years ahead into the War of the Elves and Sauron — and killing off a fan-favorite character in episode four.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power is back. The Prime Video fantasy series returned on Wednesday, November 11, with the first four episodes of its third season. The season has eight episodes in total, with episodes five and six arriving November 18 and the two-episode finale on November 25.

The new season jumps five years forward from the fall of Eregion at the end of season two, and it opens with Middle-earth already at war. Sauron, played by Charlie Vickers, no longer needs to hide behind another identity: he is lording over his stronghold in Mordor, still working to perfect the formula for the One Ring, while his armies march across a world that has mostly lost. Galadriel, played by Morfydd Clark, leads the resistance — and the season centers on the creation of the ring itself, the event the whole series has been building toward.

The first batch of episodes wastes no time on goodbyes. Episode four, titled “Gundabad,” features a devastating confrontation involving Arondir, played by Ismael Cruz Córdova, and Theo, played by Tyroe Muhafidin. Arondir is killed during the episode, which premiered November 11, while attempting to escape the Orc fortress of Gundabad alongside Galadriel, her long-lost husband Celeborn and their Orc ally Marnûkh.

The death lands hard because of who delivers it. During the escape, Arondir discovers that one of Sauron's mysterious ring servants is actually Theo, the son of his late love Bronwyn. Theo has accepted one of Sauron's Rings of Power, placing him on the path toward becoming a Nazgûl. Arondir tries to convince Theo to remove the ring and leave with him, but Sauron arrives and orders Theo to kill Arondir — and Theo obeys, stabbing the Elf with his sword.

Cruz Córdova confirmed he knew his character's story would end during season three, learning of Arondir's fate several months before filming began, when showrunners J.D. Payne and Patrick McKay arranged a meeting to discuss the season. “There's an element of choice,” he told TV Insider, explaining that he viewed Arondir's final decision as a sacrifice, with the character remaining behind to protect the others. He said he would miss portraying Arondir, a character he has played since the show's 2022 debut.

The season's other big arrival is Celeborn, Galadriel's long-thought-dead husband, played by Jamie Campbell Bower, the Stranger Things star now worlds away from his last high-profile character, the villainous Vecna. The pair share flashbacks showing how they first met, and the final moments of episode four bring Galadriel and Celeborn back together after centuries apart — though their reunion is interrupted by the confrontation that kills Arondir.

Elsewhere, the season spreads across the map. In Khazad-dûm, Durin IV's brother Thráin, played by Eddie Marsan, arrives to stir up trouble. Gandalf, played by Daniel Weyman, is now fully Gandalf, wizard staff in hand, in the eastern land of Rhûn. In Númenor, tensions between the King's Men, led by Pharazôn, and the rebel Faithful have reached a boiling point, with assassination attempts, spy craft and kidnappings all at play. And a new orc named Marnûkh, played by Adam Young, claims to want to join forces with Galadriel and Arondir to stop Sauron.

Critics who saw the season early are warmer than they have been before. Radio Times gave the season four out of five stars, calling it tighter and more interesting, and noting that unpopular characters have been cut entirely — including the Harfoots — leaving more space for the storylines that work. Decider's review says the third season is “finally truly epic,” darker and more mature than ever before, with spectacularly brutal action sequences and storylines that were not working ruthlessly cut. Prime Video sent critics the first seven of the eight episodes.

The compressed release schedule is itself a change of strategy. The first two seasons unspooled weekly across two months; this time the whole season lands inside three weeks, with half of it available on the first night. As of November 11, four episodes are streaming, the next two arrive November 18, and the finale closes the season November 25 — and with Arondir gone and Theo on the path to becoming a Nazgûl, the remaining episodes have a darker road to travel than anything the show has attempted before.

Why it matters: This season matters because it is the moment The Rings of Power stops being a prequel with homework and becomes the war story it was always meant to be. Two divisive seasons built the world; the third, by jumping five years ahead and killing a beloved character in its first batch of episodes, is betting that viewers are ready for stakes instead of setup. The early reviews suggest the gamble is paying off, and the compressed three-week release means the verdict will arrive fast. If the back half holds the quality of the first four episodes, the show's reputation — and Prime Video's billion-dollar investment in Middle-earth — gets a real second wind.

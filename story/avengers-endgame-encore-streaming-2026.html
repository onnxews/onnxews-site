'Avengers: Endgame Encore' Arrives on Disney+ With New Scenes Pointing to 'Doomsday'

The extended cut of the 2019 blockbuster, with four new post-credits scenes, streams Friday — and it has already pushed the film back past 'Avatar' as the highest-grossing movie of all time.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

"Avengers: Endgame Encore," an extended cut of the 2019 Marvel blockbuster, begins streaming on Disney+ on Friday, November 13, after a theatrical re-release in September.

The new version adds about five minutes of new footage across four post-credits scenes designed to bridge the gap to "Avengers: Doomsday," which opens in theaters on December 18.

The most talked-about new scene belongs to Bruce Banner, played by Mark Ruffalo. It finds him in a secure facility of his own design, telling the camera that "Hulk isn't getting out of here, and no one else is getting in," as he works on a "gamma suppression serum" to keep the Hulk contained.

The scene ends with a green glow and a figure telling Banner, "I'm here to collect you." The film implies the visitor is Doctor Doom, played by Robert Downey Jr., effectively kidnapping the Hulk ahead of "Doomsday."

The scene also answers a loose end from "Spider-Man: Brand New Day," in which Banner was carted away after a Hulk rampage at the headquarters of the Department of Damage Control. The new footage explains where he went.

The other new scenes include Loki interrupting the dance between Steve Rogers and Peggy Carter, a sequence set at the Time Variance Authority dealing with the collapsing multiverse, and a version of the film's hammering gag that reveals Doctor Doom's mask instead of Iron Man's.

The re-release has already rewritten the record books. It pushed "Endgame's" lifetime gross to $2.925 billion, moving it past "Avatar" to reclaim the title of highest-grossing film of all time. The re-release itself opened to $26 million domestically and $86 million worldwide, according to Hypebeast.

Disney is also preparing a new premium theater format, "Infinity Vision," for the "Doomsday" release, betting that the next Avengers film will be one of the biggest theatrical events of the year.

For fans, the "Encore" cut is less a new film than a trailer in disguise — four scenes that turn a rewatch into a preview of the next chapter, and a reminder that Marvel is building its next event film out of the loose ends of the last one.

The streaming debut also gives the film a second life with viewers who skipped the re-release in theaters, and it sets up a five-week runway of speculation before "Doomsday" arrives in December.

The original 'Endgame' was already the biggest film of its era, closing its first run just short of $2.9 billion before the re-release pushed it past 'Avatar' and back to the top of the all-time chart. That the record changed hands again over a four-minute extension says something about how Marvel treats its back catalogue: not as finished history, but as a platform for the next chapter.

The strategy is also a commercial one. A re-release costs a fraction of a new film, fills screens in a quiet autumn window, and gives Disney a fresh streaming title for November — all while keeping the Avengers in the conversation for the five weeks until 'Doomsday' opens. If the model works, other studios will be watching closely: the re-release as a trailer, the box office record as a marketing hook.

Why it matters: The 'Encore' release matters for two reasons: it shows the economics of the re-release, which turned a four-minute extension into a record-breaking gross and a new streaming draw, and it shows how Marvel now builds its event films, seeding the next chapter inside the last one. The Banner scene is the clearest example — a loose end from a Spider-Man film, resolved in a re-release, setting up a Doctor Doom confrontation. What to watch is 'Doomsday' on December 18, and whether the scenes Marvel planted here pay off there.

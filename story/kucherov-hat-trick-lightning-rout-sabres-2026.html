Kucherov's Third-Period Hat Trick Powers Lightning Past Sabres 8-3

Nikita Kucherov scored three times in the third period as Tampa Bay blew away Buffalo 8-3 on Wednesday, while Edmonton needed overtime to beat Philadelphia and Nashville won its sixth straight.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Nikita Kucherov produced a third-period natural hat trick after Tampa Bay nearly wasted a four-goal lead, and the Lightning blew away the host Buffalo Sabres 8-3 on Wednesday night. Kucherov figured in on all four Tampa Bay goals in the third period, scoring at 1:25, 4:11 and 14:13 to put the Lightning up 7-3 after Tage Thompson had cut the lead to 4-3, and he added an assist for good measure. The full roundup of Wednesday's games is on Reuters

The night also belonged to defenceman John Carlson, who reached 800 career points with his first career five-point game, scoring a goal and adding four assists. Brandon Hagel had two goals and an assist for the Lightning, who earned their fourth win in five games, while Anthony Cirelli posted three assists in his 600th NHL game and Andrei Vasilevskiy made 28 saves. Scott Sabourin and Yanni Gourde also scored for Tampa Bay, and Jake Guentzel added four assists of his own.

Buffalo's night fell apart in goal. Josh Doan and Jason Zucker had goals for the Sabres, who lost for the third time in four games, but Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen surrendered five goals on 14 shots before Colten Ellis relieved him early in the third period and allowed three goals on eight shots. The Sabres had scored three in a row to cut the deficit to 4-3, only for Kucherov to take the game over, scoring his first two goals in a span of two minutes and 46 seconds.

The hat trick was the eighth of Kucherov's NHL career, and it came with an unusual backdrop: the Russian star requested a trade on Oct. 30, and his future in Tampa Bay has been a talking point ever since. On this evidence, the Lightning are still getting elite production from him while that situation plays out, and his third-period burst turned a game that was slipping away into a rout. Tampa Bay now heads to Columbus on Thursday night, while Buffalo travels to Chicago the same evening.

Edmonton needed overtime to beat Philadelphia 3-2, with Evan Bouchard scoring 47 seconds into the extra period after the Oilers had relinquished a two-goal lead. Bouchard also assisted on one of Zach Hyman's two goals, and Tristan Jarry made 30 saves as the Oilers improved to 14-3-3, worth 31 points, matching the second-highest point total through 20 games in franchise history. Tyson Foerster and Travis Sanheim rallied the Flyers to force overtime and extend their point streak to three games, while Dan Vladar turned away 21 shots.

Pittsburgh edged San Jose 3-2 in a shootout, with Ben Kindel scoring the lone goal in the tiebreaker after also scoring in regulation. Kindel beat Yaroslav Askarov five-hole in the first round of the shootout, and Samuel Girard had the other goal for the Penguins, who improved to 2-4-1 in their past seven games. Sergei Murashov made 34 saves in regulation and overtime. Macklin Celebrini scored and added an assist while Mason Marchment also scored for the Sharks, who have lost six of seven, and Askarov stopped 33 shots in regulation and overtime.

The New York Rangers beat Calgary 3-1 behind two goals from Mika Zibanejad, with Tye Kartye also scoring, Alexis Lafreniere collecting two assists and Joonas Korpisalo making 29 saves for the Rangers' fourth victory in five outings. Adam Klapka scored for the Flames, who have two wins in their past six games, and Devin Cooley stopped 21 shots. In Las Vegas, Ivan Barbashev scored two goals and Victor Olofsson had a goal and two assists as the Golden Knights beat Utah 5-3 for their fourth straight victory, extending their point streak to five games.

Nashville extended its winning streak to six games with a 4-2 victory at Anaheim, as Shane Wright had a goal and two assists, Jayden Strubel and Nils Hoglander each had a goal and an assist, and Steven Stamkos added a late insurance goal. Roman Josi had two assists and Justus Annunen made 27 saves. Cutter Gauthier continued his scoring surge with a goal in his seventh consecutive game, but the Ducks saw their five-game winning streak end, with Mikael Granlund adding a goal and an assist and Laurent Brossoit making 18 saves.

The results leave Edmonton setting the early pace in the West, with 31 points from 20 games, while Tampa Bay's fourth win in five and Nashville's six-game streak show two teams gathering momentum. Buffalo, San Jose and Philadelphia all have work to do after losing nights, and the goaltending picture in Buffalo in particular will be under scrutiny after Luukkonen was pulled for the second time in short order. With the Lightning and Sabres both back in action on Thursday night, neither side has long to dwell on Wednesday's game.

Why it matters: Kucherov's hat trick is the headline, but the wider story of the night is how many teams are already separating themselves in the standings. Edmonton matching the second-best 20-game start in franchise history shows a team playing at a serious level, while Tampa Bay and Nashville are both on runs that suggest they have found their rhythm. For Buffalo, the concern is structural: a team that can score three in a row to get back into a game cannot keep conceding eight, and the goaltending change in the third period underlines a problem the Sabres need to solve before their trip to Chicago.

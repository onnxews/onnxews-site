'Madden' Opens in Theaters Today, With Music From Memphis Musician Scott Bomar

The David O. Russell film starring Nicolas Cage as John Madden opens in select theaters today, with original music from Memphis musician Scott Bomar.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

'Madden,' the new film about the life of football legend John Madden, opens in select theaters on Wednesday, November 11, before streaming on Amazon's Prime Video starting November 18. The film stars Nicolas Cage as Madden and Christian Bale as Oakland Raiders owner Al Davis, and it is directed by five-time Oscar nominee David O. Russell.

The film traces Madden's journey from the sidelines to becoming a household name. It follows his Super Bowl-winning coaching career with the Raiders, his early retirement from coaching because of health issues, his reinvention as football's most recognizable voice in the broadcast booth, and his role as co-founder of the bestselling 'Madden NFL' video game franchise. Kathryn Hahn, John Mulaney and Sienna Miller co-star.

The film also has a Memphis connection. Memphis musician Scott Bomar contributed original music to the project, credited with providing 'additional music' alongside a score by composer Marcelo Zarvos. Bomar recorded much of his music at his home base in Memphis, the Sam Phillips Recording Service, the studio named for the Sun Records founder.

Bomar made the connection with Russell through several industry contacts, chiefly music editor Philip Tallman, with whom he had previously worked on 'Dolemite Is My Name.' It is the latest Hollywood credit for a musician who has spent two decades scoring films, most of them as a longtime collaborator of Memphis writer-director Craig Brewer.

Over the past 20 years, Bomar has composed the scores for most of Brewer's projects, including 'Hustle and Flow,' 'Black Snake Moan,' 'Dolemite Is My Name,' 'Fight Night: The Million Dollar Heist' and, most recently, 2025's 'Song Sung Blue.' His work on 'Madden' puts his music in a very different setting: a big-budget Amazon feature film about one of American sport's most famous figures.

The film ends with a striking musical choice. The Outlaws' 1975 hit 'Green Grass and High Tides' plays at the close, and after that, over the credits, there is a suite of Bomar's score. It is a prominent placement for a musician whose film work has usually been heard rather than spotlighted, and it gives Memphis a small but audible part in a major release.

Madden himself remains one of the most remarkable figures in American sports. As a coach, he won a Super Bowl with the Raiders and retired with one of the best winning percentages in NFL history. As a broadcaster, he became the voice of football for a generation, famous for his enthusiasm, his telestrator and his love of the Thanksgiving turducken. As a businessman, he lent his name to a video game that became a cultural institution and a rite of passage for football fans.

The casting is deliberately unexpected. Cage, known for intense and unpredictable performances, takes on a man remembered for warmth and humor, while Bale plays the imperious Al Davis, the Raiders owner whose partnership with Madden helped shape football history. Russell, whose films include 'Silver Linings Playbook' and 'American Hustle,' is known for drawing big, emotional performances from his casts.

The release strategy is a hybrid one. The film opens in select theaters on November 11, giving it a brief window on the big screen, before moving to streaming on Prime Video on November 18. That pattern has become common for Amazon feature films, which use a short theatrical run to build attention before reaching the much larger streaming audience.

For football fans, the film arrives at the heart of the season, when Madden's voice and his game are part of the weekly ritual. For Memphis, it is a reminder that the city's musicians still find their way into the biggest rooms in Hollywood, sometimes one phone call and one recording session at a time.

TJX Rises on Strong Quarter as Bargain Hunters Keep Coming

The off-price retailer beat sales estimates and raised its full-year outlook, with comparable sales up 4% and shares climbing about 6% in premarket trading.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The TJX Companies beat quarterly sales estimates on Wednesday, November 18, as bargain-hunting shoppers kept coming to its off-price stores. The company reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 results before the open, with the quarter ending October 31, 2026, and raised its full-year outlook.

Net sales rose to $15.952 billion from $15.12 billion a year earlier, an increase of about 5.5%, beating the $15.84 billion average estimate compiled by LSEG. Consolidated comparable sales rose 4%, above the company's plan of 2% to 3% and above the 3.8% consensus. Diluted earnings per share were $1.39, up 9% from $1.28 a year earlier, while adjusted diluted earnings per share were $1.34, up 5% and in line with the roughly $1.33 to $1.34 analysts expected.

The quarter also included a tariff refund. TJX received $132 million in tariff refunds, partly offset by $55 million in related compensation and bonus accruals, for a net pretax benefit of $77 million, or $0.05 per share. Pretax profit margin was 13.0%, up 0.3 percentage points from 12.7% a year earlier, while the adjusted pretax margin was 12.5%, down 0.2 points. Gross margin was 32.9% versus 32.6% a year earlier, and excluding the refund the adjusted gross margin was 32.1%, down 0.5 points.

The divisions all grew. Marmaxx, the company's largest business, posted comparable sales growth of 2% on sales of $9.329 billion. HomeGoods comparable sales rose 5% on sales of $2.737 billion. TJX Canada comparable sales rose 6%, and TJX International comparable sales rose 8% on sales of $2.279 billion, up 11%. The breadth of the growth, across every division, is what stood out in the quarter.

TJX returned $1.5 billion to shareholders in the quarter, with $1.0 billion in share buybacks and $529 million in dividends. Operating cash flow was $1.7 billion, and the company held $4.6 billion in cash at the end of the quarter. Inventory was $9.8 billion as of October 31, flat per store on a constant-currency basis. The company added 64 stores in the quarter, bringing its total to 5,349, with square footage up 1.2%.

The guidance was mixed. TJX expects fourth-quarter comparable sales growth of 2% to 3% and diluted earnings per share of $1.39 to $1.42, with adjusted earnings per share of $1.42 to $1.45, below the $1.48 analysts expected. For the full year, the company expects comparable sales growth of 4%, up from 3% to 4%, diluted earnings per share of $5.33 to $5.36, and a pretax profit margin of 12.4%. The full-year figures are reported earnings per share, not adjusted, which is why some coverage cited a lower adjusted range.

Chief executive Ernie Herrman said he expects Marmaxx to improve in the fourth quarter, though unseasonably warm weather is holding back demand in some categories, and he expects sales momentum to accelerate through the holiday season. The off-price model has been a winner as consumers hunt for value, and TJX's ability to buy branded merchandise at a discount and sell it below department-store prices has kept its stores full.

Shares rose about 6% in premarket trading on Wednesday, with the stock up 5.63% to $137.16 in premarket trading, according to market data. The holiday season will test whether the momentum holds: the company's own guidance for the fourth quarter is cautious on earnings, even as it expects comparable sales to keep growing, and warm weather is already weighing on demand for cold-weather categories.

Why it matters: TJX is the clearest winner of the value-hunting consumer era, and this quarter shows why. Every division grew, comparable sales beat the plan, and the company returned $1.5 billion to shareholders while still adding 64 stores. The off-price model works precisely when shoppers are stretched: branded goods at a discount, no need for markdowns, and a treasure-hunt experience that keeps people coming back. The caution is in the guidance: fourth-quarter adjusted earnings are expected to come in below Wall Street's forecast, and warm weather is already hurting cold-weather categories. The question for the holidays is whether the traffic that carried the third quarter can carry the fourth, when the comparisons get tougher and the weather has not cooperated.

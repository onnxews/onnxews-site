Anthropic Sets IPO Price Range of $70 to $78, Eyeing a $1.4 Trillion Valuation

The Claude maker launched its roadshow for what would be one of the largest listings ever, even as AI stocks wobble.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude artificial intelligence assistant, set the terms of its long-awaited stock market debut on Tuesday, launching its IPO roadshow with a price range of 70 to 78 dollars per share. The offering would value the company at as much as 1.404 trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis, making it one of the largest listings in history even after a sharp markdown from the valuations discussed earlier this year.

The terms appear in Amendment Number 2 to the company's Form S-1, the IPO registration filing submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission on November 10. Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares, a sale expected to raise between 57.75 and 64.35 billion dollars. The company itself is selling about 801 million of those shares, worth roughly 62.5 billion dollars before expenses at the top of the range, while existing stockholders are selling about 23.6 million shares.

Underwriters have a 30-day option to buy a further 123.75 million shares from the company, which at 78 dollars would take the total offering to about 74 billion dollars. Anthropic expects net proceeds of between 84.6 and 93.7 billion dollars once a concurrent private placement is included, and the shares are set to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol ANT.

The valuation math depends on how the shares are counted. A 78 dollar price implies an equity value of about 1.02 trillion dollars based on the 13.09 billion Class A and Class N shares that would be outstanding after the offering and a concurrent private placement. On a fully diluted basis, adding options, unvested restricted stock units and shares reserved for employee plans, the value rises to about 1.26 trillion dollars at 70 dollars and about 1.40 trillion at 78 dollars.

Trading is expected to begin close to November 19, according to CNBC, with the Wall Street Journal reporting a possible November 19 to 20 window. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the deal. The company filed its S-1 publicly on October 26 and amended it on November 2 with third-quarter results, and the final offer price and official trading date remain unconfirmed.

The filing also revealed the scale of Anthropic's business. Third-quarter revenue came in at 17 billion dollars, up 46.7 percent from the second quarter, and revenue for the first nine months of 2026 reached 33.2 billion dollars, against 2.55 billion dollars a year earlier. Run-rate revenue reached 81 billion dollars by the end of October, about 10 billion dollars more than at the end of September.

Growth has come with heavy spending. The company swung to a 567 million dollar operating loss in the third quarter after a 974 million dollar operating profit in the second, and it has committed to spend 895.4 billion dollars, mostly on computing power. It also expects about 14.2 billion dollars of cumulative compensation expense related to restricted stock units at IPO completion.

The valuation has fallen a long way from the summer's ambitions. In August, the New York Times reported that Anthropic could raise more than 100 billion dollars at about a 2 trillion dollar valuation, and in October Reuters reported that some investors were considering about 1.5 trillion dollars. By November 6, Bloomberg reported that potential buyers were discussing 1.2 to 1.3 trillion dollars after the tech sell-off. The 1.4 trillion dollar fully diluted figure at the top of the range sits above the 965 billion dollar valuation from Anthropic's May financing, but well below the 2 trillion once targeted.

The deal also includes a 26 billion dollar concurrent private placement, in which investors have agreed to buy non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price, a structure that underscores how much capital the company is raising in a single stroke. Morningstar, in research published on November 9 before the terms were set, put Anthropic's fair value at 1.7 trillion dollars in its base case.

Anthropic is not debuting alone. Its biggest rival, OpenAI, was reportedly valued at 1.25 trillion dollars last week after raising 11 billion dollars, nearly half the 20 billion it had first tried to raise. Both companies are racing to turn enormous revenue growth into sustainable profits while spending staggering sums on computing, and the market's verdict on Anthropic's price range will be one of the clearest signals yet of how much investors still believe in the AI boom.

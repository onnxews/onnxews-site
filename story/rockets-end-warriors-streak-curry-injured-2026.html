Rockets End Warriors' Eight-Game Winning Streak as Curry Exits Injured

Houston beat Golden State 115-108 on a chaotic Wednesday night in the NBA, with Stephen Curry leaving in the first quarter for an MRI and Rockets guard Marcus Smart ejected for throwing a punch.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

The Houston Rockets ended the Golden State Warriors' eight-game winning streak with a 115-108 victory on Wednesday night, pulling away in the fourth quarter of a game that had far more than basketball to talk about. Alperen Sengun flirted with a triple-double, Kevin Durant led the Rockets with 20 points, and Houston won its second game in a row, but the night belonged to the injury and ejection drama that unfolded around the result. The full roundup of Wednesday's games is on Reuters

The biggest concern for Golden State was Stephen Curry, who played only six minutes before leaving with right abdominal soreness in the first quarter. Curry was seen in pain after coming off a Draymond Green screen and attempting a 28-foot three-pointer, and he soon headed to the locker room, where he stayed for the rest of the night. He later left the arena to undergo an MRI, and the results were not immediately known. Warriors coach Steve Kerr did not hide the worry in the organisation, saying: "Concern is high. But I have no idea the extent." Curry told Kerr the injury was a tweak in his abdomen, but also something he had "never felt before".

The game also turned heated in the third quarter, when Rockets guard Marcus Smart was ejected after throwing a right hook that grazed the nose of Warriors centre Charles Bassey. The incident came after a transition basket by Amen Thompson, and Smart was assessed an automatic Flagrant 2 ejection in front of referee Mitchell Ervin. Rockets coach Ime Udoka said afterwards that Smart claimed his previously injured elbow had been bent during a tangle, but Bassey was blunt in his own assessment, saying: "He's been playing dirty the whole game, man." Later in the fourth quarter, Green clashed with fans seated near the Rockets bench, and arena security removed two spectators with about 4:28 remaining.

On the court, Sengun posted 19 points, 13 rebounds and nine assists, while Amen Thompson added 19 points and seven rebounds for Houston. Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg produced 25 points and 10 rebounds for the Warriors, and Brandin Podziemski added 22 points, but Golden State could not overcome Curry's absence. The defeat was the Warriors' first in nine games, and it came against a Houston side that is building momentum in the Western Conference.

San Antonio kept its own remarkable run going with a 124-119 win at Chicago, the Spurs' 10th straight victory. Victor Wembanyama had 32 points and nine rebounds, and Dylan Harper added 18 points and 10 assists, as San Antonio recovered from a 22-point deficit late in the second quarter and built a 15-point lead in the fourth. De'Aaron Fox contributed 17 points, all in the first half, and nine assists, and the Spurs had seven scorers in double figures. The Bulls lost their eighth straight game despite a career-high-tying 23 points from Jalen Smith, and rookie Caleb Wilson left in the third quarter with a right hand injury after scoring 15 points in 15 minutes.

The Los Angeles Lakers beat the Milwaukee Bucks 123-112 for their seventh consecutive win, becoming the league's first team to 11 wins as of Wednesday night. Luka Doncic scored 32 points and Sandro Mamukelashvili added 23 against the team that waived him earlier in his career. "It's always great to come back and beat the team that waived you," Mamukelashvili said. Austin Reaves scored 18 points before leaving with a left heel injury, while Doncic and Collin Sexton also had injury scares, with Sexton needing stitches above his right eye after bumping heads with Milwaukee's Ryan Rollins. The Bucks took their eighth straight loss despite 27 points and seven assists from Tyler Herro, who was back from a knee injury, and 25 points from Jaime Jaquez Jr.

Boston staged the night's other great comeback, erasing a 22-point first-half deficit to beat Toronto 98-93. Jayson Tatum logged 24 points, eight assists and seven rebounds, and the Celtics trailed 93-90 with one minute left before scoring the final eight points of the game. Derrick White added 19 points, and Neemias Queta had 11 points and 13 rebounds, as Boston extended its winning streak to five games while playing without Paul George, who sat out due to illness a night after scoring 36 points in his return to Philadelphia. Ja'Kobe Walter scored 17 points and Allen Graves added 13 for the Raptors, who took their fifth loss in seven games.

Elsewhere, Denver beat Memphis 123-118 behind 30 points and eight assists from Jamal Murray and 27 points, 11 rebounds and eight assists from Nikola Jokic, although an 18-point first-half lead shrank to two points in the final minute. The Grizzlies have now dropped four straight at home and four of their past five overall after opening the season 6-0. Portland beat Oklahoma City 118-108 for the second straight night, with Deni Avdija recording 28 points, six assists and four steals, while Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 31 points in defeat for the Thunder.

The Pacers beat the Washington Wizards 114-101 for their first home win of the season, as Pascal Siakam collected 28 points and made all 12 of his foul shots. Brooklyn cruised past New Orleans 145-111, the fifth time in franchise history the Nets have scored 145 points in a game that did not go to overtime, and the Pelicans have now allowed at least 138 points in each of their past three games. Zion Williamson, who scored 29 points in the defeat, called his team's performance "unacceptable" afterwards. Orlando beat Utah 123-108 as Franz Wagner tied his career high with 38 points, sending the Jazz to their 10th consecutive loss.

Why it matters: Wednesday night was a reminder of how quickly the early-season picture can shift. Golden State's eight-game streak is over and its season now hinges on Curry's MRI, while Houston's win, San Antonio's 10th straight and the Lakers reaching 11 wins first show a Western Conference with several teams hitting form at once. The injuries matter beyond one night: Curry's abdomen, Reaves' heel and Wilson's hand will all be watched closely, and the struggling Bulls, Bucks, Jazz and Pelicans are all now on losing runs of eight, eight, 10 and four games respectively, which puts real pressure on their next outings.

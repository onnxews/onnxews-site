Zverev Beats Shelton in Straight Sets to Reach ATP Finals Semifinals First

The world No. 1 beat Ben Shelton 6–2, 7–6(4) in Turin, losing just two points on serve in the second set to become the first semifinalist.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

World No. 1 Alexander Zverev became the first player through to the semifinals of the 2026 Nitto ATP Finals on Tuesday, beating Ben Shelton 6–2, 7–6(4) in just over 90 minutes at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. The win was a rematch of the 2026 US Open final, which Zverev also won, and it extended his perfect record against the American to 7–0.

Zverev was close to flawless on serve. He hit 14 aces with no double faults, won 88% of his first-serve points, 38 of 43, and lost only two points on serve in the entire second set, winning 22 of 23 points behind his first serve. From the end of the first set to 6–6 in the second, he won 25 points in a row on serve. He won 74 points in the match to Shelton's 55.

The first set was over quickly. Zverev broke Shelton in the opening game and again in the fifth, taking it 6–2 in 38 minutes, and he converted two of six break points while saving the only two he faced all match. Shelton hit 15 aces of his own but made 24 unforced errors to Zverev's 10, and he could not find a way through a serve that barely missed all night.

The second set was a serving contest that neither man would give up. Zverev did not face a break point, and the set went to a tiebreak, where he led 3–1 and then 5–3 before earning two match points at 6–4. He converted the first with a forehand passing shot on the run, sealing a straight-sets win that never looked in doubt even when the scoreboard stayed close.

The victory was Zverev's tour-leading 67th of the season and his 20th at the ATP Finals, making him only the ninth man to reach that mark at the event. It was his fifth semifinal at the season-ending championship, after 2018, 2019, 2021 and 2024, and he won the title in 2018 in London and 2021 in Turin. His record against left-handers now stands at 49–1 since Roland-Garros in 2023.

Zverev leads the John McEnroe Group at 2–0, with a 4–0 record in sets and a 25–16 edge in games, and he is the only player in the group guaranteed a semifinal place. Arthur Fils beat Rafael Jodar 4–6, 6–2, 6–2 earlier in the day for his first win in Turin, leaving the group standings at Fils 1–1, Shelton 1–1 and Jodar 0–2. Zverev plays Fils and Shelton plays Jodar on Thursday, with the semifinals on Saturday, November 21 and the final on Sunday, November 22.

Zverev was pleased with how he started. "I think what was best today was the way I came out. I think the first set was close to perfect," he said. "If he's playing with confidence, it's really, really tough against him." Shelton was generous in defeat. "It’s always difficult to play against Sascha, the way that he serves, the way he returns, the weight on his shots."

The field in Turin is missing two of the sport's biggest names. Defending champion Jannik Sinner is absent with a knee injury that ended his season early, and Novak Djokovic did not qualify. On Wednesday, the Ivan Lendl Group takes the court, with Carlos Alcaraz facing Daniil Medvedev and Flavio Cobolli playing Frances Tiafoe. Alcaraz beat Tiafoe 6–2, 6–2 and Medvedev beat Cobolli 7–6, 6–3 in the group's opening matches on Monday. Tennis Majors and Tennis Grandstand both covered the match on Tuesday.

Why it matters: Zverev's win was less a match than a demonstration of what makes him the world No. 1: a serve that gave away two points in an entire set, and a game built to win the biggest events without drama. The 7–0 record against Shelton, including a US Open final, is becoming one of the sport's defining mismatches, and the 49–1 mark against left-handers since Roland-Garros shows how complete his game has become. With Sinner injured and Djokovic absent, the path to a third ATP Finals title is open, and Zverev is playing like a man who knows it. The semifinal on Saturday will be the first real test of whether anyone in Turin can touch him.

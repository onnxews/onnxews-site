Anthropic Debuts on Nasdaq After Record $81 Billion IPO Values AI Lab at $1.5 Trillion

The maker of the Claude chatbot begins trading Thursday under the ticker ANT after selling 980 million shares at $83 apiece — the largest initial public offering in history, eclipsing SpaceX's $75 billion debut.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI chatbot, is set to begin trading Thursday in the largest stock market debut of all time. The AI developer sold 980 million shares at $83 a share in its initial public offering, raising $81.3 billion — a figure that surpasses SpaceX's record-setting $75 billion debut earlier this year.

The company sold 980 million shares at $83 each, pricing the IPO above its initial range of $70 to $78 set when it launched the roadshow. The additional $26 billion raised through a private placement takes the total haul to over $107 billion. At $83 a share, the company's valuation could be roughly $1.5 trillion. It will trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT."

As the first frontier AI company to go public, Anthropic is poised to become the sector's benchmark, with its arch-rival OpenAI reportedly eyeing a 2027 listing. When it debuts on the Nasdaq later on Thursday, Anthropic will join the world's trillion-dollar club, alongside technology giants such as Microsoft, Apple and Nvidia. The listing will also give retail investors a chance to pile into one of the AI industry's hottest names, potentially amplifying swings in the stock as trading begins.

The blockbuster IPO underscores the enormous amount of money being invested in AI, and the growing influence that the technology has on everything from the stock market to the broader U.S. economy. "Both of them are much bigger than any previous IPO," said Jay Ritter, professor emeritus and director of the IPO Initiative at the University of Florida. "What's also remarkable is that Anthropic is less than six years old." The third- and fourth-largest IPOs of all time are Saudi Aramco at $29.4 billion in 2019 and SK Hynix at $26.5 billion this year.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 when a group of employees at OpenAI left to start a new company with a focus on AI safety. That startup released its first version of Claude to the public in March 2023, a few months after OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public in November 2022. When Anthropic released Claude Code in early 2025, it quickly gained traction with software developers. Revenue continued to soar after the launch of Claude Cowork in early 2026 and as Anthropic established itself as a core provider of agentic AI tools.

But, as a private company, there was less visibility into Anthropic's finances. Going public means the company will now be required to disclose more information about its revenue, spending and losses every quarter, offering a pulse check on the health of the AI boom. Anthropic is not profitable yet because it is still investing in its AI tools. It has spending commitments of roughly $900 billion in the coming years as it spends money to get compute power to train its cutting-edge AI models and support people using them.

The strong demand for Anthropic shares offers hope to the sector, even as a rocky market reception for Nvidia's results, which looked strong at first blush, signals the euphoria around the sector has cooled. "I've looked at this whole week as a referendum on AI. Nvidia gave us a read on the buildout, and Anthropic is about to give us a read on investor appetite for the next generation of AI companies," Jake Behan, head of capital markets at Direxion, said before the Anthropic debut.

Debate has intensified across global markets in recent months over whether the AI boom is veering into bubble territory, with economists, some of the world's biggest CEOs, and political leaders split over its sustainability. Much of the scrutiny has focused on circular financing arrangements. A complicated web of deals between AI companies, chipmakers, cloud providers and investors has raised questions over how much of the industry's growth is being fueled by money circulating within the same ecosystem.

"Some of the demand may simply reflect investors not wanting to miss out on the rare opportunity to gain exposure to a leading AI company, even if its long-term profitability remains uncertain," IPOX associate Lukas Muehlbauer said. "It doesn't have to be a bad company for it to be a bad investment at the IPO price," said Nic Puckrin, cross-asset analyst and founder of Coin Bureau. London-based index rebalance analytics firm Intropic estimates passive demand from index-tracking funds will account for about 28% of Anthropic's shares offered, or roughly $19 billion.

The IPO also comes as enthusiasm for AI stocks is cooling: the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite is down more than 10% from its record high six weeks ago. Anthropic's IPO could help restore confidence in the AI trade, which has been shaken in recent weeks by concerns about a slowdown to the data center buildout. But if it falls short of expectations during trading, that could add to investors' doubts. Stock market strategists point to mid-October 2022 as the start of the AI bull market, which took off after ChatGPT was released on November 30, 2022. The Nasdaq Composite has gained roughly 135% since the start of the bull market, adding $26 trillion in market value.

Investors will be watching Anthropic's debut and subsequent trading closely, with analysts expecting the stock to emerge as a proxy for the broader AI trade as questions over valuations, spending and returns weigh on sentiment. The company will also need to balance growth with calls to slow the development of advanced AI models over concerns that they pose cybersecurity threats and could be exploited by bad actors. Anthropic warned in its IPO prospectus that AI could behave unpredictably and advance faster than oversight mechanisms, posing potentially "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity."

Why it matters: Anthropic's debut is the biggest test the AI boom has faced in public markets. A $1.5 trillion valuation for a company that is not yet profitable, with nearly $900 billion in computing commitments, is either the fair price of the defining technology of the decade or the top of a bubble — and the first days of trading will not settle that argument, though they will set its tone. The deeper significance is structural: the first frontier AI lab is now a public company, which means quarterly disclosure, index inclusion and retail ownership of a business whose own prospectus warns of catastrophic risks. OpenAI is reportedly watching for a 2027 listing of its own. If ANT holds up, the floodgates open; if it stumbles, the whole AI trade feels it.

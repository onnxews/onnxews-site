Bitcoin Falls Below $60,000 and Gold Slides as Sell-Everything Rout Hits Markets

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since June and gold slid 4 percent as a synchronised sell-off hit stocks, crypto and metals at once, with record outflows from the biggest bitcoin fund.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday in a broad “sell-everything” rout that spared almost nothing — not stocks, not crypto, and not even gold, the haven investors traditionally run to when markets panic. The largest cryptocurrency was trading at $59,000 by Friday evening, its lowest level since June, after sliding to an intraday low near $58,800. It was bitcoin’s seventh consecutive weekly decline, with the price down another 9.49 percent on the week, as a technology stock sell-off spread from Wall Street to Asia and leveraged traders were forced out of their positions.

The slide began before U.S. markets opened. Bitcoin fell to nearly $61,200 in Asian trading before recovering above $61,700 as the technology sell-off spread and U.S. technology futures declined more than 1 percent. South Korea’s Kospi index slid more than 5 percent, with chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics dropping around 6 percent. By the end of the U.S. session, bitcoin had pushed well below $60,000, and the total cryptocurrency market capitalisation stood at $2.01 trillion, down 26.88 percent over one month. Ethereum fell 7.39 percent to $1,625, while Dogecoin dropped 8.23 percent and Pepe 9.70 percent.

The selling was driven in part by a record rush for the exits from the biggest bitcoin fund. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust recorded $541.1 million in daily net outflows on Thursday, its largest single-day outflow since its introduction more than two years ago, surpassing the $528.3 million set on January 30, according to Farside Investors. Bitcoin ETF outflows hit $124.5 million in 24 hours and $1.245 billion over seven days. The outflows are a sign that institutional investors, not just retail traders, are retreating from risk as the AI trade unwinds and the Federal Reserve signals further interest rate rises.

The derivatives market told the same story of forced selling. Cryptocurrency liquidations reached $718.6 million over 24 hours, jumping 72.5 percent, with longs accounting for 86.2 percent of the total — meaning leveraged bets on higher prices were forced shut as prices fell. The largest single liquidation was a $9.3 million bitcoin position on Bybit. Bitcoin open interest fell to $18 billion, down from $20 billion a week ago, while 24-hour liquidations hit $712.9 million, of which $614 million came from longs. Unlike prior flushes, leverage is not rebuilding: funding has flipped broadly negative and the three-month basis compressed from 5.5 percent to 4.5 percent, a sign of true de-leveraging, with longs capitulating and shorts and hedgers taking control.

Gold, the classic haven, fell just as hard. Gold futures dropped 4 percent to $3,540 an ounce, their lowest point since September 2025, and spot gold fell as low as $3,515 an ounce before retracing to $3,580. Gold was down 8.9 percent on the week. Silver slid to $41 an ounce, about 66 percent below its January peak of $120, and down 7.41 percent on the day. Even the traditional haven is being sold as liquidity takes priority, analysts said — when investors need cash, they sell what they can, not what they want to. Copper fell 9.94 percent on the week to another more-than-six-month low.

The rout in crypto and metals mirrored the collapse in stocks. The Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.28 percent on Friday and the S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent into correction territory, while the Cboe Volatility Index jumped 26 percent to near 36. The U.S. Dollar Index held just below 104. WTI crude fell a further 2 percent to around $81 a barrel in morning trading, its lowest level since August, before recovering later in the session. The synchronised nature of the sell-off — equities, metals and crypto all falling together — is what has alarmed market watchers most: in a normal risk-off move, gold rises while stocks fall, but on Friday almost everything fell at once.

For bitcoin, the damage is now measured in months, not days. The cryptocurrency has fallen for seven straight weeks, with the slide over the past six weeks adding up to a decline of more than 24 percent. Options flow has turned defensive as implied volatility breaks higher, with puts dominating at 61 percent of 24-hour options volume and bitcoin’s volatility index up 7 percent to 49. The key cluster to watch sits just below the current price in the $60,500 to $60,900 band, where a significant pocket of long positions would come into play on a further drop. Whether the July lows between $57,800 and $58,566 hold as support — or give way to a deeper slide — is the question traders will carry into the weekend.

Not everything in crypto fell equally. AI-linked tokens held up better than the rest of the market on Friday morning in Asia: Venice’s VVV rose 9 percent, Pearl gained 4 percent and Bittensor added 1 percent, even as Jupiter’s JUP fell 14 percent and Ethena’s ENA dropped 8.9 percent. Tokens tied to Anthropic’s private shares also repriced now that the stock trades publicly, with one tokenised version down 21 percent in 24 hours. The split suggests traders are still distinguishing between crypto tied to the AI story and crypto tied to leverage — but in a week when bitcoin lost 9.49 percent and the total market shed more than a quarter of its value in a month, that distinction offered little shelter.

Why it matters: The striking thing about Friday is not that bitcoin fell — it has fallen for seven straight weeks — but that gold fell with it. In a normal panic, money runs from stocks into gold; on Friday it ran out of everything at once, including the haven. That is the signature of a liquidity squeeze, not a simple rotation: investors are selling what they can sell to raise cash, and leveraged crypto traders are being liquidated in the process. For bitcoin, the record outflows from BlackRock’s fund matter more than the price itself — they show institutions, not just speculators, are stepping back. If the $58,000 level breaks, the next test is whether crypto’s seven-week slide becomes a full-blown capitulation.

Gibbs Piles Up 252 Total Yards as Lions Roll Patriots 35-10 in Munich

Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 153 yards and two touchdowns and caught seven passes for 99 more as the Detroit Lions beat the New England Patriots 35-10 at Allianz Arena in Munich, extending their winning streak to five games.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Detroit Lions turned their trip to Germany into a showcase on Sunday, beating the New England Patriots 35-10 at Allianz Arena in Munich behind a spectacular performance from running back Jahmyr Gibbs. The Lions improved to 7-2 and extended their winning streak to five games, while the Patriots fell to 5-5 heading into their bye week.

Gibbs was the best player on the field, rushing 22 times for 153 yards and two touchdowns and catching seven passes for 99 yards, giving him 252 yards from scrimmage on just 29 touches. He is the first NFL player since Priest Holmes in 2002 to produce more than 150 rushing yards with two rushing touchdowns and at least 95 receiving yards in a single game, and he led Detroit in both rushing and receiving.

The Lions wasted no time setting the tone. Quarterback Jared Goff, who completed 20 of 31 passes for 303 yards and three touchdowns with two interceptions, found Amon-Ra St. Brown for a 56-yard touchdown on Detroit's opening scoring drive to take a 7-3 lead. St. Brown, whose mother is from Leverkusen and who spent childhood summers in Germany, wore lederhosen to the stadium and carried a German flag onto the field before the game.

St. Brown finished with four receptions for 98 yards and two touchdowns, adding a 28-yard scoring catch in the third quarter that made it 28-3 after teammates pushed him over the goal line in a scrum with Patriots defenders. In between his two scores, Goff found Isaac TeSlaa for a 12-yard touchdown to cap a 97-yard drive, and Gibbs ran in a 5-yard touchdown one play after a 50-yard reception down the sideline.

Detroit led 21-3 at halftime and never let New England back into the game. Gibbs added a 28-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter to make it 35-10, capping a day in which the Lions' offense moved the ball almost at will against a Patriots defense that had no answer for either Gibbs or St. Brown.

The game turned decisively on its opening possession, when Patriots quarterback Drake Maye was injured. Maye led a promising drive to the Detroit 15-yard line, but Lions edge rusher Aidan Hutchinson sacked him on third-and-goal, ending the drive and Maye's day with an ankle injury. Hutchinson hurt his right knee while celebrating the sack, and a person familiar with the situation later told The Associated Press that his injury was season-ending, a major blow for Detroit.

Backup quarterback Tommy DeVito replaced Maye and could not get the Patriots' offense going, completing 12 of 24 passes for 81 yards with one interception. After DeVito entered the game, New England punted on four straight possessions, and his only touchdown pass of the day, to Lan Larison in the fourth quarter, came far too late to matter. Mack Hollins led the Patriots with five catches for 48 yards.

The Lions' defense set the tone from the start, and the offense kept the pressure on all afternoon. Goff's three touchdown passes spread the damage around, but the day belonged to Gibbs, whose blend of speed and power left Patriots defenders grasping at air in the open field. The Patriots have now lost back-to-back games and three of their last four, and they head into their bye week at 5-5 with plenty to fix on both sides of the ball. New England coach Mike Vrabel said after the game that the team had to figure out ways to give itself a better chance, and the health of Maye's ankle will dominate the bye week.

Why it matters: The win moves Detroit into a share of first place in the hyper-competitive NFC North at 7-2, tied with Minnesota and Chicago, and keeps the Lions in the No. 2 seed in the NFC playoff picture as of Sunday night. The concern now is Hutchinson: losing one of the league's best edge rushers to a season-ending knee injury would test Detroit's depth on defense just as the schedule tightens. But if Gibbs keeps producing days like this one, and Goff keeps spreading the ball to St. Brown and the rest of the receiving group, the Lions have the offensive firepower to contend with anyone in the conference.

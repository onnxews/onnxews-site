McDavid Ties Gretzky's 23-Game Season-Opening Point Streak as Oilers Beat Panthers 5–2

The Oilers captain tied Gretzky's 1988–89 run and passed him for the franchise power-play points record in a 5–2 win over the Panthers.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Connor McDavid tied Wayne Gretzky for the fourth-longest season-opening point streak in NHL history on Tuesday night, recording a goal and an assist as the Edmonton Oilers beat the Florida Panthers 5–2 at Amerant Bank Arena in Sunrise, Florida. The point streak reached 23 games, matching Gretzky's run to open the 1988–89 season.

Gretzky still holds the record at 51 games, set in 1983–84, and is tied for second with a 30-game run from 1982–83, level with Mats Sundin in 1992–93. McDavid's 23-game streak is now the fourth-longest to start a season, and it is the longest in the league in decades. His goal, a power-play rebound at 16:27 of the first period, made it 2–0 and extended the streak. He added an assist later in the game.

The night brought a second piece of history as well. With his assist, McDavid passed Gretzky for the most power-play points in Oilers franchise history, reaching 441 on 101 goals and 340 assists, against Gretzky's 439 on 125 goals and 314 assists. It was a fitting milestone for a player who has spent his career chasing the records of the man who defined the franchise before him.

McDavid now has 13 goals and 42 assists for 55 points in 23 games this season, leading the NHL in scoring. His 55 points through 23 games is a total that only Gretzky and Mario Lemieux had previously reached that quickly, each doing it three times, according to the Associated Press. The pace has put him in company that no active player has approached.

The Oilers won the game with balance as well as star power. Vasily Podkolzin scored twice and added an assist, Leon Draisaitl had a goal and two assists, including an empty-netter at 19:27 of the third period, and Kasperi Kapanen had a goal and two assists. Devon Levi made 20 saves, and his bid for a second straight shutout ended with 6:19 left in the third period.

Edmonton improved to 17–3–3 with a sixth straight win and nine wins in 11 games, and the Oilers are now 10–0–1 on the road, only the fifth team in NHL history to win 10 road games in 11 or fewer. The Oilers entered the night with the league's second-ranked power play at 34.2% and went 2-for-4 with the man advantage. Draisaitl's assist gave him a 10-game point streak for the 12th time in his career, with 20 points in that span on nine goals and 11 assists.

Florida's Matthew Tkachuk scored twice, at 13:51 and 16:50 of the third period, to extend his own point streak to nine games, but it was not enough. The Panthers fell to 12–8–3, losing two in a row and seeing a four-game point streak end. Jacob Markstrom made 13 saves for Florida.

The game was a rematch of the past two Stanley Cup Finals, both won by Florida, and it carried the weight of that rivalry. Oilers coach Mike Babcock praised his captain after the game. "Connor is an unbelievable player," Babcock said. "A spectacular talent. His drivetrain and his push for excellence is the thing that impresses me more than the talent." Tkachuk was just as direct: “McDavid’s controlling every single game he’s playing right now. He’s on a whole different level than anyone else in the League right now.”

The Oilers will try to keep the streaks going on Thursday, when they visit the Tampa Bay Lightning. McDavid needs one more game with a point to pass Gretzky's 1988–89 run and move into sole possession of third place on the season-opening list. Reuters and NHL.com both covered the game on Tuesday.

Why it matters: A 23-game point streak to open a season is the kind of number that puts a player in the record books on its own, but the context is what makes this one historic. McDavid is not just chasing Gretzky's numbers, he is passing them: the power-play points record fell on the same night, and his 55 points in 23 games is a pace only Gretzky and Lemieux have ever matched. The Oilers are 10–0–1 on the road and have won six straight, which means the streak is not a one-man show propping up a struggling team — it is the engine of the best team in the league. If he keeps scoring into December, the conversation will shift from Gretzky's 23-game run to the 30-game mark, and then to the record itself.

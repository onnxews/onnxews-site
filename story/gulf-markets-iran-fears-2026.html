GULF MARKETS SLIDE ON IRAN WAR FEARS — BUT QATAR SURGES 4.6%

Gulf stock markets fell on Sunday as investors braced for the risk of renewed conflict between the United States and Iran after the midterms — except in Qatar, where the benchmark jumped 4.6% in its sharpest intraday gain in more than eight years.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 1, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Gulf stock markets fell on Sunday as investors remained wary of the risk of renewed conflict between the United States and Iran after this week's U.S. midterm elections, Reuters reported — although Qatar's market bucked the regional trend.

Countries across the Gulf have been bracing for the possibility that President Donald Trump could resume military action against Iran after the November 3 vote. Tehran has repeatedly warned it would retaliate against any new attacks by targeting U.S. and Israeli interests in neighbouring countries.

Saudi Arabia's benchmark index slipped 0.8% to 9,838, with nearly all constituents ending lower. Saudi Arabian Mining Co lost 2.6%, while Jabal Omar Development fell 7.3%. IT solutions provider Elm Co gained 9% after reporting a 39% rise in quarterly net profit.

Qatar's benchmark index rebounded from the previous session's decline to rise 4.6% to 9,575, marking its sharpest intraday percentage gain in more than eight years. Qatar National Bank advanced 3%, while QatarEnergy-linked Industries Qatar jumped 7.9%.

Investor sentiment in Qatar was supported by QatarEnergy's efforts to secure long-term liquefied natural gas supplies from the United States and other producers, as part of a trading strategy designed to protect revenues should exports through the Strait of Hormuz be disrupted.

Elsewhere, Kuwait lost 0.8% to 8,826, Bahrain fell 0.6% to 1,819, Oman dropped 0.7% to 7,293, and Egypt's blue-chip index fell 1.9% to 48,650 after Egypt's central bank raised its key interest rates by 100 basis points on Thursday, its first rate increase since March 2024.

Reporting: Reuters

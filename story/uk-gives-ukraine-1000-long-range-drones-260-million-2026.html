Britain to Give Ukraine More Than 1,000 Long-Range Strike Drones in £260 Million Package

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced the package in Berlin, where allies also discussed joining peace talks, as Russia steps up its winter campaign against Ukraine's power grid.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Britain will give Ukraine more than 1,000 long-range strike drones as part of a new £260 million military assistance package, Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced on Thursday in Berlin, where he was co-chairing a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, the group of Ukraine's allies. The package, worth about $342 million, is the latest British effort to strengthen Kyiv's ability to strike back as Russia pounds its energy infrastructure ahead of winter.

Some of the drones can fly more than 1,000 kilometers, roughly the distance from London to Berlin, and carry heavy warheads capable of flattening targets in a single hit, the government said. That range would let Ukraine target military production facilities and other legitimate military targets far behind the front lines, deep inside Russia. The package will also include maritime, logistical and surveillance drones, which the government said will be used to resupply frontline forces, ensuring stocks such as blood and fridges for medical stores reach troops on the front line.

The announcement was deliberately framed as a jobs story as well as a military one. More than 80 percent of the funding will be spent in the United Kingdom, including with small and medium-sized businesses in Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland, the government said. “Today's announcement of further support won't just boost Ukraine's front lines; it will also bring jobs and opportunity around the country,” Burnham said, adding, “That backing is not just supporting them on the battlefield but also bringing jobs and opportunity here at home.”

The drone package follows a £240 million air defense package announced on Tuesday at the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, which included more than 1,000 short-range air defense missiles and more than a dozen radar systems. Last week the government also imposed a sweeping set of sanctions, making Britain the first country to sanction all ten of Russia's major oil producers. Burnham said the new support was a pledge made at the Budget, now being delivered.

The timing reflects the pressure Ukraine is under. The government said Ukraine has faced more than 2,000 attacks on its energy infrastructure, degrading its ability to move power from generation sites to the center and east of the country. Daily outages have reached 16 hours in multiple regions and up to 22 hours in Kyiv, with emergency cuts causing multi-day blackouts that leave people without water supplies to their homes, the ability to keep fridges running or use ovens. “Plunging millions of Ukrainians into the freezing cold and starving them of heat and warmth won't see Putin win this war, it will only cement our resolve to support Ukraine,” Burnham said.

Defense Secretary Wes Streeting said the package turns a political commitment into hardware. “President Putin uses winter as a weapon in Ukraine. We are converting our steadfast commitment for Ukraine into capability that will help defend against Russia's barbaric attacks on civilians,” he said. “We promised more support for Ukraine at the Budget, and we are delivering on that pledge, providing over 1,000 long-range strike drones as part of our ongoing commitment to mobilise allies and strengthen Ukraine's defences.”

The Berlin meeting also produced other commitments. Britain said it would fund two British-built fast attack boats for Ukraine alongside Norway, and that it has begun delivering additional Lightweight Multirole Missiles announced for Ukraine, which have proven effective against the jet-powered drones Russia is using to strike far beyond the front lines. Twelve British defense firms have been awarded access to Ukraine's Avengers AI Labs, a program that gives them battlefield data collected by thousands of cameras and sensors to train AI models, after 92 companies applied. Ukraine is now shooting down close to 70 percent of jet drones, compared with 30 percent in early September, the government said.

The package drew criticism from Washington, where the Trump administration has questioned the value of Ukraine's deep strikes inside Russia. Under Secretary of Defense for Policy Elbridge Colby said long-range strikes “have not been effective in driving an end to the war, nor do they contribute to blunting Russia's advances on the front line.” German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius later dismissed the criticism as “stupid.” The disagreement has become one of the sharpest public splits among Ukraine's allies over how the war should be fought.

The government did not name the drone models in the package or give a delivery timetable, and no supplier has been named. What is clear is the direction of travel: Britain is doubling down on giving Ukraine the means to hit targets far behind Russian lines, even as the United States questions whether those strikes help end the war, and even as Russia answers with some of the largest air attacks of the conflict. Burnham told reporters in Berlin that at the Coalition of the Willing, Ukraine's friends and partners would make clear that “our steadfast support for Ukraine will not waver and we are united as ever in securing a just and lasting peace.”

Why it matters: This package matters because it is Britain's answer to Russia's winter campaign against Ukraine's power grid: not just more air defense, but the ability to hit the factories and launch sites behind the attacks. It also sharpens the split with Washington, which says deep strikes do not work, while London and Berlin say they save lives by degrading Russia's war machine. With no delivery timetable named, the test now is whether the drones arrive fast enough to matter this winter, and whether the allies can hold a common line on striking inside Russia.

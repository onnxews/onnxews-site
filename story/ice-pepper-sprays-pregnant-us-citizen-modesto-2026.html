ICE Agents Pepper-Sprayed a Pregnant U.S. Citizen in Modesto. She Was Released Without Charges.

Lizbeth Vargas Andrade, 23 and 12 weeks pregnant, says she was thrown to the ground, pepper-sprayed and held for about five hours outside the Stanislaus County jail. ICE says she interfered with an arrest.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Federal immigration agents pepper-sprayed and detained a pregnant U.S. citizen outside the Stanislaus County jail in Modesto, California, on Sunday, Nov. 15, according to her account, her lawyer and video of the encounter. She was held for about five hours and released without charges.

Lizbeth Vargas Andrade, 23, of Ceres, is 12 weeks pregnant and a mother of two. She was acting as a legal observer monitoring immigration enforcement when the altercation happened in the parking lot of the Stanislaus County jail, and she was live-streaming on TikTok at the time.

The encounter outside the Stanislaus County jail began about noon on Sunday. Vargas Andrade said she was parked in the lot, watching ICE vehicles as part of a volunteer network that alerts residents to agents' movements. Her sister-in-law, Monica Alvarado, offered a ride to a man walking out of the jail, and as Alvarado drove off, about five ICE agents pulled her over. Vargas Andrade said the agents then forced themselves into Alvarado's vehicle to pull the man out and take him into custody, and no warrant or deportation order was shown.

Vargas Andrade and her husband, Adolfo Talavera Alvarado, approached Alvarado's vehicle and began to interact with the immigration agents. During the altercation, one of the agents alleged that Vargas Andrade slapped him, and then agents turned their attention to her. She denies assaulting the officer, and was live-streaming on TikTok at the time. On video, agents can be seen taking her to the ground, where she landed on her stomach as bystanders shouted that she was pregnant. An agent deployed pepper-spray at her and her husband. "We kind of bumped into each other, and then he was like, 'She hit me,'" Andrade said of an ICE agent at a news conference. "They threw me to the ground, they Maced me, and then they handcuffed me. When they threw me to the ground, that's when I hit my stomach area pretty hard."

Vargas Andrade was taken to the ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations office in Stockton. Despite repeatedly telling officials she was pregnant, she said she was held without medical care for more than two hours, and she was not allowed to speak to her family or her legal counsel on the scene. Her younger sister, Valeria Vargas, called an ambulance for her, and paramedics found she had high blood sugar and blood pressure initially, but she has since been cleared by doctors. Vargas Andrade spent about five hours in ICE custody before being released without charges.

ICE gave a different account of the encounter, saying Andrade interfered with agents' arrest of Fabian Santiago Martinez, whom the agency described as a Mexican national with multiple DUI charges. "Our ICE officer relied on his training to safely control the situation despite the combative, aggressive, and heinously vulgar conduct of the woman interfering with his lawful duties," a spokesperson said. "The individual immediately received medical care and was transported to the San Joaquin General Hospital for medical evaluation." ICE said its officers are trained to use the minimum force necessary and receive ongoing training in de-escalation tactics.

The two accounts directly conflict on the question of medical care. Andrade and her lawyer say she was denied care for hours until her family called paramedics, while ICE says she received medical care immediately. In a written statement Monday, her attorney, Patrick Kolasinski, said multiple videos of the incident contradict the agents' claim that Vargas Andrade assaulted them. "ICE is not supposed to be detaining U.S. citizens," said Kolasinski, who said her family is exploring the possibility of a civil suit.

The medical stakes were high. Vargas Andrade's younger sister told reporters that her sister is considered a high-risk pregnancy with her Type 2 diabetes, and that their family was extremely concerned because she had experienced two previous miscarriages. Andrade said she was determined to keep supporting her community. "We're not going to stop helping the community, even though this happened," she said. "We're not going to stop helping the community because we want our immigrant neighbors to feel safe here, even if there's all this going on."

Stanislaus County officials have scheduled a community town hall to discuss immigration enforcement at 6:30 p.m. Wednesday at the Salvation Army Community Center in south Modesto. Speakers were to include prominent area immigration attorneys, Sheriff Jeff Dirkse and staff from U.S. Rep. Adam Gray's office. The Modesto Police Department released a statement on Monday afternoon, reiterating it upholds the state law, and does not use personnel or resources to conduct or assist ICE. County supervisors and council members have pressed the sheriff for clarity on how deputies interact with federal agents.

The incident is not the first violent encounter between ICE and Stanislaus County residents to draw national attention. In April, ICE officers shot Carlos Ivan Mendoza Hernandez during a traffic stop near Interstate 5 in Patterson. ICE agents said he tried to run over an officer and was wanted for questioning in a homicide in El Salvador. Mendoza Hernandez, who is also represented by Kolasinski, denied both claims, and Salvadoran records show he was acquitted in that case in 2019. He remains in ICE custody. In January 2025, Sheriff Dirkse released a video discussing a California law that prevents state and local law enforcement resources from being used for federal immigration enforcement, including information, personnel and facilities, except in cases of those accused of serious or violent felonies.

As of Thursday, no charges have been filed against Vargas Andrade, and her lawyer says the family is exploring a civil suit over the detention, the force used and her treatment in custody. She does not know what happened to the other man who was detained by ICE on Sunday. "He thanked me for doing what we're doing," she said. "We're still worried about him as a community, because he didn't make it back to his family, so hopefully, if he was let out, hopefully he's home now."

Why it matters: This case matters because it sits at the intersection of two things the public is watching closely: how federal immigration agents use force, and whether U.S. citizens are being swept up in enforcement aimed at someone else. The video, the disputed assault claim and the conflicting accounts of medical care are exactly the kind of facts that decide whether an incident becomes a lawsuit or fades away, and the family's exploration of a civil suit means the Modesto encounter is likely to be tested in court rather than settled on social media. It also raises a practical question for California counties: when federal agents operate in a sheriff's parking lot, residents want to know where local cooperation ends, which is why the county scheduled a town hall and why supervisors and council members are demanding answers from the sheriff.

Chris Christie Weighs a Third White House Run and Declares 'the Trump Era Is Over'

The former New Jersey governor says he is thinking about a third presidential bid, dismisses JD Vance and Ron DeSantis, and warns Democrats the results were no mandate for socialism.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Former New Jersey Governor Chris Christie is considering a third run for the White House in 2028, telling his party to go in "a completely different direction" after the Republicans' sweeping midterm losses and declaring that the Trump era is over. The comments, made in interviews after Tuesday's elections, mark the most direct challenge yet to the leadership that has defined the party for a decade.

"The Trump era is over after last night, and the people who are going to lead the party going forward are the ones who will be able to articulate a message that's forward-looking and different than just sitting around and talking about Donald Trump," Christie told the New York Post. He said the midterm results were a clear rebuke of how Republicans governed under the president, rather than a vote of confidence in Democrats.

Christie, 64, also dismissed the two men most often mentioned as the party's next standard-bearers. He said Vice President JD Vance was not a viable candidate for president because, in his words, "in two years he will reek from the stink of Donald Trump and what has happened in his administration, and I don't think he can win the presidency." He also dismissed Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, saying the governor had no standing to challenge him after the midterm results.

The former governor, who unsuccessfully sought the Republican presidential nomination in 2016 and again in 2024, said he sees himself as better positioned to run than other prominent Republicans. He told Politico that he is going to think about making a third run at the party's nomination, with a decision potentially coming within months. "All good minds in our party should be thinking about what our new direction should be. I said I was open to considering running again, and I am," he said.

Christie leaned on history to make the case for a third attempt. "If I run this time, it'll be my third time running. Reagan got elected on his third time running, so maybe there's a bit of a charm to that," he said, referring to former President Ronald Reagan. He cautioned, however, that he had no timeline for a decision and that any campaign would need a clear rationale and a viable path to victory. His wife, he said, wants to see the path before he commits.

At the same time, Christie warned Democrats against overreading the results. The midterm outcome, he said, does not mean voters want socialism, and the Democratic Party's own standing remains weak. Democrats' approval rating is underwater by 15.6 percentage points, while the Republican Party's approval rating is down by 19 points, according to the RealClearPolitics aggregate of polls.

Christie's intervention comes as the Republican Party begins its first serious debate about its future since Trump took control of it. The party lost both chambers of Congress on Tuesday, and the president has responded by reshuffling his White House staff and blaming messaging, the war with Iran and his own immigration crackdown for the defeat. Potential contenders for the 2028 nomination, including Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, have begun drawing attention, but none has declared.

Christie has long pushed for the party to move past Trump, and he was one of the few voices in the Republican wilderness over the past two years warning that the president posed a danger to the party's future. His calculus is straightforward: if the party is left in shambles, Republicans will finally break away from Trump and the Make America Great Again brand, and look for a more centrist, establishment figure to guide it back to power.

Whether that opening exists is far from certain. Christie has lost twice before, and his brand of confrontational centrism has a limited base inside a party that Trump still dominates. But his willingness to say aloud what many Republicans are saying privately, that the Trump era is finished, guarantees him a place in the argument over what comes next. The question now is whether that argument produces a campaign, or only a commentary.

Trump's Board of Peace Warns Gaza Ceasefire Is Near Collapse as Israel and Hamas Miss Commitments

A 12-page report to the U.N. Security Council faults both sides, saying a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained and warning there would be no road map back if it fails.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Board of Peace, the body established by US President Donald Trump to oversee the Gaza ceasefire, has warned the United Nations Security Council that the truce is in danger of collapsing, faulting both Israel and Hamas for failing to carry out the commitments they made when the ceasefire took effect. The warning came in a 12-page report submitted to the Council on Tuesday, the board's second since the ceasefire began 13 months ago.

The report says the distance between what was agreed and what is happening on the ground is the principal risk to the process, and that a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely. "Should it collapse, there would be no road map to return to, no administration to deploy and no enabling environment to rebuild," the report says, in one of its starkest passages.

The board faults both sides. Hamas agreed in July to a roadmap to surrender its weapons and transfer governance to a civilian transitional authority, but Israel rejected the roadmap and said it would not withdraw until Hamas was dismantled and Gaza was demilitarized. The report says that every day Israel continues strikes against Hamas targets, it delays putting Hamas' agreement to the roadmap to the test.

The report also says that neither party may treat the other's delay as license for its own inaction, and that together these obligations are the only route to Israeli withdrawal and reconstruction at scale. The board's findings are to be presented to the Security Council at a meeting in New York at the end of November.

The ceasefire took effect on Oct. 10, 2025, after two years of war that began when Hamas attacked Israel in October 2023, killing about 1,200 people and taking more than 250 hostage, according to Israeli tallies. Israel's assault on Gaza has since killed 74,000 Palestinians, according to Gaza health authorities, and more than 1,600 Palestinians and five Israeli soldiers have been killed in Gaza since the ceasefire began, according to Reuters.

More than 2 million Palestinians are confined to around a third of Gaza's territory, mostly in tents and damaged buildings, under Hamas control, while Israel occupies two-thirds of the territory, largely razed and depopulated. The report says the population is facing another winter in tents and rubble, with heavy rain inundating displacement camps in recent weeks and more rain expected later in November.

The report criticizes Israel's absence of decisions on training and deploying a civilian Palestinian police force, and says Israeli restrictions on supplies and equipment, combined with funding shortfalls, have hindered flood preparedness in the territory. It also says Hamas is collecting taxes on goods entering Gaza, affecting the availability of basic commodities for the population.

The report calls for an immediate scaled-up entry of shelter and winterization materials into Gaza, and for approvals for pumps, generators and heavy machinery to help the territory cope with the winter. It also says the international stabilization force whose deployment is seen as key to Israeli withdrawal, Hamas disarmament and the start of reconstruction has been delayed, and that a technocratic civil administration created by the board has still not been allowed by Israel to take up its job.

The board was established and is headed by Trump, and its first report was delivered in May by its director-general, Nickolay Mladenov, a former UN Middle East envoy. The latest report says the end result of the process must lead to a credible pathway to Palestinian self-determination and statehood, a position that goes further than the ceasefire agreement itself, which did not address the long-term political future of Gaza.

Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Wednesday that Israel would not withdraw until Hamas was dismantled and Gaza was demilitarized, a position that matches the government's longstanding stance. Netanyahu's office and a spokesperson for Hamas' political office did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the report.

Why it matters: This report matters because it is the first time the body Trump created to oversee the ceasefire has said plainly that the truce is failing, and because it faults both sides rather than taking one side's case. The warning that there would be no road map back if the ceasefire collapses is a direct challenge to the governments in Jerusalem and Gaza, both of which have treated the other's delays as a reason for their own inaction. With winter setting in and more than 2 million people living in tents and rubble, the report's message is that time is running out: without a decision on the police force, the stabilization force and the flow of supplies, the ceasefire will not survive the season.

Larry Poindexter, Days of Our Lives and S.W.A.T. Actor, Dies at 66 Days After Being Hit by a Car

The veteran character actor, whose career spanned more than four decades and 170 film and television credits, died suddenly in a Los Angeles hospital while awaiting surgery for broken ribs.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Larry Poindexter, the veteran character actor best known for roles in Days of Our Lives, S.W.A.T. and JAG, has died at the age of 66. His manager confirmed his death to USA TODAY on November 19, and Deadline was first to report the news. Poindexter died on Wednesday, November 18, just days after being struck by a car near his Los Angeles home.

Poindexter was crossing the street near his home in Studio City on Friday, November 13, to pick up food when he was hit by a vehicle. The collision happened around 6 p.m. near the corner of Moorpark Street and Lankershim Boulevard in North Hollywood, according to the Los Angeles Police Department, which responded to a radio call of a vehicle versus pedestrian collision at the intersection. He was taken to a local hospital, where he was treated for broken ribs.

His injuries were not believed to be life-threatening. His agent, Harry Gold, told NBC News that doctors did not diagnose anything serious, put him in the hospital for observation and to repair some broken bones, and then he passed away. Poindexter was scheduled to have surgery on Wednesday to repair the broken ribs, but he died suddenly that morning before the operation. No official cause of death has been released, and the police investigation is ongoing.

Poindexter's career spanned more than four decades, with credits in more than 170 movies and television shows, according to his IMDb page. He was likely best known for playing LAPD Captain Tom Fuller in the 2003 film S.W.A.T., which also starred Samuel L. Jackson, Colin Farrell and Michelle Rodriguez, and he made a brief appearance in the CBS television show of the same name in 2019.

To soap opera fans, he was a familiar face across decades of daytime television. He first played Ben Welch on Days of Our Lives in 1984, and he returned to the long-running show as Father Louis, playing the role from 2014 with appearances through 2018. He played psychiatrist Dr. Asher Thomas on General Hospital in 2005, with his character killed off in a dramatic confrontation, and he also had stints on Santa Barbara. His 30-episode run on Days of Our Lives, playing two separate characters more than 30 years apart, was a rare feat in daytime television.

His television work stretched far beyond the soaps. Fans of CBS' JAG will recognize him as Dalton Lowne, the lawyer and boyfriend of Sarah Mac MacKenzie who was shot and killed by a stalker in Season 3. He was also a series regular on MTV's The Hard Times of RJ Berger, playing the title character's father Richard Berger. His other credits included Quantum Leap, NYPD Blue, How I Met Your Mother, Friends, NCIS, Will and Grace, Scandal, CSI and its Miami and New York spinoffs, Party of Five, Nip/Tuck, Supernatural, Bones, Station 19, The Rookie and 9-1-1.

Poindexter was born in Dallas in 1959 to Tony Award-winning lighting and set designer H.R. Poindexter and opera singer Sue Ann Erdman. He grew up performing in productions by Dallas Summer Musicals, now known as Broadway Dallas, and found what his website called his true passion on the stage before moving to Los Angeles, where he built a career that touched almost every corner of film and television.

Tributes from colleagues and fans have focused on the breadth of that career and on the suddenness of the loss. A working actor for more than 40 years, Poindexter was the kind of performer audiences recognized instantly even when they could not name him, moving between soaps, procedurals, comedies and films with the ease of someone who had done it all. His death at 66, days after an accident that was not expected to be fatal, has left many in the industry stunned.

He is survived by his wife, casting director Carol Kritzer, and their twin sons, Rees and Jake. Poindexter's manager confirmed the death to USA TODAY, and his agent shared the details of the accident and the hospital stay with NBC News, according to USA TODAY's report.

Why it matters: Poindexter's death is a reminder of how much of television rests on working character actors whose names audiences may not know but whose faces they recognize instantly. Across more than 170 credits and four decades, he moved from Dallas stage productions to daytime soaps, prime-time procedurals and a blockbuster film, playing cops, lawyers, doctors and fathers with the reliability that keeps a show running. The circumstances make the loss harder to absorb: he was struck crossing the street to pick up food, hospitalized with broken ribs that were not thought to be life-threatening, and died suddenly while awaiting routine surgery. The story is not only an obituary for a prolific actor, but a stark illustration of how quickly an ordinary accident can turn fatal.

HEGSETH FIRES PENTAGON'S TOP WAR PLANNER AS IRAN STRIKES LOOM

The Pentagon's chief planning officer was abruptly ousted over alleged classified leaks — just as U.S. forces in the Middle East stand ready to launch major new operations against Iran.

ONOrbit News NetworkOct 30, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Update (Nov. 17, 2026): This article has been expanded and merged with our Oct. 31 coverage of the same firing. It now includes the Pentagon’s account of the allegations, the Central Command clarification, and the Signal-scandal context, with Military.com added as a second source. The Oct. 31 article remains live with a pointer to this fuller version.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has fired Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Pentagon’s top planning officer, as President Donald Trump weighs new strikes against Iran, according to a defense official and another source familiar with the matter cited by CNN.

Sylvia served as director for strategy, plans and policy — known as the J5 — for the Joint Chiefs of Staff. He was abruptly dismissed for alleged “misconduct,” with officials claiming he is under investigation for allegedly leaking classified information.

Chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell appeared to confirm the firing in a post on X on Thursday morning, writing that after an extensive Air Force Office of Special Investigations inquiry, the department took “necessary personnel action to protect sensitive information.” The post did not name Sylvia, and no details or evidence of wrongdoing were provided publicly. Parnell added that the Department of War has “zero tolerance for the intentional, unauthorized disclosure of classified information,” warning that anyone who compromises sensitive national security information “puts the lives of our Warriors at risk.”

The Air Force Office of Special Investigations confirmed to CNN that it conducted an investigation into allegations of unauthorized disclosure by Sylvia, and a spokesman for Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine confirmed Sylvia’s ouster. The Justice Department said it has ongoing investigations related to possible leaks of classified information, but did not confirm details specific to Sylvia.

What the Pentagon alleges is that Sylvia disclosed classified information to journalists about the Iran war, Russia’s war in Ukraine and other subjects, and the matter has been referred to the Justice Department for possible legal action. A Defense Department spokesperson described the firing as due to “severe misconduct.” But accounts of the evidence against Sylvia differ, and an internal investigative report alleges Sylvia admitted disclosures on video, according to Axios reporting cited by Military.com. The Pentagon has not publicly identified the specific information disclosed, or explained how it compromised national security.

The Pentagon also pushed back on the idea that Sylvia was steering the Iran planning. A Defense Department spokesperson told Military.com that Sylvia “was not a central planner for the Iran war” — that role belongs to Adm. Brad Cooper, who leads U.S. Central Command. Sylvia, who served for 32 years and previously commanded the Army’s 101st Airborne Division, was nominated for the J5 post by former Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin near the end of the Biden administration. His predecessor, Lt. Gen. Joe McGee, also left the position in October 2025 under pressure from Hegseth, according to CNN.

The firing renews scrutiny of Hegseth’s own handling of sensitive military plans. In March 2025, Hegseth shared details of impending strikes against Houthi targets in Yemen — including aircraft movements and strike times about two to four hours before execution — through Signal on his personal cellphone. The chat included Jeffrey Goldberg, editor-in-chief of The Atlantic, whom then-national security adviser Mike Waltz had inadvertently added. The Pentagon’s inspector general later concluded Hegseth violated department policy by using his personal device and an unapproved messaging application to transmit sensitive, nonpublic operational information, warning that adversaries could have used it to counter U.S. forces. Hegseth has maintained he shared an unclassified summary and had authority to determine what required classification, and has called the findings “total exoneration.”

The allegations against Sylvia remain accusations, not charges or convictions. Federal law provides grounds for prosecuting certain unauthorized disclosures: 18 U.S.C. § 793(d) covers willful transmission of national defense information to someone not entitled to receive it, carrying up to 10 years in prison — but whether a particular disclosure satisfies those elements depends on the information, the recipient and the conduct. Senior rank is no exemption: retired Marine Gen. James Cartwright, a former Joint Chiefs vice chair, pleaded guilty in 2016 to lying to FBI investigators about unauthorized classified disclosures to two reporters. When asked about Sylvia’s firing, President Trump misidentified the general as a woman, saying, “She’s going to have big problems, long-term problems.”

The timing is striking. Two U.S. officials told CNN earlier in the week that U.S. forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch new, potentially major military operations against Iran within 96 hours of receiving an order from Trump — and Sylvia’s departure removes an experienced planner just as that decision looms, with no named replacement for the Joint Staff’s top strategist. Whether his dismissal is connected to any specific planned operation is unconfirmed. Sylvia is the latest in a long line of senior officers to leave the Pentagon under Hegseth, and his biography had been removed from the Joint Chiefs’ website by Wednesday evening. His removal also comes amid bipartisan congressional criticism of Hegseth’s senior leadership changes, including Republican lawmakers questioning earlier dismissals such as the removal of Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George.

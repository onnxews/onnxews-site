Cousins Is Back: Vikings' Reunion Sets Up Lambeau Return Against Packers

Kirk Cousins was reintroduced in Minnesota on Wednesday, a day after the Vikings traded a 2027 third-round pick to the Raiders for their old quarterback, and he could start Sunday at the same stadium where his first stint ended with a torn Achilles.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Kirk Cousins is starting over in a familiar place. The Minnesota Vikings reunited with their old quarterback ahead of the NFL trade deadline, solving their quarterback crisis by making a deal with the Las Vegas Raiders to acquire the 38-year-old, and he was reintroduced to the media in Minnesota on Nov. 11, one day after his breakfast was interrupted by the trade call from Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak. The New York Post covered the trade and its ironic Lambeau twist

"Grateful to be back," Cousins said at the start of his press conference, thanking the Raiders for the opportunity they gave him this past offseason and the chance they gave him to play. He spoke about finding a new place to live, telling reporters the family sold their old house without even having to list it, and his wife and children joined him on the journey back to the Twin Cities, leaving their new life in Las Vegas behind to restart their old one in Minnesota.

The trade itself was straightforward. The Vikings sent a 2027 third-round pick to the Raiders for Cousins, who went 4-5 as the starter with the Raiders in 2026. He has completed 64.1 percent of his passes for 2,340 yards, with 20 touchdown passes and eight interceptions, and his 97.1 passer rating ranks 11th-best in the league entering Week 10, while his 20 touchdown passes are second behind only Joe Burrow's 23. Las Vegas, meanwhile, turns to No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza, the quarterback Cousins was brought in to bridge towards.

Cousins last played for the Vikings in 2023, and his first stint in Minnesota ended in the cruellest way. On Oct. 29, 2023, he tore his Achilles in a Vikings victory over the Packers at Lambeau Field, and he never suited up for Minnesota again. In his first go-around with the Vikings, from 2018 to 2023, he piled up a 50-37-1 record while throwing for 23,265 yards, 171 touchdowns and 55 interceptions. He then played two seasons for the Atlanta Falcons, eventually losing his job to first-round pick Michael Penix Jr., before signing with the Raiders this past offseason.

If he starts on Sunday, it will be in the same building where that Achilles injury ended his first spell, and Cousins did not pretend the symmetry was lost on him. "Knowing that was where I was carted off," he said. "I had thoughts of, 'Is this my last game? Is this my last game as a Viking?' To be back out there, wearing the purple-and-gold. It has meaning, but you have to go make it mean something with the way you play."

Head coach Kevin O'Connell has not yet committed to Cousins as the starter, saying: "It's still too early to commit whole-heartedly to what the plan on Sunday looks like, but I will say (I'm) excited about the opportunity of spending every moment we can between now and then to get him comfortable and allow him to play for us this Sunday." His alternatives are both hurt: Kyler Murray remains week-to-week with a hamstring injury and only started his running progression on Nov. 9, while backup Carson Wentz was listed as a limited participant on the Nov. 11 injury report.

The Vikings' need for a quarterback was urgent after injuries hit the position. General manager Nolan Teasley said everything aligned for the move, pointing to Cousins' familiarity with the offence, the organisation and the coaching staff, and his production this season. "We acquired Kirk to join a 6-2 football team to play," O'Connell said, adding: "He's joining a 6-2 football team with nine games to go."

Cousins also reunites with Justin Jefferson, hoping to recapture the connection the two had from 2020 to 2023. "For me, with Justin, it's just play to play," Cousins said. "Play in and play out, you're always just going where your reads take you, and Kevin (O'Connell) will always do a great job of trying to give him opportunities." The scheme is the same as when Cousins last played for Minnesota, and many of the same players remain, which should shorten the adjustment period with little time to prepare before Sunday.

As for what drives him at 38, Cousins was clear that the goal has not changed. "I think my whole career has felt like unfinished business," he said. "I don't know that I would say today is any different than so many other years or moments where you feel like you've got something to prove, you've got something to accomplish and there's more to get." He added: "It's all about winning a Super Bowl. It always has been in every organization when the year begins." The Vikings finish with five of their last seven games at home, and O'Connell said the aim is to build progressively so the team is playing its best football in December.

Why it matters: This trade is a bet that a 6-2 team with a strong defence and a proven quarterback can still make a deep run, and the cost, a 2027 third-round pick, is modest for a starter with 20 touchdown passes this season. The Lambeau setting adds a layer of theatre that no scriptwriter would dare invent: the same field, the same opponent, three years after the Achilles tear that ended his first Vikings career. If Cousins starts and wins on Sunday, the reunion becomes a defining story of the season; if he is not ready, the Vikings still have a quarterback problem, just a more expensive one.

Tencent Releases the Full Weights of Hy4, Its 770-Billion-Parameter Flagship AI Model

Anyone can now download and run Tencent's best AI model, a 770-billion-parameter system with a million-token context window, but the licence carries a catch for large commercial operators.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Tencent released the weights of its flagship Hy4 artificial intelligence model on Friday, November 20, allowing developers to download and deploy the model themselves. The release puts the instruct model, a smaller FP8 build and the pre-trained base on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode, a fortnight after the model first reached Tencent's own API.

Hy4 is a mixture-of-experts model with 770 billion total parameters, of which 49 billion are activated for each token. It supports text, image and video understanding, with a context window of about one million tokens, or 1,048,576 tokens to be exact. The model has 78 layers with 256 routed experts per layer, of which eight fire per token, plus a native vision encoder and a built-in speculative-decoding system.

The release follows Hy4's initial launch on November 4 and the separate opening of the smaller Hy4 Flash model, which arrived on November 17. Tencent says the full model improves on the earlier Hy4 preview in instruction following, long-context understanding, extended tasks and research, and it supports workflows spanning graphical interfaces and command-line tools, alongside frontend development, game development and office tasks.

The licence is the detail that matters most for anyone planning to use the model commercially. The weights do not ship under the plain Apache 2.0 licence used for Tencent's earlier Hy models. Instead they use the Tencent Hy License, whose section 10 requires a separate written licence from model-as-a-service operators once their worldwide gross revenue passes $50 million in any twelve months or their product passes 100 million monthly active users. Purely internal use is exempt, and below those thresholds the weights are free to download and run.

The sheer size of the download is a barrier in itself. The full Hy4 checkpoint is about 1.55 terabytes, the FP8 build about 0.79 terabytes and the base model about 1.54 terabytes, plus a system card with safety and alignment evaluations. Tencent publishes serving recipes for the vLLM and SGLang frameworks with tensor parallelism across eight GPUs, and ships a prebuilt vLLM image for the FP8 build. In practice, an eight-GPU node of high-end cards is the realistic serving unit, and at full precision the job becomes a multi-node one.

Tencent's own benchmarks, published on the model card, put the base model at 91.6 on MMLU and 73.9 on MMLU-Pro, ahead of the DeepSeek and in-house baselines it lists. Those numbers come from the maker's own evaluations and have not been independently reproduced, and the card is candid about the gap that remains to the top proprietary models on research-level mathematics and long-horizon coding. The benchmarks should be read as Tencent's measurements, not as independent results.

The release is the latest move in a fast-moving open-weights race. Chinese labs have been publishing ever-larger models with downloadable weights, while American frontier labs have mostly kept their best systems behind APIs. A 770-billion-parameter model with vision and computer-use abilities, available to download, narrows the gap between what anyone can run on their own servers and what only the biggest labs can offer.

For developers, the practical question is whether the model is worth its weight. The million-token context window and the built-in computer-use features make Hy4 attractive for long documents, research tasks and agent-style workflows, but the hardware bill is steep and the licence means that any business that succeeds in selling Hy4 as a service at scale will have to negotiate with Tencent first. Small teams and researchers get a frontier-class model for free; large commercial operators get a negotiation.

What happens next is a test of the open model in the wild. Independent evaluations will show whether Hy4's benchmark claims hold up outside Tencent's own testing, and the first deployments will reveal how many organizations are willing to dedicate an eight-GPU node to running it. The release also sets up a comparison with the next round of open models from rival labs, which will now have to match a 770-billion-parameter flagship that anyone can download.

Why it matters: Hy4 matters because it is the largest open-weights model Tencent has ever released, and its licence shows where the open-model movement is heading: free to download and run, but not free to resell at scale. The $50 million revenue trigger and 100-million-user threshold are Tencent's way of keeping control of the commercial upside while still claiming openness. For the wider AI race, the release puts pressure on Western labs that keep their best models closed, and it gives every developer with enough hardware a frontier-class system to build on.

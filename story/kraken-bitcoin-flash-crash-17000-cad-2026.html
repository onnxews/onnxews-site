Bitcoin Briefly Traded at $17,000 on Kraken's Canadian Market — and the Exchange Says the Trades Stand

Bitcoin briefly traded at the equivalent of about $17,000 on Kraken's Canadian-dollar market — 71 percent below its price elsewhere — after heavy selling overwhelmed a thin weekend order book. The exchange says every trade stands.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

For a few hours over the weekend, Bitcoin traded at about $17,000 on one of the world's largest cryptocurrency exchanges — while everywhere else it was worth more than three times as much. Crypto exchange Kraken said on Sunday that it would honor every trade executed during the dislocation, telling customers that the trades stand.

The episode began on Friday, Nov. 20, when intense selling pressure hit Kraken's Canadian-dollar trading pairs starting around 20:00 UTC. Prices on those pairs slid far below where the same assets were trading on other markets. By 08:00 UTC on Saturday, Bitcoin on Kraken's BTC/CAD book had reached a low of C$24,250. At the Bank of Canada's reference rate of C$1.4325 per U.S. dollar on Friday, that put Bitcoin's price at about $16,900 — roughly 71 percent below the $57,650 it was fetching on Kraken's own BTC/USD market during the same five-minute interval.

The gap did not close quickly. The dislocation lasted about 14 hours before BTC/CAD snapped back, climbing above C$80,000 by early afternoon on Saturday. Bitcoin was not the only asset caught in the downdraft: similar price gaps showed up across other Canadian-dollar markets on Kraken, including Ether, Solana and stablecoins. Kraken's BTC/USD pair, meanwhile, held steady, trading between $55,000 and $58,000 through the episode.

Kraken's position is that nothing broke. The exchange said every order was processed correctly and that its security teams found no evidence of a breach or compromised accounts. 'Executed trades stand,' Kraken said in its statement. That decision means bargain hunters keep their haul and sellers keep their regret: at the lows, roughly 20.8 Bitcoin changed hands in two sales placed about 15 minutes apart, with those buyers saving about $832,000 compared with prevailing prices, according to research compiled on the episode.

What caused the crash is not settled. Blockstream chief executive Adam Back suggested that a market maker on Kraken's USD/CAD spot market may have exhausted its funds, causing prices to diverge from broader foreign exchange rates, with arbitrage then spreading the distortion to other Canadian-dollar trading pairs. Kraken has not confirmed that explanation. Other analysts pointed to thin weekend order books and limited access to fresh Canadian-dollar deposits — Kraken's options for depositing Canadian dollars run through Interac, with limits and banking hours that can leave the market exposed to concentrated sell orders.

The episode came during a brutal stretch for crypto overall. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, extending its recent losses amid a broader selloff in technology stocks and other risk assets. It fell as low as $58,811 before recovering slightly to trade around $59,503, down 5.6 percent over the past 24 hours as of late Friday afternoon, according to CoinGecko data. The cryptocurrency has fallen 13.8 percent over the past week and nearly 30 percent over the past month.

That broader selloff was driven by the same AI-stock rout that hammered equities on Friday. The Nasdaq Composite closed down 5.28 percent at 22,617.73, while the S&P 500 fell 3.69 percent to 6,910.77. Shares of Anthropic, which made its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, closed down 8.50 percent at $88.91 amid concerns over valuations and spending in the artificial intelligence sector. The Kraken dislocation was a local event inside that global slide: global Bitcoin prices stayed stable throughout, and the research on the episode describes it as having no systemic implications for Bitcoin or other crypto assets.

Exchanges facing a flash crash have two broad options: cancel or reverse the trades, or let them stand. Reversals tend to happen when something actually broke, such as a hack, a pricing engine bug or compromised accounts placing rogue orders. Kraken's position is that none of that occurred here, and that its matching engine did exactly what it was built to do. The exchange has not identified a technical malfunction, and no breach or account compromise has been found.

The most immediate lesson lands on anyone trading smaller fiat pairs. A Bitcoin price on one order book is not a universal truth; it reflects only the buyers and sellers present on that specific market at that specific moment. Traders using market orders on thinner books carry the most risk, since a market order says 'fill me at whatever is available' — and during a liquidity vacuum, whatever is available can be shockingly far from fair value. Limit orders, which cap the price a trader will accept, are the basic defense. Stop-loss triggers tied to a local pair can also fire into a dislocation like this one and sell at the worst possible moment, even if the asset's global price never moved.

Why it matters: Kraken's decision to let the trades stand is the story, not the price glitch itself. Exchanges routinely unwind trades after fat fingers and software bugs; by refusing to do so, Kraken is treating a 71 percent price gap as a legitimate market outcome — a liquidity failure, not a system failure. That is defensible on its own terms, but it sets a precedent every Canadian crypto trader will remember: on a thin weekend book, your stop-loss can execute at a price the rest of the world would call absurd, and the exchange will not save you. The research warning is blunt — recurring dislocations could dent trader confidence in the region.

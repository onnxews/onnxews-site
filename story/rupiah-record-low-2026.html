Indonesia's Rupiah Hits a Record Low as the Central Bank Steps In

Indonesia's rupiah touched a record low of 18,272 per dollar on Thursday as Bank Indonesia intervened, with MSCI index worries, a surging dollar, and political uncertainty piling pressure on Asia's weakest currency of 2026.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Indonesia's rupiah fell to a record low against the dollar on Thursday, November 12, prompting the country's central bank to intervene in currency markets as a stronger dollar, worries about the country's place in global stock indexes, and political uncertainty combined to batter Southeast Asia's largest economy.

The currency pair climbed to a fresh record high of 18,272 rupiah per dollar during Asian hours on Thursday, passing its previous record. Bank Indonesia intervened in currency markets on Thursday as the rupiah reached record lows, operating in both domestic and overseas markets and using offshore non-deliverable forwards, domestic non-deliverable forwards, and transactions in the spot market. The central bank also said it was buying government securities, known locally as SBN, in the secondary market, where investors trade bonds already issued.

The immediate trigger was index provider MSCI. The latest drop followed MSCI's decision to remove a local firm from its indexes, a fresh setback after the index provider warned last month that persistent investability issues could lead to the country being reclassified as a standalone market. MSCI launched a consultation proposing to reclassify its Indonesia Indexes from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status because of persistent concerns about investability. Goldman Sachs estimates that excluding Indonesia from MSCI indexes could trigger about 5.6 billion dollars in passive investment outflows.

Political uncertainty has added to the pressure. Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has resigned, raising the risk of a split within the ruling coalition of Southeast Asia's largest economy, which could complicate policymaking for an administration already facing scrutiny over broader economic management and flagship initiatives like its free school meals programme. The rupiah is the currency least able to absorb dollar strength, said iFast research analyst Kok Jun Yong, noting that domestic political uncertainty has made the currency especially fragile.

The rupiah's slide is part of a broader dollar surge. The dollar index edged above 103 for the first time since April 2025 on Thursday, touching levels not seen since the shock announcement of U.S. tariffs on "Liberation Day", according to Dow Jones Newswires carried by Morningstar. The dollar has strengthened this week since U.S. inflation data came in hotter than expected, fueling expectations of tightening by the Federal Reserve, and remarks from Fed officials added fuel to the fire, with markets now fully pricing in a December move. The rupiah had lost more than 8% against the dollar since the start of the year, making it Asia's weakest currency over that period.

Other Asian currencies are feeling the same pressure. The yen is once again approaching the potential intervention zone, within striking distance of the historically sensitive 160 to 163 yen range against the dollar, and was last trading at 159.73 yen. The combination of a firmer greenback, higher U.S. yields and elevated oil prices creates a tough near-term backdrop for Asian currencies, said Lloyd Chan, senior analyst at MUFG. The Australian dollar was also struggling, trading at its weakest levels against the greenback since March, at 68.41 U.S. cents, below the 70-cent barrier seen as a key support level. South Korea's won and India's rupee are holding up better than their regional peers.

Fed officials have turned more hawkish in recent days, adding to the dollar's strength. Fed Governor Christopher Waller noted earlier this week that he would back a quarter-point increase in December, or even a half-point move if inflation worsens, and Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the latest inflation reading "borderline disturbing" while highlighting that services inflation is "not coming down". According to the CME FedWatch Tool, markets are now pricing in around an 87% probability of a December rate hike, alongside a nearly 13% chance of a larger 50 basis point increase, and the Fed meets on December 8 and 9.

The central bank's intervention tools include its "triple-intervention" framework, operating across spot, derivative, and government bond markets to smooth out currency volatility, complemented by monetary instruments to bolster capital inflows. As a major exporter of commodities such as palm oil, coal, nickel, and natural gas, Indonesia benefits from higher global commodity prices, but as a net importer of crude oil, elevated oil prices increase the foreign exchange burden and weigh on the currency.

The next dates shaping the market are the MSCI consultation deadlines and Bank Indonesia's own policy meeting, with investors watching whether the central bank raises rates to defend the currency or lets the rupiah find its own level. For now, the rupiah's record low is a warning that the combination of a hawkish Fed, an index provider losing patience, and political uncertainty at home can overwhelm even a central bank armed with intervention tools.

Why it matters: The rupiah's record low is what happens when three separate pressures hit the same currency on the same day. A hawkish Federal Reserve has pushed the dollar to its strongest since April, MSCI is threatening to strip Indonesia of its emerging-market status and trigger billions in forced selling, and political uncertainty at home has left investors with no reason to stay. Bank Indonesia has a triple-intervention framework, but intervention can smooth a fall, not reverse the forces behind it. The real deadline is not this week's trading but MSCI's June 2027 decision: if Indonesia is reclassified as a standalone market, the outflows Goldman Sachs puts at 5.6 billion dollars would turn a record low into a new normal.

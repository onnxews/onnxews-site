Glenn Close, Ridley Scott and Floyd Norman Honored at 2026 Governors Awards

Glenn Close finally holds an Oscar after eight nominations without a win, Ridley Scott accepts from afar in tribute to his late brother Tony, and Disney pioneer Floyd Norman gets his happy ending at the Academy's 17th Governors Awards.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences handed out its Honorary Oscars on Sunday night, November 15, 2026, at the 17th Governors Awards in the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood in Los Angeles. Actress Glenn Close, director Ridley Scott and Disney animator Floyd Norman each received an Academy Honorary Award, while producers Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award. The evening, presented in partnership with Rolex, is regarded as the unofficial kickoff to Hollywood's Oscar season.

For Glenn Close, 79, the night ended one of the longest waits in Academy history. She has been nominated for a competitive Oscar eight times without a win, a run that began with her feature film debut in 1982's The World According to Garp and continued through The Big Chill, Fatal Attraction, Dangerous Liaisons and, most recently, 2020's Hillbilly Elegy. Accepting her statuette, she thanked George Roy Hill, the director who gave her that first film role, and dedicated the award to her daughter, actress Annie Starke, telling her, “You are my heart and soul. I share this with you, my darling girl.”

Ridley Scott, 88, was unable to attend the ceremony while recovering from a minor medical procedure, so he accepted his honorary Oscar in a prerecorded speech that became the emotional centrepiece of the evening. The Gladiator and Thelma & Louise director dedicated the award to his late younger brother Tony Scott, the Top Gun director who died in 2012, saying he accepted it for Tony as well as himself. The speech ended with footage of the brothers' very first film, made for $100 in 1962.

Scott has never won a competitive Oscar despite four nominations, including best director nominations for Thelma & Louise, Gladiator and Black Hawk Down, and a best picture nomination as a producer of The Martian. His career spans nearly six decades and includes Alien, Blade Runner, American Gangster, Prometheus, House of Gucci and Napoleon.

Floyd Norman, 91, received his honorary Oscar for a career in animation that spans seven decades and bridges the hand-drawn Disney era and the Pixar age. He began at Walt Disney Animation Studios in 1956, becoming the studio's first Black animator, and worked on Sleeping Beauty, The Jungle Book and Mary Poppins before later contributing to Pixar's Toy Story 2 and Monsters, Inc. Moonlight star Mahershala Ali presented the award. Norman thanked his wife, Adrienne Brown-Norman, and paid tribute to Walt Disney, telling the room that while the movie of his life did not have tremendous turmoil, “on occasion, even movies have a happy ending,” and calling the evening his happy ending.

Producers Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler, the co-founders of the independent production company Killer Films, received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, presented by Todd Haynes and Julianne Moore. Moore, who has starred in eight films produced by the longtime collaborators, praised them for pursuing bold filmmaking rather than commercial success and noted that they are the first all-female producing team to receive the Thalberg Award. Haynes pointed out that the only Oscar legendary director Alfred Hitchcock ever received was the Thalberg Award in 1968.

Vachon and Koffler founded Killer Films in New York in 1995 and have produced films including Hedwig and the Angry Inch, Boys Don't Cry, Far from Heaven, Carol, Still Alice, First Reformed, May December and Materialists, earning a best picture Oscar nomination for Past Lives in 2024. The Thalberg Award goes to a creative producer whose body of work reflects a consistently high quality of motion picture production, and the Academy praised the pair for championing bold, ambitious and distinctive storytelling at the centre of American independent cinema.

The Honorary Award is given to honour extraordinary distinction in lifetime achievement, exceptional contributions to the state of motion picture arts and sciences, or outstanding service to the Academy, and the recipients are chosen by a vote of the Academy's Board of Governors. The Academy announced this year's honorees earlier in the year, with president Lynette Howell Taylor praising Close's emotional range, Norman's barrier-breaking career, Scott's visionary legacy and the producing pair's central role in independent film.

The room on Sunday night reflected the occasion's standing, with guests ranging from Riz Ahmed and Timothée Chalamet to Tom Cruise and Zendaya among the more than 200 filmmakers and stars on hand. For Close, the evening finally put a golden statuette in the hands of an actor whose eight nominations had made her one of the most celebrated performers never to win. For Scott, it was recognition delivered from afar but accepted in his brother's name. And for Norman, whose first Disney feature was Sleeping Beauty, it was a happy ending seven decades in the making.

Why it matters: The Governors Awards matter because they are where the Academy corrects its own history, honouring lifetime achievement that the competitive races never rewarded. Close's eight nominations without a win and Scott's four made them the most prominent oversights of their generations, and the ceremony doubles as the starting gun for Oscar season, putting contenders and voters in the same room months before nominations. Norman's honour also carries weight beyond film: recognising Disney's first Black animator, whose career ran from Walt Disney himself to Pixar, writes a pioneer into the Academy's official record. For Vachon and Koffler, the first all-female Thalberg team, the award signals that independent producing is now central to the Academy's idea of excellence, not a sideline to it.

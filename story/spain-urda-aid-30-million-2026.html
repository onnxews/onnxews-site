Spain Approves 30 Million Euros in Aid for Families of the 69 Killed in Urda Train Crash

Relatives of each person killed will receive 72,121 euros in aid plus the same amount as an advance. The transport minister resigned the same day the decree was adopted.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Spain has approved 30 million euros in aid for the victims of the train crash near Urda, offering the relatives of each person killed a total of 144,242 euros in aid and advance payments, under a decree adopted this week.

The Council of Ministers adopted Royal Decree-Law 32/2026 on November 3, and it took effect when it was published in the Official State Gazette the following day. The Transport Ministry split the 30 million euros into 15 million euros of direct aid and 15 million euros of advances on civil liability compensation.

For each person killed, eligible relatives receive 72,121.46 euros in aid that does not have to be repaid, and may request an advance of the same amount, bringing the total to 144,242.92 euros per death. Payments for injured passengers will depend on severity, ranging from about 2,404 euros to 84,142 euros.

The collision on October 28 at kilometer 118 of the Madrid-Seville high-speed line near Urda, in Toledo province, killed 69 people and injured more than 100. A Renfe AVE train carrying 397 people struck the rear of an Iryo train carrying 147 passengers at high speed. All 69 victims were identified by November 2.

Transport Minister Oscar Puente resigned on November 3, six days after the crash, and withdrew from the November 29 general election. The high-speed line between Madrid and Seville remains closed with no confirmed reopening date, and investigators are still examining railway signalling systems and decisions at the Adif control centre.

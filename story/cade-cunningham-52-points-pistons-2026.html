Cade Cunningham Drops Career-High 52 as Pistons Outlast Wizards 144–140 in Double Overtime

The Detroit star scored 30 of his 52 points in the fourth quarter and the overtimes, becoming the first player in nearly 50 years with 50 points, 10 assists and no turnovers in a game.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Cade Cunningham scored a career-high 52 points on Thursday night, November 12, leading the Detroit Pistons to a 144–140 double-overtime win over the Washington Wizards at Capital One Arena.

Cunningham added 11 assists without a single turnover, shooting 16 of 25 from the field and 17 of 18 from the free-throw line, in a performance that put him in company no player has kept in nearly half a century.

He is the first player since at least 1978 — when the league began tracking turnovers — to record 50 or more points, 10 or more assists and zero turnovers in a game. He is only the fifth player with a 50-point double-double and no turnovers since 1977–78, joining Michael Jordan, who did it twice, Moses Malone, Carmelo Anthony and Giannis Antetokounmpo.

Cunningham did most of his damage late. He had 22 points through three quarters, then scored 30 in the fourth quarter and the two overtime periods, including 18 in the fourth alone.

The game needed all of it. Trae Young's buzzer-beating three-pointer tied the score at 121 to force overtime, and the Wizards scored the first seven points of the extra period. Cunningham tied it at 130, Duncan Robinson put Detroit ahead, and rookie AJ Dybantsa — the No. 1 pick in the 2026 draft — hit a buzzer-beater to force a second overtime at 132.

Cunningham opened the second overtime with a three-pointer and sealed the win with a midrange jumper that made it 142–138 with 25.3 seconds left. The win improved Detroit to 8–3, while Washington fell to 4–9 with a sixth straight loss.

Cunningham's 52 points tied George Yardley's 52 from 1958 for the fourth-highest total in Pistons history, behind Jerry Stackhouse's 57, Kelly Tripucka's 56 and Dave Bing's 54. His previous career high was 46, set against the Wizards on November 10, 2025.

He had help. Ausar Thompson recorded his first career triple-double with 17 points, 11 rebounds, 11 assists, four steals and three blocks, and Daniss Jenkins scored 21 points for a third straight 20-point game.

Washington got plenty of scoring too. Young finished with 37 points, Anthony Davis had 30 points and 15 rebounds before fouling out in the second overtime, and Alex Sarr added 30 — the first time three Wizards have scored 30 in the same game since November 18, 1969, when the franchise was the Baltimore Bullets.

It was a night that said as much about where the two teams are going as about one performance: Detroit, now 8–3, looks like a team on the rise behind a star who keeps finding new levels, while Washington, on a six-game slide, keeps finding new ways to lose games it could have won, according to the Detroit Free Press.

Detroit's start to the season is the backdrop to Cunningham's night. At 8–3, the Pistons are winning games that would have slipped away a year ago, and the supporting cast is growing into its roles: Thompson's first triple-double, with four steals and three blocks on top of the points, rebounds and assists, is the kind of all-around performance that turns a good team into a hard one to beat.

Washington's problems are the mirror image. At 4–9 with six straight losses, the Wizards keep producing scoring — three 30-point games in one night — and keep losing anyway. Young, Davis and Sarr combined for 97 points and it still was not enough, which tells the story of a team that can score with anyone and cannot stop anyone. The front office will have to decide whether the answer is patience or a change.

Why it matters: Cunningham's night matters because of the company it puts him in: a 50-point, 10-assist, zero-turnover game has not happened since the league started tracking turnovers in 1977–78, and the only names on that list are all-time greats. It also matters for Detroit's season — the Pistons are 8–3 and winning the kind of game that used to slip away, with Cunningham delivering in the fourth quarter and both overtimes. What to watch is whether this is a one-night explosion or the start of a genuine MVP-level leap.

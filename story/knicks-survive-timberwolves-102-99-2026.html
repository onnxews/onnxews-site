Knicks Survive Fourth-Quarter Collapse to Edge Timberwolves 102-99

Karl-Anthony Towns scored 22 points against his former team as New York nearly squandered a 19-point lead in Minnesota before holding on for a third straight win.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Karl-Anthony Towns scored 22 points and grabbed 12 rebounds against his former team as the New York Knicks held on for a 102-99 win over the Minnesota Timberwolves on Thursday night in Minneapolis, surviving a fourth quarter in which they scored just 12 points.

Towns scored 17 of his 22 points in the first half, and Jalen Brunson added 21 points and seven assists for New York, which won its third game in a row. OG Anunoby scored 15 points, Josh Hart chipped in 11, and Landry Shamet put up 10 off the bench.

For three quarters, the Knicks were in complete command. They led by as many as 19 points in the third quarter and by 16 early in the fourth after Tyler Kolek converted a four-point play to start the final period. They had led since early in the first quarter and shot 57.1 percent from the field by halftime.

Then the offence stopped. The Knicks missed their next 13 shots after Kolek's four-point play, went 3-for-18 from the field in the fourth quarter and failed to score over the final 3:26 of the game. Towns shot 1-for-5 in the period and Brunson went scoreless.

Minnesota, which trailed 90-78 entering the fourth quarter, chipped away steadily. A basket by Jonathan Kuminga brought the Timberwolves within 97-90, and a three-pointer by LaMelo Ball made it 97-93 with 5:27 remaining. Ayo Dosunmu then drained a three-pointer with 2:34 left to cut the gap to 102-99.

Dosunmu would have tied the game on the next possession with another shot from deep, but it did not count because of a premature whistle for a shot clock violation. Replay review determined that Ball's jumper had grazed the rim, and the basket was wiped out.

Ball missed a tying three-point attempt with 9.9 seconds left, and the Timberwolves could not get another shot off. Kuminga grabbed the rebound but threw it away to Shamet, who sealed the victory with a steal in the closing seconds.

Kuminga scored 23 points to lead Minnesota in his return from a two-game absence because of a bruised right shoulder. Jaden McDaniels scored 17 points, and Ball had 15 points on 6-for-21 shooting, along with eight assists and eight rebounds.

The win moved the Knicks to 9-6 and closed out a four-game road trip at 3-1, a strong return against a Timberwolves side that entered the game with the league's best defence and had not missed much of a beat without injured superstar Anthony Edwards. Minnesota fell to 11-5, and its 99 points were its fewest this season.

Towns, who was traded to the Knicks in 2024 after nine years with the Timberwolves, was greeted loudly and warmly by the crowd before the game. It was his third game back in Minnesota as an opponent, and once again he delivered: he had 32 points and 20 rebounds in his first return two years ago, and 40 points in his return last season. New York now returns home to host Portland on Saturday, while Minnesota hosts Memphis the same day, a quick turnaround for two teams that both left everything on the floor on Thursday night.

Why it matters: The Knicks will take the win, but the fourth quarter should worry them more than the first three pleased them. A team with championship ambitions cannot go scoreless for the final three and a half minutes and survive on a steal, and Minnesota's defence showed exactly how to bog New York down when the pace slows. The encouraging part is that the Knicks built a 19-point lead on the road against the league's best defence in the first place, and Towns keeps producing in the building where he spent nine years. The next step is learning to close games the way they start them.

Trump Says U.S. Struck Iranian Air Defenses on Qeshm Island in 'Minor' Operation

Trump said American forces destroyed a newly built Iranian air-defense system in minutes, while insisting the U.S. is not fighting a war and talks are progressing.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump said on Friday that U.S. forces carried out a limited strike against an Iranian air-defense system on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, renewing military pressure on Tehran even as he insisted that negotiations to end the confrontation were making progress.

Trump described the operation as minor. Speaking to reporters in West Palm Beach, Florida, he said Iran had built a small anti-aircraft system on the island and that American forces had destroyed it in about 15 minutes, or perhaps five. He did not say when the strike took place, what weapons were used, or how much damage was done.

Iranian state media reported that two missiles struck the Suza area of Qeshm Island, Iran's largest island, which sits at the northern approach to the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes. Iran's state broadcaster said Iranian forces had shot down an American MQ-9 Reaper drone over the island, a claim the United States did not confirm. U.S. Central Command issued no statement on the operation.

Trump was careful to frame the strike as something short of war. We are not fighting, he told reporters, while adding that Washington had made a lot of progress with Iran in talks mediated by Qatar. He has also described the American pressure campaign against Tehran as the largest blockade in military history, and he has said repeatedly that he wants a deal, not a wider war.

The strike was the second American military action against Iran in a week. On Monday, November 2, the day before the midterm elections, U.S. forces carried out what the military called defensive strikes on Qeshm and Hengam islands and near the mainland port of Bandar Abbas, after explosions were reported in the area. The two episodes have not been confirmed as part of a single operation, and the exact targets of the latest strike remain unverified by independent reporting.

The timing is politically charged. The strike came three days after Republicans lost control of both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections, a defeat Trump has partly blamed on the war and on rising oil prices. Democrats who take control of the House and Senate in January have signaled they intend to scrutinize the Iran campaign, and the party's leaders have said the Pentagon budget and the war will be among their first targets.

The confrontation has been building for months. The United States has struck Iranian targets repeatedly since the summer, when oil prices surged above 100 dollars a barrel on fears of a wider war, and Tehran has sent a nuclear proposal to Washington through Qatar, according to reporting this week. Trump has said a decision on the war is coming soon, and the two sides have been talking even as the strikes continue.

Gulf states are watching the escalation nervously. Qatar, which is mediating the talks, has insisted that negotiations are continuing despite the strikes, and other governments in the region have warned that a miscalculation in the Strait of Hormuz could disrupt global energy supplies. Oil prices, which fell sharply after the midterm results, remain sensitive to any sign that the conflict is widening.

For now, both sides are keeping the door to diplomacy open while continuing to use force. Trump says the talks are progressing and that Tehran wants a deal; Iran's army has vowed an immediate and effective response to any attack, without geographical limits. Whether the limited strike on Qeshm becomes a step toward a settlement or a step toward a larger war will depend on what happens in the talks Qatar is mediating, and on whether either side decides that the other has crossed a line.

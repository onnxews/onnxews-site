Google Opens Gemini 4 Carbon to Paying Developers, With Prices Set to Double on December 1

Google's strongest AI model is now generally available at $2 per million input tokens, but the introductory rate expires November 30.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon, its most capable artificial intelligence model, generally available on November 10, opening it to paying developers and businesses nearly two weeks after it was unveiled with a limited rollout. The wider release brings the company's flagship model into direct price competition with Anthropic's new Claude models, and Google is undercutting them sharply, at least for now.

Carbon is not new, but until now almost nobody could use it. Google first announced the model on October 28 with what it said were industry-leading benchmark results, then limited access to a subset of cybersecurity defenders through its gated Fairwind program, where they used it for work such as finding and fixing software vulnerabilities. November 10 is the wider launch of the general-use model.

Developers can now use Carbon through the paid tier of the Gemini API in Google AI Studio and Android Studio, and businesses can select it in Gemini for business. For consumers, it is rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, Search's AI Mode and Antigravity, Google's coding platform. Google has not published a country-by-country rollout schedule, and availability on Vertex AI was left unspecified in the launch materials.

The price is the story. Carbon starts at 2 dollars per million uncached input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens, but only through November 30. From December 1, both rates double, to 4 dollars and 20 dollars. Cached input costs 10 cents per million tokens now and 20 cents from December, and batch processing costs half the standard rate. Google's own documentation warns that Carbon spends more thinking tokens per turn than its sibling model Argon, and thinking tokens are billed as output, so an identical per-token price can still mean a larger bill.

The comparison with Anthropic is deliberate. Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on November 10, priced at 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens. Carbon undercuts Fable 5.2 by 75 percent during the introductory period and by 50 percent after December 1, and it matches Claude Opus 5.5, priced at 4 dollars and 20 dollars, once the introductory rate expires. Google is pricing its flagship as a direct challenge to Anthropic's newest models.

There is no free way to try Carbon through the API. The model is not available on the Gemini API free tier, so a free API key will not work, and Google AI Pro subscribers do not get it in AI Studio either. The consumer route is Google AI Ultra, which costs 99.99 dollars a month for the Ultra 5x plan and 199.99 dollars a month for Ultra 20x, which offers higher usage limits. Google has announced no downloadable weights and no self-hosting license for the model.

Google describes Carbon as its strongest model, built for frontier cybersecurity work and long-horizon agent tasks that require sustained reasoning over many steps. Even so, the company still recommends Gemini 4 Argon for most coding and knowledge work, a sign that Carbon's extra thinking power comes with extra cost and is meant for the hardest problems rather than everyday use.

The launch sharpens a price war at the top of the AI market. Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are all shipping frontier models within weeks of each other, and the competition is now being fought openly on price per token as well as on benchmarks. For developers, the result is a brief window in which the most powerful model Google has ever shipped costs less than half as much as its main rival.

What remains unclear is how Carbon performs outside Google's own benchmark claims. The launch materials left some benchmark scores unspecified, and independent tables did not show a Carbon row for some tests. Developers will now be able to find out for themselves, though only if they pay, and only until the price doubles.

The practical advice for anyone planning to use Carbon is to watch the calendar. The 2-dollar introductory rate is a promotion with a known end date, not a permanent price, and any budget built on it will need to double on December 1. After that, Carbon costs exactly what Claude Opus 5.5 costs, and the choice between them will come down to performance, not price.

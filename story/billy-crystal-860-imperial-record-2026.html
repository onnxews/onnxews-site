Billy Crystal's 860 Smashes Imperial Theatre Record for a Play

The solo show grossed $1,720,253 in the week ending November 15, beating the record Crystal himself set with 700 Sundays in 2013.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Billy Crystal's solo show 860 set a new weekly box office record at the Imperial Theatre in the week ending November 15, 2026, grossing $1,720,253. It is the highest weekly gross ever recorded for a play at the theatre, and it beat a record Crystal set himself more than a decade ago.

The previous record was $1,403,415, set by Crystal's earlier solo show, 700 Sundays, in Christmas week 2013. The new figure beats it by $316,838, or about 23%, a remarkable margin for a record that had stood for 13 years. The show played eight performances in the week, one more than its usual seven, which helped push the total higher.

The show ranked fourth among all Broadway productions that week, an unusual feat for a play in a field dominated by big musicals. The top five that week included four plays: Other Desert Cities, Much Ado About Nothing, 860, and Harry Potter and the Cursed Child. Broadway as a whole took in $40.30 million across 39 productions, up about 10% on the previous week.

860 opened at the Imperial Theatre on October 21, 2026, and was originally scheduled to close on January 3, 2027. Demand has been so strong that the run has already been extended through February 14, 2027, adding more than a month of performances.

The show is written by Crystal and directed by Scott Ellis, with music by Marc Shaiman. It tells the story of Crystal's family and career, and it was born out of the Palisades fires, which destroyed the home Crystal had lived in for decades. The title, 860, is the street number of that home. The extension gives audiences more than a month of extra performances, and it means the show will still be running on Valentine's Day, nearly four months after it opened.

Crystal is no stranger to Broadway records. 700 Sundays, the show whose record 860 has now broken, was a Tony Award-winning hit that also played to packed houses. The new show is, in a sense, a sequel to that story, picking up where the earlier one left off and carrying it through the loss of the family home.

The extension through Valentine's Day is a sign of how the show has landed with audiences. Solo shows are a gamble on Broadway, depending entirely on one performer's ability to hold a theatre for two hours, and the grosses suggest that Crystal is doing exactly that, night after night.

The record also says something about the health of Broadway this season. A week in which four plays sit in the top five, and in which total grosses rise 10% week over week, is a strong one for the industry, and it comes as the season builds toward the holidays, traditionally the busiest time of the year.

For Crystal, the record is a personal milestone as well as a commercial one. Thirteen years after 700 Sundays, he has returned to the same theatre, told a new chapter of the same story, and broken his own record by nearly a quarter.

Why it matters: This is a record that tells a story beyond the box office. Crystal lost his home in the Palisades fires, and the show he built from that loss has now broken the record set by the show about his family that came before it. The $1.72 million week, and the extension through February, show that audiences are responding to a deeply personal piece of theatre, not just a famous name. It also matters for Broadway as a whole: a season in which a one-man play can sit fourth in the grosses, ahead of most musicals, is a season in which the audience is rewarding something other than spectacle.

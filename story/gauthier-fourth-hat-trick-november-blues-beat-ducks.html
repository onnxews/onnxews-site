Gauthier's Record Fourth Hat Trick of November Not Enough as Blues Outgun Ducks 8–6

Cutter Gauthier became the first player in NHL history with four hat tricks in a calendar month, but St. Louis outscored Anaheim 8–6.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Cutter Gauthier scored a hat trick on Tuesday night, his fourth of November, and it still was not enough. The Anaheim Ducks lost 8–6 to the St. Louis Blues at the Enterprise Center in St. Louis, in a game that featured 14 goals, a 10-game goal streak and a piece of NHL history.

Gauthier, 22, became the first player in NHL history to record four hat tricks in a single calendar month. It was his fourth hat trick of the season and the fifth of his career, with his first NHL hat trick coming on November 4, 2025. He has now scored 21 goals and added eight assists for 29 points in 21 games this season, tying Leon Draisaitl for the NHL goal-scoring lead.

The hat trick extended Gauthier's goal streak to 10 games, the longest by an NHL player in 29 years and the first 10-game goal streak since Teemu Selanne in 1997–98. He is the third player in NHL history to record a 10-game goal streak before the age of 23, joining Babe Dye, who had an 11-game streak in 1920–21, and Mike Bossy, who had a 10-game streak in 1978–79. He has scored 19 goals during the streak and has 24 points, on 19 goals and five assists, during a career-high 12-game point streak.

November has been a historic month for Gauthier in more ways than one. He has scored 17 goals in the month, within reach of the NHL record for most goals in a calendar month: 20, shared by Joe Malone in February 1921 and Selanne in March 1993. He is one goal short of tying that record. He also set a Ducks franchise record for the most hat tricks in a single season with four, passing Frank Vatrano, who had three in 2023–24, Corey Perry, who had three in 2014–15 and 2010–11, and Selanne, who had three in 1997–98. He is the second American player in NHL history with 20 goals in 21 games or fewer, after Brett Hull, who did it in 20 games in 1990–91.

Gauthier's three goals told the story of the night. His first came at 1:02 of the second period on a one-timer from the right circle, and his second was a power-play one-timer at 16:19 of the second, eight seconds into the man advantage. The hat-trick goal came at 19:19 of the third, from the left circle with the Anaheim net empty, pulling the Ducks to 7–6. Pavel Buchnevich answered with an empty-net goal at 19:22 to make it 8–6.

The Blues, though, scored in bunches all night. Jonatan Berggren opened the scoring at 2:13 of the first period, and after Alex Killorn tied it at 11:38 and Connor McMichael made it 2–1 at 19:07 on a goal that was upheld after video review, St. Louis took control. Robert Thomas made it 3–2 at 11:41 of the second and Dalibor Dvorsky made it 4–2 at 13:37, before Gauthier's second pulled one back. Jake Neighbours made it 5–3 at 5:36 of the third, Philip Broberg made it 6–3 at 6:19 and Logan Mailloux made it 7–3 at 8:34, his first goal of the season, as the Blues scored three times in a 2:58 span.

Anaheim kept coming back. Ian Moore made it 7–4 at 14:59 and Mikael Granlund scored on a 6-on-4 power play at 18:35 to make it 7–5, setting up Gauthier's late hat-trick goal. But the comeback fell short, and the Ducks fell to 11–10–1, losing three of four. The Blues improved to 10–9–2, winning three of four.

Thomas and Mailloux each had a goal and two assists for St. Louis, Berggren and Neighbours each had a goal and an assist, and Justin Barron had two assists. Jordan Binnington made 25 saves in his first start since November 5. For Anaheim, Granlund had a goal and an assist and Jackson LaCombe had three assists, while Ville Husso allowed seven goals on 24 shots and was replaced by Lukas Dostal, who made three saves, with 8:48 left in the third.

Gauthier was matter-of-fact about the night. "I'm just keeping it simple," he said. "Just came a little short tonight." Blues coach Jim Montgomery praised his team's start to the third period: "The first 10 minutes, 11 minutes of the third were really good... Don't play too loose." Ducks coach Joel Quenneville called it a "crazy game," adding: "We got ourselves in the game a couple times." Thomas added: "It was nice to see some go in finally." Gauthier can tie Selanne's Ducks franchise record of 11 straight games with a goal, set from October 21 to November 10, 1997, with a goal on Thursday at Colorado. Reuters and NHL.com both covered the game on Tuesday.

Why it matters: An 8–6 game in which the losing team's star makes history is the best kind of regular-season hockey, and it says something about both teams. Gauthier's November — 17 goals, four hat tricks, a 10-game goal streak — is the most remarkable scoring run by a 22-year-old in decades, and the monthly record of 20 goals, untouched since 1993, is now within a single goal. But the Blues won by scoring in bunches, with three goals in under three minutes in the third period, which is a reminder that one player's brilliance cannot cover for a goaltender allowing seven goals on 24 shots. Anaheim's problem is not Gauthier. It is everything around him.

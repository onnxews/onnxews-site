Wall Street Rally Stalls as Hot Inflation Data and Nvidia Slide Hit Stocks

The S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7% as Treasury yields jumped toward 5% and Nvidia shed $470 billion in two sessions.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Wall Street's rebound stalled on Tuesday, November 10, as hotter-than-expected inflation data reignited concerns about Federal Reserve interest rate policy and sent Treasury yields higher. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.74 percent to 25,066, retreating from Monday's gains, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.38 percent to 7,521 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.30 percent to 52,232, giving back some of the previous day's rally.

Monday had been the market's best day in months. The Dow surged 1,104 points in its best session since April as an oil slide fueled a relief rally, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq had climbed with it. Tuesday's reversal was a reminder of how quickly the mood can change when inflation data disappoints, and it left all three major indexes lower on the day.

The inflation report was the trigger. Consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, above the 0.3 percent forecast, and core inflation climbed 0.3 percent against expectations for 0.2 percent. The reading strengthened expectations that the Fed will raise rates again at its December meeting, and traders quickly moved to price in a faster pace of tightening, pushing bond yields sharply higher.

The single heaviest weight on the S&P 500 was Nvidia, which fell 5.5 percent to pace Dow decliners. The chipmaker has been under pressure since its board announced an investigation into Chief Executive Jensen Huang over his relationship with a woman who, according to the New York Times, United States intelligence determined was gathering information for China. The stock has lost nearly 10 percent already this week and now trades about 30 percent below its record close set in early October.

The scale of Nvidia's slide is striking. The drop erased about 248 billion dollars from Nvidia's market value on Tuesday alone, and Reuters reported the stock was on track to shed more than 470 billion dollars in market value over two sessions. Microsoft briefly topped Nvidia's market value earlier in the session for the first time since June 2025, a symbolic changing of the guard at the top of the AI trade.

Selling pressure extended beyond equities. Gold fell 1.84 percent to 4,038.70 dollars, while the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.88 percent and touched 5 percent during the session, approaching the psychologically important 5 percent threshold. The 2-year yield jumped 17 basis points to 4.74 percent, its biggest one-day move since April 2025, and the 30-year yield climbed to 5.36 percent.

Not every stock fell. Meta Platforms surged more than 5 percent after JPMorgan added the social media giant to its Focus List, and Sea Limited jumped 8.1 percent following stronger-than-expected quarterly revenue. But the decliners were concentrated in the year's biggest winners: server maker Dell Technologies fell 4.8 percent, chip firm SanDisk dropped 4.5 percent, and among the smaller AI infrastructure firms, Iren dropped 10 percent, Nebius fell 7 percent and CoreWeave slid 3 percent. Shares of power equipment maker GE Vernova slid more than 6 percent.

The technology sector bore the brunt of the selling. The Technology Select Sector SPDR Fund fell 1.6 percent, the worst performer among the 11 S&P 500 sectors, as Nvidia's slide and higher yields hit richly valued growth stocks. The Nasdaq has now dropped by 1 percent or more eight times in the past month, and risen by that much six times, a sign of how unsettled the market has become.

By Wednesday morning, the mood had steadied. Nasdaq 100 futures rose about 1.8 percent, S&P 500 futures gained 0.9 percent and Dow futures edged up 0.2 percent, buoyed by CoreWeave's better-than-expected earnings and a steadying in Nvidia shares after hours. The bond market is closed on Wednesday for Veterans Day, giving investors a chance to catch their breath after the sharp run-up in yields.

What comes next will be decided by earnings and data. Applied Materials and Cisco report on Thursday, the Producer Price Index arrives on Friday, and Nvidia's own earnings land after the close a week from today, followed by Anthropic's stock market debut. Tuesday showed how fragile the rally remains when inflation runs hot, and the next two weeks will test whether the AI trade can recover or whether the selloff has further to run.

Houston Orders Police to Limit Cooperation With ICE After Democrats Sweep Texas

Mayor John Whitmire signed an executive order barring officers from alerting immigration agents over routine stops and minor offences.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Houston's mayor on Friday directed the city's police department to sharply limit its cooperation with federal immigration officers, a decision made within days of Democrats sweeping statewide races in Texas and reclaiming a majority in Congress.

Mayor John Whitmire signed an executive order directing Houston police to contact U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement only when a person is already going to jail on new charges or other warrants and a national crime database check shows an ICE administrative warrant. Suspected immigration status alone will no longer be a basis to contact ICE, prolong a detention or hold anyone in custody.

Houston Police Chief Noe Diaz, who stood with Whitmire at the announcement, said he supported the change and that the department had already begun putting it into effect. He gave a plain example of how routine encounters will now work: if officers pull someone over for a minor traffic infraction and write a citation, that person receives the citation and goes about their day, without any call to ICE.

Whitmire said the policy was about reducing fear and anxiety, not just within certain communities but across all of Houston. He added, in a phrase he repeated at the briefing, that elections matter. The mayor said he had consulted with the state's newly elected governor, lieutenant governor and comptroller before acting.

The move is a turnaround from the posture Houston adopted earlier this year. In April, the City Council passed an ordinance limiting police cooperation with ICE, but the administration then negotiated a policy that restored officers' discretion to contact federal agents. Governor Greg Abbott, a Republican, threatened to withdraw about 110 million dollars in public safety grants if the city did not repeal the ordinance.

City data showed the scale of the cooperation that followed. Officers contacted ICE nearly 200 times from April through September, including at least 36 instances in which Houston police handed people directly to immigration agents. Under the new order, those handoffs would happen only when the person is already headed to jail.

The timing is tied directly to Tuesday's election results. Abbott lost his bid for a fourth term to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, and Democrats swept the state's top offices. Hinojosa does not take office until January, but Whitmire described his decision as responsible policy that the election results had made possible.

The order puts Houston, America's fourth-largest city, at odds with a Republican-backed state law, Senate Bill 4, which requires local cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. The law remains in force, and the governor's office and the Texas attorney general's office had not immediately responded to requests for comment on Friday.

What happens next will depend on how the incoming state government responds and whether the courts are asked to weigh in. For now, Houston has drawn a line between ordinary policing and federal immigration enforcement, and the city's immigrant communities, who have lived for months under the threat of a routine traffic stop ending in detention, will be watching whether the new policy holds.

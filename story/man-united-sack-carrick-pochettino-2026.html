Manchester United Sack Michael Carrick as Pochettino Deal Closes In

United fired their head coach on Thursday after one win in 10 league matches left the club 18th in the Premier League, with USMNT coach Mauricio Pochettino lined up to take over.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Manchester United have fired head coach Michael Carrick after a dire start to the season that leaves the once-great club languishing in the Premier League relegation zone. The club confirmed the decision in a statement on Thursday, Nov. 12, and said it intends to appoint a permanent successor as quickly as possible. It is not yet confirmed who will take over, but CNN Sports understands that US men's national team coach Mauricio Pochettino has reached a verbal agreement with the club. CNN reported the sacking and the Pochettino agreement

The numbers behind the decision are stark. United have won just once in 10 Premier League matches this season, the club's worst league start in 40 years, and sit in 18th place in the table. The final straw appeared to be Sunday's 4-4 draw with Aston Villa, a result that left United in the relegation zone and, according to The Athletic, created a feeling that a change needed to be made before the season resumed after the November international break. "After a disappointing start to the season, the club has come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary in order to achieve our ambitions," the club said in its statement on Thursday.

Carrick's departure had been expected for much of the week. Reports emerged that the club's hierarchy was due to meet in Monaco on Wednesday to make it official, and co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe held that meeting with chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox. Berrada and Wilcox then informed Carrick of the decision on Thursday morning. Meanwhile, Carrick took training as normal on Wednesday, even when it was clear who United were lining up as his potential replacement, and he was photographed leaving the club's Carrington training centre on Thursday.

The club's statement paid tribute to a player and coach who will remain a popular figure at Old Trafford. "Michael is, and always will be, a Manchester United legend. Just as he did as a player, he has conducted himself with the highest standards of dedication and professionalism throughout his time back with the club. His leadership and achievements last season will always be fondly remembered." Carrick took over on an interim basis in January after Ruben Amorim's sacking, before overseeing a turnaround in the squad's fortunes at the tail end of the last campaign that was enough to earn him the permanent job in May, when he signed a two-year contract.

Carrick becomes the seventh permanent head coach to be sacked by United since the departure of legendary manager Alex Ferguson in 2013, and he is the third head coach to fail under Ratcliffe and INEOS, following Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim. Millions have been spent on compensation to fix those mistakes, and performances have suffered. The season has also been punctuated by flashpoints, including public outbursts from club captain Bruno Fernandes and fans turning on many of the players, even as supporters continued to back Carrick himself and put the blame on the players and the club's hierarchy.

All signs now point to Pochettino taking the job. Reports on Wednesday indicated that the two sides were finalising a deal to make him United's next manager, and that the club had made contact with U.S. Soccer. Pochettino signed a four-year contract extension to keep him with the USMNT through the 2030 World Cup, but that contract reportedly has a $10 million buy clause. The Athletic reported that United spent Tuesday ironing out personal terms with Pochettino's representatives, although the club had yet to speak to U.S. Soccer at that stage.

The timing of any move is complicated by the international break. The USMNT are set to play Haiti on Saturday and Tuesday, and as things stand Pochettino is expected to be in charge of both matches before any move to Old Trafford, with talks between United, Pochettino and U.S. Soccer continuing this week before anything is made official.

Pochettino brings extensive Premier League experience. He started in management at his former club Espanyol, made his name at Southampton, then moved to Tottenham in 2014, where he transformed the team and most notably led them to the UEFA Champions League final in 2019, before losing to Liverpool. Spells at Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea followed before he took charge of the USA in 2024, and while his project took a while to get going, he led the US team to an impressive World Cup campaign, and the team now looks well set for the future. He has been on United's radar before, but the two parties could never quite line up until now.

If Pochettino does leave, U.S. Soccer will need a replacement quickly. The Athletic reported on Wednesday that U.S. Soccer executives are already eyeing legendary former Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola as their top choice, while Under-23 coach Steve Cherundolo could also be among the candidates. For United, the job facing the new head coach is gigantic: working out how to fit into a failing hierarchy, improving a playing roster that looks short of quality, motivating players who look mentally fragile, and keeping the fans on side in a culture that many around the club now describe as toxic.

Why it matters: This is the latest low point in United's long decline since Ferguson retired, and the handling of Carrick's exit has put the spotlight firmly back on the ownership. Reports of his imminent sacking were allowed to spread for days while he kept taking training, and he now becomes the third coach to fail under INEOS. Pochettino is a strong appointment on paper, a proven Premier League manager with a record of developing young players, but he inherits a club in the relegation zone, a squad low on confidence and a hierarchy that has now burned through three managers. Whether he or anyone else can succeed where so many have failed is the question that will define United's season.

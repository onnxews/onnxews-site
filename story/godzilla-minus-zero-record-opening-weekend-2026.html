Godzilla Minus Zero Roars to a Record $32 Million Opening, the Biggest Ever for a Live-Action Foreign Film

Takashi Yamazaki's sequel is tracking for a $32 million North American debut, nearly triple its predecessor's start, with premium screens driving almost three quarters of the gross.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Godzilla Minus Zero is stomping its way into the record books. The sequel to the Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One is on course for an industry-estimated $32 million opening weekend in North America, a figure that would make it the biggest domestic opening ever for a live-action foreign-language film.

The film earned $14.5 million on Friday, including $6.7 million from Thursday previews, from 3,254 theaters, according to Box Office Mojo and trade reports. Deadline's Saturday morning projection put the three-day total at about $32 million, within a range of $30 million to $33 million. That is nearly triple the $11.4 million that Godzilla Minus One earned in its opening weekend in December 2023.

The record on the books belongs to Jet Li's Hero, which opened to $17.8 million in August 2004 and held the mark for 22 years. Godzilla Minus Zero is set to nearly double that figure, and it would become only the fourth non-English-language film to finish a weekend at No. 1 in North America, joining The Passion of the Christ, Hero and Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle.

The overall record for a foreign-language opening still belongs to an anime. Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle opened to $70.6 million in September 2025, a figure no live-action subtitled film has approached. But within live action, Godzilla Minus Zero stands alone, and its opening is all the more remarkable for a film with a reported production budget of around $15 million.

One number explains much of the success. About 74 percent of the domestic gross is coming from IMAX and other premium large-format screens, a share the trades describe as a never-before-seen stat. Audiences are not just going to see the film, they are paying extra to see it on the biggest screens available, and the premium-format turnout has turned a niche release into a genuine event.

The film also earned an A CinemaScore from audiences, the same grade its predecessor received, suggesting strong word of mouth heading into the rest of the weekend and beyond. With Veterans Day falling on Wednesday, the film is expected to keep drawing traffic through the middle of the week, and studio estimates arrive on Sunday with confirmed actuals following on Monday.

Japan embraced the film first. Godzilla Minus Zero opened there on November 3, Godzilla Day, earning an estimated 910 million yen, or about $5.7 million, on its opening day, a record for a live-action film in Japan that beat the mark held by Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets. Its running total in Japan has reached about $11.8 million since Tuesday.

The near-simultaneous launch is itself a first. The film landed in North America just three days after its Japanese opening, the closest the two markets have ever been for a Japanese entry in the series. Director Takashi Yamazaki returns after winning an Academy Award for the visual effects of Godzilla Minus One, and the sequel has performed far above the distributor's projected opening in the mid-$20 millions.

The wider weekend is strong too. Industry estimates put the total North American box office at about $114 million, which would make it the strongest opening weekend of November since the COVID-19 pandemic. Warner Bros.' The Cat in the Hat is second with around $18 million to $20 million, and Angel Studios' Drummer Boy is third with about $8.8 million, both also earning A CinemaScores.

For Toho and its North American distributor GKIDS, the opening is a validation of a strategy that treats a subtitled Japanese blockbuster as a mainstream theatrical event rather than a specialty release. Godzilla Minus One proved in 2023 that American audiences would turn out for a Japanese-language monster movie. Godzilla Minus Zero proves they will turn out in far greater numbers, pay premium prices to do it, and come back for more.

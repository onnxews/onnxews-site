Belgian King Rejects De Wever's Resignation and Sends Him Back to the Budget Table

King Philippe refused to accept the prime minister's resignation on Friday and ordered the five-party coalition back to work on a 10 billion euro budget deal, with a report due November 16.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Belgium's King Philippe rejected Prime Minister Bart De Wever's resignation on Friday and told him to get back to work on the country's budget, ending — for now — a four-day political crisis that had left the government in limbo.

The Royal Palace said in a statement on Friday morning that, following discussions with the leaders of the five parties in the governing coalition, the king had decided to reject the resignation and asked De Wever to resume budget negotiations immediately. De Wever is due to report back to the king on November 16.

De Wever, leader of the New Flemish Alliance (N-VA), had handed in his resignation on Monday after budget talks inside the five-party "Arizona" coalition collapsed. The coalition could not agree on a budget plan for 2027 built around roughly 10 billion euros in spending reductions and tax measures to be delivered by 2029.

Rather than accept or reject the resignation at once, the king said he was withholding his decision and spent the week consulting the leaders of all five coalition parties — the N-VA, the Reform Movement (MR), Les Engagés, Vooruit, and the Flemish Christian Democrats (CD&V). The palace said each leader told the king they wanted to conclude the budget negotiations as quickly as possible and gave a formal commitment that their parties were prepared to make concessions to reach an agreement.

The stakes are high. Belgium's budget deficit stood at 5.2 percent of gross domestic product in 2025, and government debt reached 108 percent of GDP, according to Eurostat — well above the European Union's fiscal limits. Without an agreed budget, Belgium faces pressure from Brussels and from bond markets, and EU fiscal rules require a credible path back toward deficit targets.

The central dispute is how to close a fiscal gap of about 10 billion euros — roughly $11.2 billion — by 2029, and how to share the burden between spending cuts and tax increases. The five parties have been divided on both questions for weeks, and the talks that collapsed on Monday were the latest in a series of failed attempts to bridge the gap.

De Wever, who took office in February 2025, had described the breakdown bluntly to members of parliament, calling it "a terrible failure on the part of the government" as he announced he would submit his resignation. He had previously ruled out starting the new year with an emergency budget, but this week confirmed the country would have no choice but to fall back on one again if no deal is reached.

The king's decision buys the coalition a narrow window, not a resolution. The five party leaders have pledged to make concessions, but they still have to agree on the same 10 billion euros that defeated them on Monday. If the talks fail again, the options the king weighed this week — asking De Wever to try again, seeking a new prime minister, or early elections — will be back on the table.

For now, the government continues in office while the negotiations resume. All eyes are on November 16, when De Wever returns to the palace to report on whether the coalition has found the deal that eluded it this week.

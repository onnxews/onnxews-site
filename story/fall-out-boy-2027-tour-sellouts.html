Fall Out Boy Sells Out Stadium Shows Worldwide as Twilight of the Punks Tour Nears

The band's 2027 world tour has sold out its London stadium, its Chicago homecoming, and the entire UK and Ireland run, with Boston, New York, and Berlin now running low.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Fall Out Boy has sold out stadium shows around the world on its Twilight of the Punks World Tour, the band's biggest global tour ever, which runs across Europe and North America in 2027. The tour, announced on November 9 alongside the band's new single "Aces," will play 36 dates in its 25th anniversary year.

The sellouts include some of the biggest venues on the tour. Soldier Field in Chicago, the band's hometown, sold out for the July 24 show, and Tottenham Hotspur Stadium in London sold out for June 5. The entire UK and Ireland run is gone, including Glasgow, Dublin, and two nights in Manchester, with the second Manchester date added after the first sold out and then selling out as well.

Other shows are close to selling out. Fenway Park in Boston, Citi Field in New York City, and the Zitadelle in Berlin are all reported to be running low on tickets, a sign that demand is strong across the whole tour rather than concentrated in a few cities.

The tour is split into two legs. The European and UK leg runs from May 17 to June 13, opening at the O2 Arena in Prague and closing at the Atlas Arena in Lodz, Poland, with A Day to Remember and Neck Deep supporting. The North American leg runs from July 13 to August 26, with 21 stadium shows from Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas, to Providence Park in Portland, Oregon, supported on most nights by The Offspring, The Story So Far, and Good Charlotte.

Presales began on November 11, with general sales following on November 12 in most cities. The speed of the sellouts is notable given that the tour was only announced 10 days ago, and that the band has not released a new album in several years.

The tour announcement came with "Aces," the band's first new original single in more than three years, produced by Neil Avron. A second single, "Doomsday Generation (Slacker For The Ages)," featuring Walter Schreifels, was released on November 13. The band also surprised fans with a pop-up performance at the Koreatown Plaza in Los Angeles on November 10, where they handed out VHS tapes.

Fall Out Boy's staying power is backed up by the numbers. The band has now earned two diamond certifications from the Recording Industry Association of America, for "Sugar, We're Goin Down" and "Centuries," each of which has sold more than 10 million units in the United States. Few rock bands of their generation have that kind of catalogue.

The tour's name, Twilight of the Punks, and its stadium scale mark a step up for a band that has spent much of the last decade playing arenas. Stadiums are a different test, and the sellouts so far suggest the band's audience has grown rather than shrunk since its last tour.

For fans who missed out, the remaining shows in Boston, New York, and Berlin are the last chance to see the tour in some of its biggest markets. With the UK and Ireland run already gone, the band's 25th anniversary year is shaping up to be its biggest yet.

Why it matters: This is a rare story in rock music: a band 25 years into its career selling out stadiums on announcement, with no new album to promote. The sellouts in Chicago, London, and across the UK and Ireland show that Fall Out Boy's audience has not faded but grown, and the two diamond singles underline how deep the catalogue runs. The tour also shows how the live business has changed, with legacy acts now able to fill stadiums that were once reserved for pop stars. If the remaining shows in Boston, New York, and Berlin sell out too, the Twilight of the Punks tour will be one of the biggest rock tours of 2027.

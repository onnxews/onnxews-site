Johnny Depp's Ebenezer Tops Box Office With $19 Million Opening

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer opens at No. 1 with an estimated $19 million domestically and $42 million worldwide, giving the actor his first chart-topping opening in eight years.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Johnny Depp is back on top of the North American box office. His new film Ebenezer, Paramount's gothic take on Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol, opened at No. 1 this weekend with an estimated $19 million in ticket sales across the United States and Canada, according to Sunday estimates from Rentrak. It is the first film starring Depp to open at No. 1 since 2018's Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald, and his first major studio movie since that same year, making the result the most closely watched box office test of his attempted Hollywood comeback.

The opening weekend finished just short of the $20 million mark, but the wider picture was stronger. Ebenezer added $23 million from international markets, bringing its worldwide opening to $42 million, which already exceeds its reported $38 million production budget. Its modest budget, low by the standards of a holiday studio release, means the bar for a respectable run is far lower than for a $150 million blockbuster. All figures are Sunday estimates as of November 16.

Directed by Ti West, best known for the horror franchise X, Ebenezer casts Depp as the miser Ebenezer Scrooge in a dark, supernatural retelling of Dickens' Christmas classic, rated PG-13. The film leans into the holiday season, arriving in theatres on November 13 with weeks of Christmas moviegoing still ahead of it, and its marketing leaned directly into Depp's return, with the first trailer running through his most iconic roles, from Jack Sparrow to the Mad Hatter, before his Scrooge declared it was good to be back.

Audiences liked the film considerably more than critics did. Moviegoers polled by CinemaScore gave Ebenezer an average grade of A-minus, the same grade Depp's versions of Alice in Wonderland and Charlie and the Chocolate Factory received, while reviews from critics were mixed. That split between audience enthusiasm and critical scepticism is a familiar pattern for holiday releases, which often build through word of mouth and repeat family viewing rather than opening-weekend reviews.

The result carries weight because Depp's career has spent years in the shadow of his legal battles. After he lost a libel case in Britain in 2020 against the newspaper The Sun, he was dropped from his role as the villain Grindelwald in the Fantastic Beasts films, with Mads Mikkelsen taking over the part. In 2022 he won a defamation lawsuit against his ex-wife Amber Heard in the United States, after a high-profile six-week trial in Virginia in which the jury sided with Depp on three of his claims and with Heard on one of her counterclaims. Ebenezer is his first big studio movie since that trial.

The economics of the opening look workable even if the headline number was not spectacular. JoBlo estimated that once marketing costs are factored in, Ebenezer probably needs to do around $150 million worldwide to break even, a target its holiday positioning makes reachable if it holds through Thanksgiving and December. A Christmas-themed film has a longer runway than most releases, with school holidays and family outings keeping it in play for weeks, and its $42 million global start already covers the production budget.

Elsewhere on the chart, the weekend was quiet. The Cat in the Hat held second place with $11.4 million in its second weekend, a drop of 43 percent, taking its domestic total to $38 million. Last weekend's champion, Godzilla Minus Zero, fell sharply, losing 73 percent of its audience to take $8.31 million, though its worldwide total is closing in on $90 million. Ramayana continued its strong run with $5.5 million, pushing its domestic total past $24 million and its worldwide total to $147.5 million.

The rest of the top ten underlines how soft the overall market was, with the Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything taking $5.47 million and Ghost Soldier and How to Rob a Bank both opening to just over $5 million.

For Depp personally, the opening answers the first and most important question about his comeback, which is whether he can still open a studio movie. JoBlo called the $19 million start more than decent for a star-anchored film, and the A-minus CinemaScore suggests the people who did show up left happy. His next test comes in March, when Day Drinker, a thriller co-starring Madelyn Cline and Penélope Cruz, opens in theatres. If that film performs similarly, the argument that Depp is once again a reliable box office draw becomes much harder to dismiss. For now, a first No. 1 in eight years is exactly the headline Paramount and Depp would have wanted.

Why it matters: This opening matters because it is the first hard evidence of whether Johnny Depp can still carry a studio film after years defined by courtrooms rather than box office. A $19 million debut is modest by blockbuster standards, but against a $38 million budget and with a $42 million global start already past the production cost, the film does not need a smash to succeed, it needs a steady holiday run. The A-minus audience score suggests word of mouth will help it leg out through December, and the result will shape how studios value Depp going forward: one No. 1 does not erase the last eight years, but it gives his next film, Day Drinker, a far warmer reception than a flop would have. For Paramount, the bet on a low-budget Christmas title looks shrewd either way.

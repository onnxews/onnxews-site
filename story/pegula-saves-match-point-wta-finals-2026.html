Pegula Saves Match Point to Beat Noskova and Reach WTA Finals Semifinals

Jessica Pegula won the final three games to beat Linda Noskova 7–6, 4–6, 7–5 in Indian Wells, knocking out Coco Gauff and booking a semifinal against Mirra Andreeva.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Jessica Pegula saved a match point and won the final three games to beat Linda Noskova 7–6 (2), 4–6, 7–5 on Thursday, November 12, advancing to the semifinals of the WTA Finals in Indian Wells.

The match lasted two hours and 45 minutes, and it ended with Noskova holding a match point on Pegula's serve at 4–5 in the third set. Pegula double-faulted to hand her opponent the chance — then held serve, broke Noskova in the next game and closed out the match when a net cord fell back on Noskova's side on her first match point.

Pegula is the first player to win a WTA Finals match after saving a match point since Simona Halep in 2019, and the win put her into a third WTA Finals semifinal, after 2023 and 2025 — and her first after losing her opening round-robin match.

Her serve was the difference. Pegula fired a career-high 16 aces, including one that tied the third set at 5–5, beating her previous best of 14, set against Noskova in Berlin. "I feel like no one's really given me credit for that stat," Pegula said afterwards. "I've turned it around this year."

The result decided Group 1. Noskova finished first at 2–1 with a 5–2 sets record, Pegula second at 2–1 with five sets won to four for Coco Gauff, who was eliminated despite beating Marta Kostyuk 6–4, 7–5 earlier on Thursday. Kostyuk finished 0–3.

Gauff, Pegula's former doubles partner, had beaten Pegula in three sets in their opening match of the event, and her win over Kostyuk — in which she rallied to take the final six games — turned out to be her last match of the year.

Pegula will face Mirra Andreeva in the semifinals in the first meeting between the two on the WTA Tour. It is the fourth-largest age gap between semifinalists in the history of the event.

Noskova, the Wimbledon champion, had already secured her semifinal place before the match and will face the winner of Friday's match between Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina.

The WTA Finals, with a $10 million prize pool, are being staged at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden.

Pegula's escape was the kind of win that can change a tournament: she was one point from going home, and instead she is two wins from the title, with a serve that is suddenly the best weapon in her game, according to the WTA.

The second set showed how close the match was. Noskova took it 6–4 in 50 minutes, and Pegula converted just one of seven break points in that set — a stat that explains why a match she eventually won felt, for long stretches, like one she was losing. The third set turned on the serve: 16 aces, a career high, and one at 5–5 that kept her alive.

Gauff's exit had its own drama. She was dealing with a back problem that had stiffened up in her first match and bothered her on serves and forehands, needed visits from a trainer and a painkiller, and still rallied to win the final six games against Kostyuk. It was not enough: with Pegula and Noskova both finishing 2–1, the sets tiebreak went against her, and her season ended on a night she won.

Why it matters: Pegula's win matters because of how it happened and what it decided: a match point saved, a career-high 16 aces, and a group decided on sets won, with Gauff eliminated despite finishing 2–1. It is also a late-career statement from a player who lost her opening match and still found a way through — her third WTA Finals semifinal, and her first after such a start. What to watch is Saturday's semifinal against Andreeva, a first meeting between two players at very different stages of their careers, and whether Pegula's serve holds up under the same pressure.

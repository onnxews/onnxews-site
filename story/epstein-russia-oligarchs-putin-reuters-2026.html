Reuters Investigation: Epstein Courted Russian Oligarchs for Years in a Failed Bid to Meet Putin

A new Reuters investigation finds Jeffrey Epstein drew at least 17 women from the former Soviet Union into his sex-trafficking network and spent years cultivating Russian elites — but found no evidence he worked for Russian intelligence or ever met Putin.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Jeffrey Epstein spent years courting Russian oligarchs and senior Kremlin officials in a bid to secure a meeting with President Vladimir Putin, a new Reuters investigation published on Friday has found — a campaign that began with an obsession with young Russian women and grew into a bid for influence among Russia's elite. The investigation found no evidence that Epstein worked for Russian intelligence, acted on behalf of the Russian government, or ever succeeded in meeting Putin.

Reuters reporters interviewed more than two dozen people familiar with Epstein's ties to Russia, including nine who knew him personally, and reviewed hundreds of pages of U.S. Justice Department records, flight logs, court filings and emails. The investigation identified at least 17 women from the former Soviet Union who were drawn into Epstein's sex-trafficking network, including three of the hundreds who have sought justice for his abuse and exploitation.

Epstein's embrace of Russia began with its young models. After the Berlin Wall fell in 1989, Western scouts descended on the former Soviet bloc in search of modeling talent. Among them was French fashion scout Jean-Luc Brunel, who crisscrossed Eastern Europe to recruit girls and young women and bring them to Paris, where American agencies selected talent for the North American market. In September 1991, Brunel hosted a party at his Paris apartment, where former model Thysia Huisman recalled a Russian girl who appeared to be in her early teens spending much of the evening on Epstein's lap. 'It was creepy,' Huisman told Reuters. 'The girl said modeling work was not good, but Jeffrey takes care of me.' Reuters could not independently confirm the party or the identity and age of the girl.

Epstein made only two known trips to Russia. The first came in April 1998, when he visited Sarov, the secretive 'closed city' where the Soviet Union developed much of its nuclear weapons program, as part of a visit led by Microsoft executive Nathan Myhrvold. The second came in November 2002, when he flew to Russia on his private jet with Ghislaine Maxwell and Brunel, supposedly to see the famous mauve room inside St. Petersburg's Alexander Palace, which an interior designer had urged him to copy in his Manhattan townhouse. During a layover in Moscow, the group rode in bulletproof cars with armed bodyguards to dine with Maxwell's friend Umar Dzhabrailov, a wealthy Chechen businessman.

By the mid-1990s, Epstein had amassed millions advising Les Wexner, the billionaire behind Victoria's Secret, and his Manhattan townhouse had become a hub for celebrities and billionaires. Modeling agencies fed a circle of wealthy men around Little St. James, his private island in the U.S. Virgin Islands, where prosecutors said he trafficked and abused women and girls. In 2008, Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida to prostitution charges, including soliciting an underage girl, and spent 13 months in jail.

After that conviction, Reuters found, Epstein increasingly saw Russia as a pathway back into elite society. From New York, Palm Beach and his island, he cultivated Russian connections while continuing to recruit women for sex. In 2010, he hosted billionaire property developer Vladislav Doronin at his New York residence to discuss a Moscow real estate project. He also sought ties with aluminum magnate Oleg Deripaska through former British minister Peter Mandelson, and tried to use former deputy economy minister Sergey Belyakov to reach senior Russian leaders. In an April 2015 email to former Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak, Epstein urged him to raise the matter of a Putin meeting.

Many of the central characters are now dead. Epstein died in August 2019, a death ruled a suicide. Brunel died in a Paris jail cell in 2022 while under investigation. Dzhabrailov was found in March this year with a fatal gunshot wound to his head in his Moscow apartment, in what police sources called a suspected suicide. Relatives of Epstein and Dzhabrailov have disputed the suicide assessments, but no conclusive evidence of foul play has surfaced. Maxwell is serving a 20-year federal prison sentence in Texas for helping Epstein sexually abuse teenage girls.

On the long-speculated question of ties to Soviet or Russian intelligence, perhaps through Robert Maxwell, Ghislaine Maxwell's father, Reuters found no evidence. Maxwell told Justice Department officials she could 'categorically' attest that Epstein and her father never met. Putin's office did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment. Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse, a Democrat, captured the enduring mystery in a Senate speech in March: 'Much about Epstein remains unknown. And over and over, it touches Russia.'

The findings arrive amid the continuing fight over the Epstein files in Washington. A year after Congress forced the Trump administration to release documents from the government's investigations, about three million pages remain withheld, and a judge has ordered the Justice Department to publish handwritten FBI interview notes by Monday, Nov. 23. The files have already produced a leak of more than 15 million Deutsche Bank documents. Wexner has denied any knowledge of the abuse, telling the House Oversight Committee in February that he was 'duped by a world-class con man.' White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said Trump has been 'totally exonerated' on Epstein matters, noting he later barred Epstein from Mar-a-Lago as 'a creep.'

The investigation matters because it replaces speculation with evidence on one of the most conspiracy-laden corners of the Epstein story. For years, the lack of hard facts about his Russian connections fueled theories that he was an intelligence asset. Reuters finds a different story: a predator who used Russia first for victims and then for reinvention, cultivating oligarchs and officials from afar, without ever reaching the Kremlin's inner circle. For Epstein's victims, who continue to seek accountability, the report also documents how the machinery of his abuse — the modeling pipeline, the introductions, the money — kept running for years after his conviction should have ended it.

Why it matters: The Reuters investigation is significant precisely because of what it does not find. After years of speculation that Epstein was a Russian intelligence asset — speculation fed by his visits to a secret nuclear city, his cultivation of oligarchs and his pursuit of Putin — the evidence points to something more banal and more damning: a wealthy predator who treated Russia as a source of victims and then as a ladder back into polite society. That distinction matters for the files fight now raging in Washington, where three million pages remain withheld and a judge's Monday deadline for the FBI's handwritten interview notes is about to test whether the Justice Department will keep fighting disclosure.

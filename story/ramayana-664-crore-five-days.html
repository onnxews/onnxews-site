Ramayana Nears 664 Crore Rupees Worldwide in Five Days After a Record-Breaking Monday

Nitesh Tiwari's epic posted the biggest first Monday in Indian cinema history, passing Dhurandhar 2's record, as it races toward the 1,000-crore mark.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ramayana: Part 1 has collected nearly 664 crore rupees worldwide within just five days of its theatrical release, according to trade estimates, continuing one of the biggest opening runs in the history of Indian cinema. The film, directed by Nitesh Tiwari and starring Ranbir Kapoor and Yash, has maintained its momentum after Diwali, setting a new record for the highest first-Monday collection by an Indian film.

According to the trade tracker Sacnilk, the film earned 107.50 crore rupees net in India on Monday, November 9, its fifth day in theatres, across 23,911 shows. That figure makes it the biggest first Monday ever for an Indian film, overtaking the Monday earnings of Ranveer Singh's Dhurandhar 2. The Monday surge was especially striking because it came the day after Diwali, when box office usually cools, and it represented a jump of more than 90 percent from the previous day's collections.

The five-day breakdown shows a film performing strongly in every market. The worldwide gross stands at about 663.90 crore rupees, made up of 413.90 crore rupees gross in India, or 346.35 crore rupees net, and about 250 crore rupees from overseas markets. Hindi-language collections alone reached 260 crore rupees net in five days, with Kannada, Telugu, Tamil and Malayalam versions adding to the total. The film also delivered the biggest single day of ticket sales on the BookMyShow platform, with about 16.60 lakh tickets sold on Monday, passing Dhurandhar 2's record of 16.40 lakh.

The scale of the production helps explain the stakes. Ramayana: Part 1 was made on a reported budget of around 2,000 crore rupees, making it one of the most expensive Indian films ever made, and it still has a considerable distance to cover before it recovers its production cost through theatrical revenue alone. The next benchmark is Dhurandhar 2, which completed its theatrical run with 1,813.39 crore rupees worldwide. Ramayana has already earned more than half of Dhurandhar 2's lifetime overseas collection in its first five days alone.

The film's success is not limited to India. It collected 2.8 million dollars at the box office in Britain and Ireland, where it beat Godzilla Minus Zero, and it also became the top-performing film at the Bangkok box office. Globally, it made 52.43 million dollars in its opening weekend, the second-biggest weekend in the world behind Godzilla Minus Zero. The international numbers matter because the film was designed from the start as a global release, with a scale and a visual ambition meant to travel beyond Indian audiences.

Directed by Nitesh Tiwari and produced by Namit Malhotra and Yash, the film features Ranbir Kapoor as Lord Rama, Yash as Ravana, Sai Pallavi as Sita and Ravi Dubey as Lakshman. The first instalment arrived in theatres on November 5, just before Diwali, and the timing was deliberate: the festival holiday is the biggest moviegoing period of the year in India, and a film of this size needs every advantage it can get. The strategy has paid off, with packed houses reported across the country through the holiday weekend and beyond.

The film also has a favourable theatrical window ahead of it. There are nearly seven weeks before Shah Rukh Khan's King arrives on December 24, giving Ramayana a long, clear run at the box office with little direct competition. Trade analysts will be watching the second weekend closely, because the size of the drop from the opening days to the second weekend is usually the best guide to how far a film can ultimately go. A strong hold would put the 1,000-crore mark well within reach.

There is also news about the sequel. The second part was initially expected to release around Diwali next year, but reports now suggest its release has been postponed to 2028. The delay may reflect the scale of the production, or a decision to give the first film room to breathe, but it means audiences will have to wait longer for the story's conclusion. For now, the focus is on the first instalment, which has already delivered more than its makers could have hoped for in five days.

What happens next is a question of endurance. The opening numbers are historic, but the biggest films are defined by their second and third weeks, not their first five days. If Ramayana holds its audience through the coming weekends, it will challenge the all-time records that have stood for years. If it fades, it will still be remembered as one of the great Diwali openings. Either way, five days in, it has already changed the conversation about what Indian cinema can achieve on a global stage.

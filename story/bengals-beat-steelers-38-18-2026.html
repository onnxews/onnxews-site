Burrow Throws for 409 Yards and Four Touchdowns as Bengals Rout Steelers 38-18

Joe Burrow passed for 409 yards and four touchdowns, Ja'Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards, and the Cincinnati Bengals beat the Pittsburgh Steelers 38-18 on Sunday Night Football to take sole possession of first place in the AFC North.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Cincinnati Bengals made a statement on Sunday Night Football, dominating the Pittsburgh Steelers 38-18 at Paycor Stadium to move into sole possession of first place in the AFC North. The Bengals improved to 7-2 with their fifth consecutive victory, while the Steelers fell to 6-3 and dropped into the wild-card race.

Quarterback Joe Burrow delivered one of the finest performances of his career, completing 35 of 44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns with no interceptions and a 135.7 passer rating. Burrow had 302 passing yards and three touchdowns by halftime alone, topping 300 yards in the first half for just the second time in his NFL career, and he was lifted for Joe Flacco midway through the fourth quarter with the game decided.

It was the 10th 400-yard passing game of Burrow's seven-year career and his third this season, and the 409 yards were tied for the sixth most Pittsburgh has ever allowed to a quarterback. The performance also extended a remarkable run: Burrow has now thrown four or more total touchdowns in five consecutive games, tying Peyton Manning's NFL record.

Wide receiver Ja'Marr Chase was Burrow's favorite target all night, catching 11 passes on 14 targets for 231 yards, including a 53-yard grab. It was the fourth game of Chase's career with at least 200 receiving yards. Running back Chase Brown added two receiving touchdowns, catching eight passes for 39 yards while rushing 10 times for 44 yards.

Cincinnati seized control from the start and never let go. The Bengals led 24-3 at halftime, scoring on four of their five first-half possessions, with touchdown passes to Brown, Drew Sample and rookie Dohnte Meyers. The only blemish in the opening 30 minutes was a missed 43-yard field goal by Evan McPherson that ended his run of 25 consecutive makes.

Pittsburgh's offense, by contrast, never got going until the game was out of reach. Quarterback Aaron Rodgers, 42, did not complete his first pass until his eighth attempt and was sacked three times by the Bengals' defense. He finished with two fourth-quarter touchdown passes, to DK Metcalf and Pat Freiermuth, but by then Cincinnati led by 27 points. Running back Jaylen Warren, who entered the game sixth in the league in scrimmage yards, was held to 15 yards on seven carries.

The Steelers had beaten the Bengals 30-27 in Week 3, but the rematch was one-sided from the opening drive. Cincinnati outgained Pittsburgh 143-3 in the first quarter, and the Bengals' offensive line gave Burrow time to pick apart the Steelers' secondary all night. Pittsburgh struggled in all three phases until the fourth quarter, when its two late touchdowns only made the final score more respectable.

The final score made NFL history. According to the Cincinnati Enquirer, citing CBS Sports, 38-18 had never occurred in a league game before, making it the 1,100th unique final score in NFL history and the second scorigami of the day, following San Francisco's 46-40 win over Dallas earlier Sunday. It was also the Bengals' second scorigami in three weeks, after their 43-36 home win over Tennessee on Nov. 1 produced the 1,098th unique final score. Cincinnati's defense deserves credit alongside the offense. The Bengals kept the Steelers out of the end zone until the fourth quarter, sacking Rodgers three times, The win also marked the first time in four chances that a team playing the week after a trip to Europe beat an opponent coming off a bye.

The Bengals now turn their attention to a Monday Night Football trip to Washington on Nov. 23, while the Steelers travel to Philadelphia next Sunday looking to steady themselves after a defeat that exposed the gap between the two rivals.

Why it matters: The victory gives Cincinnati outright control of the AFC North at 7-2, a full game clear of Pittsburgh, and puts the Bengals in the No. 4 seed in the AFC playoff picture as of Sunday night, with a No. 1 seed still in reach if Burrow keeps playing at this level. Five straight wins mark Cincinnati as a genuine Super Bowl contender rather than just a division leader. For Pittsburgh, the loss drops the Steelers to the No. 2 wild-card spot and raises real questions about whether an aging roster can keep pace with the conference's elite, with a trip to Philadelphia next on the schedule.

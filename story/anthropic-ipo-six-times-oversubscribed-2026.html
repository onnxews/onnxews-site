Anthropic IPO Orders Run Six Times the Shares on Offer, With Existing Backers First in Line

The AI startup's blockbuster listing is about six times oversubscribed, and bankers are telling new buyers to expect smaller allocations than they asked for.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Anthropic's initial public offering is about six times oversubscribed, with orders running roughly six times the number of shares available, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. The figure puts a number on the demand that has built around the year's biggest tech listing — and it comes with a catch for buyers who were not already on the company's cap table.

Bankers have told prospective investors that Anthropic will prioritize existing backers, so some new buyers are likely to receive smaller allocations than they requested, according to people familiar with the matter. The details could still change, and Anthropic could not immediately be reached for comment on the reported demand. The oversubscription is the latest signal that the offering, which is already the largest AI listing ever attempted, is running hot.

The terms of the deal have been public for days. Anthropic is selling 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 apiece, which would raise up to $64.4 billion, plus a separate $26 billion private placement that could take the combined total to $90.4 billion. The fully diluted valuation at the top of the range would be about $1.4 trillion — below the near-$2 trillion figure that had circulated earlier, but still one of the largest valuations ever attached to a public debut.

Most of the shares on offer are new stock. About 801.4 million of the 825 million shares are being sold by the company itself, with the remaining 23.6 million sold by existing shareholders, who keep the proceeds of their own sales. The company gets no money from the secondary shares. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering alongside 20 other banks.

The pricing is expected on November 18, with trading to begin the next day, November 19, on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. That leaves a week of market risk between now and the first trade — a week in which the AI sector has already been shaken by Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia, one of Anthropic's biggest backers.

The demand is built on a growth story that Anthropic's filings support. The company's annualized revenue run rate reached $81 billion as of October 31, up from $71 billion at the end of September and $65 billion in July — a measure that annualizes trailing 28-day consumption fees plus one month of subscription revenue. At a $1.4 trillion valuation, the company would be priced at about 17 times that run rate. For the nine months ended September 30, revenue was about $33.2 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to $1.3 billion from $5.2 billion a year earlier.

The private placement is as revealing as the public offering. Nvidia and Google have each agreed to buy about $10 billion of shares in the private placement, and AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC have also committed to buying in. The commitments mean that the world's biggest chipmakers and cloud companies are all, in effect, buying a piece of the AI company whose models run on their hardware — and that they have a financial interest in its success beyond their commercial relationships with it.

The Nvidia commitment is now under a cloud. Huang resigned on Thursday after an affair scandal, and the board of the company that is supposed to buy $10 billion of Anthropic stock is in the middle of a leadership crisis. There is no indication the commitment has changed, but the timing is awkward: the same week Anthropic is asking the market for $90 billion, its biggest chip supplier is in turmoil.

Separately, Moneycontrol reported that JonesTrading was offering institutional buyers a block of up to $500 million of pre-IPO shares at a markup of 3 to 5 percent over the final IPO price. Grey-market premiums are a common feature of hot offerings, and they suggest that investors who cannot get an allocation in the primary deal are willing to pay up for exposure before trading begins.

Orbit reported on Wednesday that demand for the Anthropic IPO had already swamped the order book, with bankers weighing pricing above the range. What Thursday's reporting adds is the scale: six times the shares on offer, with existing backers first in line. The question now is whether that demand survives the next week of market volatility — and whether a $1.4 trillion valuation for a company that lost $1.3 billion in the first nine months of the year holds up when the stock starts trading.

Why it matters: This is a sequential update to Orbit's November 12 report that Anthropic IPO demand had swamped the order book — the new information is the quantified six-times figure and the allocation priority for existing backers. It matters because the offering is the largest AI listing ever and a test of whether the market will pay $1.4 trillion for a company still losing money. The Nvidia turmoil the same week adds real risk to a deal whose biggest private-placement buyer is in the middle of a leadership crisis.

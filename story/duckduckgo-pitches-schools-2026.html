DuckDuckGo Pitches Schools an AI-Free Search After Google's AI Bundle Backlash

After Google said schools that switch off its AI also lose Search, DuckDuckGo is courting districts with an AI-free option and an offer to help them switch.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

DuckDuckGo is pitching itself to schools after Google created an opening for the alternative search engine, TechCrunch reported on Tuesday.

Google told schools this week they could turn off Gemini, its AI assistant, but said shutting off its other AI features would cost schools Google Search as well. There is no version of Search without AI Overviews or AI Mode, Google confirmed, so a school that rejects AI entirely also loses student Search access.

On X, DuckDuckGo founder and CEO Gabriel Weinberg pointed to the company's documentation explaining how schools can switch their default search engine to DuckDuckGo, adding "get in touch for help." Chief public affairs officer Kamyl Bazbaz wrote: "Forcing AI on school children: how a monopolist behaves when they think people are powerless. Prove Google wrong."

DuckDuckGo, which focuses on user privacy by limiting data collection, sees a moment to make inroads in education, where Google has historically dominated with roughly 50 million Chromebooks and a classroom platform that reaches over 150 million users worldwide. DuckDuckGo holds about 1.7 percent of U.S. search against Google's 85.45 percent, according to StatCounter.

DuckDuckGo is not anti-AI: it offers its own AI features, including a chat interface called Duck.ai and an AI search assistant. Unlike Google, it provides options to disable those features in its search and browser settings, and it offers a dedicated site, noai.duckduckgo.com, where AI-assisted answers, AI-generated images and AI feature suggestions are all turned off. No school district adoption has been announced.

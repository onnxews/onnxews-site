Valve Opens Direct Sales of the 512GB Steam Machine as Memory Costs Squeeze the 2TB Model

The entry-level Steam Machine can now be bought outright while supplies last, but Valve has closed the 2TB waitlist to new signups and says it does not know how many more units it can build or what they will cost.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Valve has reshaped how players buy its Steam Machine, opening direct purchases of the 512GB model while effectively shutting the door on new buyers of the 2TB version. In an update posted on November 19, the company said it had fulfilled all current reservations for the 512GB model and sent purchase invitations to everyone who reserved a 2TB machine, but warned that component shortages and rising memory and storage costs leave the future of the device uncertain.

The 512GB Steam Machine is now listed for direct purchase on the Steam store while supplies last, with an estimated delivery time of six to ten business days. Valve says that once the current inventory runs out, the listing will flip to out of stock rather than opening a new waitlist, because the company does not know how many more units it will be able to make. The 512GB model costs 1,049 dollars, or 879 pounds in the United Kingdom, with a bundle including the new Steam Controller at 1,128 dollars.

The picture is much tighter for the 2TB model, which costs 1,349 dollars on its own. Valve has finished sending purchase invitations to everyone holding a 2TB reservation, but the number of people on the waitlist exceeds current production, so the waitlist has now been closed to new signups. Those already waiting keep their place in line, but Valve stresses that a waitlist spot does not guarantee a unit. Anyone on the 2TB waitlist can buy the available 512GB model instead, but doing so means leaving the 2TB queue.

The cause is the same memory crisis rippling across the whole PC hardware market. Valve said it is continuing to encounter supply constraints for hardware components, and that memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels. In its own words, the company does not yet know the number of Steam Machines it is able to make, or at what price it will be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained, because it needs twice the storage in a market where storage prices keep climbing.

Valve has not ruled out a price increase. The Steam Machine already launched at a higher price than first planned because of RAM and SSD price hikes, and the company says it anticipated having more information to share in January, when it will update players on availability and pricing. For now it says it will keep building units, but in smaller quantities than planned for the 2TB model, and it is not committing to any price for future production.

The machine itself is a roughly six-inch cube that pairs a six-core AMD Zen 4 processor with an RDNA 3 graphics chip, 16GB of DDR5 memory and 8GB of dedicated graphics memory, running Valve's SteamOS. It launched in June 2026 after a delay from its intended early 2026 release, caused by the same shortages in memory and storage that are now squeezing supply again. Early expectations had put the price at around 750 dollars, so the launch prices of 1,049 dollars for the 512GB model and 1,349 dollars for the 2TB model already surprised buyers.

Valve's troubles are part of a wider pattern. The same memory and storage price surge has hit the Steam Deck, which has also faced shortages, and component makers across the industry have warned that prices will stay high. For a living-room PC that was meant to bring Steam into the lounge at a console-like price, the economics have moved in the wrong direction since launch, and Valve is now managing scarcity rather than growth.

For buyers, the choice is now stark. The 512GB model is available to add to a cart and pay for right now, while supplies last, according to PC Guide's report on Valve's update. The 2TB model is out of stock, its waitlist is closed, and nobody waiting for one knows when, or at what price, more will arrive. Valve's next word on the subject is not expected until January 2027, which leaves the holiday season as a test of whether the 512GB stock lasts.

The update also raises a question about Valve's hardware ambitions more broadly. The Steam Machine was the company's biggest hardware bet since the Steam Deck, and its launch was already shaped by the memory crisis. If costs keep rising into 2027, Valve will have to choose between raising prices again, accepting smaller production runs, or redesigning around cheaper components. None of those options is attractive, and the company's silence on which path it will take is itself a signal of how uncertain the component market has become.

Why it matters: Valve's update is a small story about one machine and a big story about the memory crisis. The 512GB model going on direct sale is genuinely good news for buyers who have waited since June, but the rest of the announcement is a warning: Valve cannot say how many more Steam Machines it can build or what they will cost, and the 2TB model is effectively frozen. That is the same squeeze hitting every PC maker, but it lands harder on Valve because the Steam Machine was priced as a console-like living-room box and its margins were thin to begin with. Watch the January update closely, because if Valve raises prices again, it will confirm that the memory crisis is now setting the price of gaming hardware, not the manufacturers.

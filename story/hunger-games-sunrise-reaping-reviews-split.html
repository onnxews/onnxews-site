Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping Draws Split Reviews Ahead of Friday Release

Critics are split on the sixth Hunger Games film, praising newcomer Joseph Zada as Haymitch while faulting a dragging middle and a rushed ending.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, the sixth film in the franchise, has drawn split reviews from critics ahead of its release in theaters on Friday, November 20, with praise for its young lead and its scale set against complaints about a dragging middle and a rushed ending. Reviews were published on Tuesday, three days before the film opens in the United States and Britain.

The film runs 158 minutes and is rated PG-13 for intense sequences of violence, action and thematic material. It is directed by Francis Lawrence, who has directed every Hunger Games film except the first, and adapts Suzanne Collins's 2025 novel, with a screenplay by Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie. It is set 24 years before the first film and tells the origin story of Haymitch Abernathy, the mentor played by Woody Harrelson in the original series.

Haymitch is played by Joseph Zada, a 21-year-old Australian newcomer, and the reviews agree on one thing: he is the film's biggest asset. The New York Post called him "freakishly good" at recreating Harrelson's voice, while Radio Times, which gave the film four out of five stars, called him "captivating". Mark Kennedy of the Associated Press, who gave the film three out of four stars, called Zada "awesome." He credited screenwriters Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie with crafting an engrossing plot from Suzanne Collins' latest prequel novel, expertly paced by director Francis Lawrence.

The story is the 50th Hunger Games, the Second Quarter Quell, in which twice as many tributes are sent into the arena — 48 young people fighting to survive. Haymitch is selected as punishment for defending his girlfriend, Lenore Dove, and must navigate an arena that Radio Times describes as "a picturesque meadow overlooked by a volcano", where the tributes face "poisonous streams, radioactive pollen" and each other.

Radio Times called the film "a mighty 158-minute spectacle, with the franchise's biggest budget yet" but added: "Flaws remain – a dragging middle, a rushed finale". The New York Post was positive overall, calling the film "narratively much stronger than the last prequel," The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, while noting that the ending wobbles. IGN was warmer still, calling it "one of the best installments in the franchise, almost reaching the emotional heights of its spiritual bookend, Catching Fire." Not every critic agreed: Kevin Maher of The Times gave the film two stars.

The supporting cast is stacked with names. Mckenna Grace plays Maysilee Donner, Jesse Plemons plays a young Plutarch Heavensbee, a role originated by Philip Seymour Hoffman, and Ralph Fiennes plays President Coriolanus Snow, taking over from Tom Blyth in the prior prequel. Glenn Close plays Drusilla Sickle and Kieran Culkin plays Caesar Flickerman, with Elle Fanning, Kelvin Harrison Jr. as Beetee, Ben Wang, Molly McCann and Billy Porter also in the cast. Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson, the stars of the original films, make appearances in the epilogue.

The split verdict is notable for this franchise. No Hunger Games film had ever received a "Rotten" score on Rotten Tomatoes before this one, according to MovieWeb, and the score moved through the day as reviews landed on Tuesday, a sign of how divided critics are. That makes the opening weekend a genuine test: the franchise has a built-in audience, but a mixed critical reception can shape how far the film travels beyond it.

The stakes are real for the studio. The Hunger Games began on screen in 2012 and became one of the defining young-adult franchises of the decade, but the 2023 prequel, The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, was a smaller commercial success than the original series. Sunrise on the Reaping, with the franchise's biggest budget yet, is meant to prove the series still has the pulling power of its peak. The New York Post and Radio Times both published their reviews on Tuesday.

Why it matters: A split verdict is the worst outcome a franchise film can get from critics, because it gives audiences no clear reason to rush out on opening weekend — and this film needs a rush. Sunrise on the Reaping carries the franchise's biggest budget yet and the burden of proving that a prequel series built on a 2025 novel can still fill theaters the way the originals did. The consensus around Joseph Zada is the one clear asset: a breakout lead can carry a film further than a mixed review, and the film's box office will now depend on whether audiences care more about Haymitch's origin story than the critics' complaints about its middle act.

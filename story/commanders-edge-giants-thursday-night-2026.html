Commanders Stun Giants 24–21 on Diggs' Late Touchdown

Jayden Daniels found Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard score with 57 seconds left, and Rasul Douglas sealed it with an interception, as Washington kept its season alive on Thursday Night Football.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Washington Commanders stunned the New York Giants 24–21 on Thursday night, November 12, at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, keeping their season alive in Week 10 of the NFL season.

Jayden Daniels threw a 32-yard touchdown pass to Stefon Diggs with 57 seconds remaining, Diggs catching the ball at the New York 25 and breaking a tackle from Dru Phillips on his way to the end zone. Rasul Douglas then intercepted J.J. McCarthy's pass at the Washington 11 with 25 seconds left to seal the win.

The game was scoreless through the first quarter, played in the aftermath of fall thunderstorms that had briefly threatened to delay the 8:15 p.m. kickoff. Drew Stevens opened the scoring with a 46-yard field goal in the second quarter, and the teams traded blows from there.

Cam Skattebo capped a 16-play, 72-yard drive with a 1-yard touchdown run with 2:35 left before halftime, and Ben Sinnott caught a 1-yard touchdown pass from Daniels with five seconds left in the half. Antonio Williams' 31-yard touchdown catch put Washington ahead at 6:56 of the third quarter, before Skattebo's 13-yard touchdown run at 10:22 restored the Giants' lead.

Isaiah Likely's 66-yard touchdown catch with 8:13 left gave New York a 21–17 lead, and the Giants looked set to close the game out — until Daniels found Diggs on the final drive.

Daniels finished 23 of 33 for 249 yards and three touchdowns, but also threw his first two interceptions of the season, ending a streak of 207 consecutive passes without a pick. He also rushed 11 times for 28 yards.

Diggs finished with six catches for 83 yards. Sinnott, who came into the game with one catch for six yards all season, had a career night with four catches for 42 yards, including the touchdown.

McCarthy finished 23 of 36 for 293 yards, a career high, with one touchdown and one interception. Likely caught nine passes for 127 yards, Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns, and Malik Nabers' third-quarter fumble killed a promising Giants drive.

The win improved Washington to 3–5–1 and moved the Commanders within two games of the Giants in the loss column. New York fell to 5–3–1 but still leads the NFC East. Washington has now gone 5–0–1 in the last six meetings between the teams.

The Giants will spend their mini-bye asking how they let this one go: a third-and-3 run call that failed to ice the game, a shanked punt that set up the winning drive, and two late interceptions from a quarterback who had not thrown one all season, according to USA Today.

The Giants' special teams were the quiet disaster of the night. Rookie kicker Dominic Zvada, who started the season 9-for-9, has now missed six of his last 14 attempts, and punter Jordan Stout shanked the punt that set up Washington's winning drive. In a game decided by three points, the kicking game was the difference — and the Giants have a mini-bye to work out whether Zvada keeps his job.

New York's defence made the plays that should have mattered, too. Jevon Holland's interception was his fourth of the season, a career best, and Ar'Darius Washington picked off Daniels with 2:15 remaining and the Giants leading by four — a play that should have sealed the win. Instead, the Giants gave the ball back, and Daniels made them pay. That is the cruelty of the final five minutes: two interceptions, and only one of them decided the game.

Why it matters: The result matters because it keeps the NFC East race alive: the Giants still lead the division at 5–3–1, but Washington is now two back in the loss column and owns the tiebreaker, so the gap is smaller than it looked at kickoff. It also matters for how the game was lost — the Giants had the ball and the lead late, called a quarterback run on third-and-3 instead of feeding Skattebo, then watched a shanked punt hand Washington the field position for the winner. What to watch is whether the Giants use the mini-bye to settle their kicking game and their late-game play-calling, and whether Daniels' first two interceptions of the season are a blip or a warning.

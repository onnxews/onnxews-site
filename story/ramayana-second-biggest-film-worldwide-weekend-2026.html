Ramayana Is the World's No. 2 Film This Weekend, With $52 Million Globally

The Ranbir Kapoor and Yash epic took an estimated $52.15 million worldwide for the weekend, second only to Godzilla Minus Zero, according to Comscore estimates.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Ramayana: Part I has emerged as the second-highest-grossing film in the world this weekend, according to worldwide weekend estimates released by Comscore, the box office tracking company. The Indian mythological epic generated an estimated $52.15 million worldwide for the weekend ending November 8, a remarkable result for a film that opened in far fewer theaters than its Hollywood rivals.

The only film ahead of Ramayana on the global chart was Godzilla Minus Zero, which topped the worldwide weekend with an estimated $63 million. Ramayana finished ahead of several major international releases, including The Cat in the Hat, which took $30.5 million globally, as well as Clayface and the Stray Kids world tour live viewing. Its cumulative worldwide total in the Comscore report stands at $55.655 million.

Domestically, Ramayana earned $8.65 million from 1,433 theaters in North America, good enough for fifth place on the domestic chart. The gap between its domestic and worldwide performance tells the story: the film's power is in its global audience, and particularly in India and the diaspora markets, where the Ramayana is one of the most beloved stories ever told.

The film stars Ranbir Kapoor and Yash in the retelling of the ancient Indian epic, and it is the first part of a two-part adaptation of the Sanskrit tale of Prince Rama, his wife Sita and the demon king Ravana. The scale of the production — and the star power of its two leads, among the biggest names in Indian cinema — made it one of the most anticipated releases of the year in India before it ever reached a screen.

The result is the latest sign of how far Indian cinema's global reach has come. A decade ago, a Hindi-language epic finishing second worldwide for a weekend would have been unthinkable. Now, Indian films routinely compete with Hollywood blockbusters on the global chart, powered by a domestic market of more than a billion people and a diaspora audience that turns out in force in North America, Britain and the Gulf.

The weekend also underlines a shift in what a global hit looks like. The top two films in the world this weekend were a Japanese-language Godzilla sequel and an Indian mythological epic. The Cat in the Hat, a Hollywood animated film with a major studio behind it, finished third globally. The audience for spectacle is no longer concentrated in one country or one language.

For the filmmakers, the number is a vindication of the decision to mount the epic at blockbuster scale. Mythological adaptations are a gamble: they must satisfy audiences who grew up with the story while also working for viewers encountering it for the first time. The weekend's figures suggest the film managed both, at least well enough to draw crowds in large numbers.

What comes next will be watched closely. Part I has set up the story; the second part will need to deliver the payoff, and the box office of the first film will determine how much the studio is willing to spend on the conclusion. On this weekend's evidence, the appetite is there — and the Ramayana, one of the oldest stories in the world, has found a new audience in the multiplex.

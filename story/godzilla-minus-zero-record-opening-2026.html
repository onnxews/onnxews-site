Godzilla Minus Zero Just Smashed Japan's Opening-Day Record — and America Is Next

The sequel took in $5.7 million on its first day in Japan, the biggest live-action opening ever there, and is tracking for a $30 million-plus US debut.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Godzilla Minus Zero has stomped straight into the record books. The sequel to the Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One opened in Japan on Tuesday, November 3, taking in 910 million yen, or about $5.7 million, with more than 590,000 admissions, a figure Toho says is the biggest opening day ever for a live-action film in Japan, domestic or foreign.

The date was no accident. November 3 is Godzilla Day in Japan, marking the anniversary of the original 1954 film's release, 72 years ago. The opening-day take came in at 201 percent of what Godzilla Minus One managed on its first day, roughly double the predecessor's start, and the film's three-day total in Japan has already reached 1.37 billion yen, or about $8.7 million.

The numbers kept climbing from there. North American audiences got their first look on Friday, November 6, when distributor GKIDS opened the film in 3,254 theaters. Deadline reports the film took in $13.5 million on Friday including $6.7 million in previews, putting it on course for a $30 million to $33 million opening weekend.

If it lands in that range, it will set a record as the biggest opening ever for a live-action foreign-language film at the domestic box office. The previous high was $17.8 million, set by the 2002 martial-arts epic Hero, and the debut would also be the biggest ever for GKIDS, whose current record is the $13 million opening of Hayao Miyazaki's The Boy and the Heron in 2023.

Audiences, not just ticket sales, are backing the film. Opening-night moviegoers gave Godzilla Minus Zero an A CinemaScore, matching the franchise's best-ever audience grade and a rare mark for a non-English-language film. CinemaScore remains one of the best predictors of whether audiences keep showing up beyond opening weekend, and an A suggests the film has long legs.

The film has a lot to live up to. Godzilla Minus One opened to $11.4 million in the United States in December 2023 before reaching $114 million worldwide on a tiny $15 million budget, grossing $57.1 million domestically and winning the Oscar for Best Visual Effects. Director Takashi Yamazaki returns for the sequel, which is playing in Japanese with English subtitles.

The global picture is just as strong. Industry tracking puts the worldwide opening at around $60 million, roughly triple what Minus One opened to, and Japanese forecasts project a final total above 10 billion yen, or $63.2 million, on home turf alone. Some forecasters are already asking whether a $200 million worldwide total is now on the table.

The release strategy is new for a Japan-produced Godzilla film. This is the first time one has opened in Japan and North America in the same week, and the film is also rolling out in Australia, Brazil, Mexico, France, Germany, the UK and Spain. The IMAX opening day in Japan reportedly beat even the anime hit Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle, though Deadline noted it could not independently verify Toho's record claim.

For a franchise that is 72 years old, the lesson of the weekend is that Godzilla is not slowing down. A sequel made for less than $15 million, by some estimates, is now the biggest story at the box office on two continents, and the king of the monsters looks set to rule the weekend.

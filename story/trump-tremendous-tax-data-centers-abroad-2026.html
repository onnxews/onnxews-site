Trump Threatens 'Tremendous Tax' on U.S. Companies That Build Data Centers Abroad

At a White House news conference after his Asia trip, Trump said U.S. companies that build data centers abroad would face a tremendous tax, arguing China would otherwise take them all.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump said on Friday that the United States would penalize American companies that build data centers overseas, threatening a tremendous tax on firms that move the artificial intelligence infrastructure abroad. We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country, Trump said while discussing AI at a White House news conference following his trip to Asia, adding that he would impose a tremendous tax on companies that build data centers abroad.

Trump framed the threat as a contest with China for leadership in artificial intelligence. He argued that if companies are allowed to build overseas, they will all build in China, and said the United States made a mistake in the past when it allowed companies to make chips in other countries, causing it to fall behind. We're going to fight very hard for the data centers because we're leading China, he said, and he described artificial intelligence, which he calls super intelligence, as the technology that will decide who leads in the future.

The president gave no rate, timeline or enforcement mechanism for the threatened tax. His remarks came at a news conference the day after he returned from a trip to Asia, where he met China's President Xi Jinping. Trump said Xi congratulated him on how well the United States is doing in technology, and noted that Xi took the unusual step of greeting him in person at the airport.

Data centers are critical to the AI boom because they provide the computing power needed to train and run increasingly complex AI systems, and a penalty on overseas construction could complicate the global AI buildout. Developers often locate facilities close to users to reduce delays for time-sensitive tasks such as payment processing, and transmitting large volumes of data over long distances can also add significant costs.

Trump's threat lands in the middle of a backlash against data centers in the communities where they are built. A Gallup poll in August found that 7 in 10 Americans do not want data centers in their communities, with residents blaming the facilities for noise and higher electricity prices, though industry groups counter that the facilities do not raise costs. Voters weighed in on data centers in referendums in the midterm elections in at least 45 cities and towns across the country, and in at least 44 of those contests the answer was to rein in the industry with moratoriums, new regulations or outright bans.

Trump urged communities to welcome the construction rather than block it, arguing they should use it as leverage. You ought to take advantage of data centers, he said. Make them pay. Make them pay your communities, but don't shut them off. He also said that if companies will not build in Texas or California, they should build in West Virginia, adding that there are a lot of places that want them badly.

The politics of data centers are unsettled on both sides. The Senate approved legislation on November 19 to ease the approval of data centers as part of a broader overhaul of permitting, and the bill now heads to the House, where Trump said lawmakers must pass it because it will lead to tremendous amounts of new building. That permitting bill passed the Senate 62 to 33 on Thursday night, with 15 Democrats voting in favor, after a week in which the chamber was focused on energy and infrastructure approvals.

Trump has long been a supporter of the tech industry. In October, he awarded prestigious science medals to engineers at some of the biggest artificial intelligence companies, and he has ordered the government to use the term super intelligence rather than artificial intelligence. His latest comments add a protectionist edge to that support: encouragement for building at home, and a threatened tax for building anywhere else.

Why it matters: The data center threat matters because it pulls in three directions at once. Trump wants the facilities built in America to keep the AI race from China, but the communities asked to host them voted overwhelmingly in the midterms to rein them in, and the Senate just passed a bill to make them easier to approve. A tax on overseas construction would also test the limits of presidential power, since taxing authority sits with Congress, and no rate or mechanism was offered. The result is a policy that is clear as a slogan — build here — and undefined as a law, which is where the fight will now be.

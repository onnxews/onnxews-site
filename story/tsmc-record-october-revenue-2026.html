TSMC Posts Record October Revenue of $17.88 Billion as AI Chip Demand Keeps Climbing

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported its biggest month ever in October, with revenue up nearly 60 percent from a year earlier, days after a chip sell-off rattled the market.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported October revenue of about NT$586.89 billion, worth roughly $17.88 billion, the largest single month in the company's history. The figure, announced on November 10, was 59.7 percent higher than October 2025 and 14.7 percent above September 2026, and it landed just five days after a sharp sell-off in chip stocks.

The record arrived as a direct answer to the market's doubts. On November 5, the Nasdaq slid more than 5 percent after Democrats swept the House and Senate, with investors weighing whether tighter rules could slow data center construction, and chip stocks led the losses. In Texas, Gina Hinojosa won the governor's race after campaigning on an immediate moratorium on data center development. TSMC's October sales, booked entirely before voters went to the polls on November 3, showed demand was still climbing through the month.

The scale of the month stands out against TSMC's own recent history. October cleared the previous monthly record of NT$514.81 billion, set in August, by about NT$72 billion, and September's NT$511.86 billion as well. Revenue for January through October totaled NT$4,485.62 billion, up 43.3 percent from the same period in 2025. TSMC's Taipei-listed shares closed 2.24 percent higher on November 10, the day the figures were released.

October alone covers about a third of TSMC's fourth-quarter guidance. The company told investors on its earnings call on October 15 that it expects fourth-quarter revenue of $52.3 billion to $54.3 billion, with a gross margin of 65 percent to 67 percent. Three months at October's pace would add up to about $53.6 billion, above the midpoint of that range without any further growth, leaving November and December needing to average roughly $17.7 billion each to land at the midpoint.

The quarter behind the record was itself a record. TSMC disclosed net profit of NT$763.1 billion for the third quarter, up 68.7 percent year on year, on revenue of NT$1.49 trillion, with a gross margin of 66.4 percent and an operating margin of 58.7 percent. Advanced processes dominate the mix: in the third quarter, 2-nanometer technology accounted for 8 percent of wafer revenue, 3-nanometer for 33 percent and 5-nanometer for 28 percent, putting processes at 7 nanometers and below at 79 percent of wafer revenue. High-performance computing, the segment most directly tied to AI training and inference hardware, grew 22 percent quarter on quarter to reach 68 percent of revenue.

Chief Financial Officer Wendell Huang said in the earnings release that fourth-quarter business would be supported by strong demand, including "the steep ramp-up of our 2-nanometer technology." On the October call, management told analysts that demand for its leading-edge chips was "extremely robust," while also saying "we are being prudent in our business planning going into 2027," citing pressure on consumer and price-sensitive markets.

The monthly report is deliberately narrow: TSMC publishes consolidated revenue ahead of its quarterly filings, labels the monthly figures unaudited, and gives no breakdown by customer, node or end market, so the AI contribution remains inferred rather than disclosed. The wider evidence points the same way, though. Taiwan's Ministry of Finance reported that the island's exports surged 58.9 percent year on year in October to $96.76 billion, with electronic components exports up 61.4 percent. TSMC builds chips for Nvidia, AMD and Apple, and it is also looking at making advanced memory chips, including 3D DRAM, at the request of its key client Nvidia, according to a report by The Information.

What happens next is a test of whether the pace holds after Election Day. TSMC's November sales, due in December, will show whether demand stayed strong once the political picture changed, and the bigger test comes in January, when the company reports fourth-quarter results and says more about 2027. Alongside the quarterly results, TSMC raised its full-year 2026 revenue growth forecast in U.S. dollars to approximately 44 percent, from slightly above 40 percent, and said capital expenditure from 2026 through 2028 would be significantly higher than in the previous three years. As of November 10, Taiwan's financial regulator had also cut the daily limit on intraday short-selling orders from 30 percent to 3 percent, a sign of how closely officials are watching volatility around the stock.

Why it matters: TSMC's October is a leading indicator for the whole AI buildout, not just one company's sales. Because TSMC fabricates the advanced chips used by Nvidia, AMD, Apple and the big cloud providers, its monthly revenue is the earliest hard read on whether AI infrastructure spending is still converting into silicon, and this print says it is, at a record pace. The more important shift is in the argument itself: the bear case is no longer about demand fading, but about whether politics, power and permits let data centers keep going up at this speed. That makes the November print, due in December, the next number that matters.

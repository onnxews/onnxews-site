Tyrese Proctor's First Triple-Double Lifts Shorthanded Cavaliers Past Mavericks 127-117

Tyrese Proctor recorded his first career triple-double as the shorthanded Cleveland Cavaliers, missing their entire starting five, stunned the Dallas Mavericks 127-117 on Saturday night.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Cleveland Cavaliers beat the Dallas Mavericks 127-117 on Saturday night, November 14, at Rocket Arena in Cleveland, despite missing their entire starting five. Second-year guard Tyrese Proctor led the way with his first career triple-double — 24 points, 11 rebounds and 10 assists in just over 34 minutes — and all five of Cleveland's starters finished in double figures in a win that the team's coach called special.

Proctor's night was a career night in every sense. The points and the assists were both career highs, and the triple-double was the first of his career. He was the best player on the floor for a team that had no business winning, and he played like it, controlling the game from the opening minutes and finishing it with the ball in his hands.

The rest of the starting five matched him. Mario Hezonja had 22 points, rookie Meleek Thomas had a career-high 20, Thomas Bryant had 19 points and 11 rebounds, and Jaylon Tyson had 18. It was a collective performance from a group that had never started a game together, and it came against a Dallas team led by Kyrie Irving, who had 34 points, six rebounds and five assists, and Cooper Flagg, who added 18 points, eight rebounds and five assists.

Cleveland was without Donovan Mitchell with a left knee injury, James Harden with a calf injury, Jarrett Allen with a right knee injury, Evan Mobley with a left big toe sprain and Peyton Watson with a hamstring injury, plus Sam Merrill. The absences were not the only problem: a nationwide flight ground stop on Friday had forced the team to fly home from Miami on Saturday morning, landing around 11 a.m. to noon, hours before tip-off. The Cavaliers arrived at their own arena with barely enough time to prepare for the game.

The game turned in the second quarter, when Cleveland outscored Dallas 38-20 to lead 71-53 at halftime, before a sellout crowd of 19,432. The 38-point quarter gave the Cavaliers a cushion they never gave up. Dallas closed the gap in the second half, getting within nine points, but Cleveland's starters kept scoring and the Mavericks never caught up.

Coach Kenny Atkinson called it "a special win," and the word was apt. A team missing its five starters, arriving home hours before the game after a flight disruption, does not usually beat a team with two of the league's brightest young stars. The Cavaliers did it with a second-year guard recording his first triple-double, a rookie scoring a career high, and a starting five that had never played together before.

Proctor's own account of the night was direct. "My mentality coming into tonight… was just to come in and kill," he said after the game, and the numbers backed him up: 24 points, 11 rebounds and 10 assists, with career highs in the first two categories. For a player in his second season, it was the kind of performance that changes how a team thinks about him, and how the league thinks about him too.

The win also showed the depth of Cleveland's roster. A team that can lose five starters and a sixth rotation player, fly home on the morning of the game, and still beat a playoff contender by ten points is a team with more than a starting five. The contributions from Hezonja, Thomas, Bryant and Tyson were not just numbers; they were the reason the game was won, and they came from players who had been waiting for a chance to play.

For Dallas, the loss was a missed opportunity. Irving's 34 points and Flagg's 18 were not enough against a team that had no business winning, and the Mavericks will look back on a second quarter in which they were outscored by 18 points as the moment the game slipped away. The Cavaliers' win was the most unlikely result of the NBA's Saturday night, and it came from the most unlikely group of players.

Why it matters: The win mattered because it was the most unlikely result of the NBA's Saturday night: a team without its entire starting five, arriving home hours before tip-off after a flight disruption, beating a team led by Kyrie Irving and Cooper Flagg by ten points. Tyrese Proctor's first triple-double, with career highs in points and assists, is the kind of performance that changes a young player's standing, and the contributions from the rest of the makeshift starting five showed a roster deeper than its record suggests. For Dallas, it was a game that got away in a single quarter — outscored 38-20 in the second — and a reminder that no NBA game is won on paper.

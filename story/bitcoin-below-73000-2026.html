Bitcoin Slips Below $73,000 as ETF Outflows Stretch to a Fifth Day

Bitcoin fell below 73,000 dollars for the first time since August as U.S. spot bitcoin funds lost 299.9 million dollars on Wednesday, a fifth straight day of outflows totalling about 1.44 billion dollars since November 5.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Bitcoin slipped below 73,000 dollars on Thursday, November 12, as a selloff in technology stocks spread to crypto and U.S. spot bitcoin funds recorded a fifth straight day of outflows. Ether and the wider crypto market fell with it.

Bitcoin was falling in morning trade on Thursday, changing hands below 73,000 dollars according to CoinDesk, and was down 0.7% since midnight UTC at around 73,400 dollars, hovering near the day's low as the selloff in tech stocks spread. Ether was down 0.5% at around 2,044 dollars and the CoinDesk 80 Index was down 1%. Over 24 hours, bitcoin had lost 0.8% and ether 0.7%.

Nvidia is the driver of the wider selloff. Shares in the chip giant were down around 7% in pre-market trading after the New York Times reported that chief executive Jensen Huang had a relationship with a company executive and the Wall Street Journal reported that the board is preparing to remove him. Nvidia has not announced a decision. Nasdaq-100 futures were down 0.6% to 1.2%, giving back part of Wednesday's CoreWeave-led rally, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq was dropping as stocks the AI giant invested in tumbled, with CoreWeave down more than 10% after rising on earnings the day before.

Crypto's lack of demand is not helping. U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds lost 299.9 million dollars on Wednesday, according to SoSoValue, a fifth straight day of outflows that adds up to around 1.44 billion dollars since November 5. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for 269.7 million dollars of that, its ninth straight session of redemptions. The outflows show institutional buyers stepping back just as the price tests levels that had held through the autumn.

The backdrop is a Federal Reserve that has turned hawkish. The Fed meets on December 8 and 9, with CME FedWatch pricing around a 90% chance of a rate hike, and the benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yield was recently near 5.02% after hitting 5.03% on Tuesday. Investors are pricing in a roughly 91% chance of a 25-basis-point rate hike and a 9% chance of a 50-basis-point hike, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool. Higher rates and higher yields make non-yielding assets like bitcoin harder to hold.

Derivatives markets show traders bracing for more downside. Bitcoin futures volume fell 20.64% over 24 hours to 168.6 billion dollars and open interest fell 2.92% to 125.8 billion dollars, so trading cooled and money left the market as positions were closed. Bitcoin options skew, the premium of puts over calls, stays positive and edged up to about 11% at one week, while one-week implied volatility eased to about 35% from 38% on Tuesday. Four of the six most-traded contracts are calls, led by the 80,000 dollar call expiring December 25, while the other two are puts at the 73,000 and 74,000 dollar strikes expiring November 13.

Other parts of the crypto market are faring no better. The CoinDesk DeFi Select Index is down 1.6%, with Aave, ether.fi and Ethena all down between 2.5% and 3.3%. Monero is down 4.5%, the worst performer in the CoinDesk 100, and Render, the decentralized GPU network, is down 3.1% as Nvidia slides. The fear and greed index is at 26, in fear territory, and CoinMarketCap's altcoin season index is at 39 out of 100, far below the 75 that signals an altcoin season. Gram, the renamed toncoin, is on top of the CoinDesk 100 for a second day, up 5.2% since midnight UTC, as Telegram's wallet push keeps drawing attention.

Index provider MSCI confirmed overnight that it would remove some non-operating companies from certain indexes, an expected change that was weighing on shares of bitcoin buyer Strategy in premarket trading. Strategy, the largest corporate holder of bitcoin, has seen its shares move with the crypto market all week, and the index change adds another headwind for a stock that trades as a leveraged proxy for the coin. Initial jobless claims land at 13:30 UTC on Thursday, and traders are also watching for any decision from Nvidia's board, which met late on Wednesday night to consider Huang's future.

Analysts who track the chart say the next levels matter more than the last one. With bitcoin below 73,000 dollars, the buyers who were supposed to defend it, the ETF investors, have now sold for five straight days, according to CoinDesk.

Why it matters: Bitcoin's break below 73,000 dollars is less about crypto than about everything around it. The coin is falling for the same reasons stocks are falling: Nvidia's boardroom crisis has shaken the AI trade, the Fed is now expected to raise rates in December, and the 10-year Treasury yield is above 5%. The ETF outflows are the tell — 1.44 billion dollars in five days, with BlackRock's fund redeeming for nine straight sessions — because they show the institutional buyers who were supposed to put a floor under the price are the ones selling. Unless Nvidia's board settles its leadership question or the Fed softens, there is little in Thursday's tape to suggest buyers are about to return.

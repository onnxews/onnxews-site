Qualcomm Slides on Weak Outlook as Memory Squeeze Hits Phones

The chipmaker beat on earnings but missed on revenue as handset sales fell 26 percent. Its automotive business surged 89 percent, but investors focused on the weak guide.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Chipmakers Qualcomm, Arm and GlobalFoundries struggled on Wednesday after reporting financial results that left investors wanting more, a day after a strong earnings beat from Advanced Micro Devices had raised expectations.

Qualcomm reported fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.17 per share, just beating Wall Street's $2.16 target, on revenue of $10.44 billion, slightly below the $10.48 billion analysts expected. Its stock fell more than 6 percent in after-hours trading.

The biggest disappointment was the smartphone business, where handset chip sales fell 26 percent from a year earlier to $5.14 billion, short of the $5.21 billion StreetAccount estimate. Global smartphone shipments fell 20 percent in the September quarter to a 14-year low, according to Counterpoint Research, as surging memory chip costs drove up device prices.

There were bright spots. Automotive revenue soared 89 percent to $1.99 billion, well ahead of the $1.66 billion estimate, and internet-of-things revenue rose 4 percent to $1.88 billion. The company booked about $650 million in data-center revenue in the quarter and said it expects $5 billion in data-center revenue in fiscal 2027.

For the current quarter, Qualcomm guided to earnings of $2.15 to $2.35 per share on revenue of $10.4 billion to $11.2 billion. Arm delivered adjusted earnings of 52 cents per share on revenue up 27 percent to $1.44 billion, but its stock was down 2 percent in late trading on cautious guidance. GlobalFoundries posted third-quarter adjusted earnings of 53 cents per share on sales of $1.9 billion, up 12 percent, and its stock remained flat.

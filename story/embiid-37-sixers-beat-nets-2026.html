Embiid's Season-High 37 Keeps the 76ers Rolling Past the Nets

Philadelphia improved to 12-3, its best start since 2000-01, while the rest of Monday's NBA slate delivered a Pelicans triple-double record and a Magic upset.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Joel Embiid scored a season-high 37 points as the Philadelphia 76ers beat the Brooklyn Nets 135-123 on Monday night, extending the best start in the Eastern Conference and the franchise's best opening run in a quarter of a century. The 76ers improved to 12-3, first in the East, with 12 wins from their first 15 games — their best start since the 2000-01 season, when they reached the NBA Finals.

Embiid was dominant from the opening minutes. He scored 13 points in the first quarter and 24 by half-time, finishing 13 of 22 from the field, 2 of 4 from three-point range and 9 of 10 from the free-throw line, with five rebounds and two blocks in 32 minutes. Jaylen Brown added 31 points — matching his season best — on 9-of-19 shooting and 10-of-13 from the line, with a season-best 13 rebounds. Tyrese Maxey contributed 24 points and nine assists, and the 76ers shot 51 percent from the field.

The 76ers did it without LeBron James, who rested on the second night of a back-to-back. James had played on Sunday against Indiana, when he passed Jason Kidd for third place on the NBA's career assists list; Embiid sat that game out. Philadelphia is now 4-0 without James this season, a record that says as much about the team's depth as it does about its stars.

Philadelphia led 78-59 at half-time, a season-best 78 first-half points, and led by as many as 24. Brooklyn fought back, cutting the deficit to 106-101 early in the fourth quarter on a Julius Randle three-pointer, and got within four at 125-121 on an Egor Demin three with 1:14 left. Each time, Embiid answered: he scored six straight points during the fourth-quarter push and hit a three-pointer with 52 seconds left to seal the win. V.J. Edgecombe added 14 points and Jabari Walker 12 for the 76ers.

Brown's night ended in the trainer's room. He left late in the game after a collision with Randle opened a cut on his head, returned wearing a headband and then left again. The 76ers will hope the injury is minor; Brown's 31 points and 13 rebounds were central to the win, and his availability matters with a trip to Orlando on Wednesday. The Nets, who fell to 3-10, were led by Randle's 33 points, Michael Porter Jr.'s 25 and Nolan Traore's 23, which matched his career high.

Around the league on Monday, the Orlando Magic beat the Boston Celtics 115-105 behind Franz Wagner, snapping Boston's winning streak, and the Cleveland Cavaliers beat the Chicago Bulls 126-115 as Donovan Mitchell scored 39 points with three steals and Jarrett Allen added 13 points and 14 rebounds. The Memphis Grizzlies beat the Washington Wizards 136-120, with rookie Cameron Boozer scoring a season-high 29 points and Zach Edey adding 23 points and 10 rebounds in a third quarter Memphis won 42-18.

The Miami Heat beat the Houston Rockets 128-110 as Davion Mitchell scored 16 points with 10 assists, and the Minnesota Timberwolves beat the Portland Trail Blazers 106-98 behind 34 points from Terrance Shannon Jr. and 15 rebounds from Rudy Gobert. The New Orleans Pelicans beat the Golden State Warriors 115-94, with Jeremiah Fears recording 19 points, 10 rebounds and 10 assists to become the youngest player in Pelicans history with a triple-double. The Phoenix Suns beat the Denver Nuggets 117-105 as Devin Booker scored 20 points, while Nikola Jokic had 28 points, 15 rebounds and seven assists in defeat.

The Warriors' loss came with a significant injury absence: Stephen Curry was out with a right oblique tear, according to the NBA's injury report, with Jimmy Butler III and Moses Moody also sidelined. San Antonio, meanwhile, moved to No. 1 in NBC Sports' Week 5 power rankings on a 12-2 start. The 76ers visit Orlando on Wednesday, and the Nets host Chicago the same night.

Why it matters: The 76ers are 12-3, first in the East, and winning without LeBron James — which is the most important sentence in this article. Last season, a night without their best player was a scheduled loss; this season, it is a 12-point win on the road in which Embiid scores 37 and the team shoots 51 percent. The depth that Philadelphia built in the offseason is showing up in the standings, and the 2000-01 comparison is not a coincidence: that team reached the Finals on the back of a dominant big man and a roster that could win in different ways. This one is starting to look the same.

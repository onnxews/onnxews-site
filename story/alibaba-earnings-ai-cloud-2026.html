Alibaba Beats Revenue Estimates as AI Cloud Jumps 53%, but Earnings Miss and Cash Burn Widens

Alibaba reported September-quarter revenue of 271.52 billion yuan on Thursday, ahead of estimates, as its AI cloud business surged — but adjusted earnings missed badly and free cash flow turned more deeply negative.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Alibaba beat estimates for September-quarter revenue on Thursday as demand for its AI cloud services surged, while investment gains helped more than triple reported profit. China's biggest e-commerce company by market share said revenue from AI cloud and computing services jumped 53%, highlighting the growing payoff from heavy investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure and products.

Alibaba reported quarterly revenue of 271.52 billion yuan, ahead of analysts' average estimate of 269.81 billion yuan, according to LSEG data. In dollar terms, the company reported revenue of 271.52 billion yuan ($40.50 billion), roughly matching analyst estimates of $40.49 billion, according to Koyfin. AI cloud and computing services revenue totalled 60.97 billion yuan ($9.09 billion), up 53% year-over-year.

The headline beat came with a significant miss underneath it. Adjusted earnings per American Depositary Share were 8.10 yuan, below analysts' expectations of 11.16 yuan. The company reported non-GAAP diluted earnings of $1.21 per American depositary share, below analysts' estimate of $1.65, according to Koyfin. U.S.-listed shares were down 1.5% in pre-market trading, after falling to a 22-month low on Wednesday, the lowest level seen since January 2025.

AI-related product revenue reached 17.92 billion yuan ($2.67 billion), marking its 13th consecutive quarter of triple-digit year-over-year growth. "Our full-stack AI capabilities continue to improve commercialization and delivered a strong quarter," CEO Eddie Wu said. He added that recurring revenue from external Alibaba Cloud customers grew 67%, accelerating overall cloud revenue growth. Alibaba said demand for its AI and cloud services was broadening beyond AI labs, with customers across the internet, automotive, financial services and biotechnology sectors expanding their use of its services.

Excluding investment gains and other items, non-GAAP net income leapt 92% to 19.9 billion yuan ($2.99 billion), helped by stronger profitability and narrowing losses in its quick-commerce business. Chief Executive Eddie Wu said Alibaba's previously stated 2030 targets of $100 billion in external cloud revenue and a 20% EBITDA margin remain on track, despite new U.S. restrictions on access to computing power that could affect the company's products and services next year.

Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, and amortization for Alibaba E-commerce Group rose 243% year over year to 38.85 billion yuan. The company attributed the increase primarily to a significant reduction in losses at its China Quick Commerce business. Taobao Instant Commerce continued to improve its unit economics quarter over quarter while maintaining market share. However, customer management revenue, which includes marketing and commission-related revenue from merchants, declined 5%.

The expansion of its AI infrastructure continued to weigh on cash generation. Free cash flow was an outflow of 34.47 billion yuan ($5.14 billion), compared with an outflow of 21.84 billion yuan in the year-ago quarter. Capital expenditure rose 60% to 50.48 billion yuan, as Alibaba increased investment in computing capacity to meet customer demand. The company attributed the wider outflow mainly to higher cloud infrastructure expenditure.

Alibaba's shares are down about 40% this year, as investors worry about weak consumer demand in China — the company's e-commerce business still accounts for nearly three-quarters of total revenue — and rising spending on AI and cloud infrastructure. China's slowing growth and prolonged property crisis have weighed on consumers in the world's second-largest economy, contributing to a 5% decline in revenue at Alibaba's China e-commerce business. International e-commerce revenue also fell, down 8% following European Union customs reforms.

Chief Executive Eddie Wu said the new U.S. compute export control rules "will affect next year's products and services," and that the company was still evaluating the impact. He said the company might target cloud revenue growth of 30% to 45% next year, rebounding to 50% or higher in 2028 as its domestic compute capacity expands. In September, Alibaba unveiled plans to expand global data-centre capacity to more than 20 gigawatts by 2032 and said its next-generation Qwen 4 model was in training. Its chip-design unit T-Head, which confidentially filed for a Hong Kong listing earlier this month, also unveiled a new AI processor scheduled for commercial release in early 2027.

The company recently reorganised its businesses into four main units: e-commerce, AI cloud and computing services, AI model applications and other businesses. Alibaba launched a $10 billion share placement in August, saying the proceeds would fund computing infrastructure and AI data centres. The question for investors is the same one facing every AI spender: whether the surging cloud revenue can outrun the surging capital expenditure before the market loses patience. Thursday's share price reaction — down despite a revenue beat — suggests patience is wearing thin.

Why it matters: Alibaba's quarter is the AI trade in miniature: the growth story is real — cloud up 53%, AI products growing triple digits for a 13th straight quarter — but so is the bill, with capital spending up 60% and free cash flow deeply negative. The earnings miss is what the market punished, and it exposes the central tension in Alibaba's strategy: the e-commerce business that still provides nearly three-quarters of revenue is shrinking in China, while the AI business that is supposed to replace it consumes cash faster than it generates it. The 20-gigawatt data-center plan and the Qwen 4 model show ambition, but Thursday's 1.5% premarket drop after a revenue beat says investors want proof that the spending converts into profit, not just more spending.

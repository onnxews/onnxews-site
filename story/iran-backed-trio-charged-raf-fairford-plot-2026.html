U.S. Charges Three Men Over Iran-Backed Plot to Strike RAF Fairford

U.S. prosecutors unsealed an eight-count indictment alleging an Iran-backed network plotted nearly 20 attacks, including a foiled van bombing at RAF Fairford.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Federal prosecutors in New York have charged three men with terrorism offenses over what they describe as an Iran-backed campaign of nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across Europe and the United States, including a September plot against a British air base where American forces were stationed. The eight-count indictment was unsealed on Tuesday in Manhattan federal court.

The indictment names Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi, 32, who holds Iranian and Iraqi citizenship, Mohsen Mohajeri, 48, an Iranian, and Mohammed Ali Al-Hassani, 35, an Iraqi. Prosecutors allege the men acted on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and the Iraqi militia Kata'ib Hezbollah to orchestrate, plan and conduct the attacks through a network of associates across Europe and the United States, targeting Jewish communities and a British base hosting American troops.

The charges include conspiracy to support Kata'ib Hezbollah and the Revolutionary Guard, conspiracy to bomb public spaces and financing terrorism. The men each face two counts of conspiring to support a designated foreign terrorist organization, which could result in sentences of up to 20 years. Only Al-Saadi is in custody, held in a federal jail in New York since May on charges over about 18 attacks in Europe and a synagogue bombing attempt in New York.

The most dramatic allegation concerns RAF Fairford, a Royal Air Force base in Gloucestershire, about 100 miles west of London, that has been used by American bombers during decades of Middle East conflicts. Prosecutors say that on September 26, three vans, some carrying barrels of fuel and disguised with decals mimicking a fuel-delivery or swimming-pool supply firm, set off toward the base to be driven onto it and detonated. British forces detained the suspects in the early hours of September 27, and the attack was stopped before anyone was hurt.

The United States withdrew all of its B-1 bombers from Fairford days after the incident, and flight-tracking data showed aircraft leaving a second British base, RAF Mildenhall, on November 10. The movements underline how seriously the American military took the threat to its bomber fleet, which had been forward-deployed in Britain during the war with Iran.

Prosecutors link the three men to a group calling itself Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya, which claimed responsibility for 18 attacks across Europe in the spring of 2026, seven of them in Britain, including arson attacks and explosions. The indictment also cites plots against synagogues in London and in New York, part of what prosecutors describe as a campaign aimed at Jewish communities on both sides of the Atlantic.

The case reaches into a New York jail cell. Prosecutors allege that Al-Saadi, even after his arrest in the spring, kept coordinating with his co-defendants, using coded language with relatives in Iraq to direct money to people who could carry out attacks. Al-Hassani and Mohajeri are accused of orchestrating the plot from Iran, using contacts based in Britain to prepare the Fairford attack and the wider campaign.

British authorities have arrested seven men over the Fairford incident, with two appearing in court last month, and in a separate case British counter-terrorism police said on Tuesday they had arrested a 41-year-old member of the U.S. Army on suspicion of offenses under Britain's National Security Act of 2023. The arrests reflect a coordinated response from both sides of the Atlantic, with American, British and Israeli security agencies cooperating on the case.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the trio took part in roughly 20 attempted attacks tied to Iran's Revolutionary Guard and Kata'ib Hezbollah, describing the case as proof of Tehran's intent to strike targets in Europe and America. Iran's embassy in London rejected what it called baseless and malicious claims tying Tehran to the plot, and the Iranian government has denied any role in the attacks.

The indictment lands at a moment of acute tension between Washington and Tehran. The United States has been enforcing a naval blockade of Iran and conducting airstrikes under Operation Iron Gate, while the two sides have been exchanging proposals through Qatar for a deal to end the war. The charges, if proven, would show that the conflict has extended far beyond the Gulf, into the streets of London and the air bases of western England, and they raise the stakes for the diplomacy now under way.

Grant Wilson Throws Two Touchdowns as No. 6 Indiana Beats No. 15 USC 27-13

Grant Wilson threw two touchdown passes in his first start for No. 6 Indiana as the Hoosiers beat No. 15 USC 27-13 on Saturday, surviving three fourth-quarter scares to keep their College Football Playoff hopes alive.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Grant Wilson threw two touchdown passes in his first start for No. 6 Indiana as the Hoosiers beat No. 15 USC 27-13 on Saturday, November 14, in a game that kept their playoff hopes alive and gave their coach a quarterback decision to make. Wilson, a seventh-year player making his first career start for Indiana, finished 17 of 27 for 305 yards with one interception, and his performance turned a game that was tied late in the third quarter into a two-touchdown win.

Wilson started fast. On Indiana's third play from scrimmage he connected with Shazz Preston on a 47-yard pass, and the drive ended with a 13-yard touchdown pass to Brock Schott for a 7-0 lead. The throw set the tone for a day on which Indiana's offence was more aggressive than it had been in recent weeks, with the Hoosiers pushing the ball downfield from the opening drive.

The game was tied at 13 late in the third quarter before Indiana pulled away. Khobie Martin scored the tiebreaking touchdown on a 1-yard run with 2:39 left in the third quarter, and Charlie Becker added a 42-yard touchdown catch with 3:30 left to make it 27-13. The two scores came after a game in which USC had repeatedly threatened, and they turned a close contest into a comfortable margin.

USC's quarterback, Jayden Maiava, went 26 of 42 for 311 yards and one touchdown but was intercepted three times, twice in the fourth quarter and once inside Indiana's 5-yard line. USC scored only one touchdown in five trips to the red zone, and the Trojans were inside Indiana's 10-yard line twice in the fourth quarter but came away with zero points. The turnovers, more than any other factor, decided the game.

Indiana's defence made the decisive plays when they mattered most. Freshman linebacker Jacob Savage got his fingers on a third-down pass from Indiana's 10-yard line, tipping it and nullifying a defensive pass interference penalty that would have given USC a first down at the 2-yard line trailing 20-13. Instead the Trojans had to try a field goal, and missed. Senior linebacker Isaiah Jones then read Maiava's eyes in the fourth quarter, stepping in front of a pass from Indiana's 6-yard line with 6:35 left to turn a potential game-tying touchdown into a turnover.

The win improved Indiana to 9-1 overall and 6-1 in the Big Ten, ranked No. 9 in the College Football Playoff rankings, while USC fell to 7-3 and 4-3 in the Big Ten, ranked No. 18 in the playoff rankings. It was Indiana's first victory over a Top 25 opponent this season, and it came after the Hoosiers had slid five spots in the playoff rankings the previous week despite not playing, a drop that had angered their coach.

Curt Cignetti's decision to start Wilson was the story of the week. Wilson replaced Josh Hoover at halftime of Indiana's October 31 game against Minnesota and rallied the Hoosiers to an overtime win, and Cignetti said on Saturday night that he had decided after that game that Wilson, not Hoover, would start against USC. Hoover had opened the season with 18 touchdowns and no interceptions but had cooled down in recent weeks, and although he was apparently healthy and available, Cignetti stuck with Wilson.

Wilson's own account of the week was simple. "It's been a long time, seven years, and it's just such a blessing to be here with these fans and these players," he said after the game. Cignetti, asked whether Wilson would remain the starter, said the position would be open to evaluation this week, and he praised his team's character in close games, saying: "Our guys have really come through in tight, close games. I think it's the mark of a team that's got a lot of grit, competitive excellence, can play when the game's on the line."

Lee Beebe Jr. led Indiana's running game with 16 carries for 71 yards, while Martin had 10 carries for 70 yards and the tiebreaking score. Nick Marsh hauled in a pass of 65 yards, and safety Preston Zachman and cornerback Jamari Sharpe added interceptions alongside Jones's game-turning pick. For USC, Maiava's 51-yard pass to Kayden Dixon-Wyatt set up a 7-yard touchdown pass to Zacharyus Williams that tied the score at 10, but the Trojans could not finish their drives when it mattered.

Indiana next plays at Washington on November 21, followed by a home game against Purdue on November 28, and the Hoosiers will be heavily favoured to win both. If they do, they will be in line for a spot in the Big Ten title game on December 5 and, regardless of what happens there, a spot in the College Football Playoff bracket. USC hosts Maryland next Saturday, with its own playoff hopes now hanging by a thread. The win was Indiana's best of the season, and it came with its second-choice quarterback, which may help the Hoosiers with the playoff committee more than any single result so far.

Why it matters: The win matters because it keeps Indiana's playoff hopes alive and answers the question the committee had raised by dropping the Hoosiers five spots without them playing: can this team beat a ranked opponent? Doing it with a seventh-year backup quarterback, after a week in which the coach demoted his starter, makes the answer more convincing, not less. The quarterback question now becomes the story of the season's final two weeks — Cignetti has said the job is open to evaluation, and Hoover, who started the season with 18 touchdowns and no interceptions, is waiting.

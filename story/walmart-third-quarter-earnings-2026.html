Walmart Tops Third-Quarter Estimates and Raises Its Outlook as Holiday Shopping Nears

The retail giant reported revenue of $188.8 billion for the quarter ended October 31, beating Wall Street's expectations, and lifted its full-year profit and sales forecasts heading into the crucial holiday season.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Walmart posted fiscal third-quarter results that exceeded Wall Street's expectations on Thursday, and the retail giant once again raised its full-year guidance. The Bentonville, Arkansas, company reported adjusted earnings of 65 cents a share for the quarter ended Oct. 31, up from 62 cents a share a year earlier and ahead of the 64 cents a share that analysts polled by FactSet, on average, had been expecting.

Revenue rose 5.2% to $188.8 billion, topping the $187.4 billion that analysts were looking for. In constant currency, total revenue increased 4.5%. Walmart U.S. net sales increased 3.6% to $125.0 billion, with comparable sales excluding fuel up 2.9%. Global e-commerce sales grew 17%, led by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery, while Walmart U.S. e-commerce grew 23%.

Operating income climbed 16% to $7.8 billion, an increase of $1.1 billion, supported by a 28-basis-point improvement in the gross profit rate, led by Walmart U.S. On an adjusted basis in constant currency, operating income rose 5.5%. GAAP earnings per share were $0.62, while adjusted earnings per share of $0.65 excluded the impact, net of tax, from a net loss of $0.03 on equity and other investments.

The results from the retail bellwether set the tone for the industry's critical fourth quarter, as inflation-weary consumers become increasingly selective about purchases, from pantry staples to apparel. Walmart's scale — about 280 million customers and members visit more than 10,900 stores and numerous e-commerce websites in 19 countries each week — makes its numbers the closest thing American retail has to a national consumer health check.

Walmart also raised its forecast for full-year constant-currency sales growth to 4.9% to 5.2%, from a prior range of 4% to 5%. The company now expects full-year adjusted earnings of $2.84 to $2.89 a share, up from its increased August forecast of $2.80 to $2.87 a share. Wall Street has already penciled in a full-year profit of $2.89 a share.

Beyond the headline numbers, the quarter showed how Walmart's business is changing. Its global advertising business grew 25%, with Walmart U.S. advertising up 37%, as brands pay to reach shoppers on its platforms. Membership fee revenue grew 17% globally, and the company launched a new membership bundle in the United States combining Walmart+ and Sam's Club Plus for $120 annually.

The company also issued $6.5 billion in long-term debt at favorable rates for general corporate purposes, and global inventory rose 8.1%, reflecting strategic initiatives, the timing of seasonal events and cost inflation. Return on assets was 7.2% and return on investment was 15.4%. With fiscal year 2026 revenue of $713 billion, Walmart employs approximately 2.1 million associates worldwide.

The guidance raise comes at a delicate moment for the American consumer. Shoppers are still spending, but they are trading down to cheaper goods and leaning on groceries and delivery, and Walmart has bet that demand for lower-priced groceries and growth in its delivery business will sustain spending through the holiday shopping season. Its U.S. comparable sales gain of 2.9% suggests that bet is holding for now, even as households remain stretched.

What happens next is the holiday quarter itself. Walmart's raised forecast implies confidence that the fourth quarter, which includes the peak shopping weeks of the year, will hold up. Investors will watch whether comparable sales stay strong, whether the advertising and membership engines keep growing, and whether the company's guidance proves conservative or optimistic once the holiday numbers land in February.

Why it matters: Walmart's quarter is the clearest read yet on the American consumer heading into the holidays: still spending, but carefully, and increasingly through delivery and cheaper options. The beat-and-raise is real — revenue, earnings and guidance all moved up — but the interesting story is the mix. E-commerce up 23% in the U.S., advertising up 37% and membership fees up 17% show a retailer transforming itself into a platform business while its stores do the volume. That matters because it is how Walmart defends its margins while keeping prices low, and it sets the tone for the rest of retail heading into the peak shopping weeks.

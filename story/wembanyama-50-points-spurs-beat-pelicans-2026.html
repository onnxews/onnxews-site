Wembanyama Drops 50 Points and 19 Rebounds as Spurs Roll Past Pelicans 114-91

Victor Wembanyama had 32 points by halftime and finished two shy of his career high as San Antonio won its 14th game in 15 to stay tied atop the Western Conference.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Victor Wembanyama scored 50 points, grabbed 19 rebounds and added five blocked shots and four steals as the San Antonio Spurs rolled to a 114-91 victory over the short-handed New Orleans Pelicans on Saturday. It was the second 50-point game of the season for the 7-foot-4 Frenchman, who set a career high with 52 points against Dallas on November 4 and finished two points shy of that mark before leaving the game with 6:29 still to play.

The destruction was done early. Wembanyama racked up 32 points and 13 rebounds in just 18 minutes in the first half, making 12 of his 15 shots before the break, and he became the first player this season to record 30 points, 10 rebounds, three blocks and three steals in a game, doing it all by halftime. San Antonio led 61-45 at the break and outscored New Orleans 82-62 over the final three quarters. Wembanyama had already done the damage before the second half began.

He reached 50 in style, hitting consecutive three-pointers in the fourth quarter, the second of them putting San Antonio ahead 108-82 and prompting his exit shortly afterwards. The Spurs led by as many as 29 points in the fourth, and Wembanyama's final line, 20 of 29 from the field with five three-pointers, underlined a night on which the Pelicans had no answer for him anywhere on the floor.

Stephon Castle added 19 points, tying his career high with five three-pointers, while De'Aaron Fox handed out 10 assists and Devin Vassell scored 10 points. For New Orleans, Jeremiah Fears scored 29 points to lead the Pelicans and Jordan Poole added 25 off the bench, with Derik Queen posting 15 points and 15 rebounds and Yves Missi grabbing 13 boards. It was not nearly enough against a Spurs team that has now won 14 of its past 15 games.

The Pelicans were badly short-handed before the game even started. Zion Williamson was a late scratch because of a left thigh contusion, and New Orleans was already without Trey Murphy III with a knee injury and Bennedict Mathurin with left plantar fasciitis. Former Spurs point guard Dejounte Murray remains away from the team after having his agent request a trade on November 13. The loss was New Orleans' third in a row and its ninth in its past 10 games, including a defeat at Oklahoma City on Friday in the opener of a road back-to-back.

The game also featured two scuffles in the second quarter, and Wembanyama answered both of them with scoring bursts. After Jaron Pierre Jr. shoved him from behind following a foul, with technical fouls given to Pierre and to Keldon Johnson, who confronted the Pelicans forward, Wembanyama scored the next seven points, including a one-handed slam after spinning around Missi. Later in the half, after Fox was given a technical and had to be separated from Fears following a two-handed shove, Wembanyama responded by scoring the next five points.

The win keeps San Antonio tied with the Los Angeles Lakers atop the Western Conference at 14-3 as of Saturday night. The Spurs have won two straight since their 12-game winning streak ended on Tuesday, and they have done it while short-handed themselves, with Dylan Harper missing his second straight game with a strained left hip suffered on Tuesday against Detroit. San Antonio limited its turnovers to 13 after committing a season-high 21 in its first game without Harper.

The schedule does not let up. The Spurs travel to Sacramento on Monday, while the Pelicans return home to host Phoenix the same night. For San Antonio, the question is no longer whether this start is real, it is how far a team winning 14 of 15, with a 22-year-old centre producing 50-point nights as a matter of routine, can carry it into the spring.

Why it matters: Fifty-point games are supposed to be rare, career-defining nights. Wembanyama has now had two of them in November alone, and the second came with 19 rebounds, five blocks and four steals, a line no player had put together before halftime all season. What makes it matter is not the box score but the standings: San Antonio is 14-3 and tied for the best record in the West, winning 14 of 15 while its rookie point guard is hurt and its star is still improving. The Spurs are no longer a curiosity built around a generational talent; they are, as of November 22, a genuine contender, and nights like this one are the reason the rest of the league is starting to treat them that way.

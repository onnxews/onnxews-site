Stroud's Flu Game: Five Touchdowns as Texans Rout Winless Chargers 44-22

Sick all week and barely practicing, Stroud threw five touchdown passes for 384 yards as Houston routed the winless Chargers 44-22 at SoFi Stadium.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

C.J. Stroud had been feeling lousy all week, barely practiced and woke up with chills on game day. Then he had his version of Michael Jordan's flu game. Stroud threw five touchdown passes and the Houston Texans routed the winless Los Angeles Chargers 44-22 on Sunday at SoFi Stadium.

Stroud completed 21 of 35 passes for 384 yards with one interception and one sack, and two of his touchdown throws went for 63 yards — a pair of season-best strikes to Tank Dell and Kayshon Boutte. It was the finest passing performance of his season, delivered while he was plainly unwell, and it gave the Texans their third win in four games after an 0-4 start.

Houston, coming off its bye week, scored on its opening drive, with Stroud hitting Xavier Hutchinson for a 17-yard score after a Chargers face mask penalty away from the play. The Texans never trailed, and they blew past their status as five-and-a-half-point favorites just before halftime, when Boutte caught his first touchdown of the season, a 12-yarder that capped a seven-play, 76-yard, 34-second drive and made it 24-9.

The most emotional moment came from Dell, who scored his first touchdown since a devastating knee injury in 2024 to cap a two-play, 43-second drive and extend the lead to 17-9. For a player whose career was in doubt after the injury, the 63-yard score was a milestone as much as a highlight.

The Chargers, meanwhile, sank to 0-8, and the boos cascaded down from the upper levels of SoFi Stadium. Los Angeles did at least end a 37-drive touchdown drought — the second-longest in NFL history — when Quentin Johnston caught Justin Herbert's 58-yard scoring pass in the fourth quarter. It was small consolation on a day when the home team was outclassed in every phase.

Houston improved to 3-5 with the win, and the shape of the season has changed. After starting 0-4, the Texans have won three of four, and Stroud's performance suggested a team finding its footing at the right time. The schedule offers a chance to keep building: the Texans visit Cleveland next Sunday, while the Chargers visit Baltimore in a matchup against former Bolts defensive coordinator Jesse Minter.

The flu-game framing will follow Stroud, and fairly so. Quarterbacks are judged on what they do when everything is against them, and Stroud spent the week barely practicing before producing five touchdowns and nearly 400 yards. The comparison to Jordan's famous 1997 Finals performance is a big one, but the shape of it — sick, struggling, then unstoppable — fit the day.

For the Chargers, the questions are now existential. An 0-8 team with a 37-drive touchdown drought does not have a single problem to fix; it has a season to salvage. The boos at SoFi said as much, and the trip to Baltimore offers no relief.

The final score, 44-22, flattered the Chargers. Houston led from the opening drive and never looked in danger, and the only real drama was how many touchdowns Stroud would throw. The answer was five — and a flu game for the highlight reel.

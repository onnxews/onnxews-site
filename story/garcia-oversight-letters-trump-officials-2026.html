Incoming Oversight Chair Garcia Orders Trump Family and Top Officials to Preserve Records Ahead of Probes

Rep. Robert Garcia sent a dozen letters to Vice President JD Vance, members of the Trump family, and current and former officials, mapping Democrats' investigation targets before they take control of Congress in January.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Democrats are putting top officials in the Trump administration on notice as their investigative agenda takes shape ahead of the new year. Representative Robert Garcia of California, who is expected to take over as chairman of the powerful House Oversight Committee when Democrats control the House in January, sent a dozen letters on Wednesday to 13 recipients telling them to preserve documents and prepare to answer questions.

The recipients include Vice President JD Vance, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller, former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, Attorney General Todd Blanche, former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, and former Homeland Security adviser Corey Lewandowski. Garcia also wrote to the president's sons, Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump, his son-in-law Jared Kushner, former ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle, and Catherine Townsend, chief executive of the Trust for the National Mall. One letter was jointly addressed to Federal Bureau of Prisons Director William K. Marshall III and Tanisha Hall, warden of the federal prison camp in Bryan, Texas.

The letters signal how Garcia plans to tackle allegations of pay-to-play schemes within Trump family business dealings, a cover-up related to the Jeffrey Epstein files at the highest levels, and misconduct at the Department of Homeland Security. "Voters gave Congressional Democrats a sweeping mandate to root out corruption and ensure that the government is accountable to the people," Garcia wrote in the letters. "We intend to act on that mandate in the 120th Congress by conducting the critically important oversight essential for legislative reform."

In separate letters to Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump, Garcia wrote that their roles as co-founders of the cryptocurrency company World Liberty Financial and as key players in the Trump family's pay-to-play schemes have raked in billions of dollars for the family over the past several years. There are reports the Trumps made $1.4 billion from the venture, mostly from licensing the Trump name for the sale of crypto tokens that soon lost most of their value. The letters are allegations, not findings of wrongdoing, and the recipients have denied them.

The letter to Kushner concerns his private equity firm, Affinity Partners, and his government role as the president's envoy for the wars in Iran and Ukraine. Garcia charged that Kushner has "directly or indirectly" received billions of dollars in fees and investments with particularly troubling ties to Middle Eastern states where he also has a government role. An attorney for Kushner, Chad Mizelle, said the allegations have it "exactly backwards" and that any suggestion Kushner used public service for personal financial gain is "categorically false."

Four of the letters deal with the Epstein investigation. Garcia demanded that Vance and Wiles identify, by December 2, every meeting administration officials held concerning Epstein, the Epstein files, or the administration's response to the committee's subpoena or the Epstein Files Transparency Act, a 2025 law requiring the Justice Department to release its Epstein files. Blanche's letter asks for responses by the same date on the department's compliance with the subpoena and the law. The letter to Marshall and Hall asks for responses by November 27 about the transfer of Ghislaine Maxwell to the minimum-security Bryan facility.

Garcia also sent a letter to Lewandowski, who for a while played a top role, without an official position, at the Department of Homeland Security. Garcia wrote that almost immediately upon entering the department as a special government employee, allegations arose that Lewandowski abused his position and close relationships with the president and then-Secretary Noem to enrich himself. Lewandowski's spokesperson said Garcia's attempts to build a misleading narrative about him would be proven false at the appropriate time and in the appropriate forum.

Another letter went to Townsend, whom Garcia termed a "middleman" in the financing of the White House ballroom, a project estimated at more than $600 million in taxpayer money and private donations from people with business before the government. The Trust for the National Mall told CNN its role was "limited" and that it had no role in fundraising or soliciting donations or in the design or construction of the project, adding that it plans to cooperate with congressional inquiries.

The White House pushed back the same day. White House Counsel Will Scharf sent letters to the Democratic committee leaders saying the administration's position is that no official has to comply with the demands to preserve or turn over documents because Democrats do not yet chair the committees. The requests, he said, "all drip with an animus or pettiness revealing that the purpose of these letters is not legitimate oversight but to harass and aggrandize." White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis said the president is willing to work with anyone, but that Democrats pursuing "sham investigations" are choosing to work against the American people.

The oversight push extends beyond Garcia. Democrats on the House Judiciary Committee are launching an investigation into Cantor Fitzgerald, the financial services firm once led by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, related to allegations it profited from the recent Nvidia scandal. Representative Jamie Raskin is investigating whether any of Lutnick's decisions helped his sons' bottom line after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent accused Lutnick of delaying labeling Megaspeed a possible national security threat. Cantor said the claims were "inaccurate" and "baseless." Separately, the top Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee demanded that the State Department and six other agencies preserve records on the Iran war, a Venezuela oil deal, military boat strikes in the Caribbean, and the dismantling of USAID. Senator Bernie Sanders, who will chair the Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee next year, said he has several investigations in mind, including the health department's leadership after the country lost its measles-free status and the practices of health insurers and drug companies.

Why it matters: Garcia's letters are the opening move of the next two years in Washington, and they matter even though he cannot yet issue a subpoena: they lock in the paper trail now, before memories fade and records disappear, and they force the administration to choose between cooperating and openly defying a committee that will have subpoena power on January 3. The White House's same-day refusal sets up the central fight of the 120th Congress — whether a ranking member's preservation demand carries any weight before the gavel changes hands — and the breadth of the targets, from crypto ventures to the Epstein files to DHS contracts, shows Democrats intend to investigate the Trump family's business and the administration's conduct as a single story rather than as separate scandals.

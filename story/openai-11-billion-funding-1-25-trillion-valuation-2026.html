OpenAI Reportedly Raises $11 Billion at a $1.25 Trillion Valuation, Far Short of Its Original Target

The ChatGPT maker has reportedly closed the first part of a new funding round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, but the haul is less than half of the $30 billion it first sought.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

OpenAI has raised about $11 billion in the first close of a new funding round that values the company at $1.25 trillion, according to reports published on Friday. Bloomberg first reported the funding, citing people familiar with the matter, and Reuters confirmed the figure through its own source the same day. The money is only the opening portion of a larger round that has not yet closed.

The round could still grow to roughly $20 billion in the coming weeks, one source told Bloomberg, with a second tranche of up to $9 billion yet to close. That would still leave OpenAI well short of the ambition it started with. In late September, Bloomberg reported that the company was seeking $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, and two investors told Reuters the original target was as high as $1.5 trillion.

Two investors told Reuters that SoftBank and MGX, the Abu Dhabi investment firm, took part in the first close. OpenAI has not formally announced the round, and TechCrunch said it had asked the company for comment.

OpenAI has not confirmed the close itself, and the final terms, the full investor list and whether the valuation is pre-money or post-money have not been made public. Until the round closes, the reported figures remain just that — reported — and they could still change.

The scaled-back raise lands after a difficult stretch for artificial intelligence stocks. Shares in chipmakers and AI infrastructure companies tumbled after the midterm elections, and investors have grown more selective about big-ticket AI bets. The funding round is the clearest sign yet that the market is cooling, even for the company behind ChatGPT.

The mood shift extends beyond OpenAI. Anthropic, its closest rival, is preparing for a possible public offering, and investors expect its valuation to land between $1 trillion and $1.25 trillion, below earlier expectations of roughly $2 trillion. The numbers suggest that even the strongest AI companies are now being priced more cautiously than they were a month ago.

The new valuation is still a sharp rise from where OpenAI stood in March. The company's last closed valuation was $852 billion, set in March 2026. The $1.25 trillion figure represents an increase of about 47 percent on that March valuation.

OpenAI had earlier suggested it might go public in 2026, but those plans were delayed, and the expectation now is an initial public offering in 2027. A successful close of the remaining $9 billion would give the company more room to fund its computing buildout and its push into new products without rushing to the public markets.

For now, the $11 billion close shows that demand exists at a $1.25 trillion price for at least the first tranche of the round. It does not show that the full round will clear at the same price, and the remaining money, the final terms and the investor list are all still undisclosed. What it does show is a market that is still willing to back OpenAI, just not at any price.

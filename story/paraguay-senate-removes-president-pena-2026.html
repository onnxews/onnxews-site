Paraguay's Senate Removes President Pena and Places Him Under House Arrest as Alliana Takes Over

The 44-senator vote capped a week-long crisis over the collapse of digital bank ueno, which froze the savings of three million Paraguayans.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Paraguay's Senate voted on Friday to remove President Santiago Pena from office over the collapse of digital bank ueno, ending a presidency that had unravelled in less than a week and installing Vice President Pedro Alliana as the country's new head of state.

All 44 senators present voted to remove Pena, with one member of the 45-seat chamber absent. Senate President Basilio Nunez declared Pena guilty of poor performance in his duties, the constitutional ground for impeachment, after a one-day trial. Within the hour, Alliana, 52, was sworn in before a special joint session of Congress as Paraguay's 53rd president.

Minutes after the vote, prosecutors placed Pena under house arrest at his residence in Asuncion on suspicion of breach of trust, known in Spanish as lesion de confianza, and other offences. He is due to be questioned by prosecutors on Saturday over alleged breach of trust, influence peddling and misappropriation of funds.

The removal capped a rapid fall. Pena, who took office aged 44 in August 2023 as one of the youngest presidents in Paraguay's history, representing the long-governing Colorado Party. He had offered his resignation on Thursday, saying he acted with the stability of the country in mind, but lawmakers declined to accept it and proceeded with impeachment instead, stripping him of his political immunity.

The Chamber of Deputies had voted 78 to 0 a day earlier to approve the impeachment charges. The charges alleged that Pena had conflicts of interest, manipulated regulatory oversight, diverted pension funds and participated in an institutional cover-up involving the central bank. The attorney general has opened three criminal investigations into the affair.

At the centre of the crisis is ueno, a digital bank whose collapse froze the accounts of nearly three million Paraguayans, close to half the country's population. Regulators seized the bank on November 3 after its solvency fell below half the required level, triggering the worst political crisis in Paraguay in more than a decade. For many depositors, ueno was their first bank account, and the freeze left families unable to pay for food, rent and medicine, turning a financial failure into a national emergency within days.

Speaking for the first time as president, Alliana promised compensation for the depositors hit by the bank's collapse. He will serve out the remainder of Pena's term, which runs until August 2028. Opposition lawmakers had also sought to impeach Alliana over the crisis, but their motion failed to secure congressional approval.

The transfer of power was swift, but the banking crisis that caused it is far from resolved. Depositors are still waiting to learn how much of their savings they will recover, and the investigations into the bank's collapse and the former president's alleged links to it are only beginning.

For Paraguay, the episode is a test of whether its institutions can hold a former president to account while restoring confidence in a banking system that millions of people depend on. Alliana inherits both tasks at once, and the answers depositors receive in the coming weeks will shape his presidency from its first day.

Correction (Nov. 11, 2026): An earlier version of this article described Santiago Peña as 44. Peña, born November 16, 1978, was 44 when he took office in August 2023; he is 47.

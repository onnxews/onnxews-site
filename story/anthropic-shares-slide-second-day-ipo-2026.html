Anthropic Shares Slide 8.5% on Second Day of Trading as AI Stocks Fall

Shares of the Claude maker fell 8.5 percent to $88.91 on their second day of trading, wiping about $109 billion off the value of the biggest IPO in history.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Anthropic’s stock slid sharply on its second day of trading on Friday, wiping about $109 billion from the AI lab’s market value and nearly erasing the gains from the largest initial public offering in history. Shares of the Claude maker fell 8.5 percent to close at $88.91, after dropping as low as $85.80 in morning trading. The stock remains about 7 percent above its $83 IPO price, but the slide from its first-day pop punctuates a period of turmoil for the AI trade — and has Wall Street asking what went wrong.

The debut on Thursday had delivered fireworks. Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share — above the original top end of the $78 target range — but as investors scrambled to get in, shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, quickly climbing as high as $115.10. The exuberance dissipated quickly, and the stock spent much of the day losing steam, closing up 17 percent at $97.17 on heavy volume of around 847 million shares. At its peak, the company’s market value briefly pushed above $2 trillion, and it finished Thursday valued at $1.29 trillion, according to Dow Jones Market Data. Friday’s action brought that down to about $1.16 trillion.

Anthropic’s IPO was the largest ever. The company said on Friday that it had closed the offering, selling 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83 each, putting gross proceeds at about $93.5 billion after underwriters exercised their option to purchase additional shares. It also sold about $26 billion of a separate class of shares in a concurrent private placement at the same price to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners. Together, the IPO and placement raised about $119.5 billion before offering expenses. The listing was meant to reassure investors about AI’s future. Instead, its second day became a symbol of the AI trade’s unraveling.

The stock is falling as the excitement around its market debut fades and AI stocks sell off more broadly. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent on Friday and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.3 percent, its biggest one-day fall since November 5. But investors are also asking harder questions about Anthropic’s own economics. Last quarter, Anthropic’s revenue rose 47 percent sequentially — growth few companies can match — but spending on compute, the lifeblood of its business, jumped about 1.7 times as fast, and the company swung from its first and only operating profit to an operating loss. Even after Friday’s drop, Anthropic is valued at about $1.2 trillion, or roughly 18 times its annualised revenue.

A flood of new supply is also weighing on the shares. Anthropic’s IPO had a unique provision that allowed some current and former employees to immediately sell around 300 million shares, or around $26 billion worth at the IPO price. On December 15, another roughly 300 million shares of Anthropic will become available to trade, which could pressure the stock even more. The Wall Street Journal reported that Anthropic and its advisers knew they were taking a risk staging the offering during the most intense turbulence in the AI trade to date — and that the blame game over the turbulent debut has now begun.

Retail investors who snapped up Anthropic shares at a near-record pace after its IPO face losses a day later. The retail investors who missed out on the initial offering helped drive the third-biggest day of net retail buying for a stock on its debut on records going back to 2012, according to Vanda Research, and the biggest day of turnover overall. Anthropic said less than 20 percent of shares were allocated to retail investors. On Friday, the stock fell sharply to around $85 by midday, meaning buyers on the secondary market who drove the price up a day earlier were left facing losses. Jefferies trading-desk analyst Jeffrey Favuzza wrote in a morning note that “the vast majority of this continued selling pressure in the last 24 hours feels more to do with” the Anthropic IPO than with less-than-perfect earnings reports in the technology sector.

Anthropic has not publicly commented on Friday’s share price decline. Its president and co-founder, Daniela Amodei, told CNBC on Thursday that the listing was a milestone but “one stop on the journey,” adding that the company would be “the same company” the next day. Newly listed stocks often swing widely in their first days of trading, and Anthropic was the first frontier AI firm to go public, with no direct peers to anchor its valuation. For now, though, the market’s verdict on the biggest IPO ever is unmistakable: a stock that opened 34 percent above its offer price has given almost all of it back in 48 hours.

The bigger question is what Anthropic’s stumble means for the AI trade as a whole. The IPO was seen as a tentpole event — proof that investors would still pay up for AI growth after a brutal month for the sector. Instead, the second-day slide, the record retail losses and the looming December share unlock have turned the debut into a cautionary tale. If the most anticipated listing in history cannot hold its gains, investors will ask what else in the AI boom is priced for a future that may not arrive on schedule.

Why it matters: Anthropic’s debut was supposed to be the moment the AI trade proved itself — the biggest IPO ever, from the company behind Claude, landing at the top of the market. Instead it became the clearest evidence that the AI boom has a valuation problem: a business growing revenue 47 percent a quarter still saw its shares collapse, because its compute bills are growing faster than its sales and its valuation, at 18 times annualised revenue, leaves no room for disappointment. The December unlock of another 300 million shares will test whether there are enough buyers left at these prices. For the wider market, the lesson is that even the best AI story in the world cannot outrun a sector-wide repricing.

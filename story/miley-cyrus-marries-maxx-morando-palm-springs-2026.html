Miley Cyrus Marries Maxx Morando in Intimate Palm Springs Wedding

The pop star and the drummer tied the knot at a private ceremony in Palm Springs on Saturday, a month after performing a song called "Let's Get Married" together at the Hollywood Bowl.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Grammy-winning pop star Miley Cyrus married her longtime partner, drummer Maxx Morando, in California on Saturday, November 21, according to People magazine and other American entertainment news outlets. Cyrus and Morando tied the knot at a private ceremony in Palm Springs, a month after performing "Let's Get Married" together at the Hollywood Bowl.

The ceremony was held at a private residence in Palm Springs and was intimate by celebrity standards. The couple exchanged vows in front of close family and friends, and the ceremony was officiated by Wayne Coyne, the frontman of the Flaming Lips, with whom Cyrus is close friends and a frequent collaborator. Representatives for both Cyrus and Morando did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Family was at the center of the day. Cyrus and Morando posed for a family photo with her father Billy Ray Cyrus, Elizabeth Hurley and Hurley's son Damian. Her mother Tish Cyrus attended with her husband Dominic Purcell, and the pair were color-coordinated in all black. Her sister Noah Cyrus was also among the guests, along with other family members and close friends.

The wedding came together quickly in public view. The couple were spotted at their wedding rehearsal dinner the day before, walking hand in hand and wearing black. A pair of Instagram uploads shared by Tish Cyrus's stepdaughter, Lily-Rose Purcell, revealed that the rehearsal dinner menu included filet mignon, sherry cappuccino and hazelnut creme brulee, and the menu was inscribed with the couple's first initials, "M + M," and the date of the event, "11.20.2026."

Morando is the drummer for Liily, a Los Angeles-based alternative rock band affiliated with Atlantic Records, according to the band's website. He and Cyrus have been together for several years, and their relationship has played out largely away from the spotlight, with the couple making only occasional public appearances together, including at the Oscars in March 2025.

The song the couple performed together at the Hollywood Bowl last month now reads like a hint. "Let's Get Married" appears on Cyrus's latest studio album, Bass Persuades, and the pair performed it together on stage at the Bowl in October. At the time it looked like a playful duet; a month later, it looks like a preview.

This is Cyrus's second marriage. The "Flowers" singer was previously married to Australian actor Liam Hemsworth, who filed for divorce in Los Angeles in 2019. Since then, Cyrus has rebuilt her career around her music, winning a Grammy and releasing a string of albums while keeping her personal life more private than in her earlier years in the spotlight.

The wedding caps a busy year for Cyrus, whose album Bass Persuades and its live performances have kept her in the public eye through the fall. The Palm Springs ceremony, private and family-focused, fits the lower-key approach she has taken to her personal life in recent years, even as the news itself spread across entertainment outlets within hours.

For now, the couple have said nothing publicly about the wedding themselves, and no official photos have been released. What is known comes from the guests and the reporting that followed: a private house in Palm Springs, a small circle of family and friends, a rock star officiant and a menu dated the day before. The rest, for the moment, belongs to them.

Why it matters: The wedding is a reminder that the most-watched celebrity stories are often the ones the stars say least about: Cyrus and Morando said nothing themselves, and the news still traveled the world in hours. It also closes a circle that began on stage at the Hollywood Bowl, where the couple performed a song called "Let's Get Married" a month before doing exactly that. For Cyrus, who has spent years rebuilding her career and her privacy after a very public first marriage, the small, family-centered ceremony in Palm Springs looks like a deliberate choice rather than an accident.

Judge Blocks Trump Administration's Limits on Internships for International Students

A federal judge in Boston said the Homeland Security Department likely broke the law when it narrowed a decades-old training program used by more than 140,000 foreign students.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

A federal judge on Tuesday blocked President Donald Trump's administration from restricting the types of internships and other practical training opportunities available to international students attending US universities on visas. US District Judge Julia Kobick in Boston issued an injunction barring the US Department of Homeland Security from enforcing two August directives that higher education groups said had abruptly disrupted training opportunities for foreign students nationwide.

The case concerns curricular practical training, or CPT, a program that lets students on visas take up internships and other training linked to their studies. For more than 35 years, foreign students had been able to take part in school-authorized internships and training as long as the work was directly related to their field of study and formed an integral part of an established curriculum, according to the education groups that brought the lawsuit.

The government had long read that standard as covering training that either earned academic credit or was required for a degree. But in August, the Department of Homeland Security, which oversees Immigration and Customs Enforcement, issued messages telling universities that internships and other opportunities qualified only if they were mandatory for every student in a degree program, and did not cover elective training taken for academic credit.

Universities said the change had abruptly disrupted training opportunities for foreign students nationwide, at a time when more than 140,000 students a year were using the program. The department said it had seen an increase in CPT authorizations it believed did not comply with program requirements, and that it would increase scrutiny of approvals.

Kobick found the directives were likely arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act, the federal law governing how agencies make rules, because the department had failed to acknowledge that it was making a fundamental change to the government's longstanding interpretation of the program's rules. "Correct or not, this new interpretation is inconsistent with the government's previous position," she wrote.

Kobick, an appointee of former Democratic President Joe Biden, ruled in a lawsuit filed last month by four organizations, including the Association of American Universities and the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration. The ruling means international students can continue taking part in internships and other practical training while the legal challenge proceeds.

The program is widely used. According to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement, 140,775 student visa holders were authorized to take part in CPT in 2024, and Amazon and Tesla were the top two employers of CPT students that year. The ruling therefore affects a large share of the international students who use practical training as a bridge between their studies and their careers.

The Department of Homeland Security declined to comment on the ruling. The injunction applies while the legal challenge to the directives proceeds, and the government will have to defend the change in court if it wants the restrictions to take effect.

It marked the latest victory for plaintiffs challenging the Trump administration's efforts to impose restrictions on student visa holders as part of its hardline immigration agenda. On Monday, a different federal judge in Boston struck down a new rule that would limit how long foreign students can remain in the US without applying for extensions, a second defeat for the administration's student visa policies in as many days.

Why it matters: This ruling matters because it protects a pathway that tens of thousands of international students rely on to turn a degree into a career, and because it is the second time in two days that a Boston judge has blocked a Trump administration restriction on student visas. The administration has argued that the training program was being stretched beyond its purpose, but the court found the government changed a 35-year-old rule without admitting it was doing so. If the injunction survives appeal, universities will keep approving the elective internships that employers and students had already planned around for the spring and summer.

Lando Norris Masters the Rain to Take Sao Paulo Pole After a Marathon Qualifying Session

Norris lapped Interlagos in 1:27.470 in treacherous wet conditions, with George Russell's grid penalty promoting newly crowned champion Kimi Antonelli to the front row.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Lando Norris took pole position for the Sao Paulo Grand Prix on Saturday after a marathon wet-weather qualifying session at Interlagos that lasted nearly three hours. The McLaren driver's lap of 1 minute 27.470 seconds on intermediate tires beat George Russell by 0.204 seconds, with newly crowned world champion Kimi Antonelli third.

Thunderstorms struck the track shortly before qualifying's scheduled start, and the session was red-flagged just two minutes and 23 seconds after the pit lane opened as conditions became undrivable. Standing water covered the circuit, the spray was so thick that drivers could barely see the car in front, and race control had no choice but to stop the session.

The delay lasted nearly an hour and a half before qualifying could resume, and there was a further delay ahead of the final segment to repair barriers following a late crash by rookie Rafael Camara in the second part of qualifying. Home hero Gabriel Bortoleto hit the wall and ended up last, a bitter disappointment for the Brazilian crowd.

By the final part of qualifying, the track was dry enough for intermediate tires, but the rain was closing in again, and everyone knew the first laps would probably be the ones that counted. Fernando Alonso went out first and set the early pace, but Norris and Russell soon moved ahead, and Norris's time proved unbeatable as the rain intensified.

Norris admitted he was fortunate with the conditions. He said the team was lucky it was wet, because in the dry McLaren did not have the pace, and he was certainly more confident in the wet and on intermediate tires than in the dry. It was his third pole position in a row at Interlagos, a circuit where he has become increasingly dominant.

The penalties reshuffled the grid behind him. Russell, already carrying a five-place grid penalty for a new power unit, was furious when Mercedes told him to abandon his second flying lap as the rain intensified, believing he could have reclaimed the top spot after initially holding provisional pole. His drop to seventh promotes Antonelli onto the front row alongside Norris.

Antonelli, who became Formula 1's youngest-ever world champion a week ago in Mexico City at the age of 20, qualified third with a time of 1:28.095, 0.625 seconds off pole. He admitted he had struggled on the intermediate tire, made a big mistake and almost went off at Turn 5, but was happy with third and is now focused on the race. He has prepared a special helmet to honor Ayrton Senna at Interlagos.

Charles Leclerc qualified fourth, 0.819 seconds off the pace, but will drop to 14th with a 10-place penalty for an engine change. Oscar Piastri failed to reach the final part of qualifying for the second race in a row, a worrying run of form for the Australian. Norris topped the first segment with a time of 1:30.939 before the conditions improved.

Unlike recent editions of the Brazilian event, there is no sprint race in 2026. The weekend follows the traditional format of three practice sessions, Saturday qualifying and Sunday's Grand Prix, which means no additional championship points were awarded on Saturday and the title picture is unchanged heading into the race.

The 71-lap Grand Prix is scheduled to start at 2 p.m. local time on Sunday, and if qualifying was anything to go by, it could be just as wild. Norris is hoping for a wet race, with rain expected at times through the day, and Antonelli will start alongside him on the front row. After a qualifying session that tested every driver on the grid, the race promises more of the same.

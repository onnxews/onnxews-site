'The End of Oak Street' Hits HBO Max After a $121 Million Theatrical Run

Anne Hathaway and Ewan McGregor star as parents whose suburban street is torn from 1982 Michigan and dropped into a prehistoric world. The film streams Friday on HBO Max.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

"The End of Oak Street" makes its global streaming debut on Friday, November 13, exclusively on HBO Max, after a theatrical run that began in August.

The film, written and directed by David Robert Mitchell, the filmmaker behind "It Follows," and produced by J.J. Abrams' Bad Robot, stars Anne Hathaway as Denise Platt and Ewan McGregor as Greg Platt, a couple raising two children in suburban Michigan in 1982.

The premise is pure high concept: a mysterious cosmic event tears the Platts' entire street from the town and drops it into an unrecognizable prehistoric world, and the family has to survive together in a place where the rules of their old life no longer apply.

Maisy Stella plays their daughter Audrey and Christian Convery their son Brian, completing a family unit that has to hold together as the street becomes an island in a hostile landscape.

The film is rated PG-13 and runs 99 minutes, a compact runtime for a story that mixes family drama with survival adventure and prehistoric spectacle.

In theaters, the film grossed about $121.6 million worldwide, including roughly $54.2 million domestically, according to The Numbers, against a reported $85 million production budget. By October 8, it was down to 72 theaters as its run wound down.

The film will also air on linear HBO on Saturday, November 14, at 8 p.m. Eastern, giving viewers a second way to catch it over the weekend.

It arrives on HBO Max alongside a busy weekend slate, including "Crown of Deceit: Inside the Miss Universe Scandal," a three-part docuseries, and season two of "Tournament of Champions: All-Star Christmas," according to Parade.

Mitchell's films have always mixed the ordinary with the uncanny, and "Oak Street" is his most mainstream version of that idea yet — a family movie with a cosmic hook, built for the streaming weekend.

For Hathaway and McGregor, the film is a different kind of leading role: less a showcase for a single performance than a two-hander about a marriage under impossible pressure, with the prehistoric world as the pressure cooker.

Mitchell's earlier films explain why the premise works. 'It Follows,' his 2014 horror hit, turned an ordinary suburb into a place of dread with almost no effects. 'Oak Street' applies the same trick at a bigger scale: the horror is not a monster but the disappearance of the familiar, and the family has to learn the rules of a world that has no interest in them.

The film's modest theatrical result — about $121.6 million worldwide — also says something about the market for original ideas. An $85 million budget for a film with no franchise behind it is a gamble, and the numbers suggest it roughly broke even theatrically before streaming. HBO Max is where the film will now find its real audience, and a strong streaming weekend could do more for its reputation than its box office did. The film's arrival alongside a docuseries and a Christmas competition show suggests HBO Max sees the weekend as a package deal — a movie for Friday night, and reality and documentaries for the rest of the weekend.

Why it matters: The film's move to streaming matters because it completes a theatrical run that was respectable without being a breakout: about $121.6 million worldwide against an $85 million budget is a solid result for an original, non-franchise idea, and the HBO Max debut is where most viewers will now meet it. Mitchell's work has always found its audience after the fact, and a Friday streaming launch — paired with a Saturday linear airing — gives the film a second opening weekend. What to watch is whether it finds the word-of-mouth that eluded it in theaters.

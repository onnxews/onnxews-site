Depp's Ebenezer Eyes $20 Million Opening as How to Rob a Bank Flops

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer is projected for a $20 million opening weekend at the top of the North American box office, while How to Rob a Bank is heading for a $4.7 million flop and Godzilla Minus Zero has fallen 75% in its second weekend.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer is heading for a $20 million opening weekend at the North American box office, according to studio estimates reported on Saturday, November 14, after a $6.7 million Friday from 3,535 theatres that included $1.5 million from Thursday previews. The film, a PG-13 retelling of Dickens's A Christmas Carol directed by Ti West, is projected to take about $40 million worldwide, with roughly $20 million of that coming from overseas.

The opening would be a significant moment for Depp, whose first weekend at No. 1 in eight years was already assured by the Friday figures. Audiences have responded warmly: the film earned an A- CinemaScore and a 4.5 out of 5 on PostTrak, and half of the ticket buyers surveyed said Depp was the reason they bought a ticket. The film cost a reported $38 million to produce, plus about $50 million in marketing, so a $20 million domestic opening and a $40 million worldwide weekend would put it on course to cover its costs, though it will need a strong run through the holiday season to do so comfortably.

The weekend's big misfire is Amazon MGM's How to Rob a Bank, David Leitch's $60 million heist film starring Nicholas Hoult, Zoë Kravitz and Pete Davidson. The film took $2.2 million on Friday and is pacing for just a $4.7 million opening in seventh place, with a B- CinemaScore and a 33% score on Rotten Tomatoes. For a film that cost $60 million to make, a $4.7 million opening is a flop by any measure, and it will struggle to recover its costs even with a long run in theatres.

Godzilla Minus Zero fell steeply in its second weekend, dropping about 75% to roughly $7.5 million, for a ten-day domestic total of about $48 million. The fall is sharp even by the standards of second weekends, and it follows an opening of $30.6 million that set a record for a live-action foreign film. The film's opening weekend was the biggest ever for a live-action foreign-language release, but the second-weekend collapse suggests its audience was concentrated in the first few days.

The Cat in the Hat is holding in second place, with about a $10 million second weekend, down about 50%, for a domestic total of $36.7 million against a $92 million budget. The animated film has held up better than its rivals, but with a $92 million budget it still has a long way to go before it breaks even, and its second-weekend hold will need to continue through the coming weeks if it is to get there.

Sony's Ghost Soldier, starring Jim Caviezel, opened on Wednesday and is pacing for about a $5 million three-day weekend and $8 million over five days. The film is a smaller release than its rivals, and its five-day total will be the more useful measure of its performance, since it had two extra days of ticket sales before the weekend began.

All of these figures are Saturday estimates and projections, not final actuals, and they will be revised when the studios report their final weekend numbers on Sunday and Monday. The projections are based on Friday and Saturday ticket sales, and they can move by a million dollars or more in either direction before the final totals are published. The rankings, however, are unlikely to change: Ebenezer is comfortably ahead, and How to Rob a Bank is comfortably behind.

The weekend also tells a wider story about what audiences want at the moment. A star-driven retelling of a familiar story, a foreign-language monster film that opened huge and faded fast, and a $60 million heist movie that audiences largely ignored — the three together show a market that rewards familiarity and star power, and punishes films that cannot explain themselves in a trailer. Depp's return to the top of the box office, eight years after his last No. 1, is the headline, but the flop of How to Rob a Bank is the warning.

For Ebenezer, the next test will be the Thanksgiving holiday, when family audiences traditionally turn out in force. A $20 million opening is a strong start, but the film's $38 million production cost and $50 million marketing spend mean it will need to keep its audience through the holidays to be a real success. For now, it has the weekend, the reviews from audiences, and the top spot.

Why it matters: The weekend matters for two opposite reasons: it marks Johnny Depp's return to the top of the box office after eight years, and it marks a $60 million flop for a heist film that audiences simply did not want. Ebenezer's $20 million projection, on a $38 million budget, is a solid start rather than a triumph — the film will need the Thanksgiving holiday to turn a good opening into a profitable run. Godzilla's 75% second-weekend fall is the other story: a record opening that faded almost immediately, suggesting its audience was concentrated in the first days. All figures here are Saturday estimates; the final totals will be published on Sunday and Monday.

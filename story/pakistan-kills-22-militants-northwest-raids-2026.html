Pakistan Reports 22 Militants Killed in Two Days of Northwest Raids

Security forces raided militant hideouts in South Waziristan, Bannu and Tank over two days, the military said, in the latest round of a counterterrorism campaign that has intensified across the northwest.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Pakistani security forces killed 22 militants during intelligence-based raids on hideouts in the country's northwest near the Afghan border, the military said on Thursday. The raids took place over the past 48 hours, on November 16 and 17, in the South Waziristan, Bannu and Tank districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, and set off gunbattles that left weapons and ammunition in the hands of the troops.

The military's media wing, Inter-Services Public Relations, gave a district-by-district breakdown of the fighting. Fifteen militants were killed in an intelligence-based operation in South Waziristan, where troops engaged a militant location and, after an intense exchange of fire, killed those the military described as members of Fitna al-Khawarij, its official designation for the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan and allied groups, which it says are backed by India. Six more were killed in two separate encounters in Bannu district, and another was killed in an encounter in Tank district.

Weapons and ammunition were recovered from the militants killed, who the military said remained actively involved in numerous terrorist activities in the area. Sanitization operations were being conducted to eliminate any other militants found in the area, the military said, and it vowed that the relentless counterterrorism campaign would continue at full pace to wipe out what it called the menace of foreign-sponsored and foreign-supported terrorism from the country.

The military alleged that the militants killed were backed by neighboring India, and there was no immediate comment from New Delhi. Pakistan has repeatedly accused India of sponsoring attacks by the Pakistani Taliban from across the border, a charge India denies. The Indian backing claim is central to how Islamabad frames the campaign, but it rests on the military's own statements rather than on evidence presented publicly.

The raids were the latest in a sustained offensive across the northwest. Just last week, the military reported that security forces had killed 74 militants in raids in the northwest and in Balochistan, and the operations announced on Thursday fall under the country's broader counterterrorism campaign, Operation Azm-e-Istehkam, which has intensified through the autumn. The military did not identify those killed in this week's raids, and it provided no information about casualties among its own forces.

The two provinces at the center of the campaign, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan, have seen a surge in militant violence since 2021, when the Afghan Taliban returned to power across the border. According to a report by the Centre for Research and Security Studies, the two provinces remained the country's main hotspots for violence during the second quarter of 2026, with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emerging as the worst-affected region. Together the two provinces accounted for nearly 96 percent of all violence-related fatalities recorded during the quarter.

The scale of the violence in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is stark in the research center's figures. The province recorded 475 deaths in the second quarter, more than 61 percent of the national total, while Balochistan accounted for 34 percent, or 265 deaths. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa also recorded the highest number of violent incidents, with 151 cases, representing 57 percent of the nationwide total, followed by Balochistan with 94 incidents, or 35 percent. The numbers underline how concentrated the insurgency has become in the two border provinces.

The military's announcement gave no indication of when the current round of operations would end, and clearance operations were still under way in the districts where the raids took place. With the campaign continuing at full pace and the violence concentrated in the northwest, the region's security forces face a grinding fight against an insurgency that has survived years of operations, and against militant networks that Islamabad says operate with support from across the Afghan border.

Why it matters: These raids matter because they show how entrenched the insurgency in Pakistan's northwest has become: 22 killed in two days, 74 the week before, and still the military says clearance operations must continue. The figures from the second quarter, with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa alone accounting for more than six in ten violent deaths nationwide, explain why Islamabad treats the region as its main security front. The India allegation also matters, because it ties a domestic counterterrorism campaign to the wider rivalry between the two nuclear-armed neighbors, and it is an accusation New Delhi has not answered.

Stephen Curry Drops 41 as the Warriors Beat the Magic in Overtime for Their Seventh Straight Win

Curry scored 41 points on 13-of-19 shooting as Golden State edged Orlando 126-119 in overtime, while the Spurs won their eighth straight and Oklahoma City held Washington to 75.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Stephen Curry scored 41 points as the Golden State Warriors beat the Orlando Magic 126-119 in overtime on Saturday night, extending their winning streak to seven games. Curry shot 13 of 19 from the field, 5 of 10 from three-point range and a perfect 10 of 10 from the free-throw line, adding six rebounds and six assists in a vintage performance.

The game went to overtime after a tense finish in regulation, and Curry took over when it mattered most. He made the go-ahead three-point play with 1:38 left in the extra period, and the Warriors closed out the win from there. The victory improved Golden State to 7-3, while Orlando fell to 2-7.

Seven straight wins is the Warriors' best run of the young season, and it has come with Curry playing some of his best basketball in years. At 38, he remains the engine of everything Golden State does, and his ability to take over a game in overtime, against a young and athletic Orlando team, is a reminder of why the Warriors are never counted out.

The Spurs kept their own remarkable run going on Saturday, beating the Portland Trail Blazers 125-103 behind 29 points from Victor Wembanyama. The French star shot 9 of 13 from the field and added seven rebounds, and San Antonio built a lead as large as 35 points before Portland won a meaningless fourth quarter. The Spurs have now won eight straight games.

Oklahoma City produced the defensive performance of the night, holding the Washington Wizards to 75 points in a 114-75 rout. Washington shot just 29.6 percent from the field and 7 of 43 from three-point range, and its 75 points were the fewest by any NBA team this season, according to the Associated Press. The Thunder's defense has become one of the defining stories of the early season.

The Denver Nuggets won the day's showcase game in Mexico City, beating the Pacers 114-102 at Arena CDMX. Nikola Jokic orchestrated the attack with 19 points, 13 rebounds and eight assists, while Cameron Johnson added 18 points on 8-of-12 shooting. Pascal Siakam led the Pacers with 23 points, but the Pacers could not overcome a Denver team that led by as many as 27.

Elsewhere on Saturday, the Phoenix Suns edged the Houston Rockets 102-99 behind 24 points from Devin Booker, who closed the game out in the final minutes. The Atlanta Hawks beat the Brooklyn Nets 124-121 as Jalen Johnson returned with 30 points, seven rebounds and six assists, and the New York Knicks beat the Cleveland Cavaliers 109-97.

The results leave the early-season standings taking shape. Golden State's seven-game winning streak has moved the Warriors firmly into the Western Conference picture, while San Antonio's eight-game winning streak continues to defy expectations. Oklahoma City's defensive dominance and Denver's depth make the West look deeper than ever, and the gap between the contenders and the rest is already visible.

For Orlando, the loss is a hard one to take. The Magic pushed the hottest team in the league to overtime on the road, and for long stretches they looked like the better team. But closing out games against elite opponents is a skill that young teams have to learn, and Curry, as he has done so many times before, was the teacher.

The Warriors now turn their attention to keeping the streak alive, with the confidence that comes from winning seven in a row and the knowledge that their best player is playing at an MVP level. For the rest of the league, the message from Saturday night is familiar and unwelcome: Stephen Curry is still Stephen Curry, and the Warriors are still dangerous.

On Holding Posts Record Third-Quarter Sales as Asia-Pacific Jumps 31%

The Swiss running-shoe maker reported its first quarter above CHF 900 million in sales, with record margins and a raised outlook, even as its shares slid more than 10 percent in a week.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

On Holding, the Swiss running-shoe maker, reported record third-quarter sales on Tuesday, with net sales up 17.2 percent to 931.4 million Swiss francs in the three months to September 30, the first quarter in which the company has topped 900 million francs. Net sales rose 17.2 percent to CHF 931.4 million, up 17.8 percent in constant currency, the company said, as direct-to-consumer sales jumped 25 percent and the Asia-Pacific region grew by more than 31 percent.

The direct-to-consumer channel was the engine of the quarter. Sales through On's own stores and website rose 25.0 percent to CHF 393.3 million, or 42.2 percent of total sales, while wholesale sales grew 12.2 percent to CHF 538.2 million. By region, Asia-Pacific led the way with sales up 31.1 percent to CHF 189.9 million, or 31.9 percent in constant currency, while Europe, the Middle East and Africa grew 17.9 percent to CHF 251.4 million and the Americas rose 12.4 percent to CHF 490.1 million. By category, shoes rose 15.4 percent to CHF 843.7 million, apparel jumped 38.7 percent to CHF 69.5 million and accessories climbed 39.2 percent to CHF 18.1 million, showing that the brand is selling more than running shoes.

Profitability improved even faster than sales. Gross profit rose 29.5 percent to CHF 676.0 million, and gross margin reached 72.6 percent, up from 65.7 percent a year earlier, a record for the company. The margin included CHF 52.6 million in refunds of U.S. tariffs, but even excluding those refunds the margin was 66.9 percent, also a record. Net income rose 36.9 percent to CHF 162.8 million, adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation rose 42.7 percent to CHF 256.8 million, a margin of 27.6 percent, and adjusted diluted earnings per share came in at CHF 0.54. For the first nine months of the year, sales reached CHF 2,613.6 million, up 15.1 percent, or 21.8 percent in constant currency.

The tariff refunds are a notable feature of the quarter, and they cut both ways. On received CHF 52.6 million back in U.S. tariff payments, which lifted the reported margin by nearly six percentage points, but the underlying margin of 66.9 percent excluding refunds was still a record, suggesting the improvement is not just an accounting windfall.

The company has held full price in a promotional market, a discipline its founders credit for the margin strength. "In a promotional market, we stayed true to who we are and held full price, delivering record sales and record underlying margins," said Caspar Coppetti, On's founder and co-chief executive. Chief financial officer Frank Sluis pointed to premium positioning as the mechanism behind the record margin, arguing that the brand's pricing power is what allows it to expand profitability while growing sales at a double-digit rate.

The outlook for the rest of the year was raised on margins and held on sales. On kept its forecast for full-year 2026 sales growth in the low 20 percent range in constant currency, implying sales of CHF 3.47 billion to CHF 3.56 billion, and it raised its gross margin guidance to about 67.5 percent, or about 66 percent excluding tariff refunds, up from at least 65 percent previously. Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance was set at 21.0 to 21.5 percent, or 19.5 to 20.0 percent excluding refunds. The company expects fourth-quarter sales growth to accelerate, supported by its holiday campaign, which will be the first real test of whether the brand's momentum can carry through the most promotional quarter of the year.

The record quarter does not settle the question hanging over every premium brand in a weakening consumer environment: how long can a company hold full price when shoppers are trading down? On's answer so far has been to hold its prices and let the product and the brand do the work, and the quarter's record sales and higher margin outlook suggest that stance is working for now. The test will come in the holiday quarter, when the company expects sales growth to accelerate as its seasonal campaign runs and new products reach consumers.

Founded in the Swiss Alps in 2010, On has grown from a running-shoe startup built around its CloudTec cushioning technology into a global sportswear brand present in more than 90 countries, with its LightSpray manufacturing innovation and its distinctive hollowed-out sole making its shoes instantly recognisable. The third quarter shows a company that is still growing fast, still expanding its margins, and still winning in Asia-Pacific, where sales rose by nearly a third. Whether the share price follows the fundamentals will depend on the holiday quarter and on whether the consumer slowdown that has hit other premium brands reaches On's runners, who have so far kept buying at full price.

Why it matters: On's record quarter matters because it shows a premium brand can still grow sales by double digits and expand margins at the same time, even in a market where most consumer companies are discounting to hold volume. The 31 percent jump in Asia-Pacific and the 25 percent rise in direct sales show the growth is coming from the channels and regions that carry the highest margins, and the record underlying margin excluding tariff refunds suggests the improvement is structural rather than a one-off windfall. The contrast with the share price, down more than 10 percent in a week, is the real story: the market is pricing in a consumer slowdown that has not yet reached On's order book, and the holiday quarter will show which side is right.

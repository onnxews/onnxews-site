Fed Survey: AI Now the Top-Cited Risk to the Financial System

Eighty-seven percent of market contacts named artificial intelligence as a salient risk in the Federal Reserve's latest Financial Stability Report, up from 50 percent in May.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Artificial intelligence has rocketed to the top of the list of worries about the American financial system, according to the Federal Reserve's latest Financial Stability Report, released on Friday.

Eighty-seven percent of market contacts surveyed by the Fed named AI as a salient risk, up sharply from 50 percent in the May survey. The jump makes AI the most-cited risk in the report by a wide margin, and it comes in a year when AI stocks have driven much of the market's gains.

The fall survey covered 23 market contacts and was conducted in September and October. The spring survey covered 20 contacts. The rise in concern is therefore not a quirk of a bigger sample: the share citing AI climbed by 37 percentage points in six months.

The next most-cited risks trailed well behind. A broad correction in risk assets was named by 52 percent of contacts, higher long-term interest rates by 48 percent, fiscal debt sustainability by 43 percent and cyberattacks by 43 percent. AI was the only risk cited by more than two-thirds of respondents.

The report groups the AI worries into three buckets. The first is elevated equity valuations, as investors pour money into AI companies at prices the Fed's contacts see as stretched. The second is cyberattacks, including threats from state actors and from frontier AI models themselves. The third is the complex financing behind the AI buildout, including the debt taken on to build data centers and buy chips.

The financing numbers are striking. Debt issuance tied to advanced computing has topped $680 billion so far in 2026, as of October, roughly five times the $130 billion issued in 2023. That debt now accounts for about 31 percent of all nonfinancial investment-grade bond issuance, up from less than 6 percent in 2023.

The stock of debt is growing just as fast. Outstanding public debt tied to advanced computing has passed $1.1 trillion, and the report warns that the total could exceed $3 trillion once guarantees and leases that have not yet begun are included. The report also flags so-called "circular financing" arrangements, in which money flows in a loop between AI companies, chip suppliers and their backers.

The Fed also warned that hedge fund leverage has reached its highest level since the data series began in 2013. The report reflects data as of October 28, 2026, and the survey itself was completed before this week's turmoil in Nvidia shares and before the midterm elections, so it does not capture the latest market moves.

The report's timing is hard to ignore. It landed in the same week that Nvidia, the company at the center of the AI trade, saw its chief executive resign and its shares slide. The survey was completed before that news, which means the 87 percent figure reflects concern that was already building, not a reaction to this week's headlines.

The Fed's job is not to pick winners in the market, but to watch for risks that could spread. The combination it describes, stretched valuations, heavy borrowing and the highest hedge fund leverage in more than a decade, is the kind of mix that can turn a single company's stumble into a broader problem. The report stops short of predicting trouble, but it is a clear signal that the central bank is watching the AI boom more closely than before.

The report does not say a crash is coming. It says the financial system is more exposed to AI than it was six months ago, through stock prices, through debt and through the sheer speed of the buildout. That is a message the Fed clearly wanted markets to hear before the year is out. Read the Federal Reserve's report

Why it matters: The Fed is not usually in the business of warning about a single technology, which is what makes this report notable: 87 percent of its market contacts now see AI as a risk, not just an opportunity. The concern is not that AI will fail but that the boom is being financed with borrowed money at stretched valuations, the same pattern that has preceded past busts. With Nvidia's shares sliding this week and hedge fund leverage at a record, the report reads less like a routine survey and more like a flag planted before the market turns.

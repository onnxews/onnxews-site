Germany Thrash Slovakia 6-0 to Book Their World Cup Place

Leroy Sané scored twice in Leipzig as Germany finished top of Group A, while the Netherlands and Croatia also qualified on Monday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Germany booked their place at the 2026 World Cup with a performance of ruthless efficiency, thrashing Slovakia 6-0 in Leipzig on Monday night to finish top of Group A. The win, in the final European qualifier, took Germany to 15 points, with Slovakia second on 12, and sends the Germans directly to next summer's tournament in the United States, Canada and Mexico. Slovakia will go into the playoffs in March.

The scorers told the story of a team hitting its stride. Nick Woltemade headed in from a Joshua Kimmich cross in the 18th minute, his fourth consecutive goal in his last three games for Germany. Serge Gnabry made it 2-0 in the 29th minute from a Leon Goretzka pass, and Leroy Sané scored twice before half-time, in the 36th and 41st minutes, both from Florian Wirtz assists — his first international brace since November 2021. Ridle Baku added a fifth in the 67th minute from a Gnabry pass, his first international goal in just over four years, and Assan Ouedraogo completed the scoring in the 79th minute, finishing from a Sané back-heel less than two minutes into his senior international debut. Germany led 4-0 at half-time, and Sané was denied a first career hat-trick only by a deflection off Ivan Schranz from an indirect free-kick.

The result was also a statement of intent. Slovakia had beaten Germany 2-0 in Bratislava in September — their first-ever competitive win over the Germans, and Germany's first-ever away defeat in a World Cup qualifier. Monday's reply was emphatic: six goals, a clean sheet, and a margin that equalled Slovakia's largest-ever defeat. Germany have now won five matches in a row, and captain Kimmich said afterwards that the team had "wanted to send a message."

The win secures Germany's 19th successive appearance at the World Cup finals, and their 21st qualification overall — the second-most of any nation, behind only Brazil's 23. It also guarantees Germany a high seeding for the final draw in December, which will shape their path through the tournament. For a team that has spent the last few years rebuilding after early exits at recent tournaments, the qualification campaign has been a return to something like the old order.

Slovakia, for all the scale of the defeat, still have a route to the tournament. Their second-place finish sends them into the playoffs in March, with the draw for those ties scheduled for November 20. They will take some comfort from the September win in Bratislava, which proved they can beat the best on their day; they will take rather less from a night in Leipzig when they were outclassed in every department.

Elsewhere on Monday, the Netherlands also qualified, beating Lithuania 4-0 in Group G. Frenkie de Jong set up Tijjani Reijnders for the opener in the 16th minute, and Cody Gakpo, Xavi Simons and Donyell Malen scored three goals in five second-half minutes. Croatia came from 2-0 down to beat Montenegro 3-2, with Nikola Vlašić scoring the winner, and the Croatians dropped just two points in their entire campaign.

There were big wins elsewhere too. Czechia beat Gibraltar 6-0 with six different scorers, Northern Ireland beat Luxembourg 1-0 on a Jamie Donley penalty — his first international goal — and Poland beat Malta 3-2, with Robert Lewandowski heading in, Kamil Wszołek scoring and Piotr Zieliński netting an 85th-minute winner. The European qualifying picture is nearly complete, and the final places will be decided in the playoffs.

For Germany, the work now shifts to preparation. The December draw will determine their group, and the months before the tournament will be spent building a squad that can go deep. On Monday's evidence, the building blocks are in place: a captain in Kimmich who sets the standard, a creative core in Wirtz, a goalscorer in Sané rediscovering his best form, and a debutant in Ouedraogo who needed less than two minutes to announce himself. The message Kimmich spoke of was received.

Why it matters: Germany's 6-0 win was more than a qualification; it was a correction. The September defeat in Bratislava was the worst result in the team's modern history, and the response — five straight wins, 15 points, a six-goal demolition of the team that beat them — is the kind of answer that defines a campaign. The World Cup draw in December will now treat Germany as a top seed, and the rest of Europe will have noticed that the team which looked broken two months ago is scoring for fun. Slovakia's consolation is the playoffs, but the night belonged to a German side that looks, suddenly, like a contender again.

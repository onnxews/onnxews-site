Trump Says Dana White Press Secretary Offer Was 'Only Kidding'

A day after announcing on Truth Social that he had offered the UFC chief the job, the president said it was a joke — but confirmed White is still coming to the White House on Monday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump said on Sunday that he was only kidding when he offered UFC chief executive Dana White the job of White House press secretary, ending a day of speculation over whether one of the most unusual job offers in recent political memory was serious.

The offer came on Saturday, when Trump posted on Truth Social that he was pleased to announce White had been offered the job of press secretary of the United States. The post named a slate of UFC fighters as White's supposed assistants, including Islam Makhachev, Khabib Nurmagomedov, Justin Gaethje, Daniel Cormier, Amanda Nunes and Kayla Harrison.

White leaned into the moment. He posted a photo of himself at the White House briefing podium on his Instagram story with the caption 'On to the next chapter,' and told reporters in London on Saturday evening that he was flying to Washington and planned to meet with Trump on Monday.

But speaking to reporters in Florida on Sunday evening, Trump said the whole thing had been a joke. 'I will tell you, I was only kidding,' he said, adding that White 'loves the idea' and that the post was 'just a little fun.' He pointed to the list of fighters as proof that nobody should have taken it seriously.

According to an insider familiar with the situation cited by CNN, White had rejected the unconventional offer. Trump, for his part, insisted that White was enthusiastic about the idea and called him a very capable guy, even as he repeated that the offer was not real.

The episode also caused a case of mistaken identity. Dana W. White, a former Pentagon spokeswoman who shares the UFC chief's name, posted on LinkedIn on Saturday that she had been receiving congratulations meant for the UFC executive.

The joke landed because the vacancy is real. The White House press secretary job has been open since Katie Zacharia left in October after only five days in the role, leaving the administration's daily briefing operation without a permanent face for close to a month.

The episode fits a pattern of the president using social media to float ideas and test reactions before walking them back. Trump has long used Truth Social to make announcements, needle opponents and float personnel moves, and the Dana White post — complete with a roster of fighters as deputy press secretaries — read from the start like a provocation as much as a proposal. The difference this time was how quickly it was taken seriously, with news outlets, sports media and social media users treating the offer as real for more than a day.

The attention also reflects how closely White is tied to the president. The UFC chief is a longtime Trump ally who has appeared at campaign events and hosted fights attended by the president, and the two have spoken publicly about their friendship for years. That closeness is why the offer, however joking, was plausible enough to dominate a news cycle — and why White's visit to Washington on Monday will still draw attention, even if the job is not on the table.

Trump said White would still visit the White House on Monday, but stressed that the trip had been planned for other reasons and that the meeting would not be about the job. 'Dana is actually coming tomorrow, actually, for other reasons,' he said, adding that he expected to have more to talk about after the meeting.

For now, the press secretary post remains vacant, and none of the reporting on the episode names a successor. The White House has not announced a formal process for filling the job, and the brief, joking episode underscored how unsettled the communications operation has become.

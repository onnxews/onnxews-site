Canucks Snap Seven-Game Skid With 6-3 Win Over the Golden Knights

Filip Chytil scored twice, including a short-handed breakaway, as Vancouver won in regulation for the first time since October 3.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Vancouver Canucks finally won again. Filip Chytil and Linus Karlsson each scored twice as Vancouver beat the Vegas Golden Knights 6-3 on Sunday night at T-Mobile Arena, ending a seven-game losing streak and earning the Canucks their first regulation win since October 3. Elias Pettersson added a goal and an assist, Drew O'Connor also scored, and Leevi Merilainen made 34 saves on 37 shots.

The Canucks, who entered the game at 4-17-1 and 2-8-0 in their last 10, scored early and never let Vegas back into it. Pettersson opened the scoring on a power play at 5:20 of the first period, hammering home a one-timer set up by Jonathan Lekkerimaki and Brock Boeser. Karlsson made it 2-0 at 9:49, and after Victor Olofsson pulled one back for Vegas on a power play at 13:30, Chytil restored the two-goal lead at 17:36, tipping in a Boeser shot. The Canucks led 3-1 after one period, and they were not done.

Chytil's second goal was the highlight of the night. Short-handed at 2:14 of the second period, he took a stretch pass from Jamie Oleksiak, broke in alone on Carter Hart and deked the Vegas goaltender for his first short-handed goal in the NHL — and his first goals since October 16, 2025. Chytil had only been activated from injured reserve on Friday, and the goal was a reward for a player who has spent more time in the trainer's room than on the ice this season.

"I never played 'PK' and now, finally, I was screaming to Jamie, 'Don't throw it off the wall, just shoot it in the middle,'" Chytil said.

Vegas, who entered the game at 13-6-3, kept coming. Braeden Bowman scored at 11:57 of the second period to make it 4-2, and Mark Stone pulled the Golden Knights within one on a power play at 1:24 of the third, his 417th point with Vegas, tying Jonathan Marchessault for the franchise record. But O'Connor answered 24 seconds later, scoring at 1:48 to restore the two-goal lead, and Linus Karlsson sealed it with an empty-net goal at 17:49. Hart finished with 21 saves, and Vegas outshot Vancouver 37-27 — but the Canucks scored on their chances, and the Golden Knights did not. It was Vancouver's fourth game of six or more goals this season.

The win ended a six-game point streak for Vegas, who had gone 4-0-2 over that stretch, and it gave Vancouver something to build on after a miserable start to the season. The milestones piled up for the Canucks: Pettersson's goal tied Bo Horvat for ninth all-time in franchise power-play goals, with 67, and tied Stan Smyl for seventh in power-play points, with 181. Boeser passed Andre Boudrias for 12th all-time in Canucks assists, recording his 268th, and extended his point streak to seven games. Hertl's assist extended his own point streak to seven games, with four goals and five assists over that span.

Vancouver head coach Manny Malhotra and Vegas coach Ryan Craig watched their teams trade goals, hits and words for 60 minutes, and the Canucks left Las Vegas with two points they badly needed. Vancouver followed the win with a 3-2 victory over the Utah Mammoth on Monday night, their second win in as many days.

The Canucks' next test comes on Tuesday against the Dallas Stars, according to the NHL's recap of the Vegas game. For a team that had lost seven in a row and had not won in regulation for six weeks, two wins in two days is a start — not a turnaround, but a start. The season is still young enough to save, and the Canucks, for the first time in a while, have something to feel good about.

Why it matters: Two wins do not fix a 4-17-1 start, but they change the conversation around it. The Canucks had not won in regulation for six weeks, and the seven-game losing streak had turned a bad season into a crisis. Sunday's win in Vegas — six goals, a short-handed breakaway, a goalie standing on his head — was the kind of performance that reminds a dressing room what it feels like to win. The test now is whether it was a one-off or the start of something. Tuesday against Dallas will tell us more than Sunday in Vegas did.

Qualcomm to Power SpaceX Starlink Mobile's Planned U.S. 5G Network

Qualcomm will supply the radio gear, baseband units and software for Starlink Mobile's planned U.S. terrestrial 5G network, part of a data-center pivot the company says could reach $15 billion by 2029.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

SpaceX has selected Qualcomm's Dragonwing infrastructure platforms to power Starlink Mobile's planned nationwide terrestrial 5G network in the United States under a multi-year agreement, with deployment beginning in 2027.

Qualcomm announced the deal on Wednesday, the same day it reported fiscal fourth-quarter results. The company said SpaceX will use its Dragonwing radio equipment, baseband units and software for the planned network.

The deal is part of Qualcomm's push to change what investors think the company is. The chipmaker booked about $650 million in data-center revenue in the quarter, mostly from custom silicon and connectivity work, and Chief Executive Cristiano Amon said he expects $5 billion in data-center revenue in fiscal 2027, rising to $15 billion by fiscal 2029.

A September agreement allowing Amazon to buy up to $60 billion of Qualcomm's AI chips and related data-centre products added the e-commerce giant to a client roster that includes Microsoft and Meta. Qualcomm valued the warrants to Amazon at about $1.8 billion when the deal was signed, with Amazon able to acquire up to 25 million shares as purchase commitments and spending milestones are met.

The company said revenue from two custom chip programs with hyperscalers would begin in the current quarter, ahead of a ramp-up of its AI accelerator products next year. Qualcomm's core smartphone chip sales fell 26 percent in the quarter, to $5.14 billion, as a global memory shortage pushed up handset prices.

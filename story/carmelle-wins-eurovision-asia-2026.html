Carmelle Wins First Eurovision Song Contest Asia for the Philippines

Nineteen-year-old Carmelle Collado wins the inaugural Eurovision Song Contest Asia in Bangkok with her dance-pop anthem Sobra-Sobra, scoring 211 points to finish 41 clear of host country Thailand.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Philippines has won the first ever Eurovision Song Contest Asia. Nineteen-year-old singer Carmelle claimed the inaugural title on Saturday, November 14, 2026, at the One Bangkok Forum in Bangkok, Thailand, winning with her dance-pop song Sobra-Sobra and scoring 211 points. She topped both halves of the vote, finishing 41 points clear of the host country, and becomes the first name in the record book of a contest the Eurovision family has spent years trying to bring to Asia. The European Broadcasting Union confirmed the result on the night.

The scale of the win was emphatic. Carmelle received 93 points from the professional juries in each participating country and 118 points from the public vote, topping both polls outright. Thailand, represented by the duo MANG x DAOU with the romantic pop song Aroma, finished second on 170 points, while Vietnam's Tóc Tiên took third with EM TIÊN on 164 points. Malaysia's Aina Abdul finished fourth on 152 points and Laos' Thinla completed the top five on 139 points, in a final featuring 11 countries.

Sobra-Sobra, which means Too Much, is a women's empowerment anthem that combines dance-pop and rap with traditional Filipino instruments, including the kubing and the kulintang. The song was written by Gianina Camille “Nico” del Rosario and Mart Sam Emmanuel Mat B. Olavides. Speaking after her victory, Carmelle described the song as a tribute to all Filipino women, telling the Associated Press: “I made history, since this was the first time. It's such a big responsibility for me, because this one is a global stage, so thank you everybody who voted for me.”

Carmelle, whose full name is Carmelle Collado, grew up in the Bicol region of the Philippines and first rose to prominence through televised singing competitions. She finished as runner-up on The Voice Kids, went on to win Tawag ng Tanghalan, and also appeared on Pinoy Big Brother, building a following that carried her onto the Bangkok stage. At 19, she is the first champion of Eurovision's Asian edition.

The contest brought together artists from 11 countries: Bangladesh, Bhutan, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Mongolia, Nepal, the Philippines, South Korea, Thailand and Vietnam, each performing in their own musical identity and language. The result combined the votes of 11 national juries with the audience vote, which counted for 50 percent of the result, with a Rest of the World vote awarding a set of points equivalent to that of one participating country. Votes were received from a total of 183 countries and territories, including the 11 taking part, a measure of how widely the first edition was watched.

The Grand Final was hosted by Thai television stars Woody Milintachinda and Cindy Bishop, and featured a specially commissioned opening performance, Bangkok Sounds and Senses, celebrating the host city's contrasting rhythms and creative energy. The evening also featured guest performances from Thai superstar Jeff Satur and Dara, the Bulgarian singer who won the 70th Eurovision Song Contest earlier this year with Bangaranga. Dara performed her winning song alongside her new single Bad Idea, before presenting Carmelle with the trophy.

The full final standings underlined how clear Carmelle's victory was. Behind the top five, Bhutan's Nyendra finished sixth on 126 points, South Korea's JUNHEE seventh on 113, Mongolia's Beck eighth on 94, Nepal's Pratik Himal Rai ninth on 69, Cambodia's Monetrey tenth on 61, and Bangladesh's Beatmosphere eleventh on 35 points. Beatmosphere's entirely a cappella performance, using beatboxing to recreate drums and basslines, was one of the night's most talked-about moments even without a high placing. The full standings are published by NME.

The contest was produced by Voxovation and Bangkok-based S20 Productions, sponsored by ZOOP, and aired in all the participating countries as well as on the official Eurovision Song Contest YouTube channel. It was the first Asian edition of a contest marking 70 years of Eurovision history, and the European Broadcasting Union described it as a major milestone that brought together an extraordinary range of musical talent, cultures and creative voices from across the region.

Martin Green, director of the Eurovision Song Contest, congratulated Carmelle and the Philippines on becoming the first-ever winners of Eurovision Song Contest Asia, calling it an historic achievement and saying that seeing Eurovision's spirit of unity through music take on a new life in Asia was a significant moment. Carmelle will now perform at the Eurovision Song Contest 2027 in Burgas, Bulgaria, though she will not be competing in the European contest.

Why it matters: This result matters because it plants Eurovision's flag on a new continent after years of false starts, and it does so with a winner who embodies the format's appeal: a 19-year-old singing in Filipino and English, mixing dance-pop and rap with traditional instruments, winning on both jury and public votes. The Philippines taking the first title gives the contest an instant storyline and a huge, passionate fanbase to build on, while the 183 countries and territories that voted show the audience is already global rather than merely regional. Carmelle's guest slot at Eurovision 2027 in Burgas will now carry the Asian contest onto the European stage, and the question for the organisers is whether the second edition can keep the momentum of a debut that, by any measure, worked.

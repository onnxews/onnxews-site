Russian Military Transport Plane Crashes in Siberia, Killing All 19 on Board

The An-12 went down near a village in the Kemerovo region during a scheduled flight. The Defence Ministry said a technical malfunction was the preliminary cause.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

A Russian military transport plane has crashed in Siberia, killing all 19 people on board, according to Russia's Defence Ministry. The An-12 aircraft went down on Tuesday in the Kemerovo region of southwestern Siberia, in an uninhabited area near a village, the ministry said.

The aircraft crashed during a scheduled flight, and emergency crews were sent to the site after reports of a fire. The regional governor, Ilya Seredyuk, said emergency crews had extinguished a fire at the crash site, and that the aircraft had gone down in an unpopulated area, sparing nearby settlements from the impact.

The Defence Ministry said the preliminary cause of the crash was a technical malfunction, and that a commission from the Russian Aerospace Forces was working at the site to establish what had happened. Russia's Investigative Committee has opened an inquiry into possible violations of flight safety rules, according to the ministry's statement.

The crash happened near the village of Yagunovo, in the Kemerovo region, a coal-mining area of southwestern Siberia. The aircraft was on a scheduled flight when it went down, and the ministry said all 19 people on board had been killed, including the crew, though it has not named the victims or broken down how many were crew and how many were passengers.

The An-12 is a Soviet-built four-engine turboprop transport aircraft that has been in service since the late 1950s, used to move weaponry and personnel and to carry out search-and-rescue missions. The type is a workhorse of the Russian military transport fleet, and the ministry said the aircraft that crashed was on a scheduled flight when it went down.

The Kemerovo region is a coal-mining area of southwestern Siberia, and the crash site near Yagunovo is in an uninhabited area, which meant the impact did not threaten nearby settlements. The governor said emergency crews had extinguished a fire at the site, and the ministry said a commission from the Russian Aerospace Forces was working there to establish what had happened.

Russia's Investigative Committee has opened an inquiry into possible violations of flight safety rules, according to the ministry's statement, and the Aerospace Forces commission will examine the circumstances of the flight. The ministry has not named the victims or broken down how many of the 19 people on board were crew and how many were passengers, and it has not said where the flight began or where it was headed.

The ministry said the preliminary cause of the crash was a technical malfunction, and that a commission from the Russian Aerospace Forces was working at the site. The statement did not say when the investigation would be complete, and the ministry has not named the victims or said how many of the 19 people on board were crew and how many were passengers.

The ministry's statement gave no further details about the flight, and the investigation by the Aerospace Forces commission and the Investigative Committee is expected to establish what caused the aircraft to go down. The crash happened during a scheduled flight, and the ministry said the aircraft went down in an uninhabited area near Yagunovo, where emergency crews extinguished a fire at the site.

Why it matters: This crash matters because it killed 19 people in a single accident involving a workhorse of Russia's military transport fleet, an aircraft type that has been flying since the late 1950s. The preliminary finding of a technical malfunction points to the question that will dominate the investigation: what went wrong with an aircraft on a scheduled flight in an uninhabited area, and whether the fault lay with the aircraft, its maintenance or the conditions of the flight. For the families of the victims, the investigation by the Aerospace Forces commission and the Investigative Committee will be the only way to know what happened, and the ministry has not yet named the dead.

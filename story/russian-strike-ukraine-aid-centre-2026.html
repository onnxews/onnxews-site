Russian Strike on Ukraine Aid Centre Kills at Least 26

Ukrainian officials said aerial bombs hit a school used as a weekly aid distribution point in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region. Tuesday was declared a day of mourning.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 3, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

At least 26 people were killed and five injured after Russian aerial bombs struck a humanitarian aid distribution centre in Komyshuvakha, in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region, on Monday, Ukrainian officials said. The dead included two rescuers and four police officers.

The target was a local school where aid was distributed weekly, and the strike came as residents arrived to collect supplies, according to Ukrainian reporting. Rescue teams worked through rubble as the casualty count climbed through the day.

Tuesday was declared a day of mourning across the Zaporizhzhia region. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had intelligence that the site was a civilian aid point and struck it deliberately; Russia has not publicly commented on the specific strike.

The attack came hours before a diplomatic opening: U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff announced that Russian and Ukrainian delegations would meet in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11 to discuss security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian measures.

The contrast between the strike and the planned talks underlines how far apart the two sides remain. Ukrainian officials said the bombing showed why any agreement must include enforceable protections for civilians, while the Abu Dhabi meeting is expected to test whether either side is prepared to move.

Jets Score Five in the Second Period to Rout the Stars 8–3

Winnipeg erased a two-goal deficit with a five-goal middle frame in Dallas, winning a fifth straight game as Mark Scheifele tied Dale Hawerchuk's franchise points record.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Winnipeg Jets scored five goals in the second period on Thursday night, November 12, turning a two-goal deficit into a rout in an 8–3 win over the Dallas Stars at American Airlines Center.

The win was Winnipeg's fifth in a row, a remarkable turnaround after a nine-game losing streak in which the Jets went 0–7–2. Winnipeg improved to 9–8–3, while Dallas fell to 10–6–2 and saw a four-game point streak end.

Mark Scheifele led the way with two goals and an assist, and in doing so reached 929 career points — 378 goals and 551 assists — tying Dale Hawerchuk for the most points in Jets and Atlanta Thrashers franchise history.

Dallas struck first, with Arttu Hyry scoring at 4:19 of the first period and Roope Hintz making it 2–0 at 18:32. Dylan Samberg pulled one back 1:11 into the second, and Scheifele tied it at 7:03.

David Perron, playing his 20th NHL season after signing a one-year, $850,000 deal with Dallas on October 16, deflected in his first goal as a Star at 11:14 of the second to restore the lead at 3–2. It lasted less than three minutes.

Dawson Mercer tied it at 13:55, Scheifele scored on the power play at 18:12 to put Winnipeg ahead 4–3, and Mercer struck again with nine seconds left in the period for a 5–3 lead. Mercer has now scored twice in back-to-back games.

Kyle Connor made it 6–3 one minute into the third, and the Stars pulled goaltender Jake Oettinger after he allowed six goals on 27 shots. Gabriel Vilardi scored at 15:12 and Cole Koepke followed 14 seconds later for the 8–3 final. Casey DeSmith, who replaced Oettinger, allowed two goals on three shots.

Vilardi and Connor each finished with a goal and two assists, Samberg and Koepke also scored, Cole Perfetti added two assists, and Stuart Skinner made 30 saves for the win.

Head coach Scott Arniel said the performance showed a group whose confidence is coming back after the losing streak — the kind of night that can change a season's direction. The win came at a cost, though: Jets defenseman Dylan DeMelo left the first period with a lower-body injury and is week to week, while Stars defenseman Lian Bichsel also left the first period with a lower-body injury and is out for the weekend.

The Jets now look like a different team from the one that lost nine straight, and the test is whether the turnaround holds once the schedule toughens, according to Reuters.

Scheifele's milestone deserves its own moment. Tying Hawerchuk at 929 points puts him alongside the player who defined the original Jets, and he did it in a game that also broke a losing streak and ended a four-game point streak for Dallas. Hawerchuk's record has stood since the franchise's Atlanta Thrashers years, and Scheifele — who has spent his whole career in Winnipeg — will now chase it alone.

The goaltending told the other half of the story. Skinner's 30 saves gave Winnipeg the platform to attack, while Oettinger's night — six goals on 27 shots before being pulled — will be the number Dallas remembers. The Stars are still 10–6–2 and in good shape, but a contender does not want its starting goaltender pulled in a home game, and the response in the next few starts will matter.

Why it matters: The Jets' win matters because of the swing it represents: five straight wins after a nine-game losing streak is the kind of reversal that can define a season, and it came with a franchise milestone — Scheifele tying Hawerchuk at 929 points — as the exclamation point. For Dallas, the concern is goaltending: pulling Oettinger after six goals on 27 shots is not how a contender wants to end a homestand. What to watch is whether Winnipeg's scoring depth holds up and whether DeMelo's absence tests a blue line that is already thin.

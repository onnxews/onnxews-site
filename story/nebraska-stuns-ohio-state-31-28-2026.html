Nebraska Stuns No. 16 Ohio State 31-28, Ending a Decade Without a Ranked Win

Anthony Colandrea accounted for four touchdowns and iced the game with his legs as Nebraska beat a ranked opponent for the first time since 2016, sending fans rushing onto the field at Memorial Stadium.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Nebraska beat No. 16 Ohio State 31-28 on Saturday, ending one of the most hard-to-believe streaks in college football and sending thousands of fans rushing onto the field at Memorial Stadium. The Cornhuskers had not beaten an Associated Press Top 25 opponent since a 35-32 win over No. 22 Oregon on September 17, 2016, a run of 31 straight losses to ranked foes, and they ended it on Senior Day in front of 85,699 fans at the 417th consecutive sellout in Lincoln.

Quarterback Anthony Colandrea was the difference. He completed 19 of 28 passes for 200 yards and three touchdowns, ran 11 times for 63 yards and another score, and accounted for 263 yards of total offence without a turnover. His biggest runs came on Nebraska's final possession, when he converted a third-and-2 with a five-yard scramble just before the two-minute timeout and then picked up six more yards on third-and-5 with 55 seconds left, after Ohio State had burned its final timeout, to let the Cornhuskers kneel out the win. Colandrea accounted for four touchdowns in the biggest win of the Matt Rhule era.

The game swung on a fourth-quarter interception. Nebraska went to the final period tied 21-21, and after John Hohl's 33-yard field goal gave the Huskers a 24-21 lead, the defence produced its biggest play when Jamir Conn battled Ohio State star Jeremiah Smith for a deep ball down the sideline and Victor Evans III came down with the interception near midfield. Nine plays later, Colandrea threw a 26-yard strike to Quinn Clark, who made a spectacular catch near the pylon and spun into the end zone to put Nebraska ahead 31-21 with 7:41 remaining.

Ohio State answered quickly, marching 75 yards in 10 plays capped by Bo Jackson's second one-yard touchdown run of the day to cut the margin to 31-28 with 3:52 left, but the Buckeyes never saw the ball again. Colandrea's two third-down conversions on the final drive, plus 10 third-down conversions on the day and two more on fourth down, kept the ball in Nebraska's hands until victory formation.

The Huskers built their lead with a physical running game and a near-perfect first half. After Smith put Ohio State ahead 7-0 on a 67-yard touchdown catch on the Buckeyes' third offensive play, Nebraska answered with an 11-yard touchdown pass to Luke Lindenmeyer, added a seven-yard strike to Kwazi Gilmer on a 13-play drive that consumed 7:07, and went up 21-7 on Colandrea's 13-yard touchdown run with 4:12 left in the half. Smith's 31-yard touchdown catch closed the gap before halftime, and Jackson tied it at 21 with 6:37 left in the third quarter on a 14-play, 76-yard drive.

The final numbers told the story of a game Nebraska controlled for long stretches. The Huskers outgained Ohio State 376-335, including 176 yards on the ground, and held the ball for just shy of 21 minutes in the first half alone. Jamal Rule ground out 70 yards on 23 carries, while Lindenmeyer finished with five catches for 53 yards to break Nebraska's single-season receptions record for a tight end, pushing his total to 40 and passing the previous mark of 38 set by Austin Allen in 2021. Smith still did enormous damage for the Buckeyes, catching six passes for 165 yards and two touchdowns, and quarterback Luke Fahey threw for 259 yards on 16-of-27 passing.

The drought Nebraska ended was a decade in the making. The last win over Ohio State had come in 2011, a 34-27 victory, and coach Matt Rhule had been 0-22 against ranked opponents before Saturday, his last win over a Top 25 team coming when he was at Temple and the Owls beat No. 19 Navy 34-10 in the 2016 American Athletic Conference championship game. Nebraska had twice this season let fourth-quarter leads slip away against ranked teams, and the third time, as the home crowd knew, was the charm.

Both teams are now 7-4, with Nebraska 4-4 in the Big Ten and Ohio State 5-3, but the consequences are very different. The loss effectively ended Ohio State's hopes of reaching the College Football Playoff, just days after the Buckeyes had climbed to No. 14 in the committee's rankings. Nebraska closes its regular season with its annual Black Friday game at Iowa, kicking off at 11 a.m. Central on CBS, while Ohio State hosts Michigan next Saturday in a rivalry game that now carries far less at stake than anyone expected.

Why it matters: This was a program-defining win, not just a streak-ender. Nebraska has spent a decade as the sport's great what-if, a proud programme eighth in all-time wins that had not beaten a ranked team in ten years and had not beaten Ohio State since 2011. Beating the Buckeyes the way they did it, by running the ball 45 times for 176 yards against the nation's 12th-ranked rushing defence and by converting third down after third down when the game was on the line, gives Matt Rhule something no speech could: proof, in front of a sellout crowd, that the rebuild is real. For Ohio State, the defeat closes the book on a playoff dream and turns next week's rivalry game into a salvage job.

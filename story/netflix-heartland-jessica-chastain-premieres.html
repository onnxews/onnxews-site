Jessica Chastain's Country-Music Mystery "Heartland" Arrives on Netflix

Chastain stars as a disgraced former country singer who returns to Nashville after her niece disappears in Shana Feste's mystery thriller.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Jessica Chastain's country-music mystery "Heartland" premiered on Netflix on Friday, bringing the Oscar winner back to Nashville for a story about a fallen star, a missing girl and a homecoming two decades in the making.

The film, written and directed by Shana Feste, stars Chastain as Misty Jones, a disgraced former country star whose niece, Charlie Heart, disappears. Misty returns to Nashville for the first time in two decades to help find her, and the search forces her to confront the life she left behind.

Charlie is played by Carter Faith, a singer making her feature-film acting debut. The cast also includes Garrett Hedlund, John Hawkes as Misty's ex-husband Clark, Jennifer Nettles, Ross Lynch and Paul Sparks as Misty's brother. The film runs 112 minutes.

The movie is Feste's first film set in the music industry since "Country Strong," released 16 years earlier. It screened at Soho House in Nashville on November 9, ahead of its streaming debut, and it was filmed mostly in Atlanta, where the Fox Theatre stands in for the fictional Dove Theater.

Music is central to the film. The soundtrack includes 15 songs, with Chastain performing three of them herself. Faith co-wrote one of the film's songs, "Still the Sparrow Sings," and the music is woven through the mystery rather than sitting on top of it.

Critics have been split on the film. ScreenRant called it a compelling drama smothered by a by-the-numbers thriller, praising Chastain's commanding performance while finding the mystery thin. Chastain's performance has drawn praise even from critics who were cooler on the film itself.

The Nashville setting is more than a backdrop. "Heartland" is built around the city's music world, and Chastain will return to Nashville on November 18 for the CMA Awards. She has said she has no plans to record an album of her own, despite singing in the film.

For Netflix, the film is part of a busy fall slate of star-driven originals. For Chastain, it is a return to the kind of character-driven drama that has defined her career, with a country-music twist that lets her sing as well as act.

The film's Nashville setting gives it a texture that a generic thriller would lack. The city is not just a location but a character, with its music venues, its industry and its history of stars who burned out and came back. Misty Jones is a fictional character, but her story echoes the real comebacks and flameouts that Nashville has seen for decades.

Streaming has changed how films like this find their audience. There is no opening weekend to judge, no box office to report, only viewing numbers that Netflix keeps to itself. The film's fate will be decided by whether it appears in enough recommendations and whether viewers finish it and tell their friends. The early critical split suggests it will be a word-of-mouth film, for better or worse.

Whether "Heartland" finds a wide audience will depend on word of mouth, as with most streaming releases. The early reviews suggest a film that divides critics but showcases its star, and that may be enough for a Friday night watch. Read the review from ScreenRant

Why it matters: "Heartland" is a test of whether a mid-budget, star-led drama can still break through on streaming, where the algorithm tends to favor either huge spectacles or true crime. Chastain's performance is the draw, and the Nashville setting gives the film a specificity that generic thrillers lack. The split reviews matter less than the audience response over the weekend: if viewers embrace it, Netflix has a quiet hit; if not, it becomes another Friday premiere that fades by Monday.

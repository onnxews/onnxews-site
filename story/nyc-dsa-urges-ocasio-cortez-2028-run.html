New York Democratic Socialists Urge Ocasio-Cortez to Run for President in 2028

The New York City chapter of the Democratic Socialists of America voted 291-123 on Saturday to urge Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez to seek the White House, the latest push from the left for a 2028 bid.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

The Democratic Socialists of America's New York City chapter officially urged Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez on Saturday to launch a presidential run in 2028, passing a resolution at its convention by 291 votes to 123. The resolution is not a formal endorsement, but it is the latest effort by democratic socialists to urge Ocasio-Cortez, one of the most prominent members of their movement, to run for the White House. The resolution passed 291-123 at the chapter's convention on Saturday, and it commits the 15,000-member New York chapter to encouraging a presidential bid and building the groundwork for a potential campaign.

Gustavo Gordillo, co-chair of the local DSA chapter, described the resolution as "a commitment to encouraging and building the foundation for a presidential campaign." Speaking at a press conference after the convention, he said: "AOC and New York City DSA are committed to building a stronger democratic socialist movement and DSA will create the conditions for a presidential campaign capable of advancing a working-class political agenda." Gordillo also told Al Jazeera: "If the US can elect Trump, I think we can elect Democratic Socialists," a line that captures the confidence running through the chapter after a strong midterm showing.

New York is not the only chapter pushing her to run. DSA chapters in Atlanta, Colorado, Los Angeles and Ithaca, New York, have already approved resolutions encouraging her to run. The vote also follows recent electoral gains for the movement, with a record eight DSA-endorsed candidates elected to federal office in the midterms, according to Al Jazeera. The chapter has supported Ocasio-Cortez in all five of her congressional general elections since her 2018 primary victory over a 10-term incumbent, and it recently endorsed New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

The resolutions offer a glimpse of the priorities the chapters want Ocasio-Cortez to champion, including imposing an arms embargo on Israel, abolishing U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and enacting Medicare for All and the Green New Deal. The resolution also outlines priorities including universal health care, tuition-free public college, public housing, labor protections and support for labor organizing, and it calls for stopping ICE and backing "the anti-war and pro-Palestine movements." The proposed presidential platform would focus on affordability, including housing, health care and child care, according to NYC-DSA co-chair Grace Mausser. "The top issues at the doors that we encounter are housing, healthcare, childcare – the hallmarks of affordability," she said. "Those would all become the core of a federal platform."

Ocasio-Cortez herself has not announced a campaign, and a spokesperson did not return a request for comment. She has said she would decide her next political move after the midterms, and she is openly considering a White House run or a U.S. Senate run. As she considers both options, she has said she is weighing her ethnicity, her personal life, her age and which path is more likely to deliver a win. She rejoined the DSA in August after letting her membership lapse, and the resolution frames her potential presidential bid as a chance to champion working-class voters and expand DSA's national influence.

The vote was not unanimous, and the debate exposed real tensions inside the movement. During debate before the vote, some members expressed opposition to the resolution over their belief that Ocasio-Cortez has strayed from DSA's core identity and its priorities, and they expressed concern that a resolution encouraging a run would confuse an endorsement process expected to follow. DSA members have had previous disagreements with Ocasio-Cortez and her policies: in 2024, the national DSA withdrew its endorsement of her, saying she had failed to meet the group's required criteria regarding the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. Gordillo downplayed the dissent, saying the organization rarely reaches unanimous votes on its resolutions and that members would be ready to become foot soldiers for a democratic socialist presidential campaign.

Mausser said the chapter's growing political influence meant its position would be closely watched by politicians across the country. "Now that we have put a mayor into City Hall and we've elected three socialists in the city, I think regardless of how a politician feels about DSA personally, they're definitely looking at what we say and how we're moving," she said. "We are one of the biggest political actors in the city, in the state, and the country, frankly." The convention was held following a strong showing by DSA-endorsed candidates in the midterm elections, and the chapter has yet to finalize a national presidential platform.

The NYC-DSA vote adds to mounting efforts on the left to persuade Ocasio-Cortez to enter the 2028 presidential race, following a debate within the national DSA over her possible endorsement and the launch of an independent campaign urging her to run, called "Ready for AOC." A presidential campaign would also allow for the "continued crafting of a platform that engages the working class," according to the resolution text. For now, the decision rests with Ocasio-Cortez herself, and with a national DSA organization that has not yet decided whether, or when, to hold a membership vote on a 2028 presidential endorsement.

Whether she runs or not, the resolution is an early bellwether for the direction of the Democratic Party's presidential race. The 2028 contest is already taking shape barely two weeks after the midterms, and the left is moving first, with organized chapters, a draft platform and a candidate they believe can carry it. Ocasio-Cortez has said she will decide after the midterms, and the midterms are now over, so the waiting may not last much longer.

Why it matters: This vote matters because it is the organized left moving first in the 2028 race, barely two weeks after the midterms. The DSA is coming off its strongest election cycle yet, with a mayor in New York City Hall and a record eight endorsed candidates in federal office, and it is using that strength to draft the most prominent democratic socialist in Congress into a presidential run. The resolution is not an endorsement, and the tensions inside the chapter over Ocasio-Cortez's record on Israel show the movement is not unified behind her, but the 291-123 margin is a clear signal of intent. It also connects to prior Orbit coverage of the 2028 field taking shape: the question now is whether the party's establishment treats this as a fringe push or as the opening move of a real primary fight.

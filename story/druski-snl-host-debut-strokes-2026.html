Druski Makes History as the First Internet Personality to Host Saturday Night Live

The 32-year-old comedian and viral star takes over Studio 8H for Season 52, with The Strokes returning as musical guests for the first time since 2020.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Saturday Night Live aired a brand new episode on Saturday, November 7, and this week's host is making history. Druski, the 32-year-old comedian and social media personality whose real name is Drew Desbordes, is hosting Studio 8H for the first time, becoming the first internet personality ever to serve as host of the NBC sketch comedy series.

Druski first gained popularity through his viral comedy sketches and his Coulda Been Records videos, building a following that translated from phone screens to television. Earlier this year he became the youngest person ever to host the BET Awards, and he recently announced a new Netflix comedy series, Collect and Praise, which premieres on December 1.

The booking is a milestone for a show that has spent five decades drawing its hosts from film, television, music and sports. Internet creators have appeared as guests and in cameos, but none had ever carried an entire episode until now. Druski's debut signals that SNL sees online comedy as a legitimate pipeline for hosting talent, not just as a source of viral clips.

The musical guests are returning favorites. The Strokes, the New York rock band fronted by Julian Casablancas, are making their fifth appearance on the show and their first since October 2020. The band released its seventh studio album, Reality Awaits, earlier this year, and after the show they are headed to Latin America for a series of concerts in Lima, Mexico City and Buenos Aires.

The episode continues Season 52, which has mixed show veterans such as Kenan Thompson with half a dozen newcomers eager to make their mark. The previous episode, the Halloween show, saw the return of celebrated characters including David S. Pumpkins, played by Tom Hanks, and Drunk Uncle, played by Bobby Moynihan, setting a high bar for the weeks that followed.

Druski's path to Studio 8H reflects how comedy careers are built now. He did not come up through stand-up clubs or improv theaters in the traditional way, but through short-form video, building an audience one sketch at a time until the audience was too large for television to ignore. His Coulda Been Records parody label became a recurring bit that drew in real musicians and athletes as guests.

The hosting gig also comes at a busy moment in his career. Between the BET Awards, the Netflix series and now SNL, Druski has moved in a single year from social media star to mainstream television presence. The question for Saturday night was whether his loose, improvisational style would fit a live sketch show that runs on tight timing and cue cards, or whether the show would bend to fit him.

For SNL, the booking is a bet on relevance. The show has spent recent seasons recruiting cast members with large online followings, including several TikTok creators, and bringing in an internet-native host is the logical next step. If Druski's episode performs well, it could open the door for other creators who built their audiences outside traditional television.

The episode aired live at 11:30 p.m. Eastern and 8:30 p.m. Pacific on NBC, and streamed live on Peacock, with episodes available on demand the next day. The next new episode is already scheduled for Saturday, November 14, with John Mulaney returning for his seventh hosting appearance and Phoebe Bridgers serving as musical guest.

Whatever the reviews say on Sunday morning, the booking itself is the story. Fifty-two seasons into its run, Saturday Night Live has handed its stage to a comedian who built his career on a phone, and the show's willingness to do so says as much about where comedy lives now as it does about Druski himself.

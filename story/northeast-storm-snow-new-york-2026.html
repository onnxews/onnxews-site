Northeast Storm to Bring Rain to the Coast and the Season's First Snow to the Mountains

A developing storm will bring rain to the Interstate 95 corridor and the season's first significant snow to the mountains of upstate New York and New England, with the heaviest snow expected Friday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

A developing storm will bring widespread rain and some accumulating wet snow to parts of New York beginning Thursday, November 12, with the best chance for significant snowfall in higher elevations. The National Weather Service says the storm will bring rain and gusty winds to the Northeast, with accumulating snow possible in higher elevations of the interior Northeast. The system is expected to strengthen into a nor'easter by Friday as it moves off the coast.

Rain is expected to spread into parts of New York on Thursday, with the storm continuing to affect the state through Friday and, in some areas, into Saturday. The National Weather Service in Albany expects widespread precipitation through Friday. In western New York, the weather service in Buffalo expects rain to spread across the region Thursday, with colder air arriving Thursday night that should allow rain to mix with or change to wet snow into Saturday.

The greatest potential for accumulating snow is across higher terrain in eastern and northern New York. The National Weather Service in Albany says the probability of more than 4 inches of snow has increased to 50% to 70% in the southern Adirondacks and eastern Catskills, where heavy, wet snow could develop from late Thursday afternoon into Friday morning. Up to a foot of snow could blanket the highest elevations of the Adirondacks and Vermont's Green Mountains, with the heaviest snowfall expected Friday afternoon and evening.

The highest snow totals are expected across the White Mountains of New Hampshire and Maine, the Green Mountains of Vermont, the Adirondacks in New York and parts of the Appalachians in West Virginia and Massachusetts, which could see 5 to 8 inches of snow. Widespread accumulations of 1 to 3 inches are possible across the Allegheny Plateau in New York and Pennsylvania. Outside the higher elevations, snowfall totals will generally remain below 3 inches. Winter storm warnings are in place for the Adirondacks and the higher elevations of the Green Mountains in Vermont.

For New York City and the surrounding lower elevations, the storm looks primarily like a rain event rather than an early-season snowstorm. The National Weather Service forecast for the city calls for rain Thursday into Friday, with about a half-inch to three-quarters of an inch of rain falling Thursday and additional rainfall possible Thursday night and Friday. The storm could also produce minor coastal flooding during daytime high tides Thursday and Friday along portions of the New York City-area coastline.

The storm will also bring severe weather to the Interstate 95 corridor. A few strong thunderstorms could develop across the Mid-Atlantic on Thursday afternoon, with Newark, New Jersey, and Philadelphia in the Level 1 threat zone and New York City just outside it. The main threats are isolated wind gusts and possible small hail, though most storms are expected to remain below severe levels. Wind gusts of 20 to 30 mph are possible across the region.

Forecasters caution that uncertainty remains over precipitation types and amounts, because relatively small changes in the storm's track could affect where the rain-snow line develops. The low-pressure system is expected to move east from the Ohio Valley before passing near or south of the New York City area on Thursday and lingering offshore on Friday. It is not expected to intensify rapidly until it is farther northeast of the region, which should limit the potential for strong winds across the New York City area.

Beyond the Northeast, the storm will stir up lake-effect snow around the Great Lakes as cold air moves across the relatively warm lake waters, with the most substantial accumulations expected in Michigan's Upper Peninsula, where totals could exceed half a foot. Some snow is also expected in valley cities including Albany and Binghamton in New York, Burlington in Vermont, Manchester in New Hampshire and Augusta in Maine. No snow accumulation is expected across major population centers along the I-95 corridor south of Maine.

By Saturday, the storm is expected to pull away toward the Canadian Maritimes, though lingering moisture and colder northwest winds could keep snow showers going across parts of the Northeast into Saturday morning. New York City is expected to dry out by late Friday, while Boston could see periods of rain and gusty winds through much of Friday evening. For most New Yorkers, forecasters say, rain will be the main impact Thursday and Friday, but travelers heading through the Adirondacks, Catskills and Tug Hill Plateau should watch the forecast closely as colder air turns rain into accumulating wet snow.

Why it matters: This is the Northeast's first real taste of winter, and it arrives as a split storm: rain for the millions along the coast, snow for the mountains, the classic early-season pattern that catches drivers off guard precisely because the valleys stay wet while the passes turn white. The forecast hinges on elevation and on a storm track that could still shift, which is why the snow numbers carry wide ranges and why the weather service is talking in probabilities rather than promises. The practical message is simple and familiar: the commute will be wet, the mountains will be wintry, and anyone driving between the two on Friday should expect the road to change under them.

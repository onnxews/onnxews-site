Trump Says Iran War Decision Coming Soon as Tehran Sends Nuclear Proposal Through Qatar

After meeting his national security team for the first time since the midterms, the president said he will soon decide whether to resume major strikes, keep up economic pressure, or cut a deal.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

President Donald Trump said Thursday that he will soon decide whether to resume full-scale war against Iran, after meeting with his national security advisers for the first time since the midterm elections. Speaking to reporters in the Oval Office, Trump said, "Now we will make a decision," adding, "You'll know soon enough."

Trump is weighing three options, according to U.S. officials: launching a new round of airstrikes, continuing to tighten economic pressure on Iran, or reaching an agreement that includes significant concessions to Tehran. He framed the choice around Iran's nuclear programme, asking whether the United States wants to go further with strikes to ensure it is dead and can never return.

The U.S. military is completing preparations for a possible resumption of major combat operations, though it has received no new directive from Washington, a U.S. official said. The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group is heading toward the Middle East but is not expected to arrive in the region before mid-November.

Meanwhile, Iran has sent the United States a nuclear proposal through Qatari mediators, a senior Iranian official told Reuters on Thursday. The proposal would only be discussed after Washington meets Iran's seven demands to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the official said, adding that the United States is reviewing the proposal.

The deliberations follow a midterm election in which Republicans lost both chambers of Congress, a defeat Trump and Vice President JD Vance have acknowledged was driven in part by higher gasoline prices tied to the war. Trump said this week he would do it again because preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon was the only answer in the long term. The war, launched jointly with Israel in February, has run for eight months, and Qatari mediators are circulating a draft deal that could open the way to direct talks.

Reporting: Reuters

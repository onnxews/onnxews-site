Six Killed in Krakow Market Square Shooting as Poland Begins Mourning

Six people were killed and 17 wounded when a gunman opened fire in Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday, and three more bodies were later found at his home, as Poland begins two days of national mourning.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Poland began two days of national mourning on Sunday after a gunman killed six people and wounded 17 others in a mass shooting in Krakow's historic Main Market Square on Saturday, in an attack authorities said was likely inspired by far-right ideology. Three more bodies were later found at the suspect's home, bringing the total number of dead to nine.

The attack began shortly before 3 p.m. local time on Saturday, when a man drove a car into the Main Market Square, the Rynek Glowny, a busy medieval square filled with shops and restaurants and popular with tourists. He opened fire at random people from the vehicle, using a military-type rifle. The shooting lasted about three minutes before armed police engaged the gunman and shot him. He was detained and taken to hospital, where he remained in a critical condition.

Six people were killed in the square, four women and two men, including three Polish citizens and a British citizen. At least 17 people were wounded, among them two police officers, one of whom was shot three times while arresting the attacker and is in a very critical condition, and five foreign nationals from Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal and Russia. The death toll was first reported as five and revised to six during the evening.

Police said the suspect is a 24-year-old Polish citizen from Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, about 200 kilometres north of Krakow. He had a firearms licence, and police said many weapons and a large amount of ammunition were found in his car. He was wearing military-style clothing and acted alone, according to police, who denied rumours of a second attacker and denied reports that an officer had died.

Hours after the attack, police searching the gunman's home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski found three more bodies: his 53-year-old father, his 52-year-old mother and his 21-year-old sister. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there were strong indications they were members of the gunman's immediate family, and local reports said his sister had been visiting her parents when the tragedy unfolded. The bodies were discovered shortly before 10 p.m. on Saturday.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said evidence suggested the suspect had far-right and ultra-nationalist views, and that investigators believed the gunman was influenced by far-right ideology. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz called the attack a terror attack directed against residents and tourists staying in Krakow. Police said the suspect had a firearms permit, and the defence minister denied reports that he had served in the Polish military. No definitive motive has been established, and the investigation is continuing.

President Karol Nawrocki declared national mourning for November 8 and 9, and Krakow's mayor ordered city mourning until November 11. In Krakow, the old town's Main Market Square and the surrounding streets remained closed on Sunday, with police allowing only essential workers to pass. The traditional trumpet call from St Mary's church on the square was suspended for 12 hours to commemorate the dead, the first time this has happened since 1939.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk travelled to Krakow and praised the police response, singling out the officers who confronted the gunman. Footage authenticated by BBC Verify shows the moment a police officer confronts the suspect on the ground and fires several rounds, before being injured himself. The speed of the police response, within minutes of the first shots, is credited with preventing a higher death toll.

The attack has shocked a country that has long viewed itself as safer than many of its Western European neighbours. The mass shooting adds to a sense of unease built by a string of school stabbings in October, which authorities said was influenced by violent and extremist content online. Leading daily newspaper Rzeczpospolita said in an editorial that the attack shatters Poland's sense of security, and security fears are growing as the country comes to terms with an act of violence of unprecedented scale.

The investigation is continuing, and casualty figures and investigative findings may change as prosecutors and police establish more facts. The suspect remains in hospital in a critical condition, and no charges had been reported by Sunday morning. For Krakow, one of Poland's main tourist attractions, the square that was filled with shoppers and visitors on Saturday afternoon is now closed and silent, as the city begins two days of mourning for the dead.

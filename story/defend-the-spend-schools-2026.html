Schools Must Now Justify Every Federal Dollar in 255 Characters, or Wait to Get Paid

Starting November 1, every recipient of U.S. Department of Education grant money must attach a written justification of no more than 255 characters to each payment request.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 2, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Starting November 1, every recipient of U.S. Department of Education grant money must attach a written justification of no more than 255 characters to each payment request. The rule, called 'Defend the Spend,' applies across early-childhood programs, K-12 districts, colleges, universities, and adult-education providers that draw down approved federal awards through the department's G5 grant system.

Under a September 28 department memo, each justification must identify the federal program, the expense categories and amounts, and the beginning and ending dates for the costs covered, and the amounts described must equal the total payment requested. The explanation must stand on its own, without additional narrative, despite the tight character limit.

Requests can be approved, returned for clarification, or denied. If a justification raises what the department calls procedural or internal-control concerns, the recipient is asked for more information and the payment is not approved until the clarification is accepted. In cases such as duplicate requests or unallowable costs, the department can reject the justification outright.

The department says the process is meant to improve transparency and follows a February 2025 executive order directing agencies to scrutinize federal payments. It points to a low flag rate in a similar process already used for grants co-administered with the Labor Department, and says normal processing times should not change.

Critics, including Democratic lawmakers led by Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi, warned in a letter to Education Secretary Linda McMahon that the requirement could slow funding for services such as reading instruction and special education. They asked for the rule's legal basis, how 'invalid or incomplete' requests will be judged, how long reviews could take, and whether the department has estimated the administrative burden, questions that matter most to small districts with limited finance staff.

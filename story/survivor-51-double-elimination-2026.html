'Survivor 51' Double Elimination Sends Kilby and Kristin Home on a Single Vote

The paired elimination twist returned on Survivor 51's eighth episode, and Danny 'Kilby' Kilby and Kristin Flickinger became its victims — voted out together on the only valid vote at Tribal Council.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Two castaways went home on Survivor 51 on Wednesday night — on a single vote between them. Season 51, episode eight, titled “Load the Ark, Two by Two,” aired November 11 on CBS and Paramount+, and it brought back the paired elimination twist first seen on Survivor 50: players pair up, compete together and live or die together at Tribal Council.

Danny “Kilby” Kilby, a 30-year-old game designer from Ontario, Canada, was the ninth person voted out of Survivor 51 and the tenth person to leave the game. Kristin Flickinger, a 49-year-old from Santa Barbara, California, was the second castaway voted off on Wednesday night. The pair became the first two members of the Survivor 51 jury, after last week's eliminated player, Brady Booker, left the game to receive treatment for an injured thumb.

The vote itself was chaos. Twelve players entered the episode and split into six pairs: Carter Krull with Cristian Chavez, Devin Way with Angelica “Jelly” Loblack, Kristin with Kilby, Thien An Nguyen with Eric Macksoud, Lewis Kelly with Sharonda Cox, and Maggie Nestor with Mike Pinsky. Lewis and Sharonda won immunity — and spaghetti — in a multi-stage challenge that began with all six pairs balancing a ball and ended with Lewis and Sharonda outlasting the rest.

At Tribal Council, the vote initially appeared headed toward Jelly and Devin. Then Jelly played a hidden immunity idol that protected both players and wiped out eight votes against the pair — doubling the idol's power by using it on a double elimination. Cristian Chavez also successfully played a Shot in the Dark, protecting both him and Carter Krull and nullifying another vote. That left a single valid vote against Kilby and Kristin Flickinger, sending the pair home.

The arithmetic was brutal. Because of the paired twist, Kilby and Kristin were effectively eliminated on half a vote each — one vote cast against the pair, by Devin, after every other vote at Tribal Council was negated. Kristin had played her own Shot in the Dark and drawn “not safe,” while Cristian had earned an extra vote earlier in the episode and transferred it to Eric Macksoud, who did not use it. Kristin and Cristian did not vote because both had played their Shots in the Dark.

The twist nearly caught the players off guard. Survivor 51 began filming on April 18, and the Coach and Chrissy paired elimination from Survivor 50 aired on April 15 — while the cast was already in Fiji — so the players had not seen it air before they left. But they clearly knew about it: they referenced the Survivor 50 episode in their confessionals, and producers have in the past shown players episodes airing during the pre-game period to keep them up to date.

The episode also settled some smaller scores. Kilby, who had been constantly in the strategic mix and, along with Maggie, made the play of the season with the forced rock draw, went out on the wrong side of a single vote despite never being turned on or bamboozled. Kristin, the oldest castaway in season 51, had been applying to be on Survivor for about 25 years, and host Jeff Probst has said he could see a season 51 player as a future Survivor host — with many assuming he meant her.

The rest of the episode kept the season's running theme alive: unreserved Mike slander. Mike Pinsky leaked the Kristin vote to Kristin after being brought in last minute, and Maggie shot back that being brought in last minute showed nobody trusted he could keep it under wraps — which he proceeded to prove. Carter, meanwhile, told the cameras he was done working with Mike and would rather start an entire new alliance, the Salt of the Earth, with Carter, Lewis, Cristian, Kilby, Thien An and Maggie.

Ten castaways remain after Wednesday's double elimination. The next episode of Survivor 51 airs November 18 on CBS and streams on Paramount+, running 90 minutes from 8 p.m. ET. The “Next Time on Survivor” preview teased another major twist, with the remaining castaways shown flipping the million-dollar coin — and with the jury now seated, the endgame is officially under way.

Why it matters: This episode matters because the paired elimination is Survivor's most divisive twist, and Wednesday showed why: two players who did nothing wrong went home on half a vote each, while an idol played at exactly the right moment saved two others. It is brutal, and it is also the twist working as designed — forcing players to choose partners carefully, because a partner's fate is your fate. Kilby and Kristin chose each other and paid for it together; Jelly and Devin chose each other and survived together. With ten players left and a million-dollar coin flip teased for next week, the season's endgame is now a test of whether the players learned the episode's lesson: in a paired game, who you stand next to matters as much as how you play.

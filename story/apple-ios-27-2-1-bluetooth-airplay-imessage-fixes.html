Apple Patches iPhone Flaws That Let Attackers Pair by Bluetooth and Snoop on iMessages

iOS 27.2.1 fixes nine security flaws, including one that let a remote attacker join a group chat and read its messages.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Apple released a round of security updates on November 9 that patch 10 vulnerabilities across the iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Vision Pro, including flaws that could let a nearby attacker pair with a phone without the owner's involvement and let a remote attacker join an iMessage group chat and read its messages. The headline update is iOS 27.2.1, which arrived just two weeks after iOS 27.2.

The updates cover an unusually wide sweep of Apple's software. Five of the flaws are fixed in all nine releases: iOS and iPadOS 27.2.1, 26.7.3 and 18.7.12, macOS Golden Gate 27.2.1, Tahoe 26.7.3 and Sequoia 15.8.3, watchOS 27.2.1 and 26.6.1, and visionOS 27.2.1. The breadth shows how deeply the affected components, including Bluetooth pairing and AirPlay, are shared across Apple's platforms.

Half of the flaws involve wireless pairing, the step where a phone agrees to trust another device. CVE-2026-113964, in the CoreUtils component, could let an attacker within Bluetooth range bypass the limit on pairing code attempts and pair with a device without any user interaction. CVE-2026-113965 could let a malicious Bluetooth device establish a pairing on its own, with no action from the owner at all. A separate Bluetooth use-after-free flaw, CVE-2026-113970, could corrupt process memory.

AirPlay has the same kind of problem. CVE-2026-113968 could let someone within Bluetooth range bypass the limit on AirPlay pairing code attempts, while CVE-2026-113969, in the rapportd component, lets a person on the same network do the same thing. Apple fixed the flaws by improving how its software tracks pairing state, adding checks and tightening memory management.

The most alarming flaw for everyday users is in Messages. Two vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-113960 and CVE-2026-113961, could allow a remote attacker to join an iMessage group chat and receive its messages. The flaw was reported by an anonymous researcher, and it is the kind of bug that needs no physical access and no trickery: an attacker who can exploit it simply appears in the conversation.

The remaining fixes cover App Intents, where CVE-2026-113963 could give an app access to sensitive user information, and the Brotli decompression library, where CVE-2026-111497 could lead to memory corruption when processing a crafted stream. The Brotli bug is already listed in the U.S. government's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and federal agencies were given until November 10 to patch it, although Apple does not say the flaw was exploited on its own devices.

Apple did not forget its oldest supported devices. iOS 18.7.12 and iPadOS 18.7.12 cover the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR and the seventh-generation iPad, and they include an additional flaw, CVE-2026-113971, under which a person with physical access could pair a device with an AirPlay receiver from the lock screen. The main iOS 27.2.1 update runs on iPhone 11 and later and on recent iPad models.

Apple credits itself for finding five of the vulnerabilities. An anonymous researcher reported both Messages bugs and CVE-2026-113964, and security researcher Laszlo Varady is credited with the App Intents flaw. None of Apple's advisories mention exploitation in the wild for the Apple-specific flaws, and the company says it does not disclose or discuss security problems until it has investigated them and a fix is available.

The fixes follow an earlier disclosure known as AirBorne, which exposed AirPlay vulnerabilities in Apple and third-party devices and put billions of devices at risk. The new flaws are different bugs, but they sit in the same neighborhood: the wireless protocols that let Apple devices find each other, pair up and share audio and video with minimal friction. That convenience is exactly what makes the flaws dangerous.

The advice is straightforward. Open Settings, then General, then Software Update, and install iOS or iPadOS 27.2.1 on every iPhone and iPad that can take it, and apply the matching updates on Mac, Apple Watch and Vision Pro. Apple notes that an update cannot be rolled back once it is installed, but given that one of these flaws lets a stranger read a group chat, there is little reason to wait.

Paolo Bulgari, Who Turned a Family Jewellery Business Into a Global Luxury Brand, Dies at 89

The grandson of a Greek silversmith who helped transform the family jewellery business into a global luxury brand synonymous with Italian glamour has died in Rome. He was 89.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Paolo Bulgari, the Italian jeweller who helped turn his family's business into a global luxury powerhouse whose creations were worn by film stars and royalty, has died at the age of 89. His family announced his death on Saturday, describing him as "the greatest jeweler of our time" and praising his humanity and entrepreneurial talent. The news was confirmed by the Bulgari company, now part of the French LVMH group.

Born in Rome in 1937, Bulgari was the grandson of Sotirios Voulgaris, a Greek silversmith from the Epirus region who left Greece and settled in Italy before founding what would become the Bulgari jewellery house in the late 19th century, with the family Italianising its surname. Voulgaris came originally from Paramythia, a mountain village in northwestern Greece renowned for its silver jewelry, and fled Ottoman rule before settling in Naples and opening a shop in Rome, adapting both his craft and his family name to his new home. Paolo, who joined the family business in 1960 after initially studying law, developed a reputation as one of the industry's leading experts in precious stones.

Working alongside his brother Nicola and later his nephew, Francesco Trapani, he helped transform the famed Roman jeweller into a global luxury group. The company's flagship brand, BVLGARI, which uses the classical Latin "V" in place of "U", expanded from its jewellery roots into watches, perfumes, accessories and hotels, while opening stores in major world capitals such as New York, Paris and Geneva. Turning away from the delicate aesthetic associated with Parisian jewelry houses, the Bulgari family embraced bold gemstones, rich yellow gold, generous proportions and unexpected combinations of color, drawing inspiration from ancient Rome and Italy's artistic heritage.

As the company's chair from 1984, he oversaw decades of growth built on the house's bold use of coloured gemstones and distinctive Italian style. Bulgari designs became favourites of celebrities ranging from Elizabeth Taylor, Richard Burton, Sophia Loren and Audrey Hepburn to more recent stars including Nicole Kidman, Angelina Jolie and Zendaya. Tall and silver-haired, he occasionally appeared alongside the stars wearing his creations, maintaining a characteristically reserved presence. Despite his association with wealth and glamour, friends and colleagues recalled a reserved man standing nearly 2 metres tall, about 6 feet 7 inches, who still enjoyed serving customers at the company's historic Via Condotti boutique, sometimes introducing himself simply as a salesman.

In 2011, the family sold control of their Milan-listed company to French luxury group LVMH in a deal worth about $5 billion, ending more than a century of family control. Paolo later described the decision as painful but necessary to secure the company's future and protect thousands of employees. LVMH CEO Bernard Arnault said on Saturday that throughout his life, Paolo had played a pivotal role in the success of the Bulgari brand, while preserving and embodying its Italian spirit and deeply rooted Roman heritage.

Bulgari's life was also shaped by the story of migration at the root of his own name. In 2019, he and his wife established the Paolo Bulgari Foundation to support young people through schooling and job training in disadvantaged neighborhoods on the outskirts of Rome. "Many people don't know it," he wrote in the foundation's introduction, "but at the origin of my surname there is a story of migration." According to Italian media, he continued sketching jewellery designs almost until the end of his life.

His funeral will be held Monday at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Rome. He is survived by his family, who announced his death in a statement on Saturday. Bulgari presided for decades over a brand celebrated by supermodels, Hollywood stars and collectors for its bold designs and distinctive use of color, and his death closes a chapter in the history of Italian luxury that began with a Greek silversmith's arrival in Rome more than a century ago.

The house Paolo Bulgari built is now one of the world's best-known luxury brands, but its identity still carries the choices he made: the bold colored stones over Parisian delicacy, the Roman inspiration over imported taste, and the family name — Italianised from Voulgaris — on boutiques from Rome to New York. His passing leaves the brand in the hands of LVMH, which has owned it since 2011, and the family foundation he created in 2019 continues his work with young people on the outskirts of the city where the business began.

Why it matters: Paolo Bulgari's death marks the end of the family era at one of the world's great luxury houses, fifteen years after the family sold control to LVMH. He was the last of the generation that turned a Roman jeweller into a global brand without losing the bold, colorful Italian identity that set it apart from its Parisian rivals. The sale in 2011 was, by his own account, painful but necessary; the foundation he created in 2019, and his insistence on telling the migration story behind his surname, suggest he understood that the brand's value lay as much in its history as in its stones. The house will go on under LVMH, but the man who made it global is gone.

Trump Swaps Judicial Nominee in Last-Ditch Bid to Fill Appeals Court Seat

The White House withdrew Judge Lee Rudofsky's nomination to the 8th Circuit and named Arkansas Supreme Court Justice J. Cody Hiland instead, racing the clock before Democrats take the Senate.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The White House withdrew and replaced one of President Donald Trump's judicial nominees on Tuesday, after opposition from key Republican senators cast doubt on his ability to fill a U.S. appeals court seat before Democrats take control of the Senate. The move is the latest sign of the frantic race to confirm judges in the final weeks of the lame-duck session.

Trump rescinded the nomination of Arkansas-based U.S. District Judge Lee Rudofsky to the St. Louis-based 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals and instead nominated Arkansas Supreme Court Justice J. Cody Hiland, the White House said on Tuesday, according to Reuters. The swap was announced the same day the House voted on the impeachment of the defense secretary and elected a new Republican leader, a measure of how crowded the lame-duck calendar has become.

Rudofsky's prospects had dimmed after the midterm elections as reports emerged that Arkansas' two Republican senators, John Boozman and Tom Cotton, favored someone else to fill the Arkansas seat on the court. The two senators' opposition mattered because judicial nominations traditionally move only with the support of the home-state senators, and the White House concluded that Rudofsky could not be confirmed in the time remaining.

Hiland, who has served on Arkansas' top state court since 2023, was the choice the Arkansas senators preferred. He said at an August event at a law school in Arkansas that he had expected to receive the appeals court nomination but was instead offered a federal district judgeship, a remark that now reads as a preview of Tuesday's announcement.

The switch drew a sharp reaction from some of the president's allies. Mike Davis, a Trump ally at the conservative legal activist group Article III Project, accused Boozman and Cotton on Friday in a social media post of blocking a qualified nominee, a public split inside the conservative legal movement over a seat that will shape the law across seven states for decades.

Rudofsky, whom Trump appointed as a federal district judge in Little Rock in 2019, had the backing of prominent conservative lawyers, who supported his record on the bench. His nomination to the appeals court had been seen as a promotion for a judge in the president's mold, and his withdrawal is a reminder that even in a lame-duck session, a nomination can fail without the support of the home-state senators.

The stakes are high because the clock is nearly out. The White House and Senate Republicans are racing to confirm as many new judges as possible before Democrats take control of the Senate in January after winning at least 54 seats in the November 3 midterm elections. Once the new Congress is sworn in, the Democratic majority will control the confirmation process, and pending Trump nominees will be returned to the White House.

The 8th Circuit is one of the prizes in that race. The appeals court hears cases from federal courts in Arkansas, Iowa, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota and South Dakota, and its rulings shape the law on everything from voting rights and abortion to environmental regulation and federal power across the upper Midwest and the Plains. A single seat on the court can tip the balance in closely divided cases.

The lame-duck confirmation push extends well beyond the 8th Circuit. Dozens of judicial nominees are pending, and the Senate has only weeks to process them before the session ends. Each confirmation requires floor time, and the calendar is crowded with the December 11 government funding deadline, the Epstein files fight and the unfinished business of the current Congress.

Whether Hiland can be confirmed in time remains an open question. His nomination must clear the Senate Judiciary Committee and win a floor vote, and the Senate's schedule in the coming weeks is already packed. If he is not confirmed by January 3, his nomination will lapse with the rest, and the seat will be filled instead by the incoming Democratic majority's choice, a very different kind of judge.

China's Trade Surplus Tops $1 Trillion as AI Boom Powers Exports

Exports jumped 35.2 percent in October as the AI buildout powered Chinese factories, pushing the year-to-date surplus to $1.04 trillion.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

China's trade surplus surged past $1 trillion in the first 10 months of 2026, official customs data showed on Tuesday, as a global race to build artificial intelligence infrastructure buoyed sales of high-tech products and helped offset weak spending at home.

Exports rose 35.2 percent in October from a year earlier in dollar terms, to $412.3 billion, only slightly slower than September's 36.3 percent surge and ahead of the 33.5 percent increase economists had expected. Imports grew even faster, climbing 41.6 percent to $307.1 billion, far above the 32 percent forecast and the fastest annual growth since May 2021.

The figures lifted China's cumulative trade surplus to $1.04 trillion for January through October, topping $1 trillion for a second consecutive year and reaching the milestone a month earlier than in 2025. The country is now on course to surpass last year's record surplus of $1.2 trillion.

The driver was not cheap consumer goods. It was the global buildout of AI infrastructure. Semiconductor exports more than doubled in value in the first 10 months, rising 111.1 percent, and total high-tech exports rose 47.9 percent, as Chinese factories shipped the chips, servers and networking equipment the world needs to build data centers.

Imports told a similar story. Purchases of technology products, critical minerals and energy supplies drove the import surge, and imports of high-tech products rose 72 percent, according to CNBC's analysis of the figures. Because imports grew faster than exports, the monthly surplus narrowed to $105.3 billion from $133.5 billion in September, the smallest monthly gap since April and below the roughly $125.8 billion economists had expected.

The surplus with the United States edged down to $32.71 billion in October from $34.01 billion in September, even as exports to the United States jumped 32.9 percent. The figures landed a day after China officially announced the extension of its trade truce with Washington by two months, a truce that had been due to expire on November 10.

The milestone is likely to sharpen tensions with major trading partners, who have pressed Beijing for more balanced trade. The figures highlight China's growing reliance on overseas demand to support an economy hobbled by subdued consumption and a protracted property slump, a dynamic that has kept factories humming even as households hold back.

The data lands days before the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, where President Xi Jinping and President Donald Trump are expected to meet for the third time this year. Economists expect China's exports to accelerate further in the fourth quarter before starting to slow from the first quarter of 2027, and the trade numbers will be part of the backdrop when the two leaders sit down.

For the rest of the world, the message is mixed. China's factories are winning the AI hardware race, and its export machine is running hotter than ever. But a surplus that keeps growing at this pace, built on high-tech goods rather than the cheap manufactures of the past, is also a standing invitation to the trade friction that has defined the relationship between Beijing and Washington for years.

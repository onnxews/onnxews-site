Llano River Crests at Highest Level in Decades as Texas Hill Country Floods

The Llano River crested at its highest level in more than 40 years near Junction, and floodwater is still moving downstream toward the Highland Lakes after a slow-moving storm soaked South Texas.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Runoff from a slow-moving storm that soaked South and West Texas over the weekend pushed the Llano River out of its banks through the Hill Country early this week, cresting at levels not seen in decades. The river near Junction peaked at 20.65 feet at 8:50 p.m. Central time on Monday, November 9, above its 16-foot flood stage and higher than its previous crest of 20 feet on October 19, 1985, according to U.S. Geological Survey readings posted by the National Weather Service. By 6 a.m. Tuesday, the river at Junction had fallen to about 16.4 feet, just above flood stage.

Downstream, the water was still arriving on Tuesday morning. The Llano River near Mason reached 19.13 feet at 5:35 a.m., above its 18-foot flood stage and higher than the 18.3-foot crest the weather service had forecast the night before. Farther east, the river at Llano stood at 11.74 feet at 6 a.m. and was still rising. The National Weather Service later recorded a maximum stage of 14.2 feet there in the 24 hours ending at 1 p.m. Tuesday, topping the previous crest of 12.6 feet set on June 1, 2016.

Moderate flooding was occurring at Llano on Tuesday afternoon, with the flood warning in effect until Wednesday afternoon. At 12 feet, the weather service warned, 'moderate lowland flooding makes secondary roads and low bridges in the flood plain dangerous to motorists.' That includes the low-water crossing of Ranch Road 3404, known locally as the Slab, near Kingsland. The river was expected to fall below flood stage late Tuesday night and keep dropping to 3.8 feet by early Sunday afternoon.

The rain that fed the rivers fell hardest far to the south. In two-day totals through 3 p.m. Monday, the weather service in Corpus Christi reported 9.69 inches four miles south of Ben Bolt in Jim Wells County, and 8.54 inches at sites near Benavides and Premont. A gauge two miles north of Laredo measured 6.78 inches, and Kingsville recorded 5.35 inches. Across west-central Texas, 2 to 4 inches was common, with Junction recording 3.56 inches.

The flooding turned dangerous for drivers in Kimble County on Monday. Members of the Texas Game Warden Search and Rescue Team pulled a man and a woman from an inflatable rescue boat at the edge of fast-moving, muddy floodwater after their vehicle was swept downstream. The agency did not identify the people or give the exact location. 'Never attempt to drive through flooded roadways. Turn Around, Don't Drown,' the game wardens said.

Roads across the region were closed as the water rose. The Kimble County Sheriff's Office reported that the Flatrock Lane bridge near Junction was closed because of flooding, and U.S. Highway 377 was closed south of Junction, where low-water crossings were under water. Ranch roads 2291, 3480, 385, 2169 and North Ranch Road 1674 were closed as well. Earlier Monday, the emergency manager in Edwards County reported water over roadways, including U.S. 377 and Ranch Road 674, with some spots under 2.5 feet of water.

The water is now headed into the Highland Lakes. The Lower Colorado River Authority said Monday that it expected to open at least one floodgate at both Wirtz and Starcke dams on Tuesday morning 'to move floodwaters downstream into Lake Travis.' The rain also added water to the reservoirs that supply Corpus Christi: the Nueces watershed stood at 37.5% full early Tuesday, with Choke Canyon Reservoir at 21.1% and Lake Corpus Christi at 79.8%.

Not every flood forecast came true. Oso Creek near Corpus Christi had been forecast to rise above its 20-foot flood stage over the weekend, but it peaked at 11.64 feet on Sunday afternoon and measured 10.79 feet early Tuesday, well below flood stage. Governor Greg Abbott had directed the Texas Division of Emergency Management on November 6 to mobilize more state emergency response resources ahead of the rain, including swift-water rescue boat squads, game wardens and helicopters with hoists. 'I urge all Texans to remain weather-aware, monitor local forecasts, avoid flooded roadways, and heed guidance from state and local officials,' Abbott said.

The storm system behind the rain is moving away, with the weather service's Austin/San Antonio office saying early Tuesday that it was 'broadening out' and would exit to the northeast. A dry cold front was expected to cross the region on Wednesday, bringing sunny skies, lower humidity and cooler mornings through Friday. But the weather service warned that until the rivers fall, drivers should not go around barricades or try to cross flooded roads. 'Turn around, don't drown when encountering flooded roads,' its flood warnings said. 'Most flood deaths occur in vehicles.'

Why it matters: The Llano River floods are a reminder that Texas flood danger does not only arrive in catastrophic, headline-making form: a slow, multi-day soaking can push a river past marks set in 1985 and 2016, close highways across several counties and put rescuers in the water, even without a hurricane in sight. The same water now flowing toward the Highland Lakes is also a small piece of good news for Corpus Christi, whose reservoirs have been running low. The storm lifted Lake Corpus Christi close to 80% full. The balance of the week will be about watching the rivers fall and the roads reopen, and about whether the next round of rain, expected late Saturday into Sunday, arrives before the ground has had time to dry.

Hoax Bomb Threats Disrupt 18 More European Flights as Campaign Spreads to Newsrooms

A fresh wave of threats grounded and diverted planes across nine countries and forced evacuations at broadcasters in Ireland and Britain. No explosives were found.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

A fresh wave of hoax bomb threats disrupted at least 18 more flights across Europe on Friday, and for the first time the campaign spread beyond aviation to newsrooms, forcing evacuations at broadcasters in Ireland and Britain.

Every affected aircraft landed safely, and searches found no explosive devices on any of them. But the scale and persistence of the threats, now in their third wave in a single week, have raised concerns about an apparently coordinated campaign to cause chaos for travellers.

The latest alerts affected at least 18 flights to or from Bulgaria, Cyprus, Czechia, France, Hungary, Italy, Portugal, Romania and the United Kingdom. At least nine flights involving Prague were affected, alongside four flights over France, two flights heading to Sofia and a Lisbon-to-Rome service. A Wizz Air flight travelling from Prague to Rome diverted to Venice, where passengers were evacuated after it landed.

The disruption, while widespread, was a tiny fraction of the day's flying. Eurocontrol, which coordinates air traffic across Europe, said 26,709 flights were scheduled on Friday, meaning the affected services accounted for just 0.067 percent of them. Hungary's defence minister said fighter jets had been scrambled three times to escort civilian aircraft through Hungarian airspace.

Friday's incidents followed similar waves on Tuesday and Thursday, when at least 35 flights were diverted or searched. The week began on Monday, when a Wizz Air flight from London Luton to Sofia was diverted to Budapest. By Friday night, the week's tally of disrupted flights had passed 50.

The new element on Friday was the targeting of media organisations. Staff were evacuated from RTE, Ireland's national broadcaster, in Dublin after an emailed bomb threat, and live programming fell back to recorded output. Threats also reached Sky in London, Nova TV in Sofia, Virgin Media Television, the Irish Independent, Newstalk and Today FM.

Investigators have not established who is behind the campaign. Some of the threats sent on Thursday were signed in the name of the Male State, a Russian far-right ultranationalist movement founded in 2016, but the group has denied involvement. Authorities have said there is no evidence so far that a terror group or a state such as Russia or Iran is directing the hoaxes, though inquiries are continuing.

The Irish government described the wider threats as likely a coordinated campaign of disruption, an assessment rather than confirmation of common authorship. The European Union's transport commissioner said 11 further threats against flights were reported on Friday, a count of reported threats that is distinct from the 18 flights disrupted.

For airlines and airports, the cost of the hoaxes is measured in diverted planes, evacuated terminals, fighter escorts and hours of delay, all triggered by emails that cost almost nothing to send. The threats also force difficult choices on the ground: every warning must be treated as real until it is cleared, which means searches, delays and disruption even when, as in every case this week, nothing is found. Investigations are continuing across several countries, and with no one yet identified, aviation authorities are braced for the possibility of further disruption in the days ahead.

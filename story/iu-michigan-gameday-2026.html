THE DROUGHT IS DEAD: No. 6 Indiana Wins 13-6 in OT for First Victory in Ann Arbor Since 1967

Josh Hoover threw the overtime touchdown and the Hoosier defense shut down a freshman's first start as Indiana ended 59 years of heartbreak at the Big House.

By Peter Osibajo · Orbit News Network · Today · 4 min read

ANN ARBOR, Mich. — The drought is dead. No. 6 Indiana beat Michigan 13-6 in overtime on Saturday, winning in Ann Arbor for the first time since October 21, 1967 — a 27-20 victory that capped a Big Ten championship season and a Rose Bowl trip.

Quarterback Josh Hoover threw the touchdown in overtime, and the Hoosier defense made the stand that sealed it, keeping Indiana's College Football Playoff hopes roaring. The Hoosiers are now 7-1.

It was a defensive slugfest in front of a sold-out 107,601 at Michigan Stadium. Indiana's defense, which entered the day leading the Big Ten at just 12.9 points allowed per game, strangled a Michigan offense starting a true freshman at quarterback.

Michigan benched former five-star Bryce Underwood and handed the offense to Tommy Carr — grandson of legendary Wolverines coach Lloyd Carr — for his first career start. Carr had led Michigan to all 21 points in last week's comeback win over Penn State, but against Indiana's front, the magic ran out.

The win erases the sting of last weekend's 19-15 loss to Ohio State — Indiana's first defeat since 2024 — and flips the season back onto a playoff track. The Hoosiers return home next Saturday for a Halloween homecoming date with Minnesota.

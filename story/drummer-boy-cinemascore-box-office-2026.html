Angel Studios' Drummer Boy Wins an A CinemaScore in Its Opening Weekend

The patriotic war musical took $3.8 million on Friday and is projected for a $9 million debut, close to its $10 million budget.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Angel Studios has another crowd-pleaser on its hands. Drummer Boy, the studio's patriotic PG-13 war musical, is being warmly received by opening-weekend audiences, earning a strong A CinemaScore, and it is off to a solid start at the box office in one of the busiest weekends of the fall.

The film took $3.8 million on Friday across 2,850 theaters, putting it in third place for the day, and Deadline projects a $9 million opening weekend. That would put the film close to breaking even in its first weekend, since it reportedly cost just $10 million to make, a lean budget by the standards of a wide theatrical release.

The A CinemaScore matters more than the Friday number. It is Angel's tenth A grade for its releases, on par with Sight, Cabrini, Bonhoeffer, Brave the Dark, Truth and Treason, The Senior, His Only Son, David and Young Washington, and above Homestead's B and The Shift's B-plus. Only Sound of Hope, The King of Kings and Sound of Freedom, which earned A-plus grades, sit higher.

Angel Studios has spent the past few years building a reputation for faith-driven crowd-pleasers, and Drummer Boy continues the trend. The studio calls it its first live-action theatrical musical, a description that captures the film's unusual mix: a war story and a family drama, told through music, from the band at the center of the project.

The film is directed by Ben and Joel David Smallbone, with Joel David starring in the title role. The supporting cast includes Lucas Leach, Ioan Gruffudd, Melora Hardin, Charles Esten, Beth Easdown, Iain Glen and India Brown, and the music comes from For King and Country, the four-time Grammy-winning band fronted by the Smallbone brothers.

Drummer Boy opened on Friday, November 6, against stiff competition. Deadline projects Godzilla Minus Zero to lead the weekend with a record-setting $30 million to $33 million debut for a live-action foreign-language film, while Warner Bros.' The Cat in the Hat also debuts this weekend. Third place in that company, with a near-break-even opening, is a result Angel will take.

The studio's model is built on exactly this kind of film: modestly budgeted, aimed at an audience that feels underserved by mainstream Hollywood, and powered by word of mouth rather than a massive marketing spend. An A CinemaScore is the fuel for that model, because it tells the next wave of moviegoers that the people who saw it liked it.

Faith-based and family audiences have repeatedly shown up for Angel's releases, from Sound of Freedom's breakout run to The King of Kings earlier this year. Drummer Boy is the latest test of whether that audience will turn out for a war musical, a genre with a much narrower track record than a drama or an animated biblical epic.

If the weekend holds to projections, Drummer Boy will have done its job: a $9 million opening on a $10 million budget, an A from audiences, and a clear path to profit as the film plays through the coming weeks. For Angel Studios, that is the formula that keeps working.

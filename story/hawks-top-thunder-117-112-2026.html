Hawks Top Thunder 117-112 as Former Champs Dort and Wiggins Return to OKC

Atlanta held off Oklahoma City on Wednesday, with Onyeka Okongwu scoring 28 and Jalen Johnson posting a triple-double, in a game defined by two familiar faces on the other side.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Atlanta Hawks beat the Oklahoma City Thunder 117-112 on Wednesday night in Oklahoma City, handing the Thunder their first home loss of the season in a game that belonged, in part, to two players wearing the wrong jersey.

Onyeka Okongwu led Atlanta with 28 points and nine rebounds, hitting five three-pointers, and Jalen Johnson posted a triple-double with 16 points, 12 rebounds, and 11 assists, his second of the season. Nickeil Alexander-Walker added 19 points and seven assists off the bench for the Hawks.

The story of the night, though, was Lu Dort and Aaron Wiggins. Both were traded away from Oklahoma City in the offseason, and both played their first game back in the arena where they helped the Thunder win the 2025 championship. Dort scored 16 points off the bench with two steals, and Wiggins added 14, and the home crowd welcomed them back with tribute videos and chants of "Luuuu" for Dort. Between them, the two former Thunder players scored 30 points off the bench for Atlanta, a reminder of the depth Oklahoma City gave up when it traded them.

The game turned on a stretch at the start of the fourth quarter. Atlanta led by 13 points at halftime, but Oklahoma City erased the deficit and tied the game 83-83 going into the final period. With Shai Gilgeous-Alexander resting on the bench, Wiggins and Dort hit three straight three-pointers to open the fourth, and Atlanta rebuilt its lead to as many as 13 points.

Gilgeous-Alexander, the league's reigning most valuable player, scored 34 points, but he managed only three in the fourth quarter after scoring 31 through three. Oklahoma City made one last push, scoring seven straight points in the final two minutes to cut the lead to four, but Dort contested Gilgeous-Alexander's three-pointer with 15 seconds left, and Okongwu grabbed the rebound and hit one of two free throws to seal the win.

The loss continues a rough stretch for the Thunder, who have now dropped four of their last five games after starting the season as the team to beat. Atlanta, meanwhile, has won back-to-back games and six of its last eight, and is playing its best basketball of the season.

Alexander-Walker added a family subplot to the night. He is the cousin of Gilgeous-Alexander, and the two have played against each other many times, but this was a game in which the cousin on the visiting team came out on top.

The Hawks now return home to host the Charlotte Hornets on Friday, with a chance to extend their winning run. The Thunder, meanwhile, have to find a way out of a slump that has cost them their home-court advantage and their grip on the top of the standings.

For Dort and Wiggins, the night was a reminder of what they meant to Oklahoma City, and of what the Thunder gave up when they traded them. The tribute videos and the ovation said as much as the box score: they were champions here, and the fans have not forgotten.

Why it matters: This game matters because it shows how quickly the balance of power can shift in the NBA. Oklahoma City was the defending champion and the favorite to repeat, but four losses in five games, including a first home defeat, suggest the Thunder are vulnerable, and that the trades that sent Dort and Wiggins away may have cost them more than they expected. Atlanta, meanwhile, is winning with a young core led by Johnson's triple-double and Okongwu's 28 points, and the Hawks are now a team to take seriously. The subplot of Dort and Wiggins beating their old team in their old building is the kind of story that makes the regular season worth watching.

Doctor Kills Building Manager in D.C. Gym, Wounds Four, Then Takes His Own Life

Police say a 55-year-old emergency physician shot the manager of his condominium building inside a Logan Circle gym, then fired on people in the street from his seventh-floor balcony before barricading himself inside.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

A doctor locked in a long legal fight over mold in his luxury condominium shot and killed the manager of his building inside a busy Washington gym on Thursday morning, then wounded four more people from his seventh-floor balcony before taking his own life, police said. The shooting, in the Logan Circle neighborhood about a mile north of the White House, shut down a stretch of 15th Street Northwest and put nearby schools on alert for hours.

The first calls came in shortly before 8 a.m., according to Metropolitan Police Department Interim Chief Jeffery W. Carroll. Officers were on the scene within 30 seconds. Inside the VIDA Fitness gym in the 1500 block of 15th Street Northwest, near P Street, they found James Stuart, 46, the manager of the Metropole condominium building, dead from a gunshot wound. Police say the gunman, 55-year-old Matt Wade Lambert, a board-certified emergency physician who lived in a penthouse in the building, shot Stuart with a handgun and then returned to his apartment.

From the balcony of his seventh-floor home, Lambert opened fire on people in the street with a rifle, wounding four more people, two men and two women. One of the four was in critical condition in an area hospital as of Thursday afternoon, and the other three had injuries that were not life threatening, police said. It was not immediately clear whether Lambert knew any of the four people he shot from the balcony.

Lambert then barricaded himself inside his apartment, where he broke a gas line and started a fire, Carroll said. Police negotiators spoke with him by phone for more than an hour while he complained about the homeowners association, and he fired on officers on at least two occasions during the standoff. He ultimately shot and killed himself with a shotgun, and officers who entered the apartment found him dead just before 11 a.m. Police began leaving the scene at about 11 a.m., and the alert status at nearby schools was lifted at 11:15 a.m.

Police recovered three firearms from Lambert's apartment, a handgun, a rifle and a shotgun, and Carroll said all three had been used during the incident, which suggested a level of planning. None of the three guns was registered in the District of Columbia, where local law requires residents to register firearms, and officers from the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives are investigating. The FBI, the U.S. Marshals and the National Guard were among the agencies seen at the scene.

Investigators pointed to a protracted dispute with the building's homeowners association as the apparent motive. Lambert had been suing the Metropole Condominium Association since March 2025, alleging that facade work on the building had caused water intrusion, mold and moisture damage in his penthouse, and court filings show he said he had not been able to enjoy the unit since 2024. His attorney, Joel Braithwaite, said Lambert had spent $300,000 out of pocket, and rising, on the litigation, and that Stuart was a witness in the case. The New York Post reported that Lambert had bought the penthouse for $1.1 million.

Lambert had also taken his complaints public. He showed a Washington Sun reporter the damage in his unit last year and talked about his problems with Stuart in particular, telling the reporter, in the paper's account, that if you wanted to see the building manager, he comes in the gym about 8 o'clock. People who knew Lambert professionally described him to USA Today as a genuine, good person and said they were shocked. Braithwaite said Lambert had been scheduled to start a new, highly paid job on November 16, and that it was the anniversary of the day he buried his mother. He did not have a spouse.

Mayor Muriel Bowser called the shooting a senseless act of gun violence and said the city was devastated. Janeese Lewis George, the mayor-elect, thanked first responders and said there was no longer a threat, noting that the gunman had acted alone. VIDA Fitness said its Logan Circle location would remain closed until further notice, and police set up a reunification center at the Stead Park Recreation Center at 1625 P Street Northwest for residents of the building.

Police have asked anyone with information to call 202-727-9099 or text a tip to 50411. The investigation is continuing, and detectives are still working to establish a formal motive and to determine whether the four people shot from the balcony were targeted at random. What is already clear is the sequence police described on Thursday: a targeted killing inside the gym, gunfire rained down on a crowded street from a balcony above it, and a standoff that ended only when the gunman turned his last weapon on himself.

Why it matters: The shooting stands out even in a year of grim gun violence for how precisely it was aimed at a single grievance: a years-long fight over a leaking penthouse that ended with the building manager dead in the gym where the gunman knew he would be at 8 a.m. It also raises the questions that will follow the investigation — how a doctor with no firearms registered in the District came to have three guns in his home, and whether the warnings in the lawsuit, the media interviews and the $300,000 in legal bills added up to a crisis anyone could have seen coming. For the Logan Circle neighborhood, the immediate aftermath is a closed gym, a damaged building and four wounded neighbors, one of them fighting for his life.

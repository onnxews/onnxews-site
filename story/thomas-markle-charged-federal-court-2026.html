Thomas Markle, Father of the Duchess of Sussex, Charged in Federal Court and Placed on House Arrest

The 82-year-old was arrested in Florida after FBI agents seized about 78 items from two Los Angeles storage units he had rented for nearly two decades.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Thomas Markle Sr., the 82-year-old estranged father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, was arrested by the FBI on Friday and charged with one federal count of possessing child sexual abuse material, the Justice Department announced.

Markle was arrested at Maravida Senior Living, a senior care home in North Lakeland, Florida, and made his initial appearance the same day in the U.S. District Court in Tampa. A magistrate judge ordered him released on house arrest, and he is not allowed to travel outside the Middle District of Florida and the Central District of California.

Prosecutors allege that Markle kept sexually explicit photographs of himself with prepubescent girls in two Los Angeles-area storage units he had rented since 2008. FBI agents executed search warrants at the units last week and seized about 78 items, including photographic slides, 35 mm film negatives and books containing suspected abuse material.

First Assistant U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli, who leads the U.S. Attorney's office in Los Angeles, said the allegations were extremely serious and that prosecutors would pursue Markle to the fullest extent of federal law. FBI Director Kash Patel said in a statement that Markle allegedly targeted innocent children and sought to steal their innocence.

The case began in October, when law enforcement learned that a person had signed a sworn statement about suspected abuse material found among Markle's possessions in the Philippines. Markle had lived in Cebu City in the Philippines and remained there until May of this year, before returning to the United States for medical care and recovering at a rehabilitation centre in Florida until his arrest on Friday. Federal prosecutors said the Philippine case is separate from the American one and that no charges had been filed in the Philippines as of Thursday.

The charge carries a statutory maximum sentence of 20 years in federal prison if Markle is convicted. He has not yet entered a plea, and his arraignment in California has yet to be scheduled, though prosecutors said it would likely take several weeks. The case will eventually move to California, where the storage units are located.

Markle has denied the allegations, calling the claims untrue. A criminal complaint contains only allegations, and he is presumed innocent unless proved guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in court. Prosecutors told the Associated Press that the seized material showed no evidence of victims in the United States, and that the investigation continues.

The arrest follows a report in October that Markle had a trove of images depicting child sexual abuse, a report his daughter said at the time left her deeply distressed. The Duchess of Sussex has been estranged from her father for years, and neither she nor the royal family has commented on the arrest.

For prosecutors, the next steps are the arraignment in California and the continued investigation, including whether any charges will be brought in the Philippines, where no charges had been filed as of Thursday. For Markle, the house arrest order means he will remain at the Florida care home under electronic monitoring while the federal case moves forward.

Matt Boldy's Hat Trick Powers Wild Past Maple Leafs 5–2

Boldy's sixth career hat trick, completed early in the third period, extended Minnesota's point streak to 10 games and handed Toronto a fifth straight loss.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Matt Boldy scored a hat trick on Thursday night, November 12, leading the Minnesota Wild to a 5–2 win over the Toronto Maple Leafs at Scotiabank Arena.

It was Boldy's second hat trick of the season and the sixth of his career, and it extended Minnesota's point streak to 10 games. The Wild improved to 14–3–4 and sit first in the Western Conference, while Toronto fell to 8–11–2 with a fifth straight loss.

Boldy opened the scoring at 17:53 of the first period, picking off an errant outlet pass from Troy Stecher and exchanging passes with Kirill Kaprizov before flicking the puck past Anthony Stolarz.

Toronto answered early in the second. Auston Matthews, assisted by Zack MacEwen, scored his first goal of November to tie it 1–1 at 4:05, and MacEwen made it 2–1 at 6:59.

Boldy tied it on the power play at 9:55 of the second, exchanging passes with Quinn Hughes at the point before blasting a one-timer past Stolarz, after Toronto defenseman Darren Raddysh took a penalty midway through the period.

The winner came at 3:59 of the third. Boldy took a drop pass from Ryan Hartman, after Maple Leafs winger William Nylander and defenseman Oliver Ekman-Larsson got their wires crossed on the rush, and slipped a wrist shot under Stolarz's glove for a 3–2 lead.

Michael McCarron extended the lead to 4–2 at 7:34 of the third, and Danila Yurov finished the scoring into an empty net at 19:03. Kaprizov finished with three assists, Yurov added an assist for a two-point night, and Jesper Wallstedt made 26 saves to earn his league-leading 13th win.

The win was Minnesota's fifth in six games and its seventh straight on the road, while Toronto has now lost seven of its last eight and is 1–7–0 over that stretch, a disastrous start to a six-game homestand.

The victory came at a cost. Wild center Joel Eriksson Ek left the game after a heavy collision with Toronto defenseman Jake McCabe in the second period and did not return. MacEwen led Toronto with a goal and an assist, and Stolarz stopped 21 shots.

Minnesota closes its road trip in Boston on Saturday, and the question for the Wild is whether Eriksson Ek will be available, according to Reuters.

Kaprizov's night was nearly as important as Boldy's. Three assists extended his point streak to 10 games, a run in which he has six goals and 12 assists, and his chemistry with Boldy is the engine of the Wild's attack. When the two of them are producing at this rate, Minnesota does not need much else — though Wallstedt's league-leading 13th win suggests the goaltending is holding up its end too.

Toronto's slide is now a full-blown crisis. Five straight losses, seven in eight, and a six-game homestand that opened with a defeat at home to the hottest team in the West. The Leafs are 8–11–2 and falling, and the questions that were background noise a month ago — about the defence, the goaltending, the coach — are now the story of the season.

Why it matters: The Wild's win matters because it underlines how complete their surge has become: a 10-game point streak, first place in the West, a hat trick from their best young winger and a league-leading 13 wins from their goaltender. For Toronto, the picture is the opposite — five straight losses, seven in eight, and a homestand that is already going wrong. What to watch is Eriksson Ek's status after the collision with McCabe, because the Wild's depth down the middle is the one thing that could slow them down.

Harry Styles, Billie Eilish and Sam Fender to Headline Glastonbury 2027 as Festival Returns

Sam Fender, Harry Styles and Billie Eilish will top the Pyramid Stage bill at Worthy Farm next June, with Cher taking the Sunday Legends slot at 81.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Harry Styles, Billie Eilish and Sam Fender will headline Glastonbury 2027, the festival announced on Wednesday, as the event returns to Worthy Farm after its fallow year with the youngest set of Pyramid Stage headliners of the 21st century. Fender will top the bill on Friday, June 25, Styles on Saturday, June 26, and Eilish on Sunday, June 27, with Cher taking the Sunday Legends slot at the age of 81.

The festival runs from Wednesday, June 23 to Sunday, June 27, 2027 at Worthy Farm in Pilton, Somerset, with gates opening to 200,000 fans on the Wednesday and music on the main stages from the Friday. There was no festival in 2026, a fallow year that comes around about every five years so the pastures can recover. The site has hosted the festival since 1970.

The three headliners are all under 35, a first for Glastonbury, and the youngest top of the bill since 1997, when The Prodigy, Radiohead and Ash headlined at ages 19 to 30. Styles and Fender are both 32, and will be 33 by the time the festival opens, while Eilish is 24 and will be 25. It is a deliberate changing of the guard for a festival whose headliners have often been veteran acts.

Styles will be making his Glastonbury debut. The former One Direction singer recently completed a 30-show residency at Madison Square Garden in New York, and his fourth album, "Kiss All The Time. Disco, Occasionally.", opened with more than 183,000 sales, breaking the record for a British artist on vinyl. His Saturday headline slot is the most prominent of the weekend and the one likely to draw the biggest television audience.

Eilish is returning to a stage she knows well. She became the youngest ever solo headliner when she topped the bill in 2022 at the age of 20, and her return makes her a two-time Pyramid headliner at 25. Fender, meanwhile, is headlining for the first time after playing the festival in 2022. His collaboration with Olivia Dean, "Rein Me In", became the longest-running Official No. 1 single of all time, spending 23 non-consecutive weeks at the top.

Cher's Legends slot is a record in its own right. At 81, she will be the oldest performer ever to take the slot, which traditionally goes to a veteran act on Sunday afternoon. She has not toured since 2020, and her career spans seven decades, with four Official No. 1 singles and two chart-topping albums in Britain.

The first wave of announcements covers 66 artists. Alongside the headliners are Massive Attack, Chase and Status, The xx, Lily Allen, Ezra Collective, Kraftwerk, De La Soul, FKA Twigs, Teddy Swims, Sombr, Little Simz, Sienna Spiro, The Streets, Kacey Musgraves, Primal Scream, Adéla, Robyn, Chvrches, Jamie T, Ellie Goulding, Lola Young and Mike D of the Beastie Boys. More acts are expected to be announced in the new year.

Tickets are already gone. They went on sale on October 4 and sold out in 42 minutes, according to Reuters, at £408 including a £5 booking fee, an all-time high price. Anyone without a ticket will have to wait for the resale or for the festival's coverage, which remains one of the biggest television events of the British summer.

Organiser Emily Eavis announced the lineup on the festival's website and Instagram. "We are so happy to be coming back after our fallow year and, as we head into winter after that peaceful period, we wanted to give you an early musical taster of what's to come at Glastonbury 2027 with this spread of incredible artists. We can't wait to see you down here on Worthy Farm in June for some much-needed good times!" she said. Radio Times published its report on the announcement at 10 a.m. on Wednesday.

Why it matters: Glastonbury's 2027 lineup is a statement about the future of the festival as much as a bill of acts. Three headliners under 35, led by two of the biggest pop stars in the world and a British guitar act at the peak of his powers, signal a generational handover after years in which the Pyramid Stage leaned on heritage names. The speed of the sellout — 42 minutes at a record £408 — shows demand is not the problem, and the gamble is whether a younger bill can carry the same weight as the veteran headliners who defined Glastonbury for decades. With Cher in the Legends slot bridging the generations, the festival is trying to have it both ways.

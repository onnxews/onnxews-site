Godzilla Minus Zero Roars to $31.6 Million and Tops a Booming Box Office Weekend

The Japanese sequel opened to $31.61 million in North America and $63 million worldwide, leading a weekend that nearly doubled the previous frame. The Cat in the Hat took second with $20.5 million.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Godzilla Minus Zero stomped to the top of the North American box office this weekend, opening to $31.61 million and setting a new record for a live-action non-English-language film. The Japanese monster sequel led a weekend that nearly doubled the previous frame, as moviegoers returned to theaters in force after a sleepy Halloween.

The Toho and GKIDS release earned $14.5 million on Friday across 3,254 theaters, then held strong through the weekend to finish with $31.61 million domestically. The opening sets a new North American record for a live-action foreign-language film, excluding The Passion of the Christ and its largely Aramaic dialogue. Worldwide, the film took an estimated $63 million for the weekend, according to Comscore.

Directed by Takashi Yamazaki, Godzilla Minus Zero is the direct sequel to 2023's Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One, and it follows Godzilla battling a new monster across postwar Japan. The first film was a surprise awards-season and box-office success, and the sequel's opening shows that appetite for the franchise has only grown. The film also topped the worldwide weekend chart.

Warner Bros.' animated The Cat in the Hat finished second with $20.5 million from 4,003 theaters. The Dr. Seuss adaptation, featuring Bill Hader as the titular cat, earned $6 million on Friday and came in slightly below its initial $20 million to $25 million projections. It still took the number two spot comfortably and added $30.5 million worldwide for the weekend.

Angel Studios' Revolutionary War musical Drummer Boy opened in third with $9.31 million from 2,850 theaters, one of the company's strongest live-action openings behind Sound of Freedom and this year's Young Washington. Its soundtrack also reportedly climbed the iTunes charts over the weekend, suggesting the film found an audience beyond the opening gross.

A24's Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything took fourth with $8.86 million, while the Indian epic Ramayana: Rise of a Legend rounded out the top five with $8.65 million domestically — though its worldwide weekend of $52.15 million made it the second-biggest film on the planet. A24's Club Kid earned $390,583 from only six theaters, a strong per-screen average for a limited release.

Overall, the domestic box office generated approximately $116.6 million from reporting films this weekend. The Associated Press reported that overall ticket sales were up 96.8 percent from last weekend, a dramatic rebound after the weakest stretch of the autumn. The weekend's strength was spread across the chart rather than carried by a single title.

The result is a reminder of how much the theatrical market has changed. A Japanese-language Godzilla sequel, an animated Dr. Seuss film, a faith-based musical, a true-crime documentary and an Indian mythological epic all shared the top five, and the weekend's biggest global story after Godzilla was a film from Mumbai. The audience that showed up was not one audience but several, all arriving at the same time.

Studios will be watching closely to see whether the momentum holds. The calendar now builds toward the Thanksgiving corridor, with Wicked: For Good arriving November 21 and Zootopia 2 the following week. If this weekend is any guide, the holiday season could be one of the strongest in years.

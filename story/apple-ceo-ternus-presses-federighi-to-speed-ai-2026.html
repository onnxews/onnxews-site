Apple's New CEO Is Pressing Its Software Chief to Speed Up AI — Starting With a Smarter Siri

Bloomberg reports that chief executive John Ternus urged Craig Federighi to move faster on artificial intelligence in a closed-door meeting, as Apple races rivals that are already shipping AI agents.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Apple chief executive John Ternus has pushed the company's software engineering chief to accelerate its artificial intelligence plans, according to Bloomberg News reporter Mark Gurman, who described the pressure in his Power On newsletter on Sunday. The report is the clearest sign yet that Apple's new leadership believes the company has been moving too slowly on AI.

Gurman wrote that Ternus urged Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, to 'accelerate the company's road map and move quickly beyond merely making necessary improvements to Siri.' The push came during a closed-door meeting, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Gurman. Apple has not publicly confirmed the account, and Gurman's sources are unnamed.

According to the report, Ternus is frustrated by what he views as hesitance inside Apple to embrace AI with the same enthusiasm as its rivals. Federighi has a reputation as an AI skeptic who has voiced concerns about the technology's privacy, security, societal and employment implications. Gurman argued that the context around him has shifted, writing that Apple no longer has the power to dismiss the AI efforts of companies like Meta, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic.

The first step in the faster road map, Gurman reported, is giving Siri finer control over Apple's own apps in iOS 27.3, which is expected to reach the public in December. Third-party app control would follow in spring 2027, giving Siri the ability to carry out tasks inside other developers' apps on a user's behalf. That would move Siri closer to the agentic assistants rivals are already shipping, including OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse.

Apple has already begun opening Siri up to developers. Earlier this month it opened Siri AI to all developers through an App Intents developer preview in the iOS and macOS 27.3 betas, letting outside apps plug into the assistant's new capabilities. The company has also placed new restrictions on employee AI use, according to a separate report, a reminder of how cautiously Apple has handled the technology internally.

The pressure from the top reflects a wider anxiety inside Cupertino. Apple was late to the generative AI wave, and its first wave of Apple Intelligence features arrived to mixed reviews, with the promised overhaul of Siri delayed. Rivals, meanwhile, have moved from chatbots to agents that can book, buy, file and act, and investors have rewarded the companies seen to be leading that shift.

Ternus himself is new to the top job, and the report frames the AI push as an early test of his leadership. Speeding up Siri is the most visible way to show progress, because the assistant is on every iPhone and is the product most users associate with Apple's AI ambitions. A Siri that can actually do things inside apps, rather than just answer questions, would be a genuine change in what the phone can do.

What the newsletter did not specify is which other parts of the road map Ternus wants moved up, or on what timeline. Beyond Siri, Apple has AI work spread across its devices, from on-device models to developer tools, and it is not clear which of those efforts the chief executive wants accelerated first.

For now, the December release of iOS 27.3 is the date to watch. If Siri gains the finer first-party app control Gurman described, it will be the first public evidence of the faster pace Ternus is demanding. If it slips, or arrives in a limited form, the questions about Apple's AI speed will only grow louder.

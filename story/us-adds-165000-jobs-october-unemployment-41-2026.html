U.S. Adds 165,000 Jobs in October as Unemployment Slips to 4.1%

Hiring blew past forecasts and earlier months were revised sharply upward, giving the Fed room to focus on inflation.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs in October, snapping back from a hiring slowdown the month before, the Labor Department estimated on Friday. The figure blew past economists' forecasts of roughly 60,000 new jobs and was more than triple the average monthly gain over the previous year.

Earlier months also looked stronger after revisions. The department now reports that employers added 60,000 jobs in September, up from the initial estimate of 29,000, and 186,000 jobs in August, a combined upward revision of 84,000. Professional and business services led October's hiring with 70,000 jobs, far above its recent average.

The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1 percent in October from 4.2 percent the month before. The labor force participation rate inched up to 61.9 percent from 61.8 percent. Fewer people are joining the workforce than in past years, which means employers need to add fewer jobs to keep the unemployment rate steady, though the number of people working part time for economic reasons rose by 339,000 to 4.8 million.

Average hourly earnings rose by 14 cents to $38.00 in October and are up 3.1 percent over the past year. Pay is still not clearly outpacing inflation: consumer prices rose 3.7 percent over the year in September, the most recent reading available.

The strong report likely gives Federal Reserve officials room to prioritize concerns about persistent inflation at their next meeting. As of Friday, most forecasters believed the central bank would raise its benchmark interest rate by a quarter-point in December, according to CME FedWatch. The Fed held rates steady at 3.75 to 4.00 percent at its meeting last week, with two officials dissenting in favor of an immediate increase.

Reporting: USA Today

At Least 38 Killed in Ambush on Market Convoy in Western Niger

Gunmen ambushed villagers travelling to a weekly market in the Tillaberi region, killing 37 people at the scene, local sources told AFP. A further victim died of wounds on Friday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

At least 38 people were killed when unidentified gunmen ambushed a convoy of villagers travelling to a weekly market in western Niger on Thursday, according to local sources. The villagers, residents of Tillim, were travelling under the escort of a local self-defence group toward the town of Tera in the Tillaberi region, near Niger's borders with Burkina Faso and Mali, when the gunmen struck.

A former elected official told AFP that 37 people were killed at the scene and that another injured person died of wounds on Friday, bringing the total death toll to 38. The dead included 11 members of the self-defence escort, 20 men and seven women, according to the local accounts, and residents said children were also among the casualties. Medical teams took several wounded survivors to a nearby hospital for treatment.

A resident of Tera told AFP the attack left "37 dead, including women and children", adding that "a large number of wounded" had been taken to the town's hospital. A local source told African Perceptions the death toll could be higher than reported, and that the ambush took place south of Tera. The gunmen have not been identified, and no group has been named as responsible in the reporting.

The attack is the latest in a wave of violence around Tera. It came in a region where armed groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State operate across porous borders and have repeatedly targeted villages, markets and travellers moving along rural roads. The Tillaberi tri-border zone has become the epicentre of insurgent violence in the central Sahel, ranking as the deadliest region for civilians last year, according to the conflict monitor ACLED.

The scale of the displacement shows how deeply the violence has cut into daily life. Nearly half of Niger's 450,000 displaced people live in Tillaberi, according to United Nations figures. Villagers in the region have increasingly relied on local self-defence groups to escort them to markets and between settlements, and those militias have themselves become targets. Niger's military junta has tolerated the village self-defence militias, which suffered 25 fatalities in similar ambushes in February, and plans to deploy civilian army auxiliaries.

Beyond the western tri-border crisis, Nigerien forces also battle Boko Haram and the rival Islamic State West Africa Province in the southeast. To counter the escalating regional threat, the military juntas governing Niger, Burkina Faso and Mali have formed a confederation and established a unified 5,000-troop force to combat militant groups across their shared borders.

The Tillaberi region, on the borders with Mali and Burkina Faso, frequently suffers deadly jihadist attacks. In 2025 it became "the deadliest in the Sahel", with civilians "the most targeted", according to ACLED. Over the past decade, Niger has been hit by deadly attacks by groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State.

Thursday's ambush underscores how routine journeys have become dangerous in the region. The convoy was travelling to Tera's weekly market, a trip villagers make to buy and sell food and supplies, under an armed escort. The gunmen opened fire on the villagers and their escort as the convoy travelled from Tillim toward the town.

The toll rests on local sources speaking to AFP and has not been confirmed by officials, and the figures may change as more information reaches Tera. What is clear from the accounts is the pattern: an escorted convoy, a market-day journey, and a death toll that includes the very self-defence fighters meant to protect the travellers, along with women and children.

For the survivors, the immediate questions are practical: who will escort the next market convoy, and whether the road to Tera is safe to travel at all. Niger, Burkina Faso and Mali have formed a confederation and a unified 5,000-troop force to fight militant groups across their shared borders, but whether that force can protect roads like the one to Tera remains an open question. For the families in Tillim, the loss is immediate: 38 people who set out for a weekly market and did not come home.

Why it matters: The ambush matters because it shows how the Sahel's insurgency has turned ordinary life - a weekly trip to market - into a journey that needs an armed escort, and still is not safe. Tillaberi is the deadliest region in the central Sahel for civilians, and the killing of the very self-defence fighters meant to protect the convoy underlines how exposed villagers are. The juntas' joint force was meant to change that, but attacks on escorted convoys continue, and the toll keeps rising.

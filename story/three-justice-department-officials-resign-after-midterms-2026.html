Three Top Justice Department Officials Resign in Days After Midterm Rout

The Miami prosecutor behind the 'grand conspiracy' probe, the national security chief and the civil rights head all stepped down within two days of the midterm rout.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Three senior Justice Department officials have resigned in the days since the midterm elections, an exodus that has stripped the department of the leaders of two of the Trump administration's signature initiatives just as Democrats prepare to investigate it.

Jason Reding Quiñones, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Florida, and John Eisenberg, the head of the department's National Security Division, both stepped down on Friday, November 6. Attorney General Todd Blanche announced both departures on social media. A day earlier, Harmeet Dhillon, the head of the Civil Rights Division, resigned after submitting her resignation in October and agreeing to stay on through the elections.

Quiñones was the most prominent of the three to leave. His office in Miami was running the so-called grand conspiracy investigation into the former intelligence and law enforcement officials who investigated President Donald Trump, a probe the president has promoted as a centerpiece of his second term. The investigation has produced no indictments, and department leaders had privately criticized Quiñones over its lack of progress, according to reporting by ABC News.

CNN reported that Quiñones resigned under pressure from department leadership, despite his willingness to pursue some of the most controversial investigations the president wanted. Quiñones, a former line prosecutor in the Southern District of Florida, was the first Trump-appointed U.S. attorney confirmed by the Senate, taking office on August 13, 2025. His office had also overseen the indictment of former Cuban leader Raul Castro.

Eisenberg's departure was also not entirely voluntary, according to senior administration officials cited by Bloomberg Law. Blanche was frustrated with Eisenberg's handling of media leak investigations, among other issues, and forced him out of the role. Eisenberg, who was confirmed and sworn in on June 5, 2025, led the division responsible for terrorism, espionage and foreign intelligence matters.

The resignations leave the Justice Department without leaders for two of its defining projects: relitigating the past investigations of Trump, and targeting higher education, where Dhillon's Civil Rights Division had been central to the administration's pressure campaign on universities. It is not clear why Quiñones or Eisenberg resigned, and neither has given a public reason for leaving.

The turnover had been expected for weeks for a variety of reasons, according to people familiar with the department. Some officials are expected to return to lucrative law firm jobs, and it is not unusual for senior officials to leave government after a midterm election, especially one that delivers a sharp setback to the party in power. But the speed of the departures, three Senate-confirmed officials in two days, is striking.

The exits come as the department faces a changed political landscape. Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress on Tuesday, and the party's leaders have said they intend to investigate the administration aggressively, including the Justice Department's election-related lawsuits and its probes of the president's adversaries. The department's leadership turnover will complicate its ability to defend those programs in the months ahead.

What happens to the investigations the departing officials ran is unresolved. The grand conspiracy probe has no indictments to show for more than a year of work, and it is unclear whether a successor will continue it, wind it down, or leave it in place. The Civil Rights Division's university cases face the same uncertainty. For now, the department is operating without permanent leaders in three of its most visible posts, and the next attorney general's choices for those jobs will say a lot about how the administration intends to use the Justice Department in its final two years.

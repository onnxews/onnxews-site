Lindholm Scores Four Goals as Bruins Outlast Capitals 7-5 in a Wild Night at TD Garden

Elias Lindholm recorded the first four-goal game of his career, and his hat trick went almost unnoticed, as Boston beat Washington for a second straight win.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Elias Lindholm scored a career-high four goals and added an assist as the Boston Bruins defeated the Washington Capitals 7-5 at TD Garden on Saturday. It was the first four-goal game of Lindholm's career and his first hat trick as a Bruin, the third of his career, and his five points matched a career high in a game that produced 12 goals and a hat trick almost nobody in the building noticed.

The strangest moment of the night came on Lindholm's third goal. He deflected Connor Clifton's shot from the high slot past Washington goaltender Charlie Lindgren at 18:24 of the second period to put Boston ahead 5-4, but because the puck was tipped, most of the crowd did not realise he had completed the hat trick. The horn sounded, the goal song blared, and the hats stayed on everybody's heads until a single cap finally fluttered down onto the ice. Only after the goal was announced did a few more hats follow. Even his own team mates did not realise what he had done.

David Pastrnak, who was on the ice for all four of the scoring plays and assisted on two of them, admitted he had no idea either. He said he went into the huddle knowing it was Lindholm's goal but somehow forgot about the first-period goal because it was a while ago, and only recognised the hat trick when he saw the first hat land on the ice. Lindholm himself laughed it off afterwards, saying one hat was enough and that it was good to see a couple more out there. When he scored his fourth goal early in the third period, another tip-in off a Pastrnak shot, the hats came pouring down properly.

Lindholm's goals told the story of a night built on traffic in front of the net. His first, at 12:42 of the opening period, was a power-play tip of a Pastrnak shot that tied the game 1-1 just five seconds into the man advantage. His second, at 8:54 of the second, came after goaltender Jeremy Swayman sent the puck up the boards and Morgan Geekie pushed it to a dashing Lindholm, who settled a bouncing puck, fended off two Capitals and wired it home to make it 4-2. His third and fourth were both deflections in front of Lindgren, the fourth coming at 4:07 of the third period to make it 6-4.

The game around him was a rollercoaster. Tom Wilson gave Washington a 1-0 lead at 8:03 of the first period, and Alex Tuch's short-handed goal put the Capitals back ahead 2-1 at 15:58. A six-goal second period followed, with Boston scoring four and Washington two, and the Capitals twice fought back to level the game at 4-4 on goals from Pierre-Luc Dubois and Dylan Strome by 14:08 of the second. Jakob Chychrun cut Boston's lead to 6-5 on a five-on-three power play at 15:28 of the third, but Fraser Minten's empty-net goal at 18:34 sealed the 7-5 win.

Lindholm was not the only Bruin to fill the scoresheet. Pastrnak had a goal and two assists, while Charlie McAvoy and Morgan Geekie added three assists apiece, with McAvoy's assist on Tanner Jeannot's goal marking his 300th career assist. Swayman made 30 saves and logged his first point of the year with the secondary assist on Lindholm's second goal. The win was Boston's second straight after a three-game home losing streak, and it lifted the Bruins to 15-7-1.

The night also put Lindholm's season in a new light. The 31-year-old forward has logged 10 points in eight games so far in November, has the second-most points on the team with 22, and is now tied with Pastrnak for the team lead with 11 goals. He became only the third Bruin since 2021 to record a four-goal game, joining Marat Khusnutdinov, who did it on January 10, 2026, and Patrice Bergeron, who did it on November 4, 2021. His linemate Geekie said Lindholm gets criticised for everything and does so many things that go unnoticed, and that it was unbelievable to see him rewarded like that.

For Washington, the defeat was its fifth straight in regulation, and the Capitals fell to 10-11-0 despite getting goals from five different players. Strome and Chychrun each had a goal and an assist, and Tuch, Wilson and Dubois also scored, but the defence and goaltending could not hold up against Boston's traffic in front of the net. The Bruins are back in action on Wednesday, when they host the Winnipeg Jets for a 7 p.m. matchup at TD Garden.

Why it matters: Four-goal games are rare enough, but this one said something bigger about where the Bruins are. Lindholm is a player his own team mates describe as underappreciated, a veteran who does the unglamorous work in front of the net and absorbs more criticism than praise, and on Saturday that work produced a career night in front of a home crowd that did not even realise it was watching a hat trick until the hats arrived late. For Boston, the win was a second straight after a three-game home skid, with the top line clicking and the goaltender contributing a point. For Washington, a fifth straight regulation loss, with five different goal scorers and still nothing to show for it, is the shape of a slide that needs stopping fast.

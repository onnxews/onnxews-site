Memory Chip Selloff Deepens as Korea Market Drops and Traders Fear Data-Center Curbs

Micron, SanDisk and SK hynix extended Wednesday's plunge overnight, and South Korea's KOSPI sank 4.6 percent with trading curbs triggered, as data-center worries spread.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Memory chip stocks are having a difficult week, and Qualcomm's latest earnings have given investors another reason to be cautious. Micron, SanDisk and SK hynix are extending their losses in overnight trading, each falling around 2.5 percent.

The additional selling follows a turbulent regular session on Wednesday that saw Micron tumble 7 percent, SanDisk lose 8 percent and SK hynix retreat 5 percent. Wednesday's initial selloff followed the sweeping Democratic midterm victories, which raised concerns about tighter restrictions on data-center construction.

South Korea's KOSPI slumped 4.63 percent to 5,420.77 on Thursday after regulators moved to cool a frantic AI-linked rally, with trading sidecars triggered as chip names led the fall. Japan's Nikkei lost 1.42 percent and Taiwan dropped 2.54 percent.

UBS estimates that roughly 30 percent of expected new U.S. data-center capacity in 2028 faces suspension risk, a warning that has weighed on the whole AI infrastructure trade. Bloomberg reported that Micron had largely suspended its Taoyuan Fab 11E project in Taiwan due to labor disputes, a project with a budget of $3 billion to $4.5 billion, though the company said its existing Taiwan operations were normal.

The memory squeeze is also hitting phones: global smartphone shipments fell 20 percent in the September quarter to a 14-year low, according to Counterpoint Research, as surging memory chip costs drove up device prices amid booming demand from AI data centres.

Anthropic IPO Demand Swamps the Order Book as Bankers Weigh Pricing Above the Range

Investor demand for Anthropic's IPO has run to several times the shares on offer, and bankers now expect pricing above the 70 to 78 dollar range, which could lift the valuation past 1.5 trillion dollars and total proceeds past 100 billion dollars.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Investor demand in the bookbuilding for Anthropic's initial public offering has run to several times the shares on offer, and Wall Street now expects the maker of the Claude artificial intelligence model to price above its existing range, potentially lifting its valuation past 1.5 trillion dollars and making the listing the largest capital raise in history.

Bloomberg reported on November 11, citing several people familiar with the matter, that Anthropic had received buy interest equal to several times the number of shares it plans to sell in the IPO during a roadshow that began on November 10. Most of the interest came from institutional investors seeking large allocations. The Financial Times reported that underwriters including Goldman Sachs expect the offer price to be set above the 70 to 78 dollar range filed on Tuesday, which would allow Anthropic to lift its valuation from the maximum of 1.4 trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis disclosed on November 10 to more than 1.5 trillion dollars.

That would push total IPO proceeds past 100 billion dollars, roughly 15 billion dollars more than the record 85.7 billion dollars that SpaceX raised through its IPO in June, and about 10 billion dollars above the maximum of 90.4 billion dollars that Anthropic cited in its filing. In an amended registration statement filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 10, Anthropic said it would sell 825 million shares to raise up to 64.4 billion dollars, and separately secure 26 billion dollars through core investors in a private placement. Based on the 74 dollar midpoint of the offer price range, Google and Nvidia will each invest about 10 billion dollars, AMD 2.5 billion dollars, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft 1 billion dollars each, and TSMC Partners, a TSMC subsidiary, about 500 million dollars, all through private placements.

The range implies a fully diluted valuation of about 1.33 trillion dollars at the midpoint and about 1.4 trillion dollars at the top, or up to about 1.02 trillion dollars on shares outstanding, according to Reuters. That compares with 965 billion dollars in May 2026. A fully diluted basis calculates a company's value by including shares that may be issued in the future, such as employee stock options and restricted stock units, on top of shares already outstanding. On the filing's fully diluted share count, a 1.5 trillion dollar valuation implies about 83 dollars a share, about 7% above the top of the range.

The demand is built on extraordinary growth. Quarterly revenue rose from zero at commercial launch in the first quarter of 2023 to 17.0 billion dollars in the third quarter of 2026, and the first nine months of 2026 brought in 33.2 billion dollars, against 2.5 billion dollars a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue of 16.96 billion dollars was up 1,127% from a year earlier and 47% from the second quarter. Run-rate revenue rose from about 71 billion dollars at the end of September to about 81 billion dollars at the end of October. Customers generating at least 100,000 dollars of trailing twelve-month revenue rose from more than 950 to more than 11,900 between September 2025 and September 2026, and net revenue retention was approximately 900% as of September 30, 2026.

The filing also shows the costs behind the growth. Anthropic reported a 104 billion dollar net loss for the first nine months of 2026, including a 94.3 billion dollar noncash fair-value charge on convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs. The S-1 discloses approximately 895.4 billion dollars of purchase and lease commitments, substantially all for compute and infrastructure, including at least 114.5 billion dollars with Google, at least 110 billion dollars with Amazon, at least 44.9 billion dollars with Microsoft and about 161.2 billion dollars of largely non-cancelable Broadcom equipment leases. About 69.8 billion dollars falls due within 12 months of September 30. As of September 30, Anthropic held 146.6 billion dollars of liquidity.

The stakes for early backers are enormous. The New York Times estimated that even if Anthropic set its valuation at only 1.4 trillion dollars, the stake Amazon acquired with its 18 billion dollar investment would be worth more than 250 billion dollars, while Google's 33 billion dollar investment would rise to 140 billion dollars. Amodei and six other Anthropic co-founders will each sell about 250 million dollars of stock in the offering. Anthropic is targeting about 20% of the offering for retail investors, roughly double the typical 5% to 10%, through Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi and E*Trade. Chief Executive Dario Amodei is scheduled to hold an online investor event on November 13 that will include JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon.

Pricing is expected on November 18, with first trades on the Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT. CNBC and Bloomberg report, citing people familiar with the matter, that pricing is planned for November 18 and first trades for November 19, though the filing does not set a date and all terms remain subject to change. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the underwriting alongside 20 other banks, with an option on a further 123.75 million shares. Some observers say Anthropic could raise its final offer price on November 18, the day before its listing, if demand holds, and the Seoul Economic Daily reports that Wall Street expects the deal to surpass SpaceX's record for the largest capital raise ever.

The IPO is the defining test of whether the AI investment boom can survive its own scale. Anthropic's revenue growth is real and extraordinary, but so are its commitments: nearly 900 billion dollars promised for computing, much of it to the same companies buying its shares. The circular financing, in which chip and cloud suppliers fund the company that pays them for capacity, has prompted questions from analysts, and the filing warns that growth in its run-rate revenue measure may slow, possibly sharply. For now, though, demand has overwhelmed every caution. If the book holds, Anthropic will list as one of the most valuable companies on earth, and the only question left will be whether the business can grow into a price that already assumes it will.

Why it matters: Anthropic's IPO is the moment the AI boom stops being a story about private valuations and starts being a public-market fact. The demand — several times the shares on offer, with pricing now expected above a range that already implied a 1.4 trillion dollar valuation — shows institutional investors are still willing to pay almost any price for a seat in the AI economy, even as sentiment toward AI infrastructure investment has cooled. But the filing is a warning as much as a prospectus: 895 billion dollars of compute commitments, a 104 billion dollar nine-month loss, and a supplier circle in which the companies selling Anthropic its chips are also buying its stock. If the deal prices above the range and passes 100 billion dollars, it will be the largest capital raise ever. Whether it is also a good investment depends on whether revenue can keep growing fast enough to cover promises that already run to nearly a trillion dollars.

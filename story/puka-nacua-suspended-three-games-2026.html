Rams Star Puka Nacua Suspended Three Games for Personal Conduct Violation

The NFL handed the Los Angeles wide receiver a three-game ban over a New Year's Eve incident, but he will serve it while on injured reserve and miss no extra time.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Los Angeles Rams wide receiver Puka Nacua was suspended three games on Thursday for violating the NFL's personal conduct policy, the league announced, in a punishment that will cost him about $950,000 in pay but no additional playing time.

Nacua is already on injured reserve, which requires a four-game absence under NFL rules, and the league will allow him to serve the suspension while on injured reserve. He will be eligible to return to the Rams on Monday, Dec. 14, following their game against the San Francisco 49ers one day earlier.

The suspension is connected to accusations from a woman in Los Angeles who alleges Nacua used an antisemitic statement toward her and bit her on the shoulder during an incident on New Year's Eve 2025. According to a lawsuit she filed on March 20, the alleged incident took place after a group dinner in the Century City area of Los Angeles.

Nacua has denied the allegations but agreed to enter a rehab centre for treatment in April, after checking in to address problems with alcohol. He played in the Rams' season opener and seven games in total before his injury and the suspension, while the league reviewed the incident.

The NFL also delivered a stern warning alongside the suspension. The league told Nacua that any further violation of the personal conduct policy, including failing to satisfy the conditions of his diversion program, would subject him to additional and more substantial discipline, including banishment from the league.

Nacua was placed on injured reserve on Thursday, Nov. 12, after suffering turf toe and sprained knee ligaments, an MCL and PCL sprain, in the Rams' win over the Washington Commanders on Nov. 8. He had already missed one game, in Week 10, and will miss three more after the Rams' Week 11 bye as a result of both his injured reserve stint and his suspension.

The Rams are 6-4 and idle this week. The three games covered by the suspension are against the Green Bay Packers, the Kansas City Chiefs and the San Francisco 49ers, and Nacua would be eligible to play against the Dallas Cowboys in Week 15.

Though Nacua will not miss any more time because of the suspension, he will have to forfeit three weeks of pay. The suspension will cost the fourth-year receiver about $950,000 in forfeited game checks, and he will also be barred from practising with the Rams or attending games while suspended. He will have to apply for reinstatement once the suspension ends.

Nacua is in the final year of his four-year rookie contract with the Rams and is in line for a massive raise before the 2027 season. The fifth-round draft pick's base salary this season is just over $5.8 million, and even the one-year franchise tag, an option for the Rams to retain his rights for next season, is likely to be in the $30 million range. The 2026 franchise tag figure for wide receivers was $27.298 million.

On the field this season, Nacua has 70 receptions for 715 yards and four touchdowns in seven games. In 51 career contests, he has 383 catches for 4,906 yards and 23 touchdowns. The Rams have already been playing without him and without Davante Adams, who has a hamstring injury, so the depth chart of Konata Mumpfield, CJ Daniels, Tutu Atwell, Xavier Smith and Brennan Presley will continue to carry the receiving load.

Nacua is not the first player to serve a suspension while injured. The most recent such case was Steelers defensive tackle Daniel Ekuale, who was suspended for five games in November 2025 for a performance-enhancing substances violation while already on injured reserve with a season-ending injury.

Why it matters: The suspension is a strange piece of discipline: three games, no extra games missed, but nearly a million dollars gone and a formal warning that the next violation could end his career. That tells you how the NFL and the players' union settled a case that had hung over the Rams since the offseason — punishment that stings financially without taking a star off the field any longer than injury already had. The real stakes are now contractual. Nacua is a free agent after this season and in line for one of the biggest receiver contracts in the league, and the Rams must decide whether to pay it, tag him, or let the off-field questions change the maths.

Wembanyama Drops 40 as Spurs Rout Suns 122-96 to Extend Win Streak to Nine

Victor Wembanyama scored 40 points with 11 rebounds as San Antonio improved to 9-2, the best start in the league, with a blowout win in Phoenix.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Victor Wembanyama was a force once again on Monday night, scoring 40 points with 11 rebounds and three assists as the San Antonio Spurs routed the Phoenix Suns 122-96 at the Mortgage Matchup Center in Phoenix. The win extends San Antonio's winning streak to nine games and improves the Spurs to 9-2, the best record in the Western Conference and one of the best starts in the league.

The Spurs turned the night into a decisive blowout, and Wembanyama was the reason. He scored from everywhere, dominated the glass and controlled the paint on both ends, and the Suns had no answer for a player who is simply too big, too skilled and too fast for a conventional defence. When a 7-foot-4 player can handle the ball, shoot from deep and protect the rim, the scouting report stops being useful, and Phoenix found that out the hard way.

San Antonio's nine-game winning streak is the longest in the league right now, and it has transformed the Spurs from a promising young team into a genuine contender. The streak has been built on Wembanyama's brilliance, but it has also been built on a team that defends, shares the ball and wins in different ways. Blowouts like this one are a sign of a team that is not just winning, but winning comfortably, and that is a different thing from scraping through close games.

The Suns, meanwhile, fall to 3-6, and the loss is another reminder of how far they have to go. Phoenix has talent, but it has not found a way to put it together, and a 26-point home loss to a team that is playing with this much confidence is a harsh measure of the gap. The Suns will need to find answers quickly, because the Western Conference is not waiting for anyone, and the teams at the top are pulling away.

Wembanyama's season keeps reaching new heights. Just days ago, he scored 52 points with 24 rebounds and eight blocks in a win over the Dallas Mavericks, a performance that had the basketball world talking about him as the best player in the league. The 40-point night against Phoenix is a follow-up, not a peak, and that is the most frightening thing about it: a 40-point, 11-rebound game is now a normal night for him, not a special occasion.

The Spurs' rise is also a story about patience. San Antonio spent years rebuilding, collecting young talent and waiting for the right moment, and the moment has arrived sooner than most people expected. The team is winning now, with a core that is young enough to stay together for years, and the rest of the league is starting to realise that the Spurs are not a team of the future any more. They are a team of the present.

For the Suns, the concern is not just the record, but the manner of the defeats. Losing by 26 at home to a team on a nine-game winning streak is not a disgrace, but it is a sign that Phoenix is not in the same class right now. The Suns have time to turn it around, and the season is long, but the gap between the top of the West and the middle is growing, and Phoenix is on the wrong side of it.

What happens next for San Antonio is a test of whether the streak can survive the grind of the season. Nine wins in a row is a statement, but the schedule will get harder, the injuries will come, and the rest of the league will adjust. The Spurs have the best player in the world right now, and that covers a lot of problems, but championships are won in June, not November. For now, though, the Spurs are the hottest team in basketball, and Wembanyama is the reason why.

No. 6 Indiana Heads to the Big House: Can the Hoosiers End 59 Years of Heartbreak at Michigan?

Indiana hasn't won in Ann Arbor since 1967. Today, a true freshman quarterback stands between the Hoosiers and history.

By Peter Osibajo · Orbit News Network · Today · 4 min read

ANN ARBOR, Mich. — The No. 6 Indiana Hoosiers walk into the largest stadium in America today carrying 59 years of history on their shoulders. Indiana has not won at Michigan Stadium since October 21, 1967 — a 27-20 victory that capped a Big Ten championship season and a Rose Bowl trip.

Kickoff is at noon ET on FOX, with Gus Johnson, Joel Klatt and Jenny Taft on the call. The Hoosiers (6-1, 3-1 Big Ten) are 7.5-point favorites over Michigan (4-2, 1-2 Big Ten) in a game both teams desperately need.

Indiana arrives stinging from last weekend's 19-15 loss to Ohio State — the program's first defeat since 2024. Quarterback Josh Hoover has been surgical all season, completing 63.9% of his passes for 1,660 yards, 18 touchdowns and zero interceptions. The Hoosier defense leads the Big Ten in points allowed at just 12.9 per game and has racked up 24 sacks.

But the headline today belongs to the other sideline. Michigan is benching former five-star Bryce Underwood and handing the offense to true freshman Tommy Carr — the grandson of legendary Wolverines coach Lloyd Carr — for his first career start.

Carr led Michigan to all 21 points in last week's comeback win over Penn State, but each scoring drive was set up by a defensive turnover. Now he faces an Indiana defense ranked fourth nationally against the run, allowing just 75.6 rushing yards per game.

Michigan is the only Power Four team in the country without 1,000 passing yards this season. The Wolverines rank in the bottom half of the nation in passing, rushing and scoring.

The weather at kickoff: 58 degrees, sunny, no chance of rain. The stadium: sold out at 107,601. The stakes: Indiana's College Football Playoff hopes and a drought older than most of the players' parents.

ESPN's SP+ model projects Indiana 26, Michigan 18. The Hoosiers won the last meeting 20-15 in Bloomington in 2024. Today they try to do what no Indiana team has done in nearly six decades: win in Ann Arbor.

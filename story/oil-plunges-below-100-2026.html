Oil Plunges Below $100 as Markets Bet Midterm Sweep Will End Iran War

Brent fell 8.7 percent to $94.62 a barrel, its lowest since August, while gold jumped and the dollar slid to a five-week low.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Global oil prices plunged on Wednesday as traders bet the Democratic midterm sweep would push Washington toward ending the Iran war, sending Brent crude to its lowest close since August 31.

Brent crude, the global benchmark, fell 8.67 percent to settle at $94.62 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate fell to about $88 a barrel. Reuters reported on Thursday morning in Asia that Brent had dropped by about 20 percent so far in the week.

"This is really a broad relief rally that I'd put at the feet of oil, and on the hope for a closer end of the Iran war," Rob Haworth, senior investment strategist at U.S. Bank, said on Wednesday afternoon. Energy stocks fell about 4.8 percent on the day.

Gold posted one of its best days in months, rising to about $4,137 an ounce, as investors sought safety and the dollar slid. The dollar index fell to around 101.25, its lowest level since September 30, after losing about 0.3 percent on Wednesday.

The yield on the benchmark 10-year Treasury note fell more than six basis points on Wednesday to about 4.88 percent, and slipped further on Thursday to around 4.85 percent, as traders priced in less government borrowing under divided government and a possible end to the Iran war.

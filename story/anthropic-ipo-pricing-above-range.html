Anthropic IPO Set to Price Above Its $70–$78 Range as Orders Swamp the Offering

The AI company is expected to price its blockbuster offering on Wednesday night, with demand running about seven times the shares on offer and trading set to begin Thursday under the ticker ANT.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Anthropic is expected to price its initial public offering above its $70 to $78 per share range on Wednesday night, after demand for the stock overwhelmed the offering in one of the most anticipated market debuts in years. Trading in the shares is scheduled to begin on Thursday on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT.

The company is offering 825 million shares, made up of 801,351,354 new shares and 23,648,646 shares being sold by existing holders. At the top of the range, the public offering would raise about $64.35 billion, and a further $26 billion private placement would take the total raised to about $90.4 billion. At $78 a share, the company would be valued at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

Reports on Tuesday pointed in the same direction. CNBC's David Faber reported, citing multiple sources, that the price was likely to come in above the range. The Wall Street Journal reported that the price could exceed $80 a share, which would value the company at about $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion. Bloomberg reported that Anthropic had told investors the price would be at the top of the range or higher.

Demand has been extraordinary by any measure. Orders ran to about seven times the shares on offer, according to reports, including several large orders of more than $10 billion each. The order book closed at 4 p.m. Eastern time on Tuesday, and existing investors are set to get priority in allocations. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, and 20% of the shares have been allocated to retail investors.

The private placement is a roll call of the chip and cloud industry. Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC are among the investors putting in $26 billion alongside the public offering, a show of support from the companies that supply Anthropic's computing power and host its models.

The financials behind the listing are as extreme as the valuation. Anthropic generated $33.24 billion in revenue in the first nine months of 2026, up from $2.5 billion in the same period of 2025, and its annualized revenue run rate reached about $81 billion by the end of October. But it also reported a net loss of $104.04 billion in the first nine months, of which about $94.3 billion was non-cash accounting adjustments tied to pre-IPO convertible securities. The company's founders retain 50.1% voting control.

Retail interest is running hot as well. Vanda Research expects about $250 million in net retail buying, which would more than double the $118 million record set by the SpaceX IPO. That offering priced at $135, jumped to $150 on its first day, briefly touched $225 and then fell to $104, before recovering to about $142. At least 11 leveraged single-stock exchange-traded funds from seven issuers are being prepared around Anthropic, and GraniteShares plans both two-times bull and two-times bear versions.

The Wall Street machine is in full swing around the deal. Goldman Sachs held a launch event for wealth clients on Tuesday attended by bank president John Waldron, Anthropic chief financial officer Krishna Rao and co-founder and president Daniela Amodei. JPMorgan plans to livestream the IPO for its clients and has prepared merchandise, including T-shirts and laptop covers, while the Amodei team spent Friday meeting investors at JPMorgan's offices. Anthropic declined to comment on the two events.

Investor enthusiasm is not quite unanimous. A StockTwits poll gave the stock an "extremely bullish" score of 85 out of 100, but 67% of more than 18,000 respondents said they would not buy at the IPO price, while 20% said they would and 13% said they would wait. Anthropic also carries about $900 billion in future commitments, with about $70 billion due through September 2027, a reminder that the company must keep spending heavily to grow into its valuation. The final price, expected on Wednesday night, will show how far the market is willing to go.

Why it matters: This IPO is the defining market event of the AI boom so far, and the numbers explain why it matters beyond Anthropic itself. A valuation above $1.4 trillion for a company that lost more than $100 billion in nine months would set a new benchmark for how much investors will pay for AI growth, and the $26 billion private placement from the chip industry shows how deeply the biggest technology companies are tied to Anthropic's success. The contrast between the seven-times oversubscribed order book and the two-thirds of retail investors who say they will not buy at the IPO price captures the tension: enormous institutional demand, real skepticism about the price, and a first trading day that will be watched as a verdict on the whole AI trade.

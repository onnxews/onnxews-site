Pochettino Starts as Manchester United Head Coach Four Days Before Liverpool Trip

Mauricio Pochettino reported for work at Carrington on Wednesday, hours after flying in from the United States, with his first match a trip to Anfield on Sunday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Mauricio Pochettino officially started work as Manchester United's head coach on Wednesday, November 18, reporting to the club's Carrington training ground hours after flying in from the United States. His first match in charge is on Sunday, when United travel to Liverpool.

Pochettino flew straight to Manchester after his final game as head coach of the United States men's national team on Tuesday night. The U.S. beat Haiti 2-0 in Cincinnati to win their CONCACAF Nations League quarter-final 8-0 on aggregate, a fitting end to his 26 months in charge of the American team.

The Argentine wasted no time. On arrival at Carrington, he told United's website, "I have plenty of energy," adding, "It's a privilege to be head coach of Manchester United. And we are not wasting time. We need to be ready to compete on Sunday." He added: "Now it's not time to think about rest, it's not time to think about sleep."

Pochettino's move to United came quickly. The club announced his appointment last Friday, just over 24 hours after sacking Michael Carrick, and Pochettino is understood to have signed a deal until the summer of 2029. He takes over a team in the middle of its worst start to a league campaign since 1986, and he has just a few days to prepare for one of the biggest fixtures in English football.

His time with the United States ended on a high, but it also ended abruptly. Only three months ago, Pochettino signed a new four-year contract to lead the U.S. through the 2030 World Cup, and the American federation had planned for him to be in charge for the tournament that the U.S. will co-host in 2026. United's call changed that plan in a matter of days.

Pochettino is no stranger to the Premier League. He managed Southampton and Tottenham Hotspur, taking Spurs to the Champions League final in 2019, and he later managed Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea. His reputation is built on developing young players and playing an aggressive, high-pressing style, and United's fans will be hoping he can bring both to a team that has struggled for consistency.

The challenge he faces is enormous. United have been through a series of managers since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013, and none has been able to restore the club to the top of English football. Pochettino inherits a squad low on confidence, a fanbase that has grown impatient, and a fixture list that starts with a trip to Anfield, where United have rarely won in recent years.

Told by former Manchester City loanee DaMarcus Beasley that "Manchester is blue, it will always be blue" in the TNT Sports US studio, Pochettino responded: "You will see. Give me time." He added: "The new challenge is a big challenge, but at the same time it's adrenalin you want to feel." He also said: "Very excited to be here," adding, "It's an honour. It's a privilege to be in this amazing club."

Sunday's game against Liverpool will be the first test, and it will be a harsh one. Liverpool are one of the best teams in the league, and Anfield is one of the hardest places to go. Pochettino has four days to prepare, and his first team selection will be watched closely for clues about how he intends to play.

Why it matters: This is one of the biggest managerial appointments in English football in years, and it comes at a moment of crisis for Manchester United. The club sacked Carrick after its worst start since 1986, and it has turned to a manager who was, until this week, preparing the United States for a home World Cup. Pochettino's record at Tottenham and his work with young players make him a credible choice, but the history of the post-Ferguson years is littered with credible choices who could not fix the club. The first test is Sunday at Anfield, and the first real judgment will come not from one game but from whether he can do what his predecessors could not: build a team that wins consistently.

'Resident Evil Requiem' Wins Ultimate Game of the Year at the 2026 Golden Joystick Awards

Capcom's Resident Evil Requiem took the top prize at the 44th Golden Joystick Awards on November 11, edging Phantom Blade Zero by a single percentage point — while a tiny co-op game matched its four-award haul.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Capcom's Resident Evil Requiem won Ultimate Game of the Year at the 44th Golden Joystick Awards on Tuesday, November 11, edging out Phantom Blade Zero by a single percentage point in the public vote. Requiem took 17 percent of the vote, Phantom Blade Zero took 16 percent and Crimson Desert finished third with 15 percent.

Requiem finished the night with four awards, also winning Console Game of the Year, Best Game Trailer and Best Lead Performer for Angela Sant'Albano's portrayal of Grace Ashcroft. Capcom had plenty of other reasons to celebrate: the publisher was named Studio of the Year, Onimusha: Way of the Sword won Best Audio Design, and Grace Saif won Best Supporting Performer for playing Diana in Pragmata.

The night's biggest surprise matched Requiem's haul. Meccha Chameleon, a viral co-op hide-and-seek game that two developers in Japan made in a couple of months, also won four awards: Best Multiplayer Game, Best Indie Game (Self-Published), the Streamer's Choice Award and the Developers' Choice Award. Dispatch was the only other game with multiple wins, taking Best Storytelling and Best Soundtrack.

The full list of winners spread the prizes more widely than last year, when Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 won almost every award it was up for. Crimson Desert won PC Game of the Year, Control Resonant won Best Visual Design, Valheim won Best Indie Game, Subnautica 2 won Best Early Access Game, Iron Lung won Best Game Adaptation, Doom: The Dark Ages — Revelations won Best Game Expansion, and The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Remastered won Best Remake/Remaster. Minecraft won the Still Playing award for PC and console, Balatro won it for mobile, and the Steam Controller won Best Gaming Hardware.

The Witcher 4 won Most Wanted Game, and Big Walk took the Critics' Choice Award — a result that may sit uneasily with some voters. Developer House House revealed this week that it used AI, specifically large language models, in the final phase of the game's development. While the studio says under 0.1 percent of the game's code had anything to do with generative AI tools, voting had already closed before the disclosure, so voters were not aware of the controversy when they cast their ballots.

The awards also carried a shadow that no winner could escape: Grand Theft Auto 6, Rockstar's much-anticipated blockbuster, was not in competition because it does not release until November 19. The same cutoff kept the Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake, which came out on Switch 2 on November 5, out of the race completely. Both games will be eligible for The Game Awards on December 10, but at the Golden Joysticks they will have to wait until next year.

Phantom Blade Zero's near-miss was its own strange story. The wuxia action RPG from S-Game was nominated on October 26, before it came out on PlayStation 5 and PC three days later — after the October 25 cutoff for Ultimate Game of the Year. The rules make exceptions for games the judges have already played a lot, and review copies were sent out weeks earlier, and it still came within a point of the top prize.

The Golden Joysticks are the first major awards show of the gaming year and an early indicator for the Game of the Year race, especially at The Game Awards in December. Unlike 2025, which had a clear frontrunner in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, this year's slate has been full of quality titles without one game singularly capturing the public's interest — and Tuesday's spread of winners, with prizes going to a lot more games than last year, reflects exactly that.

What happens next is the question hanging over every winner. GTA 6 arrives November 19 and will, as Polygon put it, “undoubtedly upend the awards landscape,” so later shows could look completely different from Tuesday's. The Game Awards on December 10 will be the first test of whether Requiem's win was the start of a run or a one-night stand — and whether the Big Walk AI disclosure costs the game the goodwill it earned from critics this week.

Why it matters: The Golden Joysticks matter because they are the people's awards — 22 of the 25 categories are decided by public vote — and this year's result says the Game of the Year race is genuinely open. Last year one game swept the board; this year the prizes spread across a dozen titles, with a four-way tie at the top between a horror blockbuster and a tiny co-op game made by two people. That spread is the story: 2026 has depth but no consensus pick, and GTA 6, arriving November 19, will scramble whatever consensus forms. The Big Walk AI disclosure adds a new fault line for the rest of awards season — whether voters will punish a game for using tools they did not know about when they voted. Watch The Game Awards on December 10 for the next chapter.

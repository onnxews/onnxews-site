Voters Reject Data Centers in 40 of 41 Local Ballot Measures Across 10 States

From Ohio to Kansas to California, communities voted to ban, pause or restrict the AI facilities. Only one measure, in North Dakota, went the industry's way.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Voters rejected data centers in nearly every local ballot measure on the issue in Tuesday's midterm elections, delivering a broad setback to the technology industry's plans for artificial intelligence infrastructure.

There were 41 local data-center measures in 10 states on the November 3 ballot, according to a Ballotpedia count. By Wednesday, 40 had gone the way of tighter limits, either by approving bans, moratoriums and new requirements or by rejecting measures that would have helped data centers get built. The results are still unofficial.

Ohio accounted for the largest share, with 21 measures across 18 jurisdictions, and every one with reported results voted against development. In Wilmington, four rezoning proposals connected to data-center projects were rejected by roughly 80 percent to 20 percent.

In Kansas, voters adopted some of the most restrictive proposals, including a 10-year ban on high-impact data centers in El Dorado, approved 71 percent to 29 percent. Voters in Alhambra, California, voted to ban data centers in the city, while voters in Edgewater, Florida, said no new data centers could be built there.

The one outcome widely described as favorable to the industry was in Harwood, North Dakota, where voters rejected an added review of industrial projects, including data centers, by 325 votes to 105. More votes are still to come, in Arkansas on December 1 and in North Dakota on December 15. In a CNBC voter poll, 56 percent of voters said AI and data centers were a crucial or major factor in their vote.

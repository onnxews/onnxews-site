House Blocks Hegseth Impeachment in 211-204 Vote, Sending Articles to Committee

The referral stalls the effort for this Congress, but Democrats are expected to revive it when they take control in January.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The House of Representatives voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday evening to send eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the House Judiciary Committee, effectively halting the effort to remove him for the remainder of this Congress. The procedural vote stopped the resolution from reaching a direct vote on the House floor and means the articles will almost certainly expire when the current Congress ends on January 3.

The vote split almost entirely along party lines. Voting in favor of the referral were 210 Republicans and the chamber's one independent, Representative Kevin Kiley of California. Voting against it were 203 Democrats and Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, the Republican who wrote the resolution and forced it onto the floor. Eighteen members did not vote, including 11 Democrats and seven Republicans, and former Speaker Nancy Pelosi was among the absentees.

Representative Darrell Issa of California offered the motion to refer the resolution shortly after 5:30 p.m., and the House approved it at 6:02 p.m. in Roll Call 317, the first and only recorded vote of the evening on the impeachment question. Because the referral carried, the House never debated the eight articles and never voted on whether Hegseth should be impeached.

Massie had reintroduced the impeachment articles on Monday, accusing Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanors related to the war with Iran and to United States strikes against drug boats in the Caribbean and the Eastern Pacific. Three of the articles deal with the Iran war, arguing that Congress never authorized the fighting, while others accuse Hegseth of ordering extrajudicial killings of suspected militants and ignoring laws meant to protect civilians in conflict zones.

The Pentagon has told Congress that the Iran war's cost reached 47.1 billion dollars through October 16, up from 43.6 billion dollars through September 3, according to Stars and Stripes. That rising price tag has become a central part of the political argument against the war, and Massie's resolution put it at the heart of the case for impeachment.

Massie, who lost his May primary to Trump-backed former Navy SEAL Ed Gallrein, brought the 34-page privileged resolution to the floor on Monday knowing that House Republican leaders would try to kill it procedurally. He cautioned before the vote that referring the resolution to committee would likely end the impeachment push for the remainder of this Congress, and he said afterward that he would not bring another resolution.

Hegseth rejected the allegations at a Pentagon briefing earlier on Tuesday, framing them as political rather than constitutional in nature. The referral gives him a reprieve, not an exoneration: the vote was on procedure, not on the merits of any of the eight articles, and the charges themselves were never debated or judged by the full House.

The outcome turned in part on attendance. NPR's Eric McDaniel described the vote as a game of attendance, noting that there were enough Democrats in Washington to pass the impeachment resolution but not enough on the floor to vote, so Republicans held a simple majority. Politico reported that Massie and the Democrats might have forced a floor vote if 11 Democrats had not been absent during the Tuesday vote.

The reprieve is likely to be temporary. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in the November 3 midterm elections and will hold the majorities needed to revive proceedings when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027. With about 260 seats in the new House, Democrats, virtually all of whom support impeachment, should easily be able to vote to impeach, since initiating impeachment in the House requires only a simple majority.

Removing Hegseth would be harder. Conviction in the Senate requires a two-thirds vote of senators present, or 67 senators if all 100 vote, and Democrats have won 54 Senate seats and may win up to 56, which would mean as few as 11 Republican defections would be required. Politico reported on Tuesday that at least six Republican senators have already expressed willingness to hear evidence against Hegseth. Representative Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to take the speaker's gavel in January, has flipped to a firm yes on impeaching Hegseth, and the fight that was shelved on Tuesday is all but certain to return in January.

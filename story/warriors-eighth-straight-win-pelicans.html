Warriors Win Eighth Straight, Blowing Out Pelicans 138-87 After an 0-3 Start

Stephen Curry and Brandin Podziemski scored 25 points each as Golden State completed a stunning turnaround, going from three straight losses to the hottest team in the NBA.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Golden State Warriors have won eight straight games after blowing out the New Orleans Pelicans 138-87 at Chase Center in San Francisco on Monday night. Stephen Curry and Brandin Podziemski each scored 25 points, and the Warriors extended their win streak to eight games and advanced to 8-3, completing one of the most remarkable turnarounds of the young NBA season.

The numbers tell the story of a team transformed. Golden State started the season 0-3, and there was genuine panic among fans and pundits who wondered whether the Warriors' championship window had finally closed. The team was projected to finish 41-41, a .500 record that would have meant a play-in fight at best. Instead, the Warriors have won eight games in a row, and they now look like one of the best teams in the Western Conference, let alone the NBA.

Monday night's win was the most emphatic of the streak. The Warriors led by as many as 51 points, outscored the Pelicans in every quarter, and closed out the rout with a 26-22 edge in the fourth. The Pelicans, who fall to 4-7, were never in the game, and the Warriors' dominance was total: offence, defence, pace, effort. It was the kind of performance that makes a statement, and the statement is that this team is for real.

Curry, as ever, was at the centre of it, but the story of the streak is bigger than one player. The Warriors currently rank third in the league in defensive rating at 103.5, according to NBA Advanced Leaders, and that defensive improvement is the foundation of the turnaround. A team that defends like this, with Curry's shooting and the depth around him, is a team that can beat anyone, and the eight straight wins are the proof.

There have been costs. The Warriors suffered losses to Brandon Williams and Will Richard, who will both seemingly be out for some time, and the depth will be tested as the season goes on. But the streak has been built on the next man up, and the players who have stepped in have kept the standard high. That is the mark of a well-coached team, and it is why the Warriors' turnaround feels sustainable rather than lucky.

The contrast with the start of the season could not be sharper. After three straight losses, the Warriors were counted out, and the obituaries were being written. Eight wins later, the same team is the hottest in the league, and the conversation has flipped entirely. It is a reminder that in the NBA, three games is nothing, and a season is long enough for a good team to find itself, even after a terrible start.

The Pelicans, for their part, are going in the opposite direction. At 4-7, they have lost three straight, and the 51-point defeat is the kind of loss that lingers. New Orleans has talent, but it has not found a way to compete with the best teams in the West, and the gap between the Pelicans and the Warriors on Monday night was the gap between a team that knows who it is and a team that is still searching.

What happens next for Golden State is a test of whether the streak can keep going. Eight wins is impressive, but the schedule will get harder, and the rest of the league has now noticed. The Warriors will be circled on every opponent's calendar, and the target on their backs will only grow. But for a team that was written off three weeks ago, that is a good problem to have. The Warriors are back, and the rest of the NBA knows it.

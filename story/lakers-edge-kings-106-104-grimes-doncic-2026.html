Grimes Hits Late Three as Lakers Survive Kings 106-104

Luka Doncic scored 25 points and Quentin Grimes hit the tiebreaking three-pointer with 57 seconds left as the Lakers held off a Kings team that led by 11 in the third quarter.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Luka Doncic had 25 points and eight assists, Quentin Grimes hit a tiebreaking three-pointer with 57 seconds to play, and the Los Angeles Lakers rallied in the fourth quarter for their 10th win in 11 games, 106-104 over the Sacramento Kings on Friday night at Crypto.com Arena. The Lakers hung on when Zach LaVine missed a long three-pointer at the buzzer.

The win kept the Lakers from losing consecutive games for the first time this season. Los Angeles had seen its nine-game winning streak end on Wednesday in a loss to the Utah Jazz, and for much of Friday night it looked as if a second straight defeat was coming against the team with the worst record in the league.

Sacramento came out swinging. The Kings led 62-54 at halftime behind 15 points and eight rebounds from Domantas Sabonis, and they stretched the advantage to 11 points in the third quarter, taking their largest lead of the game at 67-56 with 11:09 remaining in the period after a three-pointer from Darius Acuff Jr.

The Kings still led 84-82 entering the fourth quarter, but the Lakers flipped the game at the start of the final period. Walker Kessler opened the fourth with his first three-pointer of the season, giving Los Angeles an 85-84 lead with 11:42 remaining, and the Lakers never trailed again.

Los Angeles built a 101-93 lead with four and a half minutes to play, but Sacramento refused to fold. The Kings scored eight straight points to tie the game on Sabonis' free throws with 2:32 left, and the teams were level again at 103-103 with 1:56 remaining.

Grimes settled it. His three-pointer with 57 seconds left, his only three of the night, put the Lakers ahead for good. Doncic turned the ball over with 23 seconds left, and Kessler missed two free throws to give Sacramento one last chance, but LaVine pulled up and his final shot clanged off the rim.

Doncic led the Lakers with 25 points, including 17 in the first half, and Kessler scored 15 points while Matisse Thybulle added 14 and Grimes finished with 13. The Lakers were again without second-leading scorer Austin Reaves, who missed his fourth consecutive game with a bruised heel.

De'Andre Hunter scored a season-high 30 points and hit five three-pointers for the Kings, who dropped to 1-14 on the season — the worst 15-game start in team history — with their eighth straight loss. Sabonis had 19 points and 16 rebounds, LaVine scored 19 and Acuff added 18 points and 12 assists.

The loss extended a skid that began with a Nov. 4 loss to the Lakers, and Sacramento's winless run in NBA Cup play continued as the Kings fell to 0-3 in group play. The Lakers improved to 2-1 in NBA Cup Group C and recorded their fifth straight home victory over Sacramento.

The Kings host Boston on Saturday, while the Lakers host Utah on Monday. For Los Angeles, the narrow escape was a reminder that the margin for error shrinks quickly without Reaves, even against a struggling opponent — and that Doncic and a late Grimes three were enough, just barely, to keep the winning run going.

Why it matters: The result matters less for the standings than for what it revealed about both teams. The Lakers keep winning — 10 victories in 11 games as of Nov. 20 — but this was their second straight shaky home performance against a rebuilding opponent, and they needed a buzzer miss to escape a one-win team. With Austin Reaves still sidelined, the offense leans heavily on Doncic, and the supporting cast has to produce nights like Kessler's and Grimes' late three. For Sacramento, the 1-14 start is now the worst 15-game opening in franchise history, yet the Kings led for long stretches in Los Angeles; the gap between competing and winning remains the story of their season, and an eighth straight loss shows how thin that gap has become.

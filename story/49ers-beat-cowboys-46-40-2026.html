Purdy Throws for Career-High 389 Yards as 49ers Outlast Cowboys 46-40

Brock Purdy threw for a career-high 389 yards and three touchdowns, and Christian McCaffrey scored three times, as the San Francisco 49ers held off a furious Dallas rally to win a 46-40 shootout at AT&T Stadium on Sunday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The San Francisco 49ers survived one of the wildest games of the NFL season on Sunday, beating the Dallas Cowboys 46-40 at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, in a Week 10 shootout that produced 86 combined points and a final score never before seen in league history. The 49ers improved to 8-1, the best record in the NFC, while the Cowboys fell to 4-6.

Quarterback Brock Purdy was the star of the afternoon, completing 22 of 28 passes for a career-high 389 yards and three touchdowns without an interception. Purdy, playing his first game at AT&T Stadium, remained undefeated against Dallas at 4-0 including the postseason.

Running back Christian McCaffrey was nearly unstoppable in his own right, piling up 223 yards from scrimmage and three touchdowns. He caught four passes for a career-high 161 yards and two scores, including a 74-yard catch-and-run in the second quarter and a 66-yard touchdown in the fourth, and he added 62 rushing yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. He became the first player in the Super Bowl era with at least 150 receiving yards, two receiving touchdowns, 50-plus rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in a single game.

The 49ers finished with 584 yards of total offense, the most the Cowboys have ever allowed at home and the third-most in franchise history. San Francisco scored three touchdowns of at least 60 yards, the most by any team in a game over the last 50 years, according to the Elias Sports Bureau. Deebo Samuel added to the big-play barrage with a 70-yard touchdown run and a touchdown catch, finishing with 109 total yards and two scores.

Dallas refused to go quietly, and quarterback Dak Prescott nearly matched Purdy blow for blow. Prescott completed 30 of 51 passes for 343 yards and five touchdowns with one interception, and wide receiver George Pickens caught 11 passes for 145 yards and three touchdowns. CeeDee Lamb added seven catches for 72 yards and a score as the Cowboys gained 439 yards of their own.

San Francisco appeared to have the game under control when it led 38-19 with 11:26 remaining in the fourth quarter, but Dallas scored 21 points in the final period to make the finish frantic. Pickens caught a 5-yard touchdown pass, though the two-point try failed, and Ryan Flournoy's 11-yard score with 3:42 left cut the deficit to 38-32 after the 49ers burned only 2:26 on the possession in between.

The 49ers answered with the decisive play of the game. Purdy found McCaffrey across the middle, and with linebacker Von Miller in coverage, the running back raced 66 yards to the end zone to restore a two-score lead. Dallas scored once more on Lamb's 1-yard reception, and Prescott ran in the two-point conversion to make it 46-40, but the Cowboys' onside kick failed and San Francisco ran out the final seconds.

The 49ers' fans made their presence felt throughout the afternoon, out-drawing and out-yelling the home crowd at AT&T Stadium and celebrating loudly as their team held on. The victory kept the 49ers alone at the top of the NFC standings as of Sunday night.

The final score itself made history. According to Niners Nation, 46-40 had never occurred in an NFL game before, making it the 1,099th unique final score in league history and the first of two so-called scorigamis on Sunday. The second came hours later, when Cincinnati beat Pittsburgh 38-18 on Sunday Night Football. Read more from USA TODAY on the 49ers' win

Rookie running back Kaelon Black added 11 carries for 53 yards and a touchdown for San Francisco, including a 25-yard scoring run, as the 49ers' ground game complemented the passing attack. The one concern for San Francisco was its secondary, which allowed Prescott to throw five touchdown passes, but the offense produced more than enough to cover for it on a day when nearly every possession seemed to end in points.

Why it matters: The win keeps San Francisco at 8-1 and alone atop the NFC, holding the conference's No. 1 seed as of Sunday night, and it strengthens Brock Purdy's case in the Most Valuable Player race after a career-best passing day. The 49ers' offense looks like the most explosive unit in the league, but the 40 points allowed are a reminder that the defense must tighten up before the postseason. Dallas, now 4-6, faces an uphill climb to reach the playoffs and must fix a defense that gave up 584 yards and 40 points on Sunday. San Francisco next faces Minnesota, while Dallas turns toward Tennessee and its annual Thanksgiving Day game.

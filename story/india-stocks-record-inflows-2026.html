HSBC and Citi Turn Bullish on India as Foreign Investors Pour In a Record $3.6 Billion

HSBC lifted India to overweight and Citi raised its Nifty target to 28,000 as overseas funds bought a net $3.6 billion of shares in four days.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Global banks are turning more bullish on Indian stocks as the market's sharpest rally in more than four years draws record foreign inflows, with investors seeking shares that look less vulnerable to concerns over stretched valuations for artificial intelligence.

HSBC Holdings lifted local equities to overweight from neutral, citing resilient earnings and more reasonable valuations. Citigroup raised its 12-month target for the NSE Nifty 50 Index by nearly 8 percent to 28,000, pointing to improving profit growth and lower oil prices. The brokerage expects Nifty earnings to grow about 15 to 16 percent annually for fiscal years 2026 through 2028.

The calls follow a rush of foreign money into the market. Overseas funds bought a net $3.6 billion of local shares in the four days through Thursday, putting them on course for their biggest-ever weekly inflow. Foreign portfolio investors reportedly purchased equities worth approximately 13,749 crore rupees during the reported period, while domestic investors recorded net selling.

The benchmark Nifty 50 is up nearly 8 percent this month, the most since July 2022, making India one of the best-performing major markets globally. The rally has been driven by improving corporate earnings, renewed foreign investor participation and encouraging domestic economic fundamentals, even as global uncertainties and geopolitical tensions persist.

Monday brought a pause in the advance. Indian benchmark indices opened higher on Monday, November 9, but quickly reversed their early gains as selling pressure emerged. The Nifty 50 slipped below the psychological 25,000 mark, trading at 24,913.80, down 72.60 points or 0.29 percent, while the Sensex fell about 0.29 percent to approximately 80,767. Information technology stocks were among the weakest, with the Nifty IT index down 1.49 percent.

The weakness followed Sunday's special Diwali Muhurat Trading session, when the Nifty closed at 24,986.40, down 0.18 percent, and the Sensex ended at 80,999.40, declining 0.04 percent. The session marked the start of the new Samvat 2083 trading year and snapped a seven-session winning streak, despite strong gains during the preceding week.

Asian markets were mostly positive in early trade on Monday, with Japan's Nikkei gaining around 3.3 percent and South Korean shares advancing more than 1.2 percent. In the United States on Friday, the Dow Jones declined 0.46 percent to 51,285.73, while the S&P 500 gained 0.31 percent to 7,376.86 and the Nasdaq Composite advanced 1.09 percent to 24,579.42, although it recorded a weekly decline.

Investors will focus this week on India's October consumer price index, due on Tuesday, along with U.S. inflation figures, Federal Reserve commentary, second-quarter earnings, foreign institutional investor activity, crude oil prices and developments in the Middle East. Stocks to watch include Tata Steel on its quarterly earnings and a string of other companies reporting results.

The new Samvat 2083 trading year begins with cautious optimism surrounding India's economic growth, improving corporate profitability and the possibility of sustained foreign capital inflows. Expected index earnings growth of 18 percent in the coming fiscal year underpins the bullish case — but as Monday's dip showed, the rally will not run in a straight line.

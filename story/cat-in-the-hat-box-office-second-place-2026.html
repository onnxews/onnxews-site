The Cat in the Hat Lands in Second Place With a $6 Million Friday and an $18 Million Weekend in Sight

Warner Bros.' animated Dr. Seuss adaptation earned an A CinemaScore but opened well behind Godzilla Minus Zero, and below some of the loftiest pre-release forecasts.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Warner Bros.' animated The Cat in the Hat opened in second place at the North American box office, earning $6 million on Friday from 4,003 theaters, including Thursday previews. The film is projected to finish the weekend with between $17 million and $20 million, with Deadline estimating $20 million and TheWrap putting it closer to $18 million.

The opening is respectable but underwhelming against the expectations that once surrounded the film. Some pre-release forecasts had the movie tracking as high as $40 million to $50 million for its opening weekend, and it finished far behind Godzilla Minus Zero, which took $14.5 million on Friday alone on its way to a projected $32 million weekend.

There is one bright spot the studio can point to. The Cat in the Hat earned an A CinemaScore from audiences, the same top grade given to Godzilla Minus Zero and to Angel Studios' Drummer Boy this weekend. Strong audience grades have historically translated into steady holds in the weeks that follow, and a family film with an A grade has room to build through word of mouth.

The film is Warner Bros. Pictures Animation's take on the classic Dr. Seuss book, directed by Alessandro Carloni and Erica Rivinoja, with a reported production budget of $86 million. That budget makes the opening weekend math more demanding than it would be for a cheaper animated release, and the film will need strong holds and a long run to justify the spend.

The comparison with the last Cat in the Hat movie is unavoidable. The live-action version starring Mike Myers opened to $38.3 million in 2003, more than double what the animated film is projected to earn in its first three days. That film was famously unloved by critics and by Dr. Seuss's estate, but it opened big on the strength of its star and its marketing.

The animated version takes a different approach, leaning on the visual style of the book and on a family audience rather than on star power. Warner Bros. has positioned it as the first in a planned series of Dr. Seuss animated features, which raises the stakes beyond a single weekend. A soft opening does not kill that plan, but it does make the next installments a harder sell inside the studio.

The timing was always going to be difficult. Opening against a Godzilla sequel that became a cultural event, with premium screens driving record numbers, left the Cat fighting for the same family and general audiences. The film also faced competition from Ramayana and Drummer Boy, making this one of the most crowded weekends of the autumn at the multiplex.

Internationally, the film is still rolling out, and family films often earn the majority of their gross outside North America. The Cat in the Hat picked up pace in India on its second day, and other major markets are yet to open. A strong overseas run could still turn the film into a profitable entry for Warner Bros., even if the domestic opening looks modest.

The wider weekend tells its own story. With Godzilla Minus Zero, The Cat in the Hat and Drummer Boy all earning A CinemaScores, audiences are plainly happy with what they are seeing, even if the grosses are unevenly distributed. Industry estimates put the total North American weekend at about $114 million, the strongest November opening weekend since the pandemic.

For Warner Bros., the question now is whether the A CinemaScore and the family audience can carry the film through the coming weeks. The Thanksgiving corridor is still ahead, and a well-liked children's film can leg out far beyond its opening weekend. The Cat may have lost the opening battle to a giant lizard, but the war for family audiences is only just beginning.

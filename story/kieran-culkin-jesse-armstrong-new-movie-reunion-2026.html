Kieran Culkin Says He Is Reuniting With Succession Creator Jesse Armstrong on a New Movie

The actor let slip on British radio that Armstrong has written a film for them to make together, their first collaboration since Succession ended three years ago.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Kieran Culkin has revealed that he is reuniting with Succession creator Jesse Armstrong for a new movie, their first project together since the HBO drama ended three years ago. Culkin let the news slip during an appearance on the UK radio show Heart Breakfast on Friday, Nov. 20, while he was promoting his new film, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping. He said he was about to work with Armstrong on a movie that Armstrong had written, but he gave no further details about the project.

Culkin's exact words made clear that the announcement was not planned. He said, I'm about to work with, I don't know if I'm allowed to talk about, I'm about to work with Jesse on a movie. He wrote something. The hesitation suggested he was unsure whether the project was public yet, and it left open the question of whether Armstrong will direct the film as well as write it, or whether a studio and release plan are already in place.

What is known is that the film has nothing to do with Roman Roy, the character Culkin played across all four seasons of Succession. Armstrong does not currently have any announced films in development, so Culkin's remark is the only public indication of what the project might be. No title, plot, studio, cast beyond Culkin or release date has been disclosed, and neither Armstrong nor a studio has commented on the film.

Culkin was equally clear that fans should not expect a return to the world of Succession itself. Asked on Heart Breakfast whether the show could ever have a spinoff, he dismissed the idea outright, saying it would be a terrible idea and joking, What are you hoping for? Cousin Greg: The Sitcom? The remark was reported by Deadline and picked up across entertainment outlets, and it underlined that the Armstrong reunion is a new story, not a revival of the old one.

Culkin did say, however, that he misses playing Roman Roy, the foul-mouthed Roy family heir who lost out in the battle for control of Waystar in the series finale. He said it was a lot of fun because he decided early on that the character was a guy who had never had to suffer consequences in his life, and that he could just talk his way in and out of any situation. He described the role as completely unhinged, and said that freedom was what made it so enjoyable to play.

Since Succession ended, Culkin has become a major film actor. He won the Academy Award for best supporting actor for Jesse Eisenberg's A Real Pain, and he is currently in cinemas as the younger Caesar Flickerman in The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, which opened on Nov. 20. The Armstrong film would be his latest step in a run that has taken him from prestige television to the biggest film franchise of his career.

Armstrong, meanwhile, has been busy since Succession ended. He made his feature directorial debut with Mountainhead, an HBO satire, and he is collaborating with his Peep Show co-writer Sam Bain on an A24 movie called Jonty, starring Jesse Plemons and Cole Escola. The new film with Culkin would add a third feature project to a slate that has moved quickly from television into film.

Culkin also has other work on the way. He is due to appear next year in Warner Bros.' untitled Nancy Meyers romantic comedy, alongside Penelope Cruz, Emma Mackey, Owen Wilson and Jude Law, a film that has yet to receive a release window. For now, the Armstrong movie exists only as a sentence spoken on a Friday morning radio show, but given the history between the two men, it is already one of the most anticipated unannounced films in Hollywood.

Why it matters: The reunion matters because Culkin and Armstrong made one of the defining television partnerships of the last decade, and their chemistry was the engine of Succession. Roman Roy's mix of cruelty, vulnerability and verbal dexterity only worked because Armstrong's writing and Culkin's performance were so precisely matched, and the prospect of that partnership applied to an original film, free of the Roy family and Waystar, is genuinely exciting. It also shows how the prestige television talent of the streaming era is now carrying its collaborations into feature films, with Armstrong following Mountainhead and Jonty by reuniting with the actor who won an Emmy for his work. Until a title or plot emerges, the news is only a promise, but it is a promise from two people with a track record of delivering on it.

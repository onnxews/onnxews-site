Arch Manning Redeems Three Interceptions With Last-Seconds Touchdown as Texas Edges LSU 22-21

Arch Manning threw three interceptions, then found Raleek Brown for a 4-yard touchdown in the final seconds as No. 8 Texas beat LSU 22-21 at Tiger Stadium on Saturday night.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Arch Manning threw three interceptions, then threw the pass that saved Texas's season. Trailing 21-16 in the final seconds on Saturday night, November 14, Manning found Raleek Brown for a 4-yard touchdown to give No. 8 Texas a 22-21 win over LSU at Tiger Stadium in Baton Rouge, capping a 12-play, 58-yard drive that began at the Texas 42 with 2:19 left.

The drive was a test of nerve for a quarterback who had spent most of the night giving the ball away. Manning finished 19 of 35 for 174 yards with one touchdown and three interceptions, and he had also rushed for an early 1-yard touchdown. On the final drive he was 6 of 8 for 51 yards, converted a third-and-2 with a 3-yard rush with 39 seconds left, and ran 7 yards to the LSU 4 with 9 seconds left before finding Brown for the winner in the final seconds.

LSU had led 21-10 after Dilin Jones's 44-yard scoring run with 5:52 left in the third quarter, and the Tigers looked in control of a game played in front of one of college football's loudest crowds. Gianni Spetic's field goals of 41 and 50 yards cut the lead to 21-16, but Texas still needed a touchdown, and it needed one from a quarterback who had already thrown three interceptions.

Manning's reaction after the game captured what the drive meant. "That's what it's all about: Death Valley at night, two-minute drive, let's go," he said, adding: "It means the world." The win improved Texas to 8-2 overall and 5-2 in the Southeastern Conference, ranked No. 6 in the College Football Playoff rankings, while LSU fell to 6-4 and 3-4 in the SEC in its first season under Lane Kiffin.

LSU's quarterback, Sam Leavitt, passed for 244 yards and two touchdowns to Jackson Harris but was also intercepted three times, all by Texas safety Jelani McDonald. The three picks matched Manning's three, and the game turned on which quarterback could overcome his mistakes. Manning did, with a final drive that erased everything that had gone before it; Leavitt did not get the chance, as Texas's defence held after the go-ahead score.

The game was a test of both teams' playoff credentials. Texas came in ranked No. 8 in the AP poll and No. 6 in the playoff rankings, and a loss in Baton Rouge would have ended any realistic hope of a playoff place. LSU, ranked No. 24 in the AP poll, was playing its first season under Kiffin, and a win over Texas would have been the signature result of his first year. Instead, the Tigers lost a game they led for most of the second half.

The two teams' seasons now point in different directions. Texas has two games left to secure its playoff position, and the win in Baton Rouge gives it a road victory over a ranked opponent that the committee will weigh heavily. LSU, at 6-4, is now playing for a bowl game and for momentum into next season, and the loss will sting because the Tigers had the game in hand with less than six minutes gone in the third quarter.

Manning's performance will be debated for the rest of the season. Three interceptions is a poor line by any measure, and his 174 passing yards were modest for a quarterback of his reputation. But the final drive — 12 plays, 58 yards, no mistakes, and a touchdown on the last play — is the kind of sequence that defines a quarterback's season, and it is the reason Texas left Baton Rouge with a win rather than a loss.

For LSU, the frustration is that the defence did enough to win. Three interceptions, a 21-10 lead, and a crowd that never stopped roaring should have been enough. But the Tigers could not finish the game, and a Texas team that had given the ball away three times found a way to take it back when it mattered most. The final drive began with 2:19 left and ended with the ball in the end zone, and it will be the drive Texas remembers from this season.

Why it matters: The game mattered because it was a playoff elimination game in all but name: Texas loses, and its season is over; Texas wins, and it stays alive. Manning's three interceptions made it look like a loss for most of the night, and the final drive — 58 yards in two minutes, from a quarterback who had already given the ball away three times — is the kind of sequence that can define a season. For LSU, it is the opposite: a game the Tigers led for most of the second half, lost in the final seconds, in the first season of a new coach who needed a signature win.

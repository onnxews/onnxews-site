John Mulaney Roasts RFK Jr. and Tech Billionaires in Seventh SNL Hosting Turn

John Mulaney hosted Saturday Night Live for a seventh time on Saturday, opening with a CIA sketch about tech billionaires and spies and welcoming Phoebe Bridgers for two songs in the last show before Thanksgiving.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

John Mulaney hosted Saturday Night Live for the seventh time on Saturday, November 14, and used the night to take aim at the week's strangest story: the tech billionaires, the spies and the fallen chip chief at the centre of it. The episode, the sixth of Season 52, paired Mulaney with musical guest Phoebe Bridgers and was the last live show before the Thanksgiving break.

The cold open was set at CIA headquarters, where Mulaney lectured a room full of tech leaders on telling "the difference between an attractive woman actually wanting to date them… and a spy." The sketch had James Austin Johnson as Peter Thiel, Ben Marshall as Sam Altman, Jane Wickline as Dario Amodei, Andrew Dismukes as Mark Zuckerberg and Jeremy Culhane as Elon Musk, and it riffed directly on Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang's reported resignation after an affair with a woman said to be a Chinese spy. Former cast member Bowen Yang returned to play Huang himself, in his first appearance since leaving the show.

Mulaney's monologue kept the jokes coming closer to home. He joked that he has decided his father is on the spectrum, compared baby boomers with Generation Z, and delivered a lengthy impression of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. The monologue is traditionally the host's chance to set the tone for the night, and Mulaney used it to remind the audience why he has become one of the show's most reliable returning hosts since his years as a writer there from 2008 to 2013.

Bridgers, in her third overall appearance on the show and her second solo booking, performed "Bobby" and "Still Standing." She opened "Bobby" with a scream over a smoky stage, and for "Still Standing," a song about her complicated relationship with her late father, blacklighting revealed skull makeup and made her white lace dress glow. Her previous appearance, in 2021, ended with her smashing a guitar during "I Know the End," a moment that became one of the show's most talked-about musical performances of that season.

The episode also brought back Mulaney's signature New York musical number, this time a Broadway-style medley about the new law banning horse-drawn carriages in Central Park. There was a Woodstock sketch about baby boomers turning corporate by 1985, a plane-crash sketch, a spoof advertisement called "Sister," the return of Ashley Padilla's "Kathy" character, and a Weekend Update that carried the night's news jokes.

Mulaney's seventh hosting turn puts him in rare company among the show's returning hosts, and the episode played to his strengths: precise writing, a fondness for musical numbers and a willingness to build sketches around the week's news. The cold open's spy joke landed because the Huang story had dominated the week's business news, and the show's decision to put Bowen Yang in the role gave the sketch an extra layer for viewers who knew he had left the cast.

Bridgers's two songs showed a different side of the night. "Bobby" was theatrical and loud, while "Still Standing" was personal and quiet, and the staging — the smoke, the skull makeup, the glowing dress — made the second performance the visual centrepiece of the episode. It was a reminder that the musical guest's slot can carry as much of the night as the host's monologue.

The show announced its next live episode for December 5, with first-time host Amanda Seyfried and first-time musical guest Don Toliver. That will be the first show back after the Thanksgiving break, and it will be a test of whether the show can keep the energy of the Mulaney episode with a host who has never done it before.

For Mulaney, the seventh hosting gig confirmed his status as one of the show's most trusted returning voices. He has now hosted in every era of the show's recent history, and the November 14 episode showed he can still find a fresh angle on the week's news — even when that news is about chip executives and spies.

Why it matters: The episode mattered because it showed how quickly Saturday Night Live can turn the week's strangest business story into comedy — the Huang resignation had been a boardroom scandal on Thursday and a CIA sketch by Saturday. Mulaney's seventh hosting turn also confirmed his place as the show's most reliable returning host, and Bridgers's two performances gave the night a musical centrepiece that matched the comedy. The December 5 show, with a first-time host and a first-time musical guest, will show whether the momentum holds.

Other Mommy and Heart of the Beast Lead a Packed Home Entertainment Slate for November 10

Two of the autumn's biggest theatrical hits arrive on digital today, alongside a new horror thriller, a Criterion drama and 4K upgrades for Con Air, National Treasure and Pan's Labyrinth.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Two of the autumn's biggest films arrive at home today, as Universal's horror hit Other Mommy and Paramount's Brad Pitt survival drama Heart of the Beast lead a packed November 10 home entertainment slate. The new releases span premium digital rental and sale, Blu-ray and DVD, and 4K Ultra HD, giving home viewers one of the strongest Tuesdays of the season.

Other Mommy, from Universal Pictures Home Entertainment, arrives for premium digital sale and rental. The horror film follows Bela, an eight-year-old girl living in a home with marital problems, who confronts a sinister entity that comes out of her closet. The film earned 115.3 million dollars at the global box office, with 58.3 million dollars domestically, a strong result for an original horror film, and its arrival on digital comes as horror continues to be one of the most reliable genres for home viewing.

Heart of the Beast, from Paramount Home Entertainment, is the bigger of the two digital releases. The film stars Brad Pitt as a former special forces soldier who, after surviving a plane crash, finds himself trapped in the unforgiving Alaskan wilderness with his combat dog. It generated 188.7 million dollars at the global box office, including 67.1 million dollars across North American screens. The film runs one hour and 41 minutes and is rated PG-13, and its mix of star power and survival spectacle makes it a natural fit for a big-screen home setup.

The rest of the digital slate is rounded out by two smaller releases. The Floaters, a summer camp comedy from Brainstorm Media, arrives for digital sale or rental, and Crawlers, a horror thriller from Saban Films released through Sony Pictures Home Entertainment, lands on digital retail platforms. Crawlers, which runs one hour and 21 minutes and is rated R, follows quarantined residents of an infested apartment complex fighting for their lives against deadly, venomous spiders, and stars Matilda Lutz, Gregg Sulkin and Melina Matthews.

For collectors, the disc releases offer a mix of arthouse and archive titles. Criterion Premieres releases the Canadian coming-of-age drama Blue Heron on Blu-ray and DVD, and Kino Lorber brings the documentary Black Zombie to Blu-ray and DVD. Anchor Bay Entertainment's 2025 action thriller The Workout arrives on Blu-ray from MVD Entertainment Group. The disc slate is smaller than the digital one, but it reflects a market where physical media has become a niche for enthusiasts and collectors rather than the mainstream.

Disney is catering directly to that collector market with new 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray Steelbooks of two Nicolas Cage action films, Con Air and National Treasure, both arriving today. Sony Pictures Home Entertainment is releasing the 2005 drama Memoirs of a Geisha on 4K Ultra HD disc for the first time, giving the Oscar-winning film its long-awaited high-definition upgrade. And Cineverse is releasing the 4K remastered 20th anniversary edition of Guillermo del Toro's Oscar-winning Pan's Labyrinth digitally, a timely arrival for a film that has only grown in reputation since its release.

The timing of the slate is no accident. Tuesdays remain the traditional day for home entertainment releases, and mid-November is a sweet spot: close enough to the holiday season that gift buyers are starting to shop, and far enough from the biggest new theatrical releases that the films arriving at home can command attention. Studios have also learned to time digital releases to the moment when a film's theatrical run has cooled but public awareness is still high, and both Other Mommy and Heart of the Beast fit that pattern.

The shift toward digital-first releases continues to shape the business. Premium digital rental and sale, where a film is available to rent or buy online weeks before it reaches subscription streaming, has become the most important window after theatrical, and the revenues it generates can now rival a film's later streaming income. For viewers, the trade-off is choice: the same Tuesday that brings two major films to digital also brings a Blu-ray of a Canadian drama, a documentary about zombies, and a 20-year-old del Toro masterpiece in 4K.

What happens next for these titles is the slow migration to subscription streaming, where they will find a second audience in the months ahead. For now, the November 10 slate is a reminder that the home entertainment business, for all the talk of streaming's dominance, still runs on a weekly rhythm of new releases, and that a good Tuesday can still feel like an event. Whether it is a horror film, a survival drama or a 4K remaster of a classic, there is something on the shelf for almost everyone this week.

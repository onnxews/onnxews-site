Kenya Discharges Last Ebola Patient and Begins 42-Day Countdown to Ebola-Free Status

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient on Wednesday, and a 42-day countdown begins on Thursday that could end with the country declared Ebola-free on December 23.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

'He has been discharged healthy and free of the virus,' Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said on Wednesday, November 11, as Kenya's last Ebola patient went home. The last 11 contacts also returned home to their families the same day, having completed their monitoring in good health. For the first time since Kenya confirmed its first case 37 days earlier, the country has no patient undergoing treatment and no contacts in quarantine. The news starts a 42-day countdown, with day one on Thursday, November 12.

'Forty-two days is twice the longest incubation period of the virus. If no new case is recorded in that period, Kenya will be declared free of Ebola on 23 December 2026,' Duale said at Afya House in Nairobi. The 42-day countdown is a critical public health requirement based on two consecutive 21-day incubation periods, which represent the maximum time Ebola symptoms can take to develop after exposure. The period allows health authorities to establish whether any previously undetected transmission has occurred before formally declaring the country free of the disease.

Kenya confirmed its first case of Bundibugyo virus disease on October 6, 2026, in a Kenyan who had travelled home from the Democratic Republic of the Congo through Uganda. The patient was isolated at a Nairobi hospital after developing symptoms, and laboratory tests confirmed the infection. The patient subsequently died on October 5, with the burial conducted in accordance with Ebola safe and dignified burial protocols.

Kenya eventually recorded nine confirmed cases, including two deaths, with all infections traced to a single transmission cluster. Eight of the cases were secondary infections involving four family members and four health workers. 'Every one of them was already in quarantine when they fell ill,' Duale said, and because of that the virus never spread to the community. The last case was confirmed on October 19, and no new case has been recorded since.

Seven patients have now recovered. On November 3, five patients were discharged, 'the first Kenyans ever to recover from this disease,' according to Duale. A sixth patient went home on November 6, and with the last discharge, seven patients have recovered. 'Sadly, two people died, and WHO extends its deepest sympathies to their families and loved ones,' the World Health Organization said.

Contact tracing proved decisive. Health teams identified 135 contacts linked to the confirmed cases and subjected them to daily monitoring for symptoms throughout the mandatory 21-day incubation period. All the contacts were successfully traced and monitored, with the final 11 completing their observation period in good health. According to the World Health Organization, Kenya had screened more than 652,000 travellers entering the country by October 6, tested 267 samples from suspected cases and trained approximately 5,000 healthcare workers in Ebola prevention and management.

The milestone drew praise from the World Health Organization, which congratulated Kenya. 'Surviving Ebola is a remarkable achievement, and we wish the survivor continued recovery, good health and successful reintegration into their community,' said Dr Abdourahmane Diallo, Director for Programme Management at the WHO Regional Office for Africa. Diallo also praised Kenya's openness, commending the government for 'its transparency, timely reporting, strong coordination mechanisms and commitment to protecting the health and well-being of its people.'

Health authorities emphasized that the work is not over. 'Let me be clear. The countdown is not the end of our response,' Duale said. Rapid response teams will stay on alert in every county, every alert will be investigated and survivors will receive follow-up care and support. 'While today's event is a moment for cautious celebration, it is not the end of our work. The discharge of the last patient is an important milestone, but vigilance must continue,' Diallo said. The Ministry of Health will maintain heightened surveillance across all 47 counties, with screening at airports and other points of entry sustained.

Ebola is still active in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with nearly 11,000 confirmed cases and more than 5,000 deaths, according to the World Health Organization. Bundibugyo virus disease is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, one of the viruses responsible for Ebola disease. It spreads through direct contact with the blood, bodily fluids and contaminated materials of an infected person, particularly after symptoms develop. There is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo virus disease, though early detection, isolation and supportive care are essential. The WHO advises against travel or trade restrictions on countries affected by Ebola, and Duale said Kenya remains open for business, tourism, trade and travel, with no restrictions on movement or public gatherings within the country.

Why it matters: Kenya's 42-day countdown is a genuine public health success, and a study in what containment looks like when it works: every case traced to a single cluster, every contact found and monitored, no community transmission at all. The contrast with the outbreak still raging across the border in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with nearly 11,000 cases, could hardly be sharper, and it is a reminder that the same virus produces very different outcomes depending on how fast a country detects it and how completely it traces it. The next six weeks are the test of whether the containment held; if it did, Kenya will have stopped an Ebola outbreak in 37 days, and other countries facing imported cases will be studying how.

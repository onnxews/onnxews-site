Keisha Lance Bottoms Wins Georgia, Becoming the First Black Woman Elected Governor in U.S. History

The former Atlanta mayor defeated Republican billionaire Rick Jackson, ending more than two decades of Republican control of Georgia's governor's office.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Keisha Lance Bottoms has won the race for governor of Georgia, becoming the first Black woman ever elected governor of a U.S. state. The Associated Press called the race on Tuesday night, with Bottoms, 56, leading Republican Rick Jackson by about 54.3 percent to 45.7 percent with nearly all votes counted.

Bottoms, a former mayor of Atlanta who later served as a senior adviser in President Joe Biden's White House, is also Georgia's first female governor and the first Democrat to win the state's governorship since Roy Barnes in 1998. Only three Black people had previously been elected governor in U.S. history, all of them Democratic men.

Her victory capped a sweeping night for Democrats in Georgia, who won every statewide race on the ballot, including the U.S. Senate, lieutenant governor, secretary of state and attorney general. Eight of the statewide winners are Black women. Bottoms succeeds Republican Gov. Brian Kemp, who is leaving office after two terms.

Bottoms campaigned on the rising cost of living, promising to expand Medicaid, raise Georgia's minimum wage to 15 dollars an hour, and increase pay for teachers, firefighters, police officers and other public employees. She frequently reminded voters that Jackson, a billionaire healthcare executive endorsed by President Donald Trump, had said he wanted to govern like Trump with a Southern tone.

Bottoms is due to be sworn in as governor in January 2027. Her win comes eight years after fellow Democrat Stacey Abrams became the first Black woman nominated for governor by a major U.S. political party, losing twice to Kemp in 2018 and 2022.

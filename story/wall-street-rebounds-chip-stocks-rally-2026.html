Wall Street Rebounds as Chip Stocks Rally After Brutal AI Selloff

The Nasdaq climbed 1.1 percent as AMD jumped 9 percent, but all three major indexes still finished the week in the red.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Wall Street rebounded on Friday as semiconductor stocks led a sharp recovery from one of the worst technology selloffs of the year. The Nasdaq Composite climbed 1.09 percent to 24,579.42 as investors returned to beaten-down chip shares, while the S&P 500 added 0.31 percent to close at 7,376.86. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.46 percent to 51,285.73.

The rally offered short-term relief, but it did not erase the concerns that triggered the selloff. Just one session earlier, the Nasdaq had plunged roughly 5.5 percent, pushing the technology-heavy index more than 10 percent below its recent high and into correction territory. The rebound was led by the same stocks that had been hit hardest.

Semiconductor stocks drove the recovery. Advanced Micro Devices jumped 11 percent after falling 17.3 percent during the previous session, while Intel gained 7.7 percent after declining 10.3 percent the day before. Nvidia climbed nearly 3 percent, reversing some of its losses from its worst day since January 2025. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 4.2 percent after suffering its steepest one-day decline since April 2025.

Despite the rebound, all three major indexes recorded weekly losses, underscoring that one strong session had not erased the damage. For the week, the Nasdaq fell about 3.4 percent, its fourth consecutive weekly decline. The S&P 500 dropped about 2.7 percent, and the Dow declined about 0.9 percent. The Nasdaq has now fallen four weeks in a row, a streak that reflects how deeply the market's mood has shifted.

The market's focus continues to center on artificial intelligence and the enormous investment required to support it. Investors are questioning whether the hundreds of billions of dollars pouring into data centers and other AI infrastructure will produce sufficient returns, and the midterm election results added a new layer of uncertainty. The prospect of tighter regulation of the AI industry under a Democratic Congress rattled a trade that had been built on years of unchecked spending.

Political uncertainty is also emerging at the state level. Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said on Thursday that the state would introduce new laws to protect local communities from the further large-scale buildup of data centers. Texas already holds a large share of American data center capacity, and her pledge added to worries about how quickly AI infrastructure can keep expanding.

The selloff spread far beyond American shores. Europe's STOXX 600 fell 1 percent, while Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's KOSPI each dropped about 6 percent. The global nature of the rout showed how interconnected the AI trade has become, with chipmakers and data center suppliers across Asia and Europe caught in the same downdraft.

A stronger-than-expected jobs report added another crosscurrent to the day. Nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October, far above forecasts of about 65,000, and the unemployment rate slipped to 4.1 percent. The report pushed Treasury yields higher, with the benchmark 10-year yield rising to 4.86 percent, and it raised the odds that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again in December.

The combination of a hot jobs report and a tech rebound left the market in an unsettled place. Strong hiring is good news for the economy, but it makes the Fed more likely to tighten policy, which is bad news for the high-growth stocks that depend on cheap money. That tension helps explain why the Dow, with its heavier weighting of old-economy stocks, fell even as the Nasdaq rose.

Whether Friday's bounce marks a bottom or merely a pause in a deeper correction is the question that will dominate next week. The Nasdaq is still in correction territory, weekly losses are piling up, and the regulatory outlook for AI is cloudier than it was a week ago. Buyers returned to chip stocks on Friday, but the forces that drove the selloff have not gone away.

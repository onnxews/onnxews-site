Scheifele Makes Franchise History as Jets Rally From 4-0 Down to Beat Blackhawks 5-4 in Shootout

Mark Scheifele passed Dale Hawerchuk as the franchise's all-time leading scorer as the Winnipeg Jets rallied from 4-0 down to beat the Chicago Blackhawks 5-4 in a shootout on Saturday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Winnipeg Jets beat the Chicago Blackhawks 5-4 in a shootout on Saturday, November 14, in Chicago, rallying from a 4-0 deficit to win their sixth straight game and make franchise history in the process. Chicago led 4-0 with four minutes left in the second period, and the Jets' comeback was the second four-goal comeback the team has managed in twelve days — only the second team in NHL history to do that.

Mark Scheifele had a goal and three assists and also scored in the shootout, while Kyle Connor scored the shootout winner. Scheifele's second-period goal was his 930th career point, passing Dale Hawerchuk's 929 as the franchise's all-time leading scorer, and he finished the game with 933 career points. The same goal also tied Hawerchuk's franchise record for goals, with 379, and Scheifele's third assist — on Gabriel Vilardi's tying goal at 18:47 of the third period — was his 554th career assist, passing Thomas Steen for the franchise assists record.

The comeback began with Scheifele's history-making goal and gathered pace through the third period. Connor and Vilardi each had a goal and an assist, and Cole Perfetti also scored, as the Jets erased a deficit that had looked insurmountable with four minutes left in the second. Vilardi's tying goal, with 1:13 left in regulation, came on Scheifele's record-breaking assist, and the shootout settled a game that neither team had deserved to lose.

Connor's goal also gave him 700 career points, a milestone that was almost lost in the noise of Scheifele's night. The two milestones together — Scheifele's 930th point and Connor's 700th — made the game a landmark for the franchise, and the comeback made it a landmark for the league. Winnipeg became only the second team in NHL history to record multiple four-goal comeback victories within a 12-day span, the other being Florida in April 2022.

Chicago had done almost everything right for the first two periods. The Blackhawks led 4-0, their goaltending had held, and the Jets looked beaten. But the game turned on a fluke goal after Chicago goaltender Spencer Knight's stick got lodged in the boards, and from there the Jets found a way back into a game that had seemed lost. Connor Bedard had a goal and an assist for Chicago, but the Blackhawks could not hold a lead that had seemed safe with four minutes left in the second period.

The goaltending told its own story. Stuart Skinner stopped 5 of 8 shots for Winnipeg before being replaced by Nico Daws, who made 12 saves through regulation and overtime and then held his nerve in the shootout. The change in goal was a gamble by the Jets' coach, and it paid off: Daws gave the team the stability it needed to mount a comeback that would have been impossible without it.

For Scheifele, the night was the culmination of a career spent with one franchise. Passing Hawerchuk, one of the greatest players in the team's history, as the all-time leading scorer is an honour that few players ever achieve, and doing it with a goal that started a four-goal comeback made it more memorable still. Tying Hawerchuk's goals record and passing Steen's assists record in the same game made it a night that will be remembered in Winnipeg for years.

The win extended the Jets' winning streak to six games, and it did so in a way that will give the team confidence for the rest of the season. A team that can come back from 4-0 down on the road, in a game it had no business winning, is a team that believes it can win any game. The comeback was the second in twelve days, and it made the Jets only the second team in NHL history to manage that feat.

For Chicago, the loss was a reminder of how quickly a game can turn. A 4-0 lead with four minutes left in the second period should be safe, and the Blackhawks will look back on the fluke goal and the third-period collapse as the moments that cost them two points. For Winnipeg, the night belonged to Scheifele, and to a franchise record that had stood for decades before a Saturday night in Chicago.

Why it matters: The game mattered for two reasons: it was one of the great comebacks in recent NHL history, and it was the night Mark Scheifele became the franchise's all-time leading scorer. Passing Dale Hawerchuk — a Hall of Famer and one of the defining players in the team's history — is a career-defining moment, and doing it with a goal that started a four-goal comeback, then adding the record-breaking assist on the tying goal, made it unforgettable. The Jets are now only the second team ever to manage two four-goal comebacks in twelve days, and a six-game winning streak built on that kind of resilience will worry every team they face next.

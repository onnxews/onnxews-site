GTA VI Leads The Game Awards 2026 With Eight Nominations Two Days Before Launch

Rockstar's sequel picked up eight nominations, including Game of the Year, ahead of its Thursday release — the most of any game this year.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 18, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Grand Theft Auto VI leads The Game Awards 2026 with eight nominations, the most of any game, the nominees revealed on Tuesday showed — two days before the game itself is released. The Rockstar sequel is nominated for Game of the Year, Best Game Direction, Best Narrative, Best Art Direction, Best Score and Music, Best Audio Design, Best Action/Adventure and Best Performance.

The nominations were announced in a livestream hosted by the show's creator, Geoff Keighley, on YouTube and Twitch. The timing is unusual: the game does not launch until Thursday, November 19, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, priced at $79.99 for the single-player edition. It qualifies because games must be commercially available by 9 p.m. Pacific time on Friday, November 20, and GTA VI clears that deadline by a day.

The Game of the Year race puts GTA VI against 007 First Light, Control Resonant, Phantom Blade Zero, Resident Evil Requiem and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, a Switch 2 remake of the 1998 classic. Control Resonant and Resident Evil Requiem each picked up six nominations, and Phantom Blade Zero has five. Nintendo is the most-nominated publisher, with 12 nominations, ahead of Capcom with 11.

One nomination made news on its own. Manni L. Perez is nominated for Best Performance, and the nomination was the first public confirmation that she has a role in the game, widely reported and rumored to be Lucia, one of the two protagonists. Rockstar has kept the cast largely under wraps, and the awards list effectively confirmed her involvement before the game is out.

Keighley pushed back on the suggestion that the show had been arranged around Rockstar's release. He told Variety that the show's dates had not been changed to accommodate GTA VI. The winners will be announced on Thursday, December 10 at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles, with nominees chosen by a jury of more than 160 media and creator outlets worldwide. Fan voting is open at TheGameAwards.com, and the Players' Voice category requires games to have shipped by November 30.

The nomination haul puts GTA VI in reach of a record. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 holds the mark with 13 nominations and nine wins in 2025, and GTA VI could at best tie the wins record, depending on the outcome of the Players' Voice vote. Whether it does will depend on reviews and on how the game lands with players in its first weeks, since the jury votes before most of them have played it through.

The launch itself has been years in the making. The release date was locked on November 6, 2025, when the game was pushed back to November 19, 2026, and digital pre-orders were able to pre-load from November 10, moved up from November 12. Reviews had not been published as of Tuesday, and the first verdicts are expected around the launch.

The Zelda remake in the Game of the Year field is a reminder of how strong the year has been. The Switch 2 version of Ocarina of Time launched on November 5 at $59.99 digital and $69.99 physical, and its nomination puts a remake of the 1998 classic up against the most anticipated release of the decade. Polygon and The Escapist both covered the nominations on Tuesday, with the full list published on the show's website.

Why it matters: Eight nominations for a game nobody outside Rockstar has finished playing is a verdict on expectation as much as achievement, and it shows how completely GTA VI has dominated this year in games. The eligibility rules let it in by a single day, which is why the nominations read as a coronation before the coronation: the industry has already decided this is the game of the year, and the December ceremony will largely ratify a decision the market made months ago. The real test is whether the game survives contact with tens of millions of players who have waited more than a decade for it, because a launch this big has further to fall than any game in history.

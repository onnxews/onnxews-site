Democrats Plan First-100-Hours Blitz as Jeffries Eyes the Speaker's Gavel

The incoming House majority plans to move fast on health care, tariffs and the Iran war once it takes power in January.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

House Democrats are planning a legislative blitz for their first 100 hours back in power, with incoming presumptive Speaker Hakeem Jeffries preparing to push a package of bills aimed squarely at President Donald Trump's agenda. The plan, described by aides to multiple outlets, would revive the enhanced Obamacare subsidies that lapsed last year, roll back roughly $1 trillion in Medicaid cuts enacted by Republicans, challenge Trump's tariffs and press for an end to the war in Iran.

"We're going to fight to make sure that we make this country affordable for every single American," Jeffries said after the election, calling the midterm result a change election that demands dramatic change. He is set to lead one of the largest House majorities in decades when the new Congress convenes in January.

The idea is to move the measures to the House floor quickly, in some cases before key committees are even fully organized, so the bills do not get bogged down in procedural delays. From there they would still have to clear the Senate, where a 60-vote filibuster applies, though some Democrats believe Republicans may not stand in their way on the most popular items.

Expected House Majority Leader Katherine Clark said the agenda would reflect what voters made clear on Tuesday. "They voted to end the war. They voted to end the tariffs," she said at an event with progressives. "They voted to end the catastrophic cuts to health care and they voted to end the corrupt pay-to-play." Senator Bernie Sanders, meanwhile, urged the new majority to go further, calling for the Medicare eligibility age to be lowered to 55 and for a pause on advanced artificial intelligence development.

Trump has signaled he will meet much of the agenda with his veto pen, having told interviewers he would become "Mr. Veto" if Democrats took Congress, though he has also said he is willing to cut deals. Democrats also plan to use their committee powers for investigations, with expected Oversight Chairman Robert Garcia vowing to probe the Trump family, corporate corruption and immigration enforcement, none of which a presidential veto can stop.

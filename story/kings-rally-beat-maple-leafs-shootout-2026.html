Kings Rally From Three Down to Beat Maple Leafs 4-3 in Shootout

Adrian Kempe scored the only shootout goal as Los Angeles erased a 3-0 deficit in Toronto, handing the Maple Leafs an eighth straight defeat.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Adrian Kempe scored the only goal in a shootout as the Los Angeles Kings roared back from a three-goal deficit to beat the Toronto Maple Leafs 4-3 on Thursday night at Scotiabank Arena, handing the Maple Leafs their eighth straight defeat.

Toronto built a 3-0 lead early in the middle period, and for a moment it looked as though the Maple Leafs had finally found some momentum. Auston Matthews had scored the 68th game-opening goal of his career, passing Mats Sundin for the most in franchise history, and added an assist. John Tavares and Dakota Joshua also scored, with William Nylander handing out two assists.

The comeback started quickly. Alex Turcotte scored 21 seconds after Toronto's third goal, Alex Laferriere added another on the power play, and Erik Haula scored at even strength to tie the game through 40 minutes. Capped by Haula's tying goal at 14:51 of the second period, the Kings erased a 3-0 deficit in the span of about 12 minutes.

Kempe then won it in the shootout, scoring on his team's first attempt by deking Anthony Stolarz to the ice and then roofing a backhand. He appeared to lose control of the puck on his way to the net but still scored the only goal on five shootout attempts, after an action-packed overtime in which neither side could find a winner.

Darcy Kuemper made 24 saves for Los Angeles, which won its second straight game and improved to 7-11-2 on the season. Stolarz turned away 25 shots for Toronto, which fell to 8-13-3 and is now 0-7-1 in its past eight games.

The numbers behind the skid are stark. The reeling Maple Leafs are 1-9-1 in their last 11 games, and they have now gone nearly three weeks without a victory, their last coming on Oct. 31 in Vancouver. Six of the losses came on a disastrous recent homestand before a 6-3 setback on Tuesday at Chicago.

Toronto entered play 27th in the NHL's overall standings, while Los Angeles was 30th, which makes the collapse all the more painful for the home side: this was a game against one of the few teams below them in the standings, at home, with a three-goal lead, and they still found a way to lose it.

There was an extra layer to the night for Toronto coach Jim Hiller, who faced his former team for the first time. The 57-year-old spent parts of three seasons with Los Angeles before being fired following the team's third straight first-round playoff exit in April, and he watched his old club take two points from his new one.

Kings winger Scott Laughton also had a homecoming of sorts, suiting up at Scotiabank Arena for the first time since being traded last March. The Toronto native had 10 goals and six assists in 63 games for the Maple Leafs after being acquired from the Philadelphia Flyers.

Both teams are back in action on Saturday. The Kings visit the Ottawa Senators, while the Maple Leafs host the Columbus Blue Jackets, still searching for a first win since Halloween and for an answer to a second period that has now undone them in two straight games.

Why it matters: Toronto's problem is no longer a slump; it is a pattern, and Thursday night was the clearest picture of it yet. A three-goal lead at home against the 30th-placed team should be a routine win, but the Maple Leafs took their foot off the gas at 3-0, conceded three goals in 12 minutes and then lost a shootout they never looked like winning. Eight straight defeats, one win in 11 and a fanbase running out of patience: the question now is not whether something has to change in Toronto, but what changes first — the lineup, the system, or the people running it.

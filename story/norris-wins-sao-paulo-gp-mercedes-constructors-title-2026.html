Norris Wins Wet São Paulo Grand Prix as Mercedes Clinch the Constructors' Title

Norris beat Verstappen by 14.6 seconds in a rain-hit race at Interlagos, while Russell and Antonelli's fourth and fifth places sealed Mercedes' ninth constructors' title.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Lando Norris won a thrilling wet São Paulo Grand Prix on Sunday, beating Max Verstappen by 14.6 seconds at Interlagos, as Mercedes clinched the 2026 Formula 1 constructors' championship with four races still to run. It was Norris's third win of the season and his second consecutive victory in Brazil.

Norris claimed the win from pole position during the 71-lap race at the Autódromo José Carlos Pace, which began wet, dried out, and then caught a late rain shower. The McLaren driver led for 47 of the 71 laps and completed the race in 1 hour, 35 minutes and 23.504 seconds, navigating the changing conditions better than anyone else on the grid.

Verstappen's drive was the story of the race. The Red Bull driver started 21st after an engine penalty and climbed through the field in a frenetic performance, reaching 13th on the opening lap, eighth by lap five and second by the finish. His teammate Isack Hadjar let him through to have a go at catching Norris, then held on for third himself, giving Red Bull a double podium.

Behind them, George Russell finished fourth and Kimi Antonelli fifth for Mercedes — and that was enough to secure the team's ninth constructors' championship, its first since 2021. Mercedes now has 652 points against Ferrari's 510 and cannot be caught. The title completed a double for the team a week after 20-year-old Antonelli became the youngest drivers' champion in history with four races to spare in Mexico City.

Antonelli, who clinched the drivers' title last weekend, finished fifth after an eventful race, one place behind his teammate. Charles Leclerc was seventh for Ferrari and Lewis Hamilton ninth, and the Italian team never threatened to prolong the constructors' fight. Hamilton had started 20th and climbed to ninth by the end of the first lap, but could get no further.

Norris's celebration was almost as eventful as his drive. The 26-year-old, the 2025 world champion, smashed a champagne bottle as he attempted his trademark celebration of banging it against the podium floor to pop the cork, and appeared to get an unexpected blast from fireworks moments later. He emerged unscathed, and the mishaps only added to the theatre of a race he had dominated.

The victory added to Norris's wins in Hungary and the Netherlands this season, and it was his second consecutive win in Brazil from pole position. Soccer great Neymar waved the checkered flag as Norris crossed the line, and the McLaren driver was so excited that his usual podium routine went awry within seconds of the finish.

In the championship standings, Norris sits sixth overall, 33 points behind second-placed Verstappen, with Antonelli already crowned champion. The constructors' title was the main prize still on offer in Brazil, and Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff celebrated the ninth title in the team's history after the race.

The next Grand Prix takes place in Las Vegas on November 21, with uncertainty hanging over the two final races of the season in Qatar and Abu Dhabi because of tensions in the Middle East. For now, though, the 2026 titles are settled: Antonelli the youngest champion in history, and Mercedes champions of the constructors' standings once again.

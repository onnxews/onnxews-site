Virginia Sen. Louise Lucas Pleads Not Guilty to Federal Racketeering and Drug Charges

Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas pleaded not guilty Monday to federal charges accusing her of leading a marijuana enterprise that prosecutors say made about $4.5 million. Her trial is set for September 13, 2027, and she says she will not resign or give up her Senate leadership post.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, one of the most powerful Democrats in Richmond, pleaded not guilty on Monday to federal racketeering, drug and money laundering charges, entering her plea during an arraignment in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia in Newport News. Lucas, 82, is the Senate's president pro tempore and chairs its powerful Finance and Appropriations Committee.

Federal prosecutors accuse Lucas of leading a criminal enterprise that acquired and sold large quantities of marijuana and laundered the proceeds. She is one of seven defendants charged in an eight-count federal indictment announced on October 22 by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Virginia. The charges include racketeering conspiracy, marijuana distribution, money laundering, conspiracy to commit honest services fraud and maintaining a drug-involved premises.

According to prosecutors, Lucas led what they describe as the Lucas Enterprise, a criminal operation involving family members and business associates that acquired and distributed at least 100 kilograms of marijuana. The indictment alleges the operation generated an estimated $4.5 million over approximately five years and that Lucas helped direct the business activities and oversee the laundering of proceeds. Investigators say the alleged operation involved businesses associated with VA Freedom Life, including The CBD Shoppe and The Cannabis Outlet in Portsmouth.

Prosecutors also allege that Lucas used her political position to protect the operation. They say she used her role as chairwoman of the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee to shield the enterprise from criminal scrutiny and sought to influence Virginia marijuana legislation to financially benefit it. At a news conference and in charging documents, authorities said Lucas used her political power to insulate the operation from scrutiny.

The charges followed FBI searches in May at Lucas' hometown office and a cannabis shop she owns. Federal authorities said investigators recovered marijuana and firearms during the searches. Lucas has denied wrongdoing and has said she intends to vigorously contest the allegations. Her attorney, Anthony Capozzolo, said by email that Lucas "looks forward to litigating this matter in the future" and declined further comment.

A federal judge scheduled the trial for September 13, 2027, with proceedings expected to last approximately four weeks. U.S. District Judge Jamar K. Walker is presiding over the case. With Monday's proceedings, all seven defendants have formally pleaded not guilty. Lucas' daughter, Theresa Lucas-Lamb, and Prince Albert William Hudson III also pleaded not guilty on Monday, while four other defendants, including her daughter Lisa Lucas-Burke and her grandson Ryan Eichelberger, entered their pleas at an earlier arraignment on November 12.

Lucas' legal team is also fighting the case on procedural grounds. Before Monday's arraignment, her attorneys filed a motion asking the court to sanction Attorney General Todd Blanche, FBI Director Kash Patel and First Assistant U.S. Attorney Theophani Stamos, arguing they violated the Eastern District of Virginia's Local Criminal Rule 57.1, which deals with the press and fair trials, in statements at the October 22 news conference announcing the indictment. Judge Walker acknowledged the motions during Monday's proceedings but did not rule on them. A Justice Department spokesperson said the department "remains confident in its case and will not comment further on pending litigation."

Lucas has made clear she is not going anywhere. She has told reporters she will not resign and will not step down from her leadership role chairing the Senate finance committee while the case plays out, and she has continued participating in legislative committee hearings while the General Assembly is out of session. She told The Virginian-Pilot that she will seek reelection next year. After the indictment was announced in October, she told reporters: "I am not a victim, I am a survivor."

The case sits at the intersection of Virginia politics and Virginia's unsettled marijuana laws. Marijuana has been legal to possess in Virginia since 2021, and many shops across the state have sold cannabis-derived products, but full recreational marijuana sales will not be allowed until July 2027. Lucas chairs the Senate committee that shapes the state budget, and her indictment has already raised questions about Virginia's plan to open a regulated retail market, with the state commission voting Monday to recommend barring lawmakers from holding retail cannabis licenses.

The allegations have not been proven in court, and Lucas is presumed innocent unless prosecutors establish her guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. With the trial more than nine months away, the case will now move into pretrial proceedings, including the pending defense motions, while Lucas continues to hold one of the most powerful jobs in Virginia government.

Why it matters: The plea itself was expected — Lucas said from the start she would fight the charges — but the case is remarkable for what it puts on trial alongside her: a sitting Senate leader who controls the state budget, accused of using that office to protect a family marijuana business. The nine months until trial will be filled with motions, including the unusual request to sanction the attorney general himself, and the case will hang over Virginia's effort to build a legal recreational marijuana market. Whatever the verdict, the indictment has already changed the politics of the state's most powerful committee.

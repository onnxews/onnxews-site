Johnson Steps Aside as House Republican Leader; Scalise and Jordan Launch Bids to Succeed Him

House Speaker Mike Johnson told staff he will not seek to lead Republicans in the minority, opening a leadership fight one day after Democrats retook the House.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

House Speaker Mike Johnson told his staff on Wednesday that he will not seek to be minority leader in the next session of Congress, a day after Republicans suffered a sweeping midterm defeat, multiple sources on his team told the New York Post.

Johnson, a Louisiana Republican who rose to the speakership in October 2023, was re-elected to a sixth House term on Tuesday and is expected to remain in Congress as a rank-and-file member. In a letter to colleagues he called serving as speaker the greatest professional honor of his life.

The race to succeed him opened immediately. House Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana and House Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan of Ohio both announced bids on Wednesday to become the party's next leader.

Scalise told colleagues his top goal is leading the conference back into the majority, while Jordan wrote that the party had operated without a clear plan and needed to change course against an emboldened Democratic majority.

House Republicans are scheduled to hold their leadership elections on November 10, with candidates asked to declare by noon on November 6. President Donald Trump told reporters he would stay out of the Republican leadership election. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become speaker when the new Congress convenes in January.

Reporting: New York Post

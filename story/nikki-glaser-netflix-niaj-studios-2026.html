Nikki Glaser to Star in a Scripted Netflix Series as the Streamer Launches Its New Comedy Studio

Netflix has launched Netflix Is a Joke Studios, an in-house incubator for turning stand-up talent into scripted stars, and Glaser is its first partner, with a series and two specials on the way.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Netflix has launched a new in-house comedy studio and signed Nikki Glaser as its first creative partner. The streamer announced on Thursday, November 19, that it is creating Netflix Is a Joke Studios, an incubator and production studio designed to take a holistic approach to comedy talent across multiple formats, and that Glaser will develop, star in and executive produce her own original scripted series, as well as star in and executive produce two stand-up comedy specials exclusively for Netflix.

The scripted series will be executive produced by Gloria Sanchez Productions, the company run by Jessica Elbaum and Will Ferrell. Netflix has not shared the premise, title, cast or release window for the series, and few details were revealed beyond the partnership itself. What is confirmed is the shape of the deal: one scripted series Glaser will develop and star in, plus two specials she will headline and executive produce, all under the new studio.

Tracey Pakosta, Netflix's vice president of comedy series, said in a statement that great comedy starts with an unmistakable voice, and few comedians have one as fully formed as Glaser's. She called Glaser fearless, funny and disarmingly real, and said she was the ideal first partner for the new studio's vision. Robbie Praw, vice president of Netflix standup and comedy formats, said he first met Glaser in a small comedy club 20 years ago and has had a front-row seat to her rise ever since.

Praw credited Glaser's breakout in The Roast of Tom Brady in 2024 with sending her career into overdrive, and said Netflix watched audiences embrace what many inside the company had seen for years. He called her one of the sharpest comedic minds working today, with instincts to build something lasting well beyond stand-up, and said the new studio was the next step in Netflix's commitment to the comedy community. Glaser herself said Netflix gave her some of her earliest and biggest opportunities in comedy, and that she could not be more excited to begin this next phase of her career with them.

The new studio grows out of Netflix Is a Joke, the streamer's flagship comedy brand, home to stand-up specials, the biennial Netflix Is a Joke Fest in Los Angeles, a YouTube channel and a SiriusXM radio channel. The studio is meant to develop projects with stand-up talent beyond the stage, crafting scripted comedies and original formats alongside both established stars and emerging voices. It is a pipeline: a breakout set, roast or hosting moment can now feed an owned series or format inside the same platform.

Netflix has already shown the model works. Stand-up stars including Nick Kroll, Shane Gillis, Bert Kreischer and Leanne Morgan have moved from specials into starring in their own scripted series, including Tires, Free Bert and Leanne, and John Mulaney expanded from stand-up into live variety with Everybody's in LA. The studio formalizes that path, giving comedians a dedicated route from the stage to a series without leaving Netflix's orbit.

Glaser's own Netflix history runs deep. Her first Netflix special, Nikki Glaser: Bangin', arrived in 2019, and she has been a fixture of the Netflix Is a Joke festival lineups in 2024 and 2026. Beyond the Tom Brady roast, she served as a judge on The Roast of Marshawn Lynch on Funny AF with Kevin Hart. She is a Golden Globe, two-time Grammy and four-time Emmy-nominated comedian, writer and actress, and a Writers Guild Award winner.

Her Netflix slate is already busy beyond the new deal. She will next be heard voicing Priscilla in the upcoming Netflix animated film Steps, premiering November 20, and she stars in the Eva Longoria-directed Netflix comedy The Fifth Wheel, premiering in 2027. The scripted series and specials announced on Thursday will add to that slate, though Netflix has given no dates for when any of them will arrive.

The launch of the studio also says something about how Netflix sees comedy now. Stand-up specials are cheap to produce and easy to promote, but a scripted series built around a proven comedian can run for years and travel further. By creating an in-house studio, Netflix is betting that it can own more of that upside, developing talent it already has a relationship with rather than bidding for finished shows on the open market. Glaser, as the first talent through the door, will be the test case for whether the model works.

Why it matters: This deal is about Netflix changing how it buys comedy. Instead of waiting for comedians to bring it a finished show, the streamer is building an in-house studio to grow its own scripted stars out of the stand-up talent it already promotes, and Glaser is the proof of concept. Her path makes the logic clear: a 2019 special, a festival fixture, a star-making roast in 2024, and now a scripted series plus two specials. If the studio works, Netflix gets a steady supply of comedian-led series without competing at auction; if it does not, the Glaser series will still be a notable bet on one of the sharpest voices in comedy. The open question is what the show will actually be, since Netflix has shared no premise at all, and that silence will keep the speculation going until the first details emerge.

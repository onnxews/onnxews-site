Constellation Energy Crushes Earnings Estimates and Raises Its Outlook Again

The nuclear power giant earned $5.06 a share in the third quarter, far above estimates, and lifted its full-year outlook for the second time this year.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Constellation Energy, the largest operator of nuclear power plants in the United States, reported a blowout third quarter on Monday, easily beating Wall Street's estimates and raising its full-year profit forecast for the second time this year.

The company reported adjusted operating earnings of $5.06 per share for the quarter ended in September, up from $3.04 a share a year earlier and well above the $3.89 analysts had expected, according to Zacks Investment Research. Revenue rose 45 percent to $9.55 billion, from $6.57 billion a year earlier, topping estimates of $9.35 billion.

On a GAAP basis, earnings per share were $5.19, against $2.97 in the third quarter of 2025. Adjusted earnings totaled $1,792 million, against $952 million a year earlier. The result was an earnings surprise of nearly 30 percent, and the fourth straight quarter in which Constellation has beaten consensus estimates.

Constellation credited the beat to favorable market and portfolio conditions and the addition of Calpine, the power generator it bought in January 2026. Those gains were partly offset by unfavorable interest costs and its nuclear production tax credit portfolio, the company said.

The company's nuclear fleet, including its owned output from the Salem and South Texas Project stations, produced 46,677 gigawatt-hours in the quarter, against 46,477 gigawatt-hours a year earlier. Steady output from the fleet remains the backbone of the business, even as the company grows its gas-fired and renewable operations.

Constellation also raised its 2026 adjusted operating earnings guidance to a range of $12.50 to $12.80 a share, up from $11.50 to $12.50. The midpoint moves from $12.00 to $12.65. Chief financial officer Shane Smith said the quarter let the company raise the midpoint for the second time this year.

The guidance increase follows a string of new nuclear supply deals. Constellation signed a 20-year agreement to supply 270 megawatts of energy, capacity and nuclear attributes from its existing nuclear fleet starting in 2028. It also announced a 20-year, 690-megawatt agreement with Amazon for its Calvert Cliffs plant in September, a deal that enables more than $3 billion of investment, including a 190-megawatt expansion expected online between 2030 and 2032. The company has also listed new nuclear supply deals with Google.

Shares rose more than 4 percent in premarket trading on Monday after the results. The stock closed at $249.95 on Friday, November 6. Looking ahead, analysts expect earnings of $3.04 a share on $8.16 billion in revenue for the coming quarter, and $12.20 a share on $35.39 billion in revenue for the full year, according to Zacks.

The results land as power demand surges from data centers and artificial intelligence, and as nuclear power enjoys a revival as a source of reliable, carbon-free electricity. Constellation, with the country's biggest nuclear fleet and a growing book of long-term deals with the world's largest technology companies, is positioned at the center of that shift — and this quarter, the numbers showed it.

The strong quarter also fits a broader earnings season that has surprised to the upside. With results in from 441 members of the S&P 500 through Friday, November 6, third-quarter earnings are expected to grow 28.4 percent from a year earlier on 14.4 percent higher revenues, according to Zacks, the eighth straight quarter of double-digit earnings growth for the index. The technology sector has been a major contributor, but the momentum has been broad-based, with 15 of 16 sectors on track for positive earnings growth.

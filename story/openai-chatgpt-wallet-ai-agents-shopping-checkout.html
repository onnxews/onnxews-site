OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet, Letting AI Agents Pay for Your Purchases

The new wallet lets ChatGPT's shopping agents check out on your behalf, using a payment token instead of your real card number.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, a new feature that lets its AI shopping agents complete purchases for users instead of just finding products and leaving the checkout to them.

The wallet is built for the company's "dots" agents, which were introduced in late September and can browse stores, compare options and carry out multi-step tasks. Until now, those agents stopped short of paying. With the wallet, they can finish the purchase after the user approves it.

At launch, the feature is available to a limited number of ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States who are 18 or older. Users can add a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. OpenAI built the wallet together with American Express, Mastercard and Visa.

The checkout flow is designed to keep the human in control. The agent gathers the items and shows the user the total and the card to be charged. The user reviews the order and approves it with a security check, such as Face ID or a fingerprint, which creates a transaction record for the purchase.

OpenAI says the wallet protects card details by using a payment token in place of the real card number. The token is not shared with the AI agent or the merchant, and users keep their card rewards and protections. The company is not taking a cut of purchases at launch, according to reports.

The company also published a Commerce Partner Playbook alongside the launch, aimed at retailers and brands that want their products to be findable and buyable through ChatGPT. The playbook sets out how merchants can make their catalogs work with the agents.

OpenAI has said it plans to go further, allowing users to fully hand off shopping to agents within pre-set spending limits, product categories and time periods. The company has not given a date for that fuller delegation, which would let an agent buy routine items without asking each time.

Not every retailer is on board. Amazon, the largest online retailer, blocks agent purchases, and United Airlines has said it will block AI agents from booking tickets. Those limits mean the wallet's reach will depend on which merchants accept agent checkouts, not just on what the technology can do.

The wallet arrives as every major AI company is trying to turn its chatbot into a place where money changes hands. Google, Amazon and others have all explored versions of agent shopping, but none has yet made it routine. OpenAI's advantage is that ChatGPT is already where many people start their shopping research, and a wallet inside the app is one tap away from the conversation where the shopping begins.

Trust will be the deciding factor. Shoppers who are comfortable letting an agent pick a product may still hesitate to let it pay, and the approval step is OpenAI's answer to that hesitation. If the early users report smooth checkouts and no surprises, the feature could spread quickly; if there are billing errors or unwanted purchases, the backlash will be swift.

The launch is the latest step in the race to turn chatbots into shopping assistants. If users trust agents with their money, the wallet could change how people buy online; if they do not, it will join a long list of payment ideas that never caught on. Read the report from TechCrunch

Why it matters: ChatGPT Wallet is OpenAI's attempt to turn its chatbot from a shopping adviser into a shopping agent, and the stakes are enormous: whoever controls the checkout controls the customer. The token-based design and the approval step are meant to answer the obvious trust problem, but Amazon's refusal and United's planned block show the real fight is over whether merchants will let agents spend on their behalf. Watch whether other big retailers follow Amazon or sign up, because that will decide whether agentic shopping becomes mainstream or stays a niche feature.

Notion Says Fireworks Breach Exposed Some Customers' AI Chat Transcripts

The workspace company told users that transcripts from fewer than 1 percent of workspaces were caught up in a breach at its AI infrastructure provider, and urged affected customers to rotate any credentials shared in chats.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Notion has told customers that a breach at Fireworks AI, one of its artificial intelligence infrastructure providers, exposed some users' AI chat transcripts, the company disclosed this week.

The transcripts came from fewer than 1 percent of Notion workspaces, according to the company, and were part of a dataset used for a feature called Insights, which showed workspace administrators how their teams were using AI. Notion has disabled that feature.

For the vast majority of affected workspaces, the transcripts were unattributable, meaning they carried no author or workspace label and included no results from tool calls, Notion said. The company advised affected users to rotate any passwords, API keys or other credentials they had pasted into the affected chats.

Workspace owners who were affected can review what was exposed through a "Security incident review" page and share a download link with their teams. Notion said customers who did not receive a notice need to take no action.

The timeline shows the exposure was discovered in stages. Fireworks alerted Notion on November 2 about an exposed dataset that was initially described as containing only synthetic test data. Notion cut off traffic to Fireworks, deleted its data there and halted training runs by November 3.

It was not until November 8 that Fireworks confirmed customer data had actually been accessed. Notion revoked the exposed Notion API keys on November 11, according to BleepingComputer.

The episode is awkward for both companies because Fireworks had contractually committed to zero data retention for Notion's data, yet the data was retained and then exposed. Notion said it has stopped serving open-weight models through Fireworks.

The Fireworks breach is wider than Notion. The provider disclosed that a breach of a third-party cloud provider hosting its research and development machines on October 23, with attackers reaching development machines on October 24, exposed billing records, resource metadata and incomplete model weights and datasets for some customers. An earlier breach on October 6 exposed API keys and tokens stored in environment variables.

Fireworks said in a bulletin on November 9 that its Inference, Training SDK and Nexus products were not affected because they retain no customer data by default. The investigation is continuing, law enforcement has been notified, and the company is working with Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42.

For Notion users, the practical question is simple: if you received a notice, check the review page and rotate anything sensitive you typed into an AI chat. For the wider industry, the incident is a reminder that the data you share with an AI feature may travel further than the company you gave it to.

The six days between the first alert and the confirmation that customer data was involved will draw scrutiny. Fireworks told Notion on November 2 that an exposed dataset contained only synthetic test data, and it was not until November 8 that the provider confirmed customer data had been accessed. In the meantime, Notion had already cut traffic, deleted its data at Fireworks and halted training runs — a fast containment, but one that left users in the dark about what had actually been exposed.

What remains unclear is the full scope of the Fireworks incident. The provider has disclosed three separate exposures — the October 6 leak of API keys and tokens, the October 23–24 breach of its research machines, and the dataset that caught Notion's transcripts — and each was disclosed in stages. Other Fireworks customers may yet come forward with their own disclosures, and the incident is likely to push enterprise buyers to ask harder questions about how their AI vendors store and retain data.

Why it matters: This breach matters because it exposes the supply chain behind AI features: Notion's customers trusted Notion, but their chats sat on Fireworks' infrastructure, under a zero-retention promise that turned out not to hold. The six-day gap between the first alert and confirmation that customer data was involved also shows how breach disclosure unfolds in stages, leaving users uncertain for days. What to watch next is whether regulators or enterprise customers demand stronger proof of retention controls from AI vendors — and whether other Fireworks customers come forward with their own disclosures.

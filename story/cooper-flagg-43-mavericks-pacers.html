Cooper Flagg Drops 43 as Mavericks Rally Past Pacers 125-122

The rookie star poured in 43 points with 10 rebounds and six assists, and Kyrie Irving added 35, as Dallas erased a halftime deficit in a high-scoring thriller.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Cooper Flagg powered the Dallas Mavericks to a 125-122 win over the Indiana Pacers on Monday night, scoring 43 points with 10 rebounds and six assists in one of the best performances of his young career. The game at the American Airlines Center in Dallas was decided by three points, and Flagg was the difference, taking over in the second half as the Mavericks erased a halftime deficit.

Flagg shot 16 of 25 from the field, including three three-pointers, and he did his damage from everywhere on the floor: driving to the rim, pulling up in the mid-range and finishing in transition. It was the kind of complete performance that explains why he was the most hyped prospect in years, and why the Mavericks built their team around him. At a time when the game was there to be won, the ball was in his hands, and he delivered.

Kyrie Irving was the perfect partner, adding 35 points on 11-of-18 shooting, including seven three-pointers. The two combined for 78 points, and their partnership was the story of the night: Irving spacing the floor and punishing the Pacers from deep, Flagg doing everything else. When Dallas needed a basket in the closing minutes, it had two players the defence could not stop at the same time.

The Pacers made the Mavericks earn it. Indiana led 66-56 at halftime after winning the first two quarters, 31-26 and 35-30, and looked in control of a game that was being played at their pace. But Dallas came out of the break with purpose, winning the third quarter 34-27 and the fourth 35-29, and the comeback was built on defence as much as on offence: the Mavericks held the Pacers to 56 points in the second half after giving up 66 in the first.

The win extends Dallas's winning streak to three games, and it is the latest evidence that the Mavericks are finding their rhythm. The early weeks of the season were about learning how to play together, with a rookie star, a veteran scorer and a roster still settling into its roles. Nights like this one, when both stars are firing and the team wins a close game against a good opponent, are the ones that build belief.

For Flagg, the 43-point night is another milestone in a rookie season that keeps getting better. He has already shown he can rebound, defend and make plays for others, and the scoring is catching up fast. A 43-point game with 10 rebounds and six assists is the kind of line that gets remembered, and it came against a Pacers team that had no answer for him in the second half. The efficiency matters too: 16 of 25 from the field is not a player forcing shots, it is a player taking what the defence gives him and making it count.

Indiana will be disappointed to let this one slip. The Pacers led for most of the night and had the game in their hands at halftime, but they could not slow down Flagg and Irving when it mattered, and they lost a game they will feel they should have won. The Pacers fall to a second straight loss, and the defeat is a reminder that in the NBA, a ten-point halftime lead is a long way from a win.

The broader picture is that the Western Conference is shaping up to be a war. San Antonio is 9-2, the Lakers are 10-2, Golden State has won eight straight, and Dallas is now rolling with its young star playing the best basketball of his life. The Mavericks are not just a team with a bright future any more; they are a team winning games right now, against good teams, in close finishes.

What happens next for Flagg is the question every fan is asking. The rookie has already produced nights that would be career highlights for most players, and he is doing it while learning the league on the job. If this is what he looks like in November, the rest of the season should be something to watch. For the Pacers, the task is simpler: learn how to close out a game they are winning. For Dallas, the task is to keep the streak going, and to keep feeding the star who is making it all happen.

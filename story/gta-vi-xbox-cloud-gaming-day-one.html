GTA VI Will Be Playable on Xbox One Through Cloud Gaming on Launch Day

Microsoft confirmed the biggest game of the year will stream to last-generation Xbox consoles on November 19, with no new hardware required.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Microsoft announced on November 10 that Grand Theft Auto VI will be playable on the Xbox One through Xbox Cloud Gaming on the day it launches, November 19, giving owners of the 13-year-old console a way into the most anticipated game of the year without buying new hardware. Streaming begins at 1 a.m. ET on launch day, and the game will also be streamable on Xbox Series X and S consoles.

The announcement matters because there is no native Xbox One version of the game. Instead, the game runs on Microsoft's cloud servers and streams to the older console over the internet, using Xbox Series S graphics settings. It is the same approach Microsoft has used to bring demanding current-generation games to older hardware, but never before for a launch of this size.

Microsoft said Grand Theft Auto VI is the most pre-ordered title of all time on the Microsoft Store, a sign of the scale of demand the company is preparing for. The game launches November 19 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, and it is widely expected to be the biggest entertainment release ever, with analysts watching whether it can top the opening weekends of the largest film and game launches in history.

There is a catch for players hoping to try it cheaply. The game must be purchased separately and will not be included with Xbox Game Pass, Microsoft confirmed. Starting November 11, players in supported markets can buy cloud playtime to stream supported games without a Game Pass subscription, but that pay-as-you-go option covers the streaming time only, not the game itself.

The cloud announcement came alongside a broader deal between Microsoft and Rockstar Games' parent company, Take-Two Interactive. Xbox chief executive Asha Sharma announced a 10-year strategic partnership with Take-Two, and a securities filing showed the two companies signed a new long-term Xbox Publisher License Agreement on September 28, effective September 17. Microsoft also said more Rockstar titles, including Grand Theft Auto V and Red Dead Redemption 2, will join Xbox Cloud Gaming in the coming months.

PC players, however, were left out of the announcement. Rockstar confirmed that Grand Theft Auto VI will not come to PC as part of its initial cloud release, and Microsoft's announcement named only Xbox One and Xbox Series X and S consoles as supported endpoints for streaming. Windows PCs, smartphones, smart TVs and web browsers are excluded from cloud streaming at launch, even though the service supports those devices for other games.

The move extends the life of a console generation that Microsoft stopped manufacturing years ago. The Xbox One launched in 2013, and millions of the consoles are still in living rooms. For those owners, the choice had been to upgrade to a Series X or S, buy a PlayStation 5, or sit the launch out. Cloud streaming offers a fourth option, provided their internet connection can handle it.

It also marks a milestone for cloud gaming itself. Streaming has long been pitched as a way to remove hardware barriers, but the biggest releases have usually arrived on the cloud weeks or months after launch, if at all. Putting the year's biggest game on the cloud on day one is a test of whether the technology is ready for a mainstream audience at a scale no cloud launch has faced before.

The timing is tight. With the announcement landing nine days before launch, Microsoft has little room to fix problems if launch-day demand overwhelms its servers. Reports have said the game is locked to 30 frames per second across consoles, and the streamed version will use the Series S visual profile regardless of the console it is played on, so players chasing the best picture will still want the newest hardware.

For now, the message to Xbox One owners is simple: the game will be there on November 19 at 1 a.m. ET, as long as they have bought it and their connection holds up. Whether the servers hold up under the weight of the most pre-ordered game in Microsoft Store history is the question the whole industry will be watching next week.

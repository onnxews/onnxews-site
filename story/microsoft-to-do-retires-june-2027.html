Microsoft To Do to Retire in June 2027, With No Free Replacement for Personal Users

The task app will shut down across Windows, Mac, iOS, Android and the web on June 30, 2027, and personal account holders are being told to find another app.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Microsoft will retire its To Do app on June 30, 2027, ending the task manager on Windows, Mac, iOS, Android, and the web. The announcement was tucked into a blog post about Microsoft's unified tasks and plans experience during the company's Ignite week, and was spotted in a Microsoft support document by Windows Latest.

The retirement is part of a consolidation of Microsoft's task tools. Work and school accounts will move to Planner, the company's work-management service, which is becoming the single home for tasks across Microsoft 365, Teams, and Copilot. To Do's features are being folded into Planner's redesigned My Tasks view, which Microsoft says will give users a single place for their work.

For people using To Do with a personal Microsoft account, the news is much worse. Planner is not available to personal accounts, including subscribers to Microsoft 365 Personal and Microsoft 365 Family, and Microsoft has said it has no plans to change that. Personal users are being told to choose another task-management app and to export their data before the shutdown.

Microsoft has promised an export tool so users can take their tasks and lists out of To Do, but it has given no date for when the tool will arrive and no details about what it will include. There is also no guarantee that an export from To Do will import cleanly into another app, which could leave long-time users rebuilding their task lists by hand.

Existing tasks and lists will not move over automatically. For work and school accounts, tasks will be visible in Planner until the retirement date of June 30, 2027, but Microsoft has not described a migration path that carries lists, attachments, or settings across. The Register reported that the underlying To Do service, its programming interfaces, and its sync features will retire on separate, later timelines.

The move also leaves some features behind. Planner has no native apps for Windows or Mac, so users who liked To Do's desktop apps will be working in a browser or inside Teams. Planner's private plan does not support file attachments on tasks, and it has no equivalent of To Do's list groups, so people who organized their tasks into grouped lists will lose that structure.

To Do was the successor to Wunderlist, the popular task app Microsoft bought in 2015 for a reported $100 million to $200 million. Microsoft rebuilt Wunderlist as To Do and shut the older app down in 2020, positioning To Do as the task app for everyone from office workers to families managing their shopping lists.

The retirement fits a wider pattern at Microsoft of folding standalone tools into its Copilot and Microsoft 365 platform. Ignite, the company's big annual conference for IT professionals, has become the stage for these consolidations, with the message that fewer apps, tied more closely to AI, will serve users better than a shelf of separate products.

For now, To Do keeps working, and Microsoft says the app will remain available until the June 2027 date. Users who rely on it have more than a year to make a decision, but the lack of a replacement for personal accounts and the missing export tool mean the clock is already ticking for anyone with years of tasks stored in the app.

Why it matters: This is a reminder that a free app from a big company is never permanent, and the people who lose out most are the ones who never paid for it. Work and school users get a landing spot in Planner, but personal users, including paying Microsoft 365 subscribers, are simply told to go elsewhere. The retirement also shows how Microsoft is consolidating its tools around Copilot and its work platform, and it raises a practical question that Microsoft has not yet answered: if the export tool is late or incomplete, millions of ordinary users could lose years of lists and reminders with nowhere to put them.

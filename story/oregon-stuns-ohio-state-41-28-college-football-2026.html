Oregon Storms Back From 14 Down to Stun Ohio State 41-28 and Damage the Buckeyes' Playoff Hopes

The Ducks scored 38 straight points after falling behind early, handing Ohio State its third loss of the season and leaving its postseason chances slimmer than ever.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Oregon stunned Ohio State 41-28 on Saturday, storming back from an early 14-point deficit to beat the Buckeyes at Ohio Stadium. Ohio State entered the week ranked No. 12 in the College Football Playoff standings, and after the loss its chances of making the postseason are slimmer than ever.

The Buckeyes led 14-0 at the end of the first quarter, and the home crowd had every reason to believe a comfortable win was coming. Then Oregon scored 38 straight points, and Ohio State went without a score for over two hours until wide receiver Jeremiah Smith caught a 13-yard touchdown pass from freshman quarterback Luke Fahey.

It was a collapse that will be remembered in Columbus for a long time. Ohio State has now lost its third game of the regular season, the first time the Buckeyes have done so in 15 years. For a program that measures itself by championships, three regular-season losses before mid-November is a mark of a season gone wrong.

The Ducks, ranked No. 23, improved to 6-3 overall and 4-2 in the Big Ten, the same conference record as Ohio State, who also fell to 6-3. Oregon's comeback was built on resilience, as the Ducks absorbed the early blows, adjusted, and then took control of the game so completely that the final score flattered the home team.

The turning point came after the first quarter, when Oregon's offense found its rhythm and its defense began to suffocate Ohio State's attack. The Buckeyes, who had moved the ball easily in the opening minutes, suddenly could not sustain a drive, and the Ducks turned the game into a rout before the home crowd had time to process what was happening.

For Ohio State coach Ryan Day, the loss raises uncomfortable questions. The Buckeyes have now lost two straight games, and the offense has looked fragile in both. Fahey, a freshman quarterback, was thrown into a difficult spot, and while his touchdown pass to Smith provided a late consolation, the damage had been done long before.

The Big Ten picture is now crowded and confusing. UCLA remains unbeaten at 9-0 and 6-0 in the conference after beating Minnesota 34-20, while Iowa improved to 7-2 with a 24-10 win over Northwestern. Oregon and Ohio State sit in the chasing pack at 4-2 in league play, and the Ducks now own the head-to-head tiebreaker that could matter in December.

Elsewhere in the Big Ten on Saturday, Maryland beat Purdue 34-29, Penn State edged Washington 28-25, Wisconsin beat Rutgers 31-10, and Michigan State beat Michigan 15-6 in a rivalry upset. Illinois had beaten Nebraska 24-13 on Friday night. The conference race is wide open, but Ohio State's path to the title game now requires help from elsewhere.

For Oregon, the win is a statement. The Ducks were written off after an uneven start to the season, but beating Ohio State in Columbus, in the manner they did it, announces them as a team that can compete with anyone. A 38-point unanswered run against a program of Ohio State's stature is not an accident, and it will be remembered as one of the great road wins in school history.

Ohio State's season is not over, but its ambitions have shrunk. The Buckeyes must now win out and hope for chaos elsewhere to have any chance of reaching the playoff, and the manner of this defeat, a 14-point lead turned into a 13-point loss, will linger. Oregon, meanwhile, heads into the final stretch of the season with momentum, belief and a tiebreaker that could yet prove decisive.

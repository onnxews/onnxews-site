Bills Wear Down Vikings 23-6 on Monday Night Football to Take the AFC's Best Record

Buffalo's defence smothered Minnesota and James Cook ground out 107 yards as the Bills improved to 7-1, while the Vikings lost their second straight after a 6-0 start.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Buffalo Bills wore down the Minnesota Vikings, winning 23-6 on Monday Night Football to move to 7-1 and take the best record in the AFC. The Week 9 matchup at U.S. Bank Stadium in Minneapolis was billed as a clash between Buffalo's high-powered offence and Minnesota's suffocating defence, but it turned into a grind, and the Bills were the team that knew how to win one.

Buffalo's victory was built on defence and ball control. The Bills ran 79 plays, gained 399 yards and held the ball for an extraordinary 42 minutes and 32 seconds, wearing down a Vikings defence that had been one of the league's best. Minnesota managed just 178 total yards and nine first downs, cracked Buffalo's red zone only one time, and turned the ball over three times. It was the second straight opponent Buffalo has held under 200 yards of total offence.

Quarterback Josh Allen was steady rather than spectacular, completing 21 of 31 passes for 234 yards with a touchdown and an interception, and adding 15 yards on the ground. The touchdown went to tight end Keleki Latu, who scored the game's first touchdown in the second quarter to give Buffalo a 10-3 lead. Allen's numbers will not make any highlight reels, but on a night when the Vikings defence was determined to take away the big play, his patience was exactly what the Bills needed.

Running back James Cook was the workhorse. He carried the ball 31 times for 107 yards and a touchdown, the second-most carries in a game in his career, and he sealed the win with a punishing drive in the fourth quarter. Buffalo put the game beyond doubt with a 15-play, 84-yard drive that consumed eight minutes and 32 seconds, with Cook carrying nine times on the sequence before scoring on a five-yard run to make it 20-6. A final Tyler Bass field goal from 52 yards set the final score at 23-6.

Minnesota's night was defined by the struggles of quarterback Carson Wentz, who squandered another excellent defensive performance. Wentz completed just eight of 25 passes for 81 yards and two interceptions before giving way to backup Max Brosmer in the fourth quarter. The Vikings' offence finished with 51 rushing yards and never found the end zone, settling for two Will Reichard field goals, including a 53-yarder just before halftime that cut Buffalo's lead to 10-6.

The Vikings' defence deserved better. It held Buffalo to 10 points in the first half and kept the game within reach into the fourth quarter, but it spent so much time on the field that it eventually broke. Buffalo's 42:32 of possession is the kind of number that tells the story of a game more honestly than the score, and by the time Cook was pounding the ball in the fourth quarter, the Minnesota defenders were running on empty.

The result shakes up the NFC North as well as the AFC. Minnesota falls to 6-2 and drops to third in the division after losing its second straight game, and the surging Chicago Bears have taken over first place, though they are only one game ahead of the fourth-place Green Bay Packers. The Vikings started the season 6-0 and looked like one of the league's best teams, but back-to-back losses have exposed an offence that cannot score enough to support its defence.

For Buffalo, the win extends a four-game winning streak and strengthens the case that the Bills are the team to beat in the AFC. They have now won on the road, in prime time, against a fellow contender, without needing Allen to be superhuman. That is the mark of a complete team, and it is the kind of win that matters more in January than a shootout would. The Bills are 4-0 on the road this season, and they have not lost since September.

What happens next for Minnesota is the harder question. The Vikings have a quarterback problem, and everyone in the league can see it. Wentz's performance on Monday night will only increase the calls for a change, whether that means a trade, a switch to Brosmer, or a different answer altogether. The defence is good enough to win a championship, but the offence is not good enough to win a playoff game. Buffalo, meanwhile, heads into the second half of the season as the AFC's best team, with a defence that is peaking and a running game that can close out games. Monday night was not pretty, but for the Bills, it was perfect.

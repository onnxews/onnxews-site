Google Brings Gemini Computer Use to the Mac, Letting Its AI Click and Type for You

Gemini can now work through multi-step tasks across Mac apps, local files and the web in a background window, putting Google in direct competition with the AI agents already living on the desktop.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Google on Thursday brought computer use to its Gemini desktop app on macOS, allowing its artificial intelligence to complete tasks on a user's behalf by navigating native Mac apps and browsing websites in the background. The feature, announced November 19, turns Gemini from a chatbot that answers questions into an agent that takes actions directly on the computer.

Computer use lets Gemini handle multi-step work across local files, desktop apps and the web. A user describes what is needed in plain language, and Gemini handles the clicking, typing, menus and forms. The work happens in an isolated background window, with a live preview shown in the chat, while the user's own screen and cursor stay free so they can keep working on something else at the same time.

Google pitched the feature as a way to clear away repetitive work. Its examples include batch color-grading photos in design software, setting up complex three-dimensional scenes, extracting weights, dimensions and addresses from local PDF files to fill out multi-box shipping quotes, and pulling metrics and screenshots from live dashboards to build a presentation in Google Slides. It can also work through a folder of clothing photos, research pricing for each item and stage listings on resale platforms such as eBay.

The agent only touches the apps and programs a user has given it access to, and it asks for approval before key actions. As with other AI agents offering computer use, users can pause, stop and approve Gemini's actions before they happen, according to TechCrunch's report on the launch.

The rollout is limited for now. Computer use in the Gemini app for Mac is available in English in the United States, and it requires the Gemini app for Mac plus a Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra subscription. It also requires an Apple silicon Mac running macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later and a personal Google account for users aged 18 and over. Google has not said when it will expand to other markets or platforms.

Google arrived at this step later than its main rivals. OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta have already been offering agentic features in their desktop applications for some time, and Google only this week announced the broader rollout of Gemini Tasks, the in-app feature that helps the AI sign into websites, make online purchases or book appointments. Google also recently began admitting early testers to a revamped CC AI agent designed to help families stay organized across email, calendars, chats and tasks.

The Mac launch is part of a wider push to make Gemini an agent rather than just an assistant. Computer use is a different kind of agentic offering from a chatbot: instead of drafting a reply or summarizing a document, the AI takes actions directly on the user's computer. That makes it more useful for chores that span several apps, but it also raises the stakes, because the AI is operating files, forms and accounts on the user's behalf.

For now, the feature's appeal will depend on how reliably it handles the messy middle of real tasks: forms that do not match the template, apps that ask unexpected questions and websites that change their layout. Google's own examples are carefully chosen, and the company says the rollout is gradual. Users who try it will quickly learn whether Gemini can finish a batch of listings or a stack of shipping forms without needing to be rescued halfway through.

The timing also matters for the Mac itself. Apple's latest macOS beta introduces a revamped permission system that separates app control privileges from accessibility permissions, a change that arrives as AI agents seek deeper access to the desktop. Google's computer use will need those permissions to work, and how Apple polices them will shape how much freedom agents like Gemini get on the Mac.

Why it matters: This is Google closing a gap it could no longer afford to leave open. Rivals have spent months putting agents on the desktop, and every week Gemini stayed a chatbot on the Mac was a week users built habits around someone else's agent. The background-window design is the telling detail: Google is trying to make an AI that does your chores without hijacking your screen, which is the only version of computer use most people will tolerate. The real test now is trust. If Gemini completes a batch of listings or a stack of forms cleanly, it becomes part of the workflow; if it mangles a form or books the wrong thing, users will revoke its access just as fast. Watch whether Google extends the feature beyond the United States and its paid tiers, because that will show whether this is a flagship feature or a niche perk.

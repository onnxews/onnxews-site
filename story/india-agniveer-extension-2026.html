India Extends Agniveer Service by Two Years, Opening a Path to Regular Enrolment

The Cabinet approved a "Second Tour of Duty" for the 75 percent of Agniveers not retained after four years. The first batch of about 18,300 was due to leave late this year.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

In a major overhaul of the Agnipath scheme, the Indian government on Wednesday announced that Agniveers set to exit the military at the end of their four-year tenure will be given an additional two-year extension period and subsequently offered permanent recruitment, subject to meeting specific eligibility criteria and individual consent.

The decision was announced by Chief of Defence Staff General N. S. Raja Subramani, shortly after the Union Cabinet approved the changes as recommended by the three services. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh described the move as part of long-term structural reforms aimed at building a younger, fitter and technologically equipped military.

Under the new changes, the 25 percent of Agniveers selected on merit after four years will continue to be enrolled into the regular armed forces, as originally envisaged. The remaining 75 percent will now be offered an additional engagement period of two years, with higher remuneration, an enhanced Seva Nidhi package and cumulative seniority benefits.

At the end of the six years, those willing to continue will be considered for regular enrolment, subject to prescribed selection criteria. "The Cabinet has created a pathway for all Agniveers to be absorbed into the defence forces," Singh said. The Agnipath scheme, introduced in June 2022, will not be discontinued, officials said.

The first batch of about 18,300 Agniveers across the Army, Navy and Air Force was nearing the completion of its four-year tenure in November-December 2026. Opposition parties questioned the timing, with one opposition leader calling the decision an election gimmick and asking what would happen to those who had already completed their tenure and moved into other occupations.

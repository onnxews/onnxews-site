Nate Frazier Scores Three Times as No. 1 Georgia Cruises Past Missouri 48-21

Nate Frazier scored three touchdowns as No. 1 Georgia scored on its first five possessions and cruised past No. 22 Missouri 48-21 on Saturday in front of a sellout crowd of 93,033.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

No. 1 Georgia scored on its first five possessions and rolled past No. 22 Missouri 48-21 on Saturday, November 14, at Sanford Stadium in Athens, in front of a sellout crowd of 93,033. The Bulldogs led 31-0 midway through the second quarter and 31-7 at halftime, and the game was effectively over before Missouri had found its footing.

The tone was set on the first play from scrimmage. Missouri quarterback Austin Simmons was intercepted by Georgia linebacker Raylen Wilson, who returned the ball to the Missouri 7-yard line, and two plays later Nate Frazier scored from 4 yards out for a 7-0 lead just 61 seconds into the game. It was the kind of start that takes the air out of a visiting team, and Missouri never recovered from it.

Frazier ran for three touchdowns and finished with 72 yards on 14 carries, and his third score made it 41-7 with 6:15 left in the third quarter. Quarterback Gunner Stockton completed 18 of 24 passes for 252 yards and a touchdown and also ran for an 8-yard touchdown, directing an offence that scored on every one of its first five drives. Georgia finished with 468 total yards to Missouri's 286, and the Bulldogs' defence held the Tigers to 286 yards all day.

Simmons threw for 175 yards and three touchdowns, but most of his production came after the game was decided, and the three scores did little to change a contest that Georgia had controlled from the first minute. Missouri's offence, which had been one of the more productive in the Southeastern Conference, found no answer for a Georgia defence that forced the early turnover and then kept the Tigers out of the end zone until the game was out of reach.

The win improved Georgia to 9-1 overall and 7-1 in the SEC, while Missouri fell to 6-4 and 3-4 in the conference. Georgia had been ranked No. 1 in the new College Football Playoff rankings released earlier in the week, and the win over Missouri was the first test of that ranking. The Bulldogs passed it easily, and they did so in a way that left no doubt about which team was better on the day.

For Georgia, the game was also a reminder of how deep the roster is. Frazier's three touchdowns came on a day when the Bulldogs did not need to ask Stockton to throw the ball 30 times, and the defence's early interception set up the offence rather than the other way around. When a team can win by 27 points while its quarterback throws for 252 yards and its running back scores three times, it is a team with more than one way to win.

Missouri will take little from the game beyond the second-half scores that made the final margin respectable. The Tigers were outplayed in every phase, from the opening interception to the final whistle, and the 93,033 fans in Sanford Stadium saw a home team that never looked in danger after the first minute. The loss drops Missouri to 6-4, and the Tigers will need to regroup quickly if they are to finish the season strongly.

The victory keeps Georgia on course for the SEC title game and the playoff, and it does so with two games still to play. The Bulldogs' only loss this season came earlier in the campaign, and they have now won every game since, with the defence getting stronger as the season has gone on. A team that scores on its first five possessions and leads 31-0 before halftime is a team that is peaking at the right time of the year.

What happens next for Georgia depends on the final two games and the SEC title race, but the Bulldogs have now shown they can win a big game at home in front of a sellout crowd without ever being threatened. For Missouri, the task is simpler: recover, win the games that remain, and try to forget a Saturday in Athens that was over almost as soon as it began.

Why it matters: The game mattered because it was Georgia's first test as the No. 1 team in the new playoff rankings, and the Bulldogs passed it in the most emphatic way possible — scoring on their first five possessions and leading 31-0 before the game was half over. A 27-point win in which the defence set the tone on the first play is the kind of performance that confirms a ranking rather than merely defending it. Missouri's late touchdowns made the score respectable, but they did not change a game that was decided in the first 61 seconds.

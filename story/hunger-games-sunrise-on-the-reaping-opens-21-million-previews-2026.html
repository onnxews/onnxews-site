Sunrise on the Reaping Opens With $21 Million in Previews as Hunger Games Roars Back

The Haymitch prequel drew $21 million from Thursday previews and early screenings and is tracking toward Lionsgate's first $100 million opening in more than a decade.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opened in theaters across the United States on Friday, Nov. 20, and the early numbers suggest the franchise still has a large audience waiting for it. Lionsgate's prequel earned $21 million from Thursday previews and early-access screenings, a start that puts it on course for one of the biggest opening weekends the studio has seen in years. For a series that last released a film three years ago, the turnout is an early sign that the return to Panem has landed with the fans who grew up with it.

The preview figure is striking in comparison with the previous film in the series. The first Hunger Games prequel, The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, took $5.75 million in previews in November 2023 before opening to $44.6 million. The new film's $21 million is more than triple that preview total, at roughly 3.7 times the earlier figure, and it is already close to half of its predecessor's entire opening weekend before a single full day of ticket sales is counted.

Within the franchise, the preview haul ranks as the second best ever, behind only the $25.5 million earned by Catching Fire in 2013. It is also the second-biggest preview total of 2026 so far, well behind the $72 million posted by Spider-Man: Brand New Day on its way to a record-breaking $360 million weekend, but ahead of The Odyssey, which earned $17.6 million in previews before opening to $123.5 million, and ahead of the $19.2 million that Wicked took in previews two years ago on its way to a $112.5 million debut.

Forecasters now expect Sunrise on the Reaping to open to $100 million or more in North America, with some estimates reaching as high as $120 million. If it clears $100 million, it would be Lionsgate's first $100 million opening since Mockingjay Part 2 launched with $102 million in 2015, ending an 11-year wait for the studio. It would also beat the $97 million opening of Michael, Lionsgate's earlier hit from 2026, and it would more than double the debut of The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes.

The film is the sixth in the Hunger Games series and the second prequel, adapted from Suzanne Collins's novel of the same name and directed by Francis Lawrence, who has overseen the franchise since Catching Fire. It tells the story of a young Haymitch Abernathy, played by Joseph Zada, competing in the Hunger Games, with McKenna Grace as fellow tribute Maysilee Donner. Woody Harrelson, who played the older Haymitch in the original films, returns for a cameo, as do Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson.

Early reaction from audiences has been warm. Critics have leaned positive, giving the film a 67% score on Rotten Tomatoes, while fans who saw it early have rated it far higher, at 95%. That gap between critics and audiences is familiar for big franchise releases, and for Lionsgate the audience number matters more: a fan-driven prequel lives or dies on whether the core audience shows up in force on opening weekend, and the preview figures say it did.

There is still reason for caution before the weekend totals arrive. A film that draws heavily on its most devoted fans can be frontloaded, meaning it opens huge and then falls sharply in its second weekend. TheWrap noted that Sunrise on the Reaping could see steep declines after the Thanksgiving period, which would keep it from matching the full run of the studio's biggest hits. But even with that risk, a domestic run above $200 million is still considered within reach, which would top the $166 million earned by The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes.

The release was staged to build momentum ahead of the holiday. Early-access screenings in premium formats began on Wednesday, Nov. 18, followed by Thursday previews, before the official opening on Friday, Nov. 20. As of Saturday morning, Nov. 21, no official Friday gross had been published, and the studio's weekend estimates are expected on Sunday. Until then, the $21 million in previews is the firmest number available, and it is a strong one for a franchise that many had written off as finished.

Why it matters: The opening matters because it answers the biggest question hanging over Lionsgate's franchise strategy: whether audiences still care about the Hunger Games a decade after the original series ended. A $100 million-plus debut would confirm that the series can still open like a major event, justifying the studio's decision to keep working with Suzanne Collins and Francis Lawrence, and it would give Lionsgate a second major hit in a single year after Michael. It also shows how much of the modern box office now depends on preview screenings and presales, with Thursday numbers setting the narrative for the whole weekend before most moviegoers have bought a ticket.

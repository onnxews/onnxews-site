Michigan Stuns No. 4 UCLA in Triple Overtime to Hand Bruins Their First Loss

Bryce Underwood found Jalen Hoffman for the winning two-point conversion in the third overtime as Michigan ended UCLA's unbeaten season 18-16 in front of 109,618 fans at the Big House.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Michigan stunned previously unbeaten No. 4 UCLA 18-16 in triple overtime on Saturday, handing the Bruins their first loss of the season and shaking up the College Football Playoff picture. Bryce Underwood threw a 3-yard two-point conversion pass to tight end Jalen Hoffman in the third overtime to end a defensive slugfest at Michigan Stadium, where 109,618 fans poured onto the field to celebrate the Wolverines' biggest win of a difficult season.

The ending was fitting for a game in which neither offence could find any rhythm. Michigan made a defensive stop against UCLA backup quarterback Madden Iamaleava to open the third overtime, when teams trade single two-point conversion plays from the 3-yard line, and Underwood then rolled right and threw a dart to Hoffman in the corner of the end zone. It was the Wolverines' first overtime win since the 2024 Rose Bowl against Alabama, and their highest-ranked victory since beating No. 2 Ohio State in 2024. Michigan's defence, not its offence, won the game.

Regulation produced almost nothing for more than three quarters. Freshman kicker Jacob Baggett opened the scoring with a 46-yard field goal, his first collegiate make, and the teams went to the fourth quarter tied 3-3. Michigan finally broke through early in the fourth when Underwood found freshman tight end Mason Bonner for a six-yard touchdown, the first score of Bonner's career and Underwood's first touchdown pass since September 26, to take a 10-3 lead with 12:55 to play.

UCLA refused to go quietly. On the final drive of regulation, the Bruins marched 77 yards in 15 plays and tied the game when running back Anthony Woods took a direct snap and threw a one-yard jump pass to tight end Brayden Loftin with 10 seconds left. The teams then traded field goals through the first two overtimes, Baggett hitting from 29 and 52 yards and UCLA's Mateen Bhaghani answering from 38 yards both times, before the game was finally settled by the two-point shootout in the third overtime.

The Bruins' task had been made far harder by an injury in overtime. Quarterback Ty Dieffenbach, who had already turned the ball over three times, was hurt when he was tackled on a run in the extra period and was put on a stretcher and carted off the field. Iamaleava, the third-string quarterback, took over and could not convert the two-point try that would have kept UCLA alive, throwing one incompletion and running twice for negative yardage in his brief appearance.

Michigan's defence was the story of the day. The Wolverines held UCLA to just 126 yards in the first half, the Bruins' lowest output over 30 minutes all season, and forced three turnovers, including two fumbles and an interception by Chris Bracy, who led the team with 12 tackles. UCLA had entered the game leading the Bowl Subdivision with 30 takeaways and second nationally with a plus-16 turnover margin, but Michigan won the turnover battle 3-1. Savion Hiter ran for 104 yards, his second straight game over the century mark, against a UCLA run defence that had allowed just 85.3 yards per game.

Underwood's own numbers were modest, 11 completions on 24 attempts for 127 yards with one touchdown, but the context mattered. He was making his first start since being benched in October after the Penn State game, and he delivered the two throws that decided the game: the go-ahead touchdown to Bonner and the winner to Hoffman. The win also made Michigan bowl eligible at 6-5 under embattled coach Kyle Whittingham, whose future will be decided by a new boss after the season.

The defeat cost UCLA far more than a perfect record. The Bruins, now 10-1 overall and 7-1 in the Big Ten, blew a chance to clinch a spot in the Big Ten championship game and to move a step closer to a first-round bye in the 12-team postseason after entering the game No. 4 in the College Football Playoff rankings. UCLA closes its regular season at home on Saturday, November 28, against 18th-ranked Southern California, while Michigan travels to 16th-ranked Ohio State the same day for a noon kickoff on Fox.

Why it matters: This was the upset that scrambled the playoff race on the wildest Saturday of the college season. UCLA had built its unbeaten run on ball security, leading the country in takeaways and turnover margin, and Michigan beat the Bruins at exactly that game, forcing three turnovers and refusing to give the ball away. For UCLA, the loss does not end the season, but it removes the margin for error entirely: the Bruins must now beat USC and hope for help to reach the Big Ten title game. For Michigan, a season that had felt miserable found its defining afternoon, and it sets up a rivalry week in which the Wolverines can now play spoiler against Ohio State with real momentum.

Sunrise on the Reaping Roars to a $120 Million Opening, Lionsgate's First $100 Million Debut Since 2015

The Hunger Games prequel earned $56 million on Friday, including $21 million in previews, and is headed for a $120 million-plus opening weekend, the franchise's best start in more than a decade.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 22, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Lionsgate's The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is deadlifting the box office this weekend, accounting for an estimated 70 percent of all ticket sales as it earned a $56 million opening day, including $21 million from previews, and headed for a $120 million-plus opening weekend from 4,018 locations.

The opening marks a return to the franchise's heyday. It is the first time Lionsgate has had a $100 million-plus opening since The Hunger Games: Mockingjay Part 2 opened to $102 million in 2015, though the studio's earlier 2026 film Michael came close with $97 million. Unadjusted for inflation, the new film would stand as Lionsgate's third or fourth highest opening weekend ever, depending on whether it tops the $121 million opening of The Hunger Games: Mockingjay Part 1 in 2014.

The film is the sixth installment in the Hunger Games franchise, which has collectively grossed $3.3 billion worldwide. Based on the Suzanne Collins prequel novel of the same name, it follows a young Haymitch Abernathy, played by Joseph Zada, participating in the 50th annual Hunger Games, 24 years before Katniss Everdeen's first Games. The cast also includes Mckenna Grace, Whitney Peak, Ben Wang, Ralph Fiennes and Glenn Close, and the film is directed by Francis Lawrence.

The preview numbers alone told the story of a franchise roaring back. The roughly $21 million from Thursday previews and early-access screenings was the second-best preview total in franchise history, behind only Catching Fire's $25.2 million in 2013. The film is projected to earn the fourth-best debut of the franchise and the best since 2014's Mockingjay Part 1, and if Sunday business comes in strong it could pass that film to claim the third-best opening in the series.

Audiences are responding more warmly than critics. The film earned an A- on CinemaScore, up from the B+ given to the 2023 prequel The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which opened to a far lower $44.6 million. Critics have been cooler: the film's Rotten Tomatoes score is the fifth-lowest of the six-film franchise, though its audience scores are among the strongest the series has ever posted.

The reversal from the last installment is striking. The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes earned the worst debut of the franchise by far with $44.6 million in 2023, though it legged out to a $166 million domestic run. The new film is set to earn roughly two and a half times what Ballad earned in its opening weekend, putting the franchise back at the financial power of its heyday. It is also one of just 16 movies ever to debut with more than $100 million at the domestic box office in November.

Theater owners are counting on the film to carry the holiday season. While it is possible that Sunrise on the Reaping will be frontloaded, driven mostly by longtime Hunger Games fans, exhibitors are turning to it as one of the pillars of next weekend's Thanksgiving box office, with secondary support from Disney's Hexed, which is projected for a five-day opening of $45 million to $55 million from a primarily family audience.

Elsewhere at the box office, Amazon MGM's I Play Rocky expanded nationwide to 2,447 locations and is earning an estimated $9.3 million wide opening. The Peter Farrelly biopic, starring Anthony Ippolito as Sylvester Stallone, explores the making of Rocky and how Stallone fought to play the role he wrote himself. Among holdovers, Paramount's Ebenezer is falling 57 percent from its opening weekend to an $8.2 million second weekend, bringing the Johnny Depp production to a 10-day domestic total of $33 million, while Warner Bros.' The Cat in the Hat continues to hold modestly well in its third weekend with $7 million, bringing its domestic total to $47 million heading into Thanksgiving.

Studio estimates for the full weekend are due on Sunday, with final figures on Monday. With no direct competition until mid-December, the film is well placed for the holiday weeks. For Lionsgate, which has waited eleven years for another $100 million opening, the weekend is already a vindication of the decision to return to Panem.

Why it matters: Sunrise on the Reaping is a reminder that franchises are not dead, they are dormant: the same series that looked finished after a $44.6 million opening in 2023 has just posted a $120 million weekend by returning to the story fans actually wanted, the young Haymitch. For Lionsgate, the opening ends an eleven-year drought and restores the studio's biggest property to the top of the box office. The test now is legs: a frontloaded fan rush would fade fast, but the A- CinemaScore and the Thanksgiving corridor ahead give it a real chance to keep playing through the holidays.

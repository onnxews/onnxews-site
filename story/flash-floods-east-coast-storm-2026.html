Flash Flood Emergencies in Georgia and Tennessee as Cross-Country Storm Reaches the East Coast

Torrential rain triggered emergencies along the Georgia-Tennessee line early Thursday. The storm, which has now touched more than 35 states, brings 1 to 3 inches of rain to the I-95 corridor.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Torrential rain triggered Flash Flood Emergencies across Polk County, Tennessee, and Fannin County, Georgia, along the state line early Thursday, as a massive cross-country storm moved its rain to the East Coast, Fox Weather reported.

Showers and storms will rumble into the East Coast through the day on Thursday, bringing an end to a more than two-week stretch of dry, sunny weather across parts of the Northeast. More than an inch of rain is possible across much of the Interstate 95 corridor, with localized amounts approaching 2 to 3 inches along the coast from Virginia to southeast New England.

The rain will continue through Friday and is expected to taper off through the day from south to north, lingering across southern New England through Saturday morning. A Level 1 flash flood risk covers parts of the New York City area through Boston and into Portland, Maine, on Friday.

The storm has already left a trail across the country: it brought deadly flooding to Arizona, heavy mountain snow to the Rockies, with more than a foot of snow in the San Juan, Sawatch, Wasatch and Uinta ranges, and states of emergency in Arizona, Utah and Colorado.

By the end of the week, the system is expected to have affected more than 35 states and 120 million people, according to Fox Weather.

Reporting: Fox Weather

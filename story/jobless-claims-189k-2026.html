U.S. Jobless Claims Fall to 189,000, Near Lowest in Decades

New applications for unemployment benefits dropped below forecasts, a sign employers are holding on to workers even as hiring stays slow. The October jobs report lands Friday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 5, 2026 · 1 min readFacebookX

The number of Americans filing new claims for unemployment benefits fell to 189,000 for the week ending October 31, the U.S. Department of Labor reported on Thursday.

The reading came in below the 196,000 economists had forecast and below the previous week's 195,000, which was revised from 194,000. It was the lowest reading in months, and claims have stayed mostly below 200,000 since mid-September.

The four-week moving average, which smooths out weekly volatility, fell by 2,250 to 194,750. Continuing claims, a proxy for hiring, rose by 23,000 to 1.718 million for the week ending October 24.

The figures point to a labor market defined by low layoffs rather than strong hiring: employers are not letting people go, but job growth has slowed sharply, with nonfarm payrolls rising by only 29,000 jobs in September.

Attention now turns to Friday's October jobs report, with economists expecting modest job growth and the unemployment rate holding around 4.2 percent. The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate last month to the 3.75 to 4.00 percent range, its first hike in three years, and economists expect another increase in December.

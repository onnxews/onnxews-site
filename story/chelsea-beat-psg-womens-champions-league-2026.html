Chelsea Beat PSG 1-0 to Top Women's Champions League Table

Veerle Buurman's 87th-minute winner at Plough Lane sent Chelsea top of the league phase and extended their record run of clean sheets in Europe.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Chelsea beat Paris Saint-Germain 1-0 in the Women's Champions League on Wednesday, November 18, with Veerle Buurman scoring the winner in the 87th minute at the Cherry Red Records Stadium in Plough Lane. The result sent Chelsea to the top of the league-phase table.

The goal came from a corner. After the PSG goalkeeper Katarzyna Kiedrzynek turned Sjoeke Nusken's header behind, Buurman squeezed the ball in at the near post from the resulting Erin Cuthbert corner. It was a scrappy goal, but it was enough.

The win extended Chelsea's remarkable defensive record in Europe. It was their sixth straight clean sheet in the Women's Champions League, a new club record, and they remain unbeaten in the competition this season, with three wins in five games. They are well placed to qualify for the last 16 through the top four automatic qualification spots.

Buurman was named player of the match. "It was really important we scored the goal. Winning and three points was really important, especially after the last Champions League game where we drew against Hacken." She added: "A good team performance. We tried really hard and that I can score a goal is really important for the team." Her manager, Sonia Bompastor, praised the team's character, saying: "I like the resilience from my team. Believing until the end of the game we could score that important goal and it was really important for us to win tonight."

The game had been tight for most of the evening. PSG's Merveille Kanjinga threatened all evening, and Chelsea's Alyssa Thompson went close before the break, but Kiedrzynek made several good saves to keep the visitors in the game. The PSG goalkeeper had kept Chelsea out for 86 minutes before the corner finally broke the deadlock. Chelsea had come into the match after a 0-0 draw with BK Hacken last time out, and the pressure was on to find a goal.

The breakthrough finally came in the closing minutes, and it was fitting that it came from a set piece. Chelsea have been strong from corners all season, and Cuthbert's delivery was the kind of ball that causes problems in a crowded penalty area. Buurman's finish, from close range at the near post, was the reward for a night of persistence.

The result was part of a good night for the English clubs in Europe. Manchester City beat Hacken 2-0 on the same evening, and Real Madrid beat Inter 3-1 while Juventus beat OH Leuven 2-0. All four of those teams sealed their progression to the knockout phase, joining Barcelona and OL Lyonnes, who had already secured their places before Wednesday's games.

Chelsea's final league-phase game is against OH Leuven, and a win there would confirm their place in the top four and a direct route to the last 16. The team has now gone five games in Europe without losing, and with the defence in this kind of form, they will be hard to beat.

For PSG, the defeat is a setback, but they remain in the hunt for qualification. For Chelsea, the night was about more than the three points: it was about a sixth straight clean sheet, a late winner, and a team that keeps finding a way to win, even when the game is not going its way.

Why it matters: This result matters because it shows Chelsea's evolution under Bompastor into a team that wins tight games in Europe. A sixth straight clean sheet in the Champions League is a club record, and it is the foundation on which their campaign is built: they do not concede, and they find a goal late. Buurman's winner, from a corner in the 87th minute, is the kind of goal that wins knockout ties, and with the top four in the league phase qualifying directly for the last 16, Chelsea are now in a strong position. The wider picture is also encouraging for English football, with four teams sealing progression on the same night, and Chelsea leading the way.

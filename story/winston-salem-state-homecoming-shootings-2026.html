One Killed, Three Hurt in Two Shootings at Winston-Salem State Homecoming

Two shootings less than an hour apart on homecoming night left a 19-year-old dead and three wounded; a 20-year-old was arrested and charged with murder in the first shooting.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

What began as a homecoming celebration at Winston-Salem State University ended in gunfire on Saturday night, when two separate shootings on campus in less than an hour left one 19-year-old dead and three other people wounded. Police arrested a 20-year-old man on Sunday and charged him with murder in the first shooting, while the second shooting remained unsolved.

Officers who were already on campus for homecoming events heard gunshots at around 9:45 p.m. on Saturday, November 7, near the 600 block of South Martin Luther King Jr. Drive. They found a 19-year-old man with a gunshot wound and rendered aid, but he died at the scene. Police identified him as Peyton Patterson. A 22-year-old woman who had what appeared to be a gunshot wound was taken to a hospital, where she was treated and later released. Winston-Salem police said the first shooting stemmed from an altercation between two groups.

Less than an hour later, at about 10:22 p.m., police responding to more gunshots near the intersection of Victory Circle and Cromartie Street found two more people wounded. A 21-year-old man was taken to a hospital in critical but stable condition, and a 20-year-old woman was in stable condition. One of the wounded in the second shooting was Makyiah Ward, a sophomore guard on the university's women's basketball team, who was reported to be in good condition and expected to recover fully. Police said they had identified a vehicle connected to the second shooting, but no arrests had been made in that case as of Sunday afternoon.

Assistant Police Chief Jason Swaim said at a news conference early Sunday that it was too early to tell whether the two shootings were connected. 'At this point, the investigation is still very early in its preliminary stages,' he said. A lockdown of the campus was lifted after police determined the campus was safe, but officers stayed on campus in extra numbers while the investigation continued. The university's chancellor, Bonita J. Brown, called the shootings 'senseless violence' and said the campus was secure.

On Sunday, the investigation led police to Ziyaire Spinks, 20, who was arrested and held without bond on charges of murder, assault with a deadly weapon with intent to kill or inflict serious injury, and felony possession of a firearm on educational property, all in connection with the first shooting. Police also arrested an 18-year-old, Cequon Warren Brower, according to NBC News,, in connection with the fighting that preceded the first shooting, charging him with affray. Both men are charged, not convicted, and are presumed innocent. No one had been charged in the second shooting as of Sunday, and police have not said whether they believe the two incidents are linked.

Saturday had been homecoming game day at the university, and the Rams had played the Fayetteville State Broncos earlier in the day before the evening's celebrations turned violent. Winston-Salem State University, founded in 1892, is a historically Black public university in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, and homecoming is one of the biggest weekends of its year, drawing students, alumni and families back to campus. A local church planned to lead a call for prayer on Sunday, as students and families tried to make sense of a celebration that ended with a teenager dead.

The shootings add to a grim pattern of gun violence intruding on American campus life, where homecoming weekends, parties and other large gatherings have repeatedly become targets or backdrops for shootings. Universities across the country have spent years adding officers, cameras and emergency alert systems for big event weekends, but the Winston-Salem shootings show how quickly a crowded celebration can turn dangerous when a dispute escalates and a gun is present. Police have not said how the firearm used in the first shooting was brought onto campus, or whether either of the men arrested was a student.

For the university, the immediate task is caring for the wounded and the shaken. Counseling services are typically mobilized after campus violence of this kind, and the investigation will determine whether the second shooting was connected to the first or a separate dispute that happened to erupt on the same night in the same place. For the family of Peyton Patterson, a 19-year-old who went to a homecoming celebration and never came home, the questions are simpler and harder: who fired the shots, and why a night meant for reunion ended in a killing on a campus street.

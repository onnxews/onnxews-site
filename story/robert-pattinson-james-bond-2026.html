Robert Pattinson Is the New James Bond

Amazon MGM Studios confirmed the 40-year-old as 007 on Monday, with Denis Villeneuve directing the 26th film in the series.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 17, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Robert Pattinson is the new James Bond. Amazon MGM Studios confirmed the casting on Monday, November 16, in a short teaser video released on the official 007 channels, ending months of speculation about who would succeed Daniel Craig. The 40-year-old will star in the untitled 26th film in the series, directed by Denis Villeneuve, with a script by Steven Knight, the creator of Peaky Blinders, and produced by Amy Pascal and David Heyman. No release date has been set.

The announcement was staged for maximum effect. The teaser shows Pattinson in a tuxedo, holding a gun, as the 007 theme plays and the words "James Bond will return" appear on screen. Pattinson said taking the role was "the honour of a lifetime." "James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime," he said. "To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement." Villeneuve said he was "overjoyed to welcome Robert Pattinson as our new James Bond," adding: "He has the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new."

Pattinson becomes the seventh actor to play 007 in the official series — the eighth to play Bond on the big screen if David Niven's 1967 spoof Casino Royale is counted. He is the first actor other than Craig to take the role since Craig debuted in Casino Royale in 2006, 20 years ago. Craig played Bond five times, from Casino Royale in 2006 to No Time to Die in 2021, and his departure left the franchise's future open for the longest stretch in its history.

The casting process was long and closely guarded. It began in May 2026, with second-round auditions in August. The finalists were reported to include Pattinson, Nicholas Hoult, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Josh O'Connor, all of whom screen-tested for the role. Puck reported on November 13 that Pattinson's screen-test deal covered three films — one fewer than the four-film terms agreed by at least two of the other finalists — and paid him considerably more than the others — a sign of how much Amazon MGM wanted him.

The film will be the first Bond picture made since Amazon MGM took creative control of the franchise in 2025. Amazon paid more than $8.56 billion for MGM in 2022, and the deal saw longtime producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson step back from the series they had run for decades. The new film is therefore a double debut: a new Bond and a new regime, with the franchise's future resting on both.

Pattinson's recent career makes the casting less surprising than it might have seemed a decade ago. His 2026 releases include The Drama, The Odyssey and Primetime, in which he plays Chris Hansen. He also appears in Dune: Part Three, due out on December 18, 2026, where he plays Scytale. He played Batman in The Batman in 2022, with a sequel due in 2028, and it is not yet clear whether he will continue in both roles — a question that will matter to Warner Bros. as much as to Amazon MGM.

The choice of Villeneuve as director and Knight as writer sets the tone for what is coming. Villeneuve is one of the most respected filmmakers working today, and Knight's Peaky Blinders gave him a reputation for writing tough, stylish men in period settings. Together, they suggest a Bond film that aims for prestige as well as spectacle — a deliberate break from the idea that the franchise is simply a machine for action sequences.

For now, the film exists only as a cast, a director and a teaser. There is no title, no plot and no release date, and the production schedule has not been announced. What is certain is that the most famous role in cinema has a new holder, and that the next chapter of the longest-running franchise in film history will be written by a team that has never made a Bond film before. That is either a risk or a reinvention. The box office will decide which.

Why it matters: The Bond franchise has always been a mirror of its moment, and this casting says a great deal about the moment. Pattinson is not the safe, square-jawed choice of the past; he is an actor who rebuilt his career on strange, difficult films after Twilight, and whose Batman was brooding rather than swaggering. Amazon MGM is betting that audiences want a Bond who feels like a real person rather than a brand, and that a director like Villeneuve can make the franchise feel like cinema again rather than content. If it works, it will be the most interesting Bond era in decades; if it does not, it will be the most expensive lesson in franchise history.

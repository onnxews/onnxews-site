Ramayana Passes ₹1,200 Crore Worldwide and Overtakes Jawan in 10 Days

Ramayana – Part I passed ₹1,200 crore worldwide in just ten days, overtaking the lifetime totals of Jawan and KGF: Chapter 2 to become the fifth-biggest Indian film of all time.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ramayana – Part I crossed ₹1,200 crore worldwide in its second weekend, just ten days after its release on November 5, and in doing so passed the lifetime worldwide totals of two of Indian cinema's biggest hits. The film, directed by Nitesh Tiwari and starring Ranbir Kapoor, Yash and Sai Pallavi, is now the fifth-biggest Indian grosser of all time, with the next film on the list already in sight.

The milestone came on a strong second Saturday. On Day 10, November 14, the film made over ₹50 crore in India, including ₹40 crore from the Hindi version alone, taking its all-India total to about ₹685 crore, according to the Sacnilk-based tracker. Bollywood Hungama, which tracks the figures separately, puts the worldwide total at ₹1,215.40 crore, made up of ₹804.80 crore gross in India and ₹410.60 crore overseas, while the Sacnilk-based tracker puts the worldwide figure at ₹1,228 crore. Both trackers agree the film has passed ₹1,200 crore.

The film has now passed the lifetime worldwide totals of Shah Rukh Khan's Jawan and Yash's KGF: Chapter 2, two films that once looked untouchable. On the all-Indian-films ranking, Ramayana now sits fifth, behind Dangal at about ₹2,023 crore, Dhurandhar: The Revenge at about ₹1,656 crore, Baahubali 2 at about ₹1,642 crore and Pushpa 2 at about ₹1,632 crore, and ahead of Dhurandhar (2025) at ₹1,219 crore, KGF: Chapter 2 at ₹1,182 crore and Jawan at ₹1,150 crore. Its next targets, according to the same tracker, are Pushpa 2, Baahubali 2 and Dhurandhar: The Revenge.

The speed of the run is as remarkable as the total. Ramayana crossed ₹1,000 crore worldwide within eight days, making it the third-fastest Indian film to reach that mark, and it has now added more than ₹200 crore in just two more days. A second Saturday that grows by more than 50% is almost unheard of for a film in its second weekend, and it suggests the film is still finding new audiences rather than running out of them.

The film's success rests on a combination that Indian cinema has been chasing for years: a mythological story that audiences already know, stars from different parts of the industry, and a scale of production that can compete with anything in the world. Ranbir Kapoor and Yash bring audiences from Hindi and Kannada cinema respectively, Sai Pallavi brings a following of her own, and Tiwari's direction has turned the Ramayana into a spectacle that works as well in multiplexes as it does in single-screen theatres.

The box office has also been helped by the film's release strategy. Opening on November 5 gave it a clear run at the box office, and the second-weekend surge shows that word of mouth has been stronger than the usual drop-off after an opening weekend. The film's makers have not yet announced a release date for Part II, but the success of the first part guarantees that the sequel will be one of the most anticipated Indian films ever made.

For the wider industry, Ramayana's run is a reminder that the biggest Indian films are now measured against each other rather than against Hollywood. The top five Indian grossers of all time are all Indian films, and the gap between them and the rest of the market is widening. A film that passes ₹1,200 crore in ten days is no longer an exception; it is the new standard for the biggest releases.

The film's next milestones are Pushpa 2, Baahubali 2 and Dhurandhar: The Revenge, and at the current pace it could pass them by the end of the second week. After that, the question becomes how far it can go: the gap to Pushpa 2, at ₹1,632 crore, is still more than ₹400 crore, and closing it would require a run that lasts well beyond the second weekend. For now, the film's makers will be content with a tenth day that broke records and a ranking that puts them among the five biggest Indian films ever made.

Why it matters: Ramayana's run matters because it confirms that the biggest Indian films now operate on a scale that rivals any cinema in the world — ₹1,200 crore in ten days, with a second Saturday that grew by more than half. Passing Jawan and KGF: Chapter 2 in ten days also shows how quickly the record books are being rewritten: the films that defined the last decade are being overtaken by a single release. The tracker gap between ₹1,215 crore and ₹1,228 crore is a reminder that these totals are estimates from different sources, but both agree on the milestone. SEQUENTIAL UPDATE: prior site coverage was the ₹1,000 crore milestone on Day 8 (Nov 12); this article reports the new Day 10 figures — the ₹1,200 crore crossing and passing Jawan and KGF: Chapter 2.

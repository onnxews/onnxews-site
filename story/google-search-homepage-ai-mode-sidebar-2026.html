Google Adds AI Chat Sidebar to Its Search Homepage

The famously spare Google homepage is getting a collapsible sidebar that lets desktop users pick up old AI Mode conversations where they left off.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Google is rolling out a collapsible sidebar on its desktop search homepage that lists a user's recent AI Mode searches, the biggest visible change to the page in years. Robby Stein, Google's vice president of product for Search, announced the change on X on November 19, saying it is rolling out globally for Google Search on the web.

The sidebar is designed for the way people now use search. AI Mode turns a query into a conversation that can run over many exchanges, and the new panel lets users jump back into an earlier thread instead of starting from scratch. "A much simpler way to pick up where you left off," Stein wrote, adding that in testing the sidebar made a big difference for making progress and continuing to explore the web.

Opening the panel is deliberately low-key. Clicking on the left side of the AI search window opens the sidebar, which shows recent searches, both AI and organic, and selecting any of them reopens an AI Mode window on that subject. It can be closed at any time from an icon at the top right, and when collapsed it shrinks to a narrow grey bar at the edge of the page.

The panel holds more than a history list. It has a button to start a new thread, a search tool for finding older threads, and an option to connect a Gemini Notebook, tying a user's saved research notes into the search page. The Google logo and the search box stay in the middle of the page, so the classic layout is disturbed far less than the scale of the change might suggest.

There is an important limit: the sidebar only appears inside Google Search's AI Mode. Users who never open AI Mode will not see it, and Google has not said whether a similar panel will come to the Google app on Android and iOS or to mobile browsers. For now the change is desktop-only, and the rollout is gradual, so some desktop users may not see it yet even though Google describes the launch as global.

The homepage has been famously spare since Google's early days, when a single search box set it apart from rivals crowded with news, weather and links. The company has added features to the search page over time but has kept it as plain as possible, which is why a persistent chat-history panel counts as one of the largest redesigns the page has had.

The move is the latest step in Google's push to put its Gemini AI at the centre of search, and it comes despite complaints from some users and publishers who think there is already too much Gemini in Google Search. Google has said its commitment to AI is a long-range plan rather than a short-term allocation, and the sidebar fits that strategy: it turns the homepage from a starting point into a place where conversations continue.

The change also arrives at a sensitive moment for Google's search business. Engadget reported that the company's market value recently fell by $500 billion after it admitted that AI search is not earning as much as organic search. Google is betting that keeping users inside AI Mode, with their history a click away, will matter more in the long run than the short-term revenue gap, and that the habit of returning to old threads will make its AI search harder for rivals such as ChatGPT and Claude to displace.

Why it matters: A sidebar sounds minor, but it changes what the Google homepage is for. For more than two decades the page has been a blank box that resets with every visit; now it remembers, and it nudges users toward continuing conversations rather than typing fresh queries. That is good for multi-step research and bad for anyone who liked search as a clean slate, and it deepens Google's bet that the future of search is a chat history, not a list of links. Publishers should watch whether the panel sends more traffic back into old AI answers instead of out to the open web, and users should know their AI threads are now one click from the homepage.

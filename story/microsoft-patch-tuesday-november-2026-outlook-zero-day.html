Microsoft's November Patch Tuesday Fixes 1,235 Flaws, Including an Exploited Outlook Zero-Day

The second straight four-figure Patch Tuesday includes one flaw already being exploited to steal account credentials, and 30 flaws rated potentially wormable.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday, released November 10, fixes a record-breaking 1,235 flaws, including one Microsoft Outlook vulnerability that attackers were already exploiting before the patch shipped. It is the second month in a row that Microsoft has fixed more than 1,000 flaws, after a record 1,411 in October, as the company leans on artificial intelligence to find and patch bugs faster than ever.

The headline number depends on how it is counted. BleepingComputer, which counts only vulnerabilities released on Patch Tuesday itself, put the total at 1,235, with 66 rated Critical, including 48 remote code execution flaws, 15 elevation of privilege flaws and three use-after-free flaws in Microsoft Edge. Other tallies that strip out republished items put the Microsoft-assigned total at about 1,094. Either way, the scale is extraordinary: Microsoft has now fixed more than 5,000 vulnerabilities in 2026.

The flaw that matters most is the one already under attack. CVE-2026-124194 is an Outlook spoofing vulnerability, rated only Moderate with a score of 6.5 out of 10, that can be exploited to steal a user's NTLM account hash. Because it was already being exploited in the wild, it is the only zero-day in the release, and the U.S. cybersecurity agency CISA has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and given federal agencies until the end of November to patch it.

The most severe flaws sit in the network services that hold corporate systems together. Five critical Windows vulnerabilities, each rated 9.8 out of 10, affect Kerberos, LDAP, DNS, the DHCP Server and iSCSI, and all five can be exploited by an unauthenticated attacker on the network with no user interaction. Microsoft also fixed a critical Exchange Server remote code execution flaw, CVE-2026-118207, that it rates as more likely to be exploited and that needs only a low-level login to attack.

Outlook appears again and again in the release. CVE-2026-110043 is the third Outlook remote code execution bug with the Preview Pane as the attack vector in three months, a pattern that should worry anyone who previews email. SharePoint Server carries a flaw scoring 9.9 out of 10, CVE-2026-120002, an elevation of privilege bug that Microsoft rates as more likely to be exploited, despite carrying only an Important label.

The Zero Day Initiative, which tracks these releases closely, classified 30 of the patched flaws as potentially wormable, meaning they could spread from machine to machine without human help, down from 59 last month. Another 42 flaws are marked 'exploitation more likely.' Security researchers said the drop in wormable flaws does not mean less risk, only that fewer zero-days were found this month, and warned that AI is now better at reversing patches than at finding and exploiting bugs in closed-source software.

The release also marks the end of the road for one version of Windows. Windows 11 version 23H2 Enterprise and Education editions received their final monthly security updates on November 10, and organizations still running them must upgrade to stay protected. Around 639 of the flaws counted by BleepingComputer sit in Windows or its components, with the rest spread across Office, Exchange, .NET, Azure, SQL Server, Dynamics and Xbox.

For IT teams, the patching order is clear, according to the researchers who analyzed the release: patch identity infrastructure first, including Active Directory, LDAP, Exchange and Schannel, then the two 9.9-rated flaws, then the Outlook Preview Pane cluster, and then the exploited Outlook bug, which should already be at the top of every list. Network-facing services such as DNS, DHCP, LDAP and Exchange deserve the earliest attention because they can be reached without a login.

The sheer volume raises a question the industry is still wrestling with. Microsoft is finding and fixing flaws at a pace no human team could match, largely because it is using AI to hunt for them, but the flood of patches also strains the teams that must test and deploy them. A thousand-flaw month was once unthinkable; it has now happened twice in a row.

The next Patch Tuesday is December 8. Until then, the advice is the same as every month, only more urgent: update Windows and Office now, start with the servers, and do not assume that a flaw rated Moderate is harmless, because this month the only bug being exploited in the wild is a 6.5.

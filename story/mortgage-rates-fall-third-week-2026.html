Mortgage Rates Fall for a Third Straight Week, but Buyers Stay on the Sidelines

The 30-year fixed slipped to 7.18 percent and dipped below 7 percent in one daily survey, yet pending home sales just hit their lowest level in five years.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Mortgage rates fell for a third consecutive week, with the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage dropping to 7.18 percent in Freddie Mac's weekly survey released November 5. The 15-year fixed-rate mortgage fell to 6.57 percent from 6.60 percent a week earlier. It is the longest stretch of declines since the summer, but buyers are still not biting.

The weekly survey tells the story of a slow grind downward. The 30-year rate stood at 7.40 percent on October 15, then fell to 7.32 percent on October 22, 7.24 percent on October 29 and now 7.18 percent. Each step down is small, but together they add up to 22 basis points of relief in three weeks for anyone borrowing to buy a home.

A separate daily measure moved even lower. Zillow's average 30-year fixed purchase rate fell to 6.99 percent on Friday, November 6, down 5 basis points from Thursday and 14 basis points below last week. It was the first time the rate has slipped under 7 percent since September. The 15-year fixed eased to 6.35 percent in the same survey.

The savings are real but modest. On a 300,000 dollar loan, this week's dip saves about 12 dollars a month against last week, and about 45 dollars a month against the 7.40 percent rate of mid-October. On a 400,000 dollar loan at 6.99 percent, principal and interest come to about 2,659 dollars a month. For buyers stretched by years of high prices, every dollar helps, but few would call the market affordable.

Rates remain well above where they were a year ago, when the 30-year averaged 6.22 percent and the 15-year averaged 5.50 percent. That gap of nearly a full percentage point is the reason the housing market remains frozen. Homeowners with mortgages locked in at 3 or 4 percent have little reason to sell, and buyers face monthly payments far higher than they would have paid just a year earlier.

The demand data is bleak. Overall mortgage applications fell 1.2 percent for the week ending October 30 compared with the week before, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association. Purchase applications dropped 19 percent from a year earlier, while refinance applications fell 60 percent. Even falling rates have not been enough to bring borrowers back.

Pending sales tell the same story. Redfin recorded the lowest pending sales levels in records dating back five years, with pending sales down 0.6 percent week over week for the four weeks ending November 1. Zillow estimated that pending sales fell 16.8 percent in October compared with a year ago, the biggest annual dip since August 2023 and the slowest pace recorded for October since 2018.

Prices are starting to bend under the pressure. Redfin's rolling four-week average put the median home sale price at 388,604 dollars, down 0.4 percent and the third straight week of declines after three years of increases. Zillow's home value index pegged the typical home at 364,812 dollars, up just 0.7 percent year over year. Inventory continues to rise, which is unusual for this time of year, giving buyers more choice than they have had in years.

The week's progress could prove fragile. Friday's strong jobs report lifted Treasury yields, and mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury closely. The week finished at the bottom of its 30-day range, but the jobs data arrived after the last published rate observation, so the market's next word on rates will not come until Monday.

For now, the housing market is stuck in a strange middle ground: rates are falling, prices are easing, and inventory is rising, yet buyers are still waiting. Whether the dip below 7 percent is the start of a real thaw or just a pause before rates climb again will be decided in the coming weeks, by the Fed, by the bond market, and by buyers who have spent a year watching from the sidelines.

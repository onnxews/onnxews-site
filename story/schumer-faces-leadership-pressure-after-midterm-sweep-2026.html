Schumer Faces Leadership Pressure as New Democrats Demand 'New Blood'

Days after Democrats swept Congress, several incoming senators are refusing to back Chuck Schumer for another term as leader.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer won the biggest victory of his career on Tuesday, yet within days he is facing open questions about whether he should keep the job. A string of newly elected Democrats have declined to commit to supporting him as majority leader when the new Congress convenes in January, and comedian Jon Stewart used his podcast to call on Schumer to step aside.

Senator-elect Adam Hamilton of Kansas, the first Democrat elected to the Senate from his state in nearly a century, has said he will not support Schumer. Senators-elect Troy Jackson of Maine, Julianna Stratton of Illinois, Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan and Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota have also withheld their backing, according to reports. El-Sayed told CNN the issue was action, saying Democrats need a leader who will get legislation onto the president's desk and force him to make a decision.

Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland has also declined to commit, saying Democrats need an open and honest discussion about who should lead them and what agenda the new majority will pursue. Van Hollen has argued that no one should be committing to any candidate for leader without clear commitments on a bold agenda, and El-Sayed has said he would back Van Hollen if he ran.

Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats for nearly a decade, sounded confident when he addressed reporters on Wednesday. "The issue is not me," he said. "I'll be re-elected as majority leader, but the issue is focusing on the American people and what they want." The caucus is expected to hold its leadership election in early December.

Stewart, praising the party's sweeping midterm gains, warned that the victory would mean nothing if it was squandered. "You have an opportunity to actually govern. Do it. Take it," he said on his podcast, arguing that Schumer does not understand the urgency of the moment. He also attacked the party's seniority system, calling it a broken way of choosing leaders.

Reporting: New York Post

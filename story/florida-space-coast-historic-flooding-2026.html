“One-in-1,000-Year” Rain Floods Florida's Space Coast as Cleanup Begins

Melbourne recorded 11.04 inches of rain in 24 hours, breaking day, month and all-time records, as a state of emergency covered seven counties.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 3, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Record rainfall drenched Brevard County, Florida, on November 1 and 2, with Melbourne recording 11.04 inches in 24 hours — breaking day, month and all-time records for the city. Estimates put totals at 18 to 22 inches in parts of Palm Bay, where streets turned to rivers and homes took on water.

Governor Ron DeSantis declared a state of emergency in seven counties as the deluge overwhelmed drainage systems. Schools in Brevard, Glades and Hardee counties closed on November 2, and Palm Bay issued a precautionary boil-water notice after a water-main break.

Forecasters described the event as a one-in-1,000-year rainfall, a statistical measure of rarity rather than a prediction that it will not happen again soon. The heaviest rain fell in a narrow band along the Space Coast, which is why totals varied so sharply from one neighborhood to the next.

Cleanup began Tuesday as water receded, with residents pumping out homes and road crews clearing debris. Officials warned that standing water can hide damaged roads and downed power lines, and urged drivers not to cross flooded streets.

No statewide damage total had been released by Tuesday morning. Emergency managers said assessments were still under way, and further rain in already saturated areas remained a concern through the week.

Reporting: Florida Today

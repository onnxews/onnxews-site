Türkiye Ratifies Defence Pact With Saudi Arabia and Pakistan Treating an Attack on One as an Attack on All

Parliament approved the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement by 279 votes to 119, completing the legislative step for a pact signed in August.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Türkiye's parliament has ratified the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, a pact that treats an armed attack on any one of the three countries as an attack on all three. Lawmakers approved the bill on Thursday by 279 votes to 119, with 398 of the chamber's 592 members taking part.

The 16-article agreement was signed in Mecca on August 7 by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. It establishes a framework for collective security and military cooperation, including intelligence sharing, joint exercises and defence industry ties.

The pact commits the three signatories to assist one another immediately if one faces an armed attack or military aggression. None of the three countries may withdraw during the first 10 years after the agreement enters into force. The agreement takes effect once it is published in Türkiye's Official Gazette.

The ratification comes as Riyadh continues to face attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels, and the three countries agreed at an emergency meeting in Riyadh last month to ensure the rapid deployment of military forces and capabilities in Saudi Arabia. Pakistan had already approved the agreement.

Opposition lawmakers in Ankara objected during the debate, warning that the pact could draw Türkiye into conflicts driven by its allies' interests and put Turkish soldiers in harm's way. Approval of the agreement does not by itself authorise troop deployments abroad, which would require a separate parliamentary mandate under the Turkish constitution.

Reporting: The Türkiye Times

Ross Stores Tops Third-Quarter Estimates but Signals a Slower Holiday Quarter

The off-price retailer raised its full-year earnings outlook after a strong quarter, but held its holiday sales forecast steady, sending shares lower in extended trading.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 20, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Ross Stores maintained its holiday-quarter sales growth forecast on Thursday despite a strong third quarter, signaling a slowdown from the off-price retailer's traffic-driven gains in recent months and sending shares down about 6% in extended trading. The cautious outlook overshadowed results that beat expectations on almost every line.

The company expects comparable store sales to rise 4% to 5% in the fourth quarter, compared with an 8% increase in the third quarter and a 9% gain in the year-ago holiday period. It also slightly lowered its quarterly earnings forecast by 1 cent at the upper and lower ends of its previous targets, to $2.16 to $2.25 a share — below the $2.27 consensus. The message was clear: the holiday quarter will be good, but not as good as the quarter that just ended.

The third quarter itself was strong. Sales for the 13-week quarter ended October 31 increased 12% to $6.3 billion, up from $5.6 billion a year earlier, with comparable store sales up 8% on top of a 7% gain last year, primarily driven by customer traffic. Net income was $606 million versus $512 million last year, while earnings per share were $1.91 compared with $1.58 in the prior-year period — and above the company's own guidance of $1.75 to $1.83.

Operating margin of 12.3% increased 70 basis points compared with last year, above the company's plan of 11.7% to 12.0%. For the first nine months of fiscal 2026, sales grew 15% to $18.5 billion, up from $16.1 billion in 2025, with comparable store sales up 11%. Net income for the nine months was $2.1 billion versus $1.5 billion last year, while earnings per share were $6.59 compared with $4.61.

The nine-month results include approximately $253 million of tariff refunds recognized in the second quarter under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, which benefited earnings per share by approximately $0.60. About 60 cents a share of the full-year outlook comes from those one-time refunds, a reminder that part of this year's earnings strength is not repeatable operating performance.

"We are extremely pleased with our third quarter sales and earnings growth, particularly given the more challenging comparisons we faced," chief executive Jim Conroy said. "Results exceeded our expectations as our marketing initiatives continue to attract more customers while our compelling combination of great brands at exceptional values and an enhanced store experience continued to resonate. As a result, customer traffic remained the primary driver of our comparable store sales growth."

Despite the cautious holiday forecast, Ross raised its fiscal 2026 earnings per share forecast to a range of $8.75 to $8.84, from $8.61 to $8.77 previously, aided by year-to-date strength. "The market share gains we have achieved reinforce our confidence in the opportunities ahead, and we believe we are well positioned to continue strengthening our competitive position and driving sustainable, profitable growth over the long term," Conroy said.

Ross also kept expanding its footprint. During the quarter it opened 51 new stores, including 41 Ross and 10 dd's DISCOUNTS locations, completing its 2026 store opening plan of 115 new locations. The company now operates 2,377 stores — 1,993 Ross Dress for Less locations in 45 states, the District of Columbia, Guam, and Puerto Rico, plus 384 dd's DISCOUNTS stores in 24 states.

The company returned cash to shareholders as well. During the third quarter, it repurchased 1.4 million shares for $319 million under a two-year $2.55 billion authorization approved by its board in March 2026, and it remains on track to buy back $1.275 billion of stock during fiscal 2026. Its board also declared a regular quarterly cash dividend of $0.445 per share, payable on December 31, 2026, to stockholders of record as of December 8.

The backdrop for the holiday quarter is a consumer under pressure. Shoppers continue to grapple with higher food costs and fuel prices amid a softening labor market, weighing on consumer sentiment amid intense competition from value-focused offerings of off-price retailers TJX and Burlington Stores, discount chains, fast-fashion retailers, and Amazon. Ross's bet is that its model — first-quality, in-season brand-name goods at savings of 20% to 60% off department store prices — is exactly what that shopper wants. The holiday guidance suggests management is confident in the model but not in the season.

Why it matters: Ross is the rare retailer that can raise its full-year outlook and still see its shares fall, because investors judge retailers on the next quarter, not the last one. The 4% to 5% holiday sales forecast is the tell: after an 8% comparable-sales quarter driven by traffic, management is signaling that the traffic surge is cooling just as the most important selling weeks arrive. The $0.60 of one-time tariff refunds inside the full-year guidance adds to the caution — strip those out, and the earnings beat looks less like operating strength and more like a well-timed refund. Ross remains the strongest name in off-price retail, but even the strongest names are now guiding like they expect a tougher holiday.

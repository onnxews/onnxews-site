Wagner's Step-Back Winner Lifts Magic Past Nets 113-111

Franz Wagner scored 35 points and buried a tiebreaking step-back jumper with 2.8 seconds left as Orlando beat Brooklyn for the 10th straight time and stayed perfect in NBA Cup group play.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 21, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Franz Wagner capped a stellar 35-point performance with a tiebreaking step-back jumper over two defenders with 2.8 seconds left, and the Orlando Magic outlasted the Brooklyn Nets for a 113-111 victory on Friday night in an NBA Cup game at Barclays Center in New York. Wagner made 13 of 25 shots and finished three shy of his career high to help the Magic improve to 3-0 in group play and beat the Nets for the 10th straight time.

The finish was pure Wagner. Tied at 111, he took an inbounds pass from Jalen Suggs after Brooklyn rookie Mikel Brown Jr. missed a 20-footer with 9.2 seconds left. Following a timeout, Wagner moved to the left side and released a 15-footer over defenders Noah Clowney and Julius Randle.

After Brooklyn called a timeout, Randle missed a three-point try from the top of the key as Wagner contested the shot, and the Magic escaped with their fifth win in six games. It was a fitting end to a night on which Wagner faced his older brother and former teammate Moritz Wagner for the first time after spending their last five seasons together in Orlando.

Moritz Wagner signed with the Nets as a free agent in July after eight seasons in Orlando, and the brothers even squared up early — Franz Wagner made a pair of free throws in the opening minute of the second quarter after being fouled by his brother.

Wagner was the best player on the floor all night. Before the dramatic finish, he scored nine straight Orlando points, sinking a 10-footer for a 77-68 lead with 7:39 left in the third quarter. He added five rebounds and seven assists to his 35 points.

Orlando had to rally just to reach the final seconds. Desmond Bane hit seven three-pointers for his 21 points as the Magic built a 15-point lead in the first half, and Nikola Vucevic added 15 points while Anthony Black contributed 13 points and seven rebounds. The Magic tied the game on Wendell Carter Jr.'s putback with 30.5 seconds left.

Orlando shot 48.1 percent overall and hit 19 three-pointers, including a team-record 10 in the opening quarter when it made six threes in a 24-4 run to take a 13-point lead. Orlando still led 91-88 entering the fourth after Brown missed a three at the horn to end the third.

Brooklyn, stung by a 137-107 loss to Chicago on Wednesday, had held a team meeting and shuffled its rotations. Coach Jordi Fernandez moved Egor Demin to the bench for the first time since Nov. 5 of his rookie season and started 6-foot-11 Danny Wolf as a jumbo off-guard. Wolf finished with 20 points, five rebounds and a team-high plus-15.

Randle collected 27 points, nine rebounds and six assists for the Nets, who dropped their fourth straight game and fell to 3-12. Brown finished with 18 points while playing heavy minutes in the fourth in place of Nolan Traore, who tweaked his right hamstring in the third. Day'Ron Sharpe contributed 17 points and 11 rebounds, and Michael Porter Jr. finished with 16 points.

Brooklyn still led 111-109 after Randle's free throws, but Randle spun in the lane and kicked to the corner expecting Brown to be there. The rookie had vacated, the ball sailed out for a turnover with 49 seconds left, and the Nets gave up the tying putback before Wagner delivered the winner. For a Nets team searching for answers after its meeting and lineup changes, the effort was better — the result was the same.

Why it matters: The win keeps Orlando perfect in the NBA Cup at 3-0 as of Nov. 20 and extends two streaks that say something about where these franchises stand: five wins in six games for the Magic, and 10 straight wins over the Nets. Wagner's 35-point night, three shy of his career high, is the latest sign that Orlando's late-game offense runs through him — he scored nine straight Magic points in the third and then took the final shot over two defenders without hesitation. For Brooklyn, the loss is harder to take because the response to Wednesday's embarrassment was real: a team meeting, a rotation shake-up, a strong Wolf performance and a lead in the final minute. But a turnover with 49 seconds left and a missed final shot mean the Nets are still searching for a win to match the effort, now losers of four straight at 3-12.

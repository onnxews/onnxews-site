Seven Top-25 Showdowns Headline a Monster College Football Saturday

No. 1 Miami visits No. 11 Notre Dame, No. 9 BYU meets No. 3 Utah, and Alabama travels to LSU in a Week 10 slate loaded with playoff implications.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Saturday, November 7, is the biggest college football Saturday of the season so far, with seven top-25 matchups on the schedule and playoff positioning on the line in nearly every one of them. The headliner is No. 1 Miami's visit to No. 11 Notre Dame, but the day is stacked from noon to night, with rivalry games, conference title races and Heisman campaigns all in play.

Miami arrives in South Bend as the top team in the first College Football Playoff rankings of 2026, released earlier this week, but the Hurricanes are underdogs. Notre Dame is favored by 7 points, and the line moved after Miami receiver Malachi Toney injured his ankle in Tuesday's practice. The game kicks off at 6:30 p.m. CT on NBC, and for Notre Dame it is close to a must-win if the Fighting Irish want to stay in the playoff race.

The Holy War is the day's other great rivalry. No. 9 BYU visits No. 3 Utah, with the Utes favored by 4.5 points in a game that could shape the Big 12 race and both teams' playoff hopes. BYU has been one of the stingiest first-quarter teams in the country, scoring in every first quarter except at TCU, when running back LJ Martin was injured, and Utah's home-field edge in the rivalry is real.

In the SEC, No. 5 Alabama travels to No. 14 LSU, with the Tigers favored by 5.5 points in Baton Rouge. Alabama's playoff hopes and LSU's season both hang on the result, and the game is one of the day's highest-stakes matchups. Elsewhere in the conference, No. 2 Georgia visits No. 10 Ole Miss in a top-ten clash, Texas visits Missouri, and Florida hosts Oklahoma for the first time in program history.

The Florida-Oklahoma game carries its own storylines. The Gators will induct former head coach Urban Meyer into the stadium's ring of honor between the first and second quarters at Ben Hill Griffin Stadium, and Oklahoma changed offensive coordinators this week, replacing Ben Arbuckle with tight ends coach and former NFL star Jason Witten. Kickoff is 7:45 p.m. on SEC Network.

The Big Ten offers a heavyweight meeting of its own, with No. 12 Oregon visiting No. 4 Ohio State. The Buckeyes are favored, but Oregon's offense has the firepower to make it a shootout, and the result will ripple through the playoff picture. No. 8 UCLA visits Minnesota, Iowa visits Northwestern, and Michigan hosts Michigan State in the in-state rivalry.

The Big 12 slate is just as busy. Kansas State hosts Oklahoma State at noon CT on TNT, with the Wildcats starting Blake Barnett at quarterback, their fourth different starter in as many weeks, while regular starter Avery Johnson remains out and could miss the rest of the season. Oklahoma State counters with quarterback Drew Mestemaker, widely regarded as the best quarterback draft prospect in the sport.

Beyond the ranked matchups, the day is full of games that matter for bowl eligibility and conference races, from Air Force's visit to Army to Texas A&M's trip to No. 21 South Carolina. With the playoff rankings now out and the regular season entering its final stretch, every result on Saturday will be read through the lens of the selection committee.

By the time the night games end, the playoff picture will look different than it did on Friday morning. Seven top-25 games in a single day do not come around often, and this Saturday has the matchups, the rivalries and the stakes to be remembered as the weekend the 2026 season truly turned.

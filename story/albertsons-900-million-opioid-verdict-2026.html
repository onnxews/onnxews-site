Albertsons Ordered to Pay $900 Million for Fueling Washington's Opioid Epidemic

A Seattle judge ruled after a nine-week trial that the grocery chain's pharmacies filled millions of prescriptions flagged for possible diversion.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Albertsons must pay the state of Washington $900 million for helping fuel the opioid epidemic by failing through its pharmacies to prevent the misuse of addictive painkillers, a state court judge ruled on Thursday, Reuters reported.

King County Superior Court Judge Janet Helson issued the decision after presiding over a nine-week non-jury trial in a lawsuit the state's attorney general filed against the grocery retailer in 2022. She found Albertsons liable for substantially contributing to a public nuisance and for violating Washington's Consumer Protection Act.

The judgment is $800 million to address the harms of the epidemic plus $100 million in penalties, along with attorneys' fees and costs. Helson said that if her abatement order were overturned on appeal, the penalties would rise to around $340 million, the amount she found the company would otherwise owe.

The state alleged that Albertsons pharmacies from 2006 to 2022 filled opioid prescriptions more than 6.5 million times despite red flags signaling they could be diverted for illicit use, and dispensed more than 641 million opioid pills into Washington. Albertsons operates 200 pharmacies in the state, mostly under its Safeway brand. The state had asked Helson to order $2.2 billion.

Albertsons said in a statement that it was disappointed in the ruling and was considering an appeal. In April, the company announced a $774 million settlement to resolve most of the opioid cases against it nationwide. Attorney General Nick Brown said the ruling holds the company accountable for failing to guard against the misuse and diversion of dangerous drugs. The lawsuit was one of thousands filed by states and local governments over the epidemic, which the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says killed about 806,000 Americans through opioid overdoses from 1999 to 2023.

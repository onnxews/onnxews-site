China Confirms First-Ever Trump–Xi–Putin Meeting for Thursday in Shenzhen

Beijing confirmed on Monday that the three leaders will meet on the sidelines of the APEC summit, completing a lineup Washington and Moscow had already announced — with Washington saying Trump will raise nuclear arms reduction, and no deal expected.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

China confirmed on Monday that Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin will hold the first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the three countries on Thursday in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. The announcement from Beijing's foreign ministry completed the lineup after the White House confirmed the meeting on Friday and the Kremlin confirmed Putin's participation on Saturday.

The meeting will take place in the southern Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen, where Xi is hosting the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18 and 19, with leaders of the forum's 21 member economies attending. It will be the first time the presidents of the United States, China and Russia have ever sat down together in a formal three-way session.

Nuclear weapons are the subject Washington has put first on the agenda. Trump has said he wants to discuss reducing the world's nuclear arsenals with Xi and Putin, and to explore a new agreement covering all three countries' weapons. Beijing named no agenda in its confirmation. The last nuclear arms treaty between the United States and Russia, New START, expired in February, leaving the world's two largest nuclear powers without binding limits on their strategic arsenals for the first time in more than half a century.

“The President has made it clear that, among other topics, he will discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world. This proliferation, President Trump believes, is very dangerous,” White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters last week. China, meanwhile, has been rapidly building its own nuclear arsenal, and Beijing has shown little enthusiasm for trilateral nuclear arms control, citing its far smaller stockpile than those of Washington and Moscow.

Officials on all sides have played down the prospect of a breakthrough. “We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement,” a senior United States official told reporters, describing the session as an entry point. “I would characterize this as the entry point, the first conversation, certainly at the leader level, between these countries.” The official added that the talks were “the beginning of hopefully a conversation that can be productive going forward”.

The war in Ukraine will not be on the table for the trilateral session, according to the senior American official, who said Washington was pursuing a separate negotiating track with Moscow on the conflict. That position is not shared by everyone involved: Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov said that a settlement would be discussed with Trump in Shenzhen, and Russia has shown reluctance to discuss its war in Ukraine in the three-way format.

The gap matters because Ukraine's president has already warned about what a meeting of the three leaders could mean for his country. Volodymyr Zelensky voiced concern in October that a trilateral session might lead to dealmaking about Ukraine's future without consulting Kyiv. “We really don't want this to turn into ‘Yalta 2, 3, 4,’ with a redistribution of influence,” Zelensky told journalists last month, drawing a comparison with the 1945 Yalta Conference at which the Allied powers effectively redrew the global map.

Iran is another subject officials say the three leaders plan to discuss. Trump does plan to raise the war with Iran with Xi and Putin, according to the senior American official. China is Iran's largest trading partner and the biggest buyer of its crude oil, giving Beijing a significant economic stake in the conflict involving Tehran. Discussions are also expected to touch on artificial intelligence, including possible commitments on regulating the use of AI in nuclear arsenals, according to the South China Morning Post.

Trump leaves Washington on Monday and will attend the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in the Philippines on Tuesday before travelling to Shenzhen. He will hold a working lunch and bilateral talks with Xi on Thursday, their fourth meeting since he returned to office. No separate meeting between Trump and Putin is planned. Trump and Putin have met in person on seven occasions, most recently in Alaska in 2025, while Xi has met Putin dozens of times since becoming president in 2013. Elon Musk is expected to travel with Trump, though he is not expected to attend the trilateral meeting.

For all the history being made, the meeting arrives with expectations deliberately set low. The three leaders have met separately but never together, and the symbolism of the gathering alone is considerable. Whether it becomes more than symbolism will depend on whether Thursday's conversation produces a follow-up — a date, a framework, or even an agreement to keep talking about the arsenals that the expired treaty used to constrain.

Why it matters: This meeting matters because it brings together the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers for the first time since the last treaty limiting American and Russian arsenals expired in February, and it does so at a moment when all three are at odds — over Ukraine, over Iran and over trade. The honest read is that no agreement is expected on Thursday, and the American side has said so plainly. What makes it worth watching is what comes after: if the three agree to keep talking, the session becomes the start of a new arms-control track; if they do not, the world will have gone a full year with no binding limits on its two biggest arsenals and no forum to discuss them. Orbit will follow the Shenzhen session and whatever the leaders say when it ends.

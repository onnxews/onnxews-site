Ella Langley Sweeps 2026 CMA Awards With Record Six Wins in One Night

The Alabama singer took entertainer of the year and swept the top five major categories at the 60th CMA Awards in Nashville, a night that also featured Taylor Swift's first performance there in 13 years.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 19, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Ella Langley made history at the 60th annual CMA Awards on Wednesday, November 18, winning six awards in a single night, more than any artist has ever won at the ceremony. The show took place at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville, and was hosted for a third year by Lainey Wilson.

Langley became the first woman to sweep the top five major categories in one night, winning entertainer of the year, female vocalist of the year, album of the year for "Dandelion," single of the year for "Choosin' Texas," and song of the year for "Choosin' Texas." She also won music video of the year for "Choosin' Texas," making it a clean sweep of every category she was up for in the major races.

She is also the first artist ever to win song of the year and single of the year in back-to-back years, and the first artist of any kind to win six CMA Awards in one night. She led the field with nine nominations going into the show, and her six wins were the most by any artist in a single CMA ceremony.

The wins were built on two songs that have defined her rise. "Choosin' Texas" became the longest-running No. 1 song in the history of the Billboard Hot 100, passing Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You" in September. Her album "Dandelion," which took two years to make, was named album of the year.

Langley could barely believe the scale of the night herself. "At this point, I'm sick of me," she said while accepting the last and highest honor. "I have watched these awards since I was a little kid, and the award I cared most about was song of the year and entertainer of the year." The Alabama native's rise has been fast, and the six wins capped a year of dominance driven by "Choosin' Texas."

The night also belonged to Taylor Swift, who made her first CMA Awards performance in 13 years and her first appearance at the show in a decade. Swift performed "I Knew It, I Knew You," her song from the Toy Story 5 soundtrack, joined on stage by a parade of country stars including Brad Paisley, Little Big Town, Sugarland, Brooks & Dunn, Lainey Wilson, Eric Church, and Bill Anderson. She then closed the show with "Our Song," her early hit, performing it with Keith Urban. Swift was nominated for single of the year and lost to Langley.

The show opened with a tribute to Dolly Parton, who died on August 25 at the age of 80. Host Lainey Wilson and Vince Gill performed "I Will Always Love You," Parton's signature song, in a quiet, emotional start to the night that set the tone for a ceremony that kept returning to the history of the genre.

Other winners included Chris Stapleton, who took male vocalist of the year for a ninth time, a record in the category. The Red Clay Strays won vocal group of the year, and Brooks & Dunn won vocal duo of the year for a 17th time, taking their overall CMA trophy count to 21, while also receiving the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award. Stephen Wilson Jr. was named new artist of the year, the music event of the year went to "McArthur," a collaboration between Hardy, Morgan Wallen, Eric Church, and Tim McGraw, and Charlie Worsham was named musician of the year. Miranda Lambert, who was also a nominee, now has 17 CMA wins in total.

Wilson also used her time as host to debut a new song, "Just a Woman," and Zach Top debuted his own new single, "Moonshine," during the show. Between the history made by Langley and the return of Swift, the 60th CMA Awards became a night that the genre will be talking about for a long time.

Why it matters: Langley's sweep is more than a big night for one singer; it is a changing of the guard in country music. The CMA Awards have long been dominated by established stars, and a singer winning entertainer of the year while also taking the album, single, and song prizes in the same night is the kind of clean sweep that resets who the genre's center of gravity is. That it happened on the same night Taylor Swift returned to the CMA stage for the first time in 13 years only underlines the point: the old guard came back to celebrate, and the new guard took the trophies home.

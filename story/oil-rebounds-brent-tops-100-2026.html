Oil Rebounds as Brent Climbs Back Above $100 After Election Week Plunge

Fresh tanker attacks and supply fears lifted crude for a second day, but both benchmarks remain headed for steep weekly losses.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 6, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Oil prices rose on Friday, with Brent crude climbing back above $100 a barrel as traders weighed renewed security threats to Middle Eastern shipments after a sharp selloff earlier in the week. Front-month Brent rose 2.1 percent to $100.34 a barrel, while U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate gained 1.6 percent to $92 a barrel.

The gains followed Thursday's rebound, when Brent settled nearly 4 percent higher after plunging more than 8 percent in the previous session. The earlier decline reflected growing expectations of a diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict after Democrats won control of Congress in the midterm elections, according to MUFG. Even after two days of gains, both benchmarks remained on course for weekly losses of around 12 percent.

Fresh attacks on commercial vessels in the Middle East revived concerns about supply disruptions, and Saudi Arabia's Civil Defense issued an alert on Thursday warning of potential danger near the port city of Yanbu, a major Red Sea oil export hub, before saying the danger had passed. The Red Sea route has become increasingly important as producers seek alternatives to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

Analysts at ANZ said the midweek selloff appeared excessive given continuing tensions in the region. JPMorgan estimated that the global oil market was in surplus by about 1.4 million barrels a day in October, as high prices weakened demand and supply conditions improved, which could limit how far the rebound runs.

The whiplash in oil prices mirrors the wider market turmoil this week. Stocks suffered their worst day since April 2025 on Thursday as the AI trade unwound, while gold rose and traders pushed back expectations for the Federal Reserve's next rate increase. For drivers, the drop in crude could eventually mean cheaper gasoline, though prices at the pump tend to fall more slowly than oil itself.

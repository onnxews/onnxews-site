Phil Collins in Tears as Rock Hall Inducts 2026 Class in Los Angeles

Phil Collins wept at a standing ovation as he was inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame for his solo career on Saturday night, joining a class that also included Billy Idol, Wu-Tang Clan, Luther Vandross and Queen Latifah.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 15, 2026 · 5 min readFacebookX

Phil Collins cried as he took his place in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame on Saturday night, November 14, completing a journey that began when he was inducted with Genesis in 2010 and ending a four-year absence from the stage. The 75-year-old, who has not performed since 2022, walked the red carpet and made his way slowly to the stage using two canes, and the standing ovation that greeted him at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles was so large that he wept before he could speak.

Blink-182 drummer Travis Barker inducted Collins, praising his unprecedented dual stardom as a drummer and a hit-making pop singer in the 1980s. Cynthia Erivo then performed a slowed-down rendition of "In the Air Tonight," with Barker playing the famous drum fill, while Collins watched from the stage. His daughter, actor Lily Collins, was in the crowd for a night that felt as much like a homecoming as an honour.

Collins told the crowd, "you haven't heard the last of me," a line that drew cheers from a room full of musicians who had grown up on his records. Backstage, he was more candid about what his body will allow, adding: "I think my drumming days are probably done." The two remarks together captured the evening: a man returning to the stage he had left, and being honest about what he can still do there.

The ceremony also brought together two of the most influential bands of the post-punk era. Joy Division and New Order were jointly inducted by Trent Reznor of Nine Inch Nails, and Peter Hook — the only member of either band to attend — performed "Love Will Tear Us Apart" and "Bizarre Love Triangle" with Deftones frontman Chino Moreno and CHVRCHES singer Lauren Mayberry. The performance honoured five musicians across the two bands: Ian Curtis, Bernard Sumner, Stephen Morris, Gillian Gilbert and Hook himself.

The class of 2026 was one of the broadest the Hall has assembled. Billy Idol was inducted, as were Wu-Tang Clan, Queen Latifah, MC Lyte and the producer Rick Rubin. The late Luther Vandross was inducted by Oprah Winfrey, with Usher singing a medley of his songs. Oasis, Sade and Iron Maiden, who had all declined to attend, were inducted in absentia: Bob Odenkirk introduced Oasis, with Stephen Wilson Jr. singing "Wonderwall," while Jack Black inducted Iron Maiden as members of Metallica, Rage Against the Machine and St. Vincent played "The Trooper."

U2 opened the show with "Silencio" and "Pride (In the Name of Love)," and the night closed with Run-DMC and Aerosmith performing "Walk This Way" together, a collaboration that first brought the two acts together four decades ago. The breadth of the bill — from hip-hop to metal, from soul to post-punk — reflected a Hall that has steadily widened its definition of rock and roll.

For Collins, the night was the culmination of a career that has spanned more than five decades, from his years as Genesis's drummer to a solo run that made him one of the biggest-selling artists of the 1980s. His health problems in recent years, including a spinal injury that left him unable to play drums, had made a return to the stage seem unlikely, which is why the sight of him walking to the podium on two canes drew such a response from the room.

The ceremony will reach a wider audience in December. The edited three-hour special airs on Tuesday, December 8, at 8 p.m. ET/PT on ABC, and streams the next day, December 9, on Disney+ and Hulu. The broadcast will include performances and speeches that were cut from the live show, giving viewers who were not in Los Angeles a fuller version of the night.

The induction does not settle what comes next for Collins, who was careful not to promise a return to performing. What it did settle is his place in the Hall as a solo artist, separate from the band that first brought him there, and it gave a room full of musicians and fans a chance to say thank you to a drummer and singer whose songs have been part of the culture for forty years.

Why it matters: The night mattered most for what it said about Phil Collins himself: a 75-year-old who had not performed in four years, walking to the stage on two canes and being met with an ovation that moved him to tears. The Hall's decision to induct him as a solo artist, sixteen years after Genesis went in, settles his status as one of the defining pop figures of the 1980s rather than just a band drummer who went solo. It also showed how wide the Hall has become, with a single class spanning hip-hop, metal, soul and post-punk.

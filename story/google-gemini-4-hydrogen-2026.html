Google Launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Its Fastest AI Model Yet, in Search and Its Apps

The new model is built for coding and everyday agent work, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens, and Google says it finishes tasks two and a half times faster than its predecessor.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 13, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday, November 12, a new artificial intelligence model the company describes as its fastest yet, built for coding work and everyday tasks handled by AI agents.

The model is priced at a flat $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with a one-million-token context window, and it is rolling out globally across Google Search, the Gemini app, the Gemini API and AI Studio, as well as in Gemini for business and the paid AI Pro and Ultra plans in Google Antigravity.

Google says Hydrogen completes tasks two and a half times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its default high setting, and scores 57 percent on Terminal-Bench 4.1, a benchmark for agentic coding, against 18.2 percent for its predecessor. The company also says the model cuts fabricated claims by more than five times.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Hydrogen scores 51 at its default high setting, just ahead of the more powerful Gemini 4 Argon at 50, and reaches 54 at a new extra-high thinking setting. The model offers four thinking levels — low, medium, high and extra-high — so users can trade speed for deeper reasoning.

Hydrogen is adapted from Gemini 4 Carbon, the larger model Google opened to paying developers earlier this month, and it ranks fifth on Agent Arena, a public leaderboard for agent-style models.

In Search, Google calls the rollout its biggest model improvement yet, promising better understanding of complicated questions, more visual answers and a wider range of links. The company says the model is designed to handle the long, multi-step questions people increasingly type into Search, according to TechCrunch.

Developers get a new tool in Antigravity, Google's coding environment: an Orchestrator that switches automatically between Carbon and Hydrogen depending on the task, reaching near-Carbon success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24 percent less quota and running 19 percent faster, according to Google.

Google also published safety figures for the model, saying it reduces overreach — cases where the model does more than asked — by up to 81 percent and under-service by up to 79 percent. Those figures are Google's own and have not been independently verified.

The launch follows Google's opening of Gemini 4 Carbon to paying developers earlier this month, as the company builds out a family of models at different sizes and prices rather than betting on a single flagship.

The speed claim is the one to watch. If Hydrogen really does finish coding and agent tasks two and a half times faster at the same quality, it changes what developers can afford to automate; if the advantage shrinks in independent testing, the pricing and the Search rollout will matter more than the headline numbers.

Hydrogen is the second member of the Gemini 4 family to reach the public in a month. Carbon, the larger and more powerful model, opened to paying developers earlier in November, and Google has said its prices will double on December 1. The family approach is deliberate: Carbon handles the hardest reasoning work, while Hydrogen is built for the high-volume, everyday jobs where speed and price matter more than maximum capability.

For ordinary users, the most visible change will be in Search. Google says Hydrogen will give better answers to complicated, multi-part questions, with more visual responses and a wider range of links than the previous model. The company is betting that people will increasingly ask Search to do work — comparing options, planning steps, reasoning through a problem — rather than simply finding a page, and that a faster model makes that kind of search feel natural.

Why it matters: Hydrogen is Google's bet that speed, not raw power, is what most AI use now needs: agents that run all day, coding assistants that answer in seconds, and Search results that reason before they reply. The pricing — flat and cheap against the larger Carbon model — is aimed at developers who run millions of calls, while the Search rollout puts the model in front of billions of users at once. What matters next is independent verification of Google's speed and accuracy claims, and whether the model's quality holds up once it is handling real-world questions instead of benchmarks.

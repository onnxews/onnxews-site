Jordan Love Throws for 372 Yards as Packers Rout Patriots 45-27

Jordan Love threw for 372 yards and three touchdowns as Green Bay led 14-0 after one quarter and never looked back at Gillette Stadium.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 9, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

The Green Bay Packers routed the New England Patriots 45-27 at Gillette Stadium on Sunday, with quarterback Jordan Love throwing for 372 yards and three touchdowns in one of the most complete performances of the Packers' season. New England never stood a chance in what became one of the worst nights at Gillette in recent memory.

The tone was set on the second play from scrimmage, when Patriots quarterback Drake Maye threw an interception. From there, the Packers took control and never let go. Green Bay led 14-0 at the end of the first quarter and 24-14 at halftime, and the second half was a procession as the visitors piled on the points.

Love was superb throughout. He picked apart the Patriots' secondary with a mix of deep shots and precise timing throws, finishing with 372 passing yards and three touchdowns. His receivers did the rest: Christian Watson and Matthew Golden did whatever they wanted on offense, repeatedly finding space behind a defense that had no answers for Green Bay's speed.

The Patriots' night was summed up by that opening interception. Maye, who had carried New England's hopes into the game, could not get the offense going against a Packers defense that swarmed from the start. The home crowd, which had hoped for the Patriots' first win against the NFC this season, watched the game slip away before halftime and never come back.

The scoreline kept climbing after the break. Green Bay led 31-14, then 34-14, then 42-14 and 42-21 before closing out the 45-27 win. The Patriots found the end zone late to make the final margin respectable, but the result had been settled long before the fourth quarter.

For the Packers, the win was a statement. Beating a Patriots team at Gillette by 18 points, with the quarterback throwing for nearly 400 yards, is the kind of performance that travels. Love's numbers — 372 yards, three touchdowns, no mention of turnovers — were the headline, but the completeness of the win mattered just as much.

For New England, the defeat was a step backward after a season of progress. The Patriots had built their campaign on a resilient defense and a young quarterback growing into the role, but neither showed up on Sunday night. The loss also carried a logistical sting: the Patriots now travel to Munich, Germany, to play the Detroit Lions next week, and a win would have made the trip a good deal sweeter.

The Packers, meanwhile, head into the second half of their season with momentum and a quarterback playing at the top of his game. If Love keeps throwing like this, and Watson and Golden keep getting open the way they did on Sunday, Green Bay will be a problem for everyone left on its schedule.

The final word belonged to the scoreboard: Packers 45, Patriots 27. In a league where most games are decided by a touchdown or less, an 18-point road win is a rout — and Green Bay delivered it in front of a national audience.

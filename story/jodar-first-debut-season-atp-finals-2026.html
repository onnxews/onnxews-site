Rafael Jodar Completes Dream Rise to Become First Debut-Season ATP Finals Qualifier

The 20-year-old Spaniard, who began the year ranked No. 168 and was playing college tennis a year ago, sealed the eighth and final place at the season-ending ATP Finals in Turin.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 12, 2026 · 6 min readFacebookX

Rafael Jodar has completed one of the fastest rises the ATP Tour has ever seen, qualifying for the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin in his very first full season on the professional tour. The 20-year-old Spaniard claimed the eighth and final place in the field for the season-ending championship, becoming the first player in the history of the event to reach it during his debut campaign at the highest level. The ATP Tour charted his rise from the Next Gen Finals to Turin

Jodar's place in Turin came down to the last two days of the Paris Masters, and he ultimately had no control over it. After losing to Casper Ruud in the second round on Tuesday, his fate was in the hands of his rivals. Alex de Minaur would have passed him with one more win, but lost to Tomas Martin Etcheverry on Wednesday, 7-6(2), 6-3. Ben Shelton then beat Tommy Paul 6-4, 4-6, 6-3, and Taylor Fritz lost a three-set battle with Carlos Alcaraz 6-7(10), 7-5, 6-3, which handed Jodar the final spot with 3,234 points in the Race to Turin.

The numbers behind his season are remarkable. Jodar began 2026 ranked No. 168 in the world, and on Nov. 2 he reached the top 10 for the first time, becoming the first man to do it in his debut season on tour. He won his first title in Marrakech in April, beating Marco Trungelliti 6-3, 6-2, made his Grand Slam main-draw debut at the Australian Open as a qualifier, winning a five-setter in the first round, and reached the Roland Garros quarter-finals in June, where he lost to Alexander Zverev. The summer brought his first ATP 500 final in Washington, where he lost to Taylor Fritz, and a Montreal semi-final that made him the first man born in 2006 or later to reach a Masters 1000 semi-final, before a run to his first Masters 1000 final in Shanghai.

He has also banked $3,348,899 in prize money during his first season on the ATP Tour, with two wins over top-10 players already to his name. The ATP Tour's own figures show he has notched up positive records at the Grand Slams (7-4) and ATP Masters 1000 events (20-7), proving an ability to find his best tennis at the most prestigious events. He reached a then career-high No. 11 in August, and despite back-to-back first-round losses at the US Open and in Tokyo, he answered with that run to the Shanghai final.

Jodar's path to Turin runs through the Next Gen ATP Finals, the year-end event for the tour's best 20-and-under talents, which he played in December 2025 after a season at the University of Virginia, where he was ITA National Rookie of the Year. He turned professional on Dec. 31. A year later, he will compete at the Nitto ATP Finals alongside the biggest stars in men's tennis, and he will be just the fifth player to qualify for the season finale the year after making his debut at the Next Gen event, following Alexander Zverev, Stefanos Tsitsipas, Carlos Alcaraz and Holger Rune.

The field he joins is a new-look one. World No. 1 Alexander Zverev, the reigning French Open and US Open champion, comfortably secured his spot as the top-ranked player in the live points race, while Alcaraz and Shelton, the two Paris Masters finalists, were assured of places with time to spare, and former world No. 1 and 2020 ATP Finals champion Daniil Medvedev is also in. The rest of the field is made up of four first-time qualifiers: Flavio Cobolli, Arthur Fils, Frances Tiafoe and Jodar, who will be the youngest of all the players competing inside the Inalpi Arena.

Two huge names will not be on the court in Turin. Jannik Sinner, the two-time reigning champion, announced in October that his 2026 season had come to an early end due to a knee injury which kept him out of the US Open, and his absence opened up the No. 9 ranking spot as a qualification place for the chasing pack. Record 24-time major champion Novak Djokovic failed to qualify after falling short in Paris with a second-round defeat to Rinky Hijikata, finishing 12th in the Race to Turin standings with 2,940 points. Alex de Minaur and Taylor Fritz took Jodar to the wire for the final spot, but narrowly missed out by finishing 10th and 11th, with 3,170 and 3,050 points respectively.

The draw takes place on Thursday, Nov. 12, at around 11:45 UK time, following a pre-tournament press conference. Two groups of four will be drawn, called the John McEnroe Group and the Ivan Lendl Group this year, and played in a round-robin format, with the top two in each group progressing to the semi-finals. Zverev and Alcaraz will be separated as the top-ranked seeds, and all matches, including the final, are best-of-three tie-break sets. The tournament itself runs from Nov. 15 to Nov. 22 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin.

Jodar himself has stayed grounded about the speed of his rise. Speaking to the ATP Tour, he looked back on his first title and said: "Winning in Marrakech was very special for me... The week went very well for me and winning my first ATP title meant a lot." Reflecting on his season, he added: "My mindset of not giving up and giving everything in every match has helped me be in this situation." He also said he was aware he had enjoyed a great year, but that if you want to be a great tennis player, you have to be on tour for many years, and that is his goal, to be a little bit better every year.

Why it matters: Jodar's qualification is the standout story of the 2026 season's closing weeks, not just because of the speed of the rise but because of what it says about the changing of the guard in men's tennis. With Sinner injured and Djokovic missing out, half the Turin field is made up of debutants, and the youngest of them got there in his first year on tour, a year after playing college tennis. The group draw on Thursday will decide whether his debut ends quickly or turns into something more, but whatever happens in Turin, a player who began the year ranked No. 168 has already made history that no one can take from him.

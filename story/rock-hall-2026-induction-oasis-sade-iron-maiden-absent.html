Rock Hall Induction Set for Saturday as Oasis, Sade and Iron Maiden Skip the Stage

Oasis, Sade and Iron Maiden will all miss Saturday's ceremony at the Peacock Theater, leaving Wu-Tang Clan, Billy Idol and Queen Latifah to carry the night.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

The Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction ceremony takes place on Saturday night at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles, but three of the night's biggest names will not be there.

Oasis, Sade and Iron Maiden are all skipping the ceremony, for very different reasons. Oasis will not attend because Noel Gallagher is playing a gig at the EFG London Jazz Festival in Britain, while Liam Gallagher posted on X that it was "too far to be going for the night" and added, "I dont like award ceremonies they freak me out."

Sade confirmed her absence in a statement on her official website, writing, "Please know that our absence takes nothing away from how much this recognition means to us." Iron Maiden is in the middle of an Australian tour, with a show in Adelaide on November 11 and further dates on November 13 and 15, making a trip to Los Angeles impossible.

The absences do not diminish the class itself. The performer inductees are Billy Idol, Phil Collins, Sade, Oasis, Iron Maiden, Joy Division and New Order, Luther Vandross, who died in 2005, and Wu-Tang Clan. The early influence inductees are Queen Latifah, MC Lyte, Gram Parsons, Fela Kuti and Celia Cruz, while the musical excellence honorees are Rick Rubin, Linda Creed, Arif Mardin and Jimmy Miller. Television pioneer Ed Sullivan receives the Ahmet Ertegun Award.

Phil Collins, 75, plans to attend but will not perform, for health reasons. He has not played live since March 2022. Key members of New Order are also out for health reasons, which will shape how the band's induction is presented on stage.

Several honorees are expected to appear and perform. Wu-Tang Clan, Queen Latifah and MC Lyte are all expected to take the stage, and Billy Idol, 70, is expected to attend and play.

The class includes two firsts. Fela Kuti is the first African-born artist to enter the Hall, and Celia Cruz is the first member who sang primarily in Spanish. More than 1,200 voters cast ballots for this year's class, and the ceremony is being held in Los Angeles for the second straight year.

There will be no live stream this year. The ceremony is being taped and will air as an edited special on ABC on December 8, then stream on Disney+ on December 9. Fans hoping to watch live will have to wait nearly a month for the broadcast.

The ceremony's move to Los Angeles for a second straight year is itself a statement. The Hall's home is in Cleveland, but the induction has become a show-business event, and Los Angeles is where the industry lives. The trade-off is that the ceremony feels less like a museum celebration and more like an awards show, which may be why some artists treat it as optional.

The absences will not stop the show, but they will shape how it is remembered. Inductees who skip the ceremony still join the Hall, and their plaques will hang in Cleveland regardless. The question is whether the Hall can keep its ceremony feeling essential when the biggest names treat it as a scheduling conflict rather than a career milestone.

The empty chairs will be part of the story on Saturday night, but so will the music. With Wu-Tang, Queen Latifah, MC Lyte and Billy Idol expected on stage, the ceremony still promises the kind of performances that have made the induction a highlight of the music calendar. Read the report from WMMR

Why it matters: The absences say something real about what the Hall of Fame has become: for Oasis, Sade and Iron Maiden, the honor is welcome but the ceremony is optional, and a global tour or a jazz festival gig takes priority. That is a shift from the days when induction night was the biggest night in music. The ceremony still matters, though, for the firsts in this class, Fela Kuti and Celia Cruz, and for the performers who do show up. The December broadcast will tell whether the night is remembered for who played or who stayed home.

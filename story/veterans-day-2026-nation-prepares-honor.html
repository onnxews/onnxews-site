Veterans Day 2026: A Nation Prepares to Honor Those Who Served

From the national ceremony at Arlington to parades in New York, Birmingham, Houston and San Diego, communities across the country will mark the holiday on Wednesday.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Veterans Day 2026 arrives on Wednesday, November 11, and communities across the United States are preparing to honor the men and women who have served in the Armed Forces. The federal holiday, observed every year on the anniversary of the armistice that ended the First World War, will be marked by a national ceremony at Arlington National Cemetery, parades in cities from New York to San Diego, and quieter gatherings at courthouses, schools and veterans posts in towns across the country.

The country's official observance takes place at Arlington National Cemetery in Virginia, where the 73rd annual National Veterans Day Observance will be held at the Memorial Amphitheater. A musical prelude is scheduled for 10:30 in the morning, followed by the national observance at 11:00. The program includes the traditional wreath-laying at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, though public attendance at that portion is restricted to official participants. The 11 o'clock hour is itself part of the tradition, marking the moment the guns fell silent on the Western Front in 1918.

In New York City, the Veterans Day Parade returns to Fifth Avenue on Wednesday, running from 12:30 to 4:00 in the afternoon. Organized by the United War Veterans Council, the procession steps off near 26th Street and proceeds north along the avenue, drawing veterans, military units, marching bands and thousands of spectators. It is one of the largest Veterans Day parades in the country and a fixture of the city's calendar, honoring veterans of every era and every branch of service.

Other major cities are holding their own observances with distinctive local touches. San Diego's Veterans Day Parade runs from 10:00 to 11:30 in the morning along Harbor Drive in front of the County Administration Building, and this year's theme commemorates the 35th anniversary of Operation Desert Storm. Houston Salutes American Heroes begins with a ceremony on the steps of City Hall at 10:00, a moment of silence at 11:00 and a parade at 11:30. Birmingham, Alabama, hosts the National Veterans Day Parade at 1:00 in the afternoon.

In Savannah, Georgia, the city's Veterans Day Parade turns 80 this year. The parade begins at 10:00 in the morning at Abercorn and Gwinnett streets, with more than 100 units expected to take part. At 11:11, the parade will pause at the reviewing stand for a special observance, and all living past recipients of the city's Veteran of the Year award will serve as grand marshals. Communities across Coastal Georgia and the South Carolina Lowcountry are holding ceremonies, school programs and gatherings throughout the week.

The day is also one of the few when the country's public lands open their gates for free. National parks and many state parks are offering free entrance on Wednesday in honor of veterans nationwide, and veterans and active-duty military members can enjoy free admission at zoos, museums and attractions in cities across the country. Many restaurants and businesses are offering free meals to veterans, and veterans posts are hosting their own gatherings, from pancake breakfasts to evening ceremonies.

The weather is cooperating for the observances across much of the East. Forecasters expect sunny skies and mild temperatures for Wednesday's ceremonies in the Mid-Atlantic, with highs in the mid-60s in Philadelphia and around 70 degrees in Maryland. Veterans Day is a federal holiday, so federal offices, banks and the post office will be closed, and many schools will hold their own programs, with students hearing from veterans and learning about the history of the day.

Veterans Day honors everyone who has served in the United States Armed Forces, living or not, a distinction that sets it apart from Memorial Day, which honors those who died in service. The holiday traces its roots to Armistice Day, first observed in 1919 to mark the end of the First World War, and it was renamed Veterans Day in 1954 to honor veterans of all wars. The poppy, inspired by the battlefields of Flanders, remains its enduring symbol, and many ceremonies will include the laying of wreaths and the playing of Taps.

For the veterans who will march, stand at attention or simply watch from the sidewalk on Wednesday, the day is both a celebration and a reckoning. The country they served has changed in the years since many of them wore the uniform, and the wars they fought have faded from the headlines. But the parades, the ceremonies and the moments of silence at 11:00 are a reminder that the nation still sets aside one day a year to say thank you, and to remember that the freedom to gather in a public square on a November morning was bought by the people being honored.

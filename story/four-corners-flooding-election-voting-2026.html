Four Corners Flooding Complicates Election-Day Voting for Some Residents

Historically high rainfall and river flooding hit Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona and Utah for a second straight week, forcing evacuations and road closures.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 3, 2026 · 2 min readFacebookX

Heavy rain and river flooding across the Four Corners region entered a second straight week on Tuesday, forcing evacuations and closing roads in parts of Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona and Utah. The flooding arrived on Election Day itself, complicating voting for some residents in affected communities.

NPR reported Tuesday that the flooding is widespread and that some residents will have difficulty reaching polling places, with voting arrangements altered in affected areas. Local election officials have had to work around washed-out roads and displaced residents.

The region's rivers rose after days of historically high rainfall, a pattern that has already strained emergency services across the Southwest. Hundreds of people were forced to evacuate in earlier rounds of flooding, and at least one death was reported in the wider event.

For voters unable to reach their assigned polling place, officials pointed to the usual fallbacks: alternate voting locations, emergency procedures set by county election offices, and state voter-information lines. Voters in line at closing time are entitled to cast a ballot under rules in every state.

The flooding is the latest in a string of weather disruptions to hit the country this fall, and a reminder of how quickly a natural disaster can collide with an election. County officials said they would continue updating residents on road closures and polling-place changes through the day.

Reporting: NPR

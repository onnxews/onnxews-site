Sengun's 43 Powers Rockets Past Jazz 125-118

Sengun scored a season-high 43 points and teamed with Kevin Durant for 65 as Houston sent Utah to its 11th straight loss.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Alperen Sengun delivered the best game of his season on Friday night, scoring 43 points to lead the Houston Rockets past the Utah Jazz 125-118.

Sengun added 12 rebounds and five assists, dominating a Jazz frontcourt that had no answer for him in the post. His 43 points were a season high and the centerpiece of a Houston win that was harder than the final score suggests.

Kevin Durant added 22 points and six assists, going a perfect 10-of-10 from the free-throw line. Together, Sengun and Durant combined for 65 points, and their two-man game carried the Rockets through the second half.

Fred VanVleet scored 17 points, including a bank shot with 11 seconds left that made it 123-118 and sealed the win. Amen Thompson added a double-double with 12 points and 10 rebounds, and Tari Eason chipped in 14 points and 11 rebounds off the bench.

Utah made Houston work for it. Rookie Darryn Peterson scored a career-high 28 points, his fifth straight game with at least 20 points, while Keyonte George added 27 points and seven assists and Ace Bailey scored 21. The Jazz led 87-77 late in the third quarter and looked poised for an upset.

Houston answered with a 15-2 run to close the third quarter, flipping the game and taking control into the fourth. Houston had taken a 63-53 lead into halftime after 7-0 and 9-0 runs in the second quarter. The Rockets' defense tightened, the Jazz's shooting cooled, and Sengun took over down the stretch.

The win moved Houston to 10-3, its third straight victory, and improved the Rockets to 7-1 at home. Utah fell to 1-12 and has now lost 11 games in a row, a slide that has left the Jazz at the bottom of the Western Conference standings.

For Utah, the bright spot is Peterson, whose scoring streak has made him one of the most exciting rookies in the league. But the losses keep piling up, and the Jazz are running out of moral victories to take from close defeats.

The Rockets' home record is becoming a weapon. At 7-1 in Houston, the team has made its building a hard place to play, and the crowd has grown louder with each win. The third-quarter comeback on Friday was the kind of run that feeds off a home crowd, and the Jazz had no answer once the momentum turned.

Sengun's night was also a reminder of how far he has come. He arrived in the league as a skilled but unproven big man; now he is the centerpiece of a 10-3 team and a player opponents have to plan around. His 43 points came on efficient shooting, and his 12 rebounds and five assists showed a complete game. If he keeps this up, the All-Star conversation will start soon.

Houston now travels to Washington to face the Wizards on Sunday, November 15, at 7 p.m. Eastern, while Utah hosts San Antonio on Sunday at 5 p.m. Eastern. The Rockets will look to keep rolling, and the Jazz will look for any win at all. Read the report from Reuters

Why it matters: The game was a tale of two trajectories: Houston is 10-3 and looks like a real contender in the West, while Utah is 1-12 and still searching for a win. Sengun's 43 points showed why the Rockets built around him, and his pairing with Durant is becoming one of the league's most productive duos. For the Jazz, Peterson's scoring streak is the one thing worth watching, but a rebuild needs wins eventually, and 11 straight losses is a hard place to find them.

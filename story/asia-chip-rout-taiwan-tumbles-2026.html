Taiwan Stocks Plunge 6% as Nvidia Turmoil Sends Asia's Chip Shares Tumbling

The Taiex suffered its second-biggest single-day points drop on record on Monday as the fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation spread across Asia, a day before Nvidia reports its first results without him.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 16, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Taiwan's stock market fell 6% on Monday, its main chipmaker TSMC lost almost as much, and the sell-off in Asian chip stocks followed the sun to Wall Street, where futures pointed lower and Nvidia looked set to extend its slide a day before it reports results for the first time without Jensen Huang in charge.

The scale of the fall in Taipei was historic. The Taiex closed at 41,040.08, down 2,635.04 points, or 6.03%, from Friday's close of 43,675.12 — its second-biggest single-day points drop on record, according to the island's Central News Agency. TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker and the main maker of Nvidia's AI chips, tumbled 5.7% to NT$2,215, and alone accounted for roughly 1,000 points of the decline. Foreign investors were net sellers of about NT$70 billion on the main board.

The rout spread across the region's other major chip markets. South Korea's Kospi closed down 3.59% at 5,100.50, with memory maker SK Hynix tumbling 7.9%. Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 2.42% to 59,662.02, after official data on Monday showed Japan's economy contracted in the latest quarter.

The selling was concentrated in technology. MSCI's All-World index was down 0.4% on Monday, with chip-equipment maker ASML, chipmaker Infineon and engineering group Siemens the largest drags on Europe's STOXX 600, according to Reuters.

The selling has a clear trigger. Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive last Thursday, November 12, after revelations about his personal relationships, including one with a woman United States officials believe is a Chinese spy, and the shock has shaken the company at the centre of the artificial intelligence boom. Nvidia lost 4.3% on Thursday and another 2% on Friday, and it has now fallen nearly 13% for the week. Its market value has tumbled to about $3.9 trillion by Friday from about $5.8 trillion in early October, according to LSEG data.

Investors were already questioning how long United States technology companies could sustain the growth in AI spending before Huang resigned, economist Zhiwei Zhang of Pinpoint Asset Management told the Associated Press. The resignation turned a valuation debate into a leadership question, and dealers pointed to three further pressures on Monday: anxiety over the leadership vacuum at Nvidia after Huang's resignation, worries about tighter United States export controls on China, and futures-related selling ahead of Wednesday's contract settlement.

Tuesday's report is the next test, and the market is braced for a big move. Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results after the market closes on Tuesday, its first report under interim chief executive Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer since 2013. Analysts expect revenue of about $109.2 billion and adjusted earnings of $2.48 a share, on the LSEG consensus, and options imply a share move of about 7% either way the day after the results, according to Reuters. Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, is also set to make its stock market debut later in the week, on Thursday, November 19.

The falls on Monday were not confined to chipmakers. The selling spread across Asia's technology-heavy markets, though performance was mixed elsewhere: India's Sensex defied the sell-off, finishing 512 points higher, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose. But in the markets most exposed to the AI trade, the verdict was unambiguous. Taiwan, South Korea and Japan all closed sharply lower, and the declines extended a sell-off that began in United States chip stocks on Friday, when Broadcom fell 4.8%.

What happens next depends on two events this week. The first is Nvidia's earnings on Tuesday, when Kress will present numbers she set as chief financial officer and give the company's first outlook since Huang left. The second is Anthropic's debut on Thursday, which will test whether public markets still believe in frontier AI after a brutal November. Until then, markets are trading on expectations, not numbers — and on Monday, those expectations were not kind.

Why it matters: This matters because the AI trade has been the engine of the global stock market for four years, and Monday was the first full session in which Asia priced the leadership fight at Nvidia — the company at the centre of it. The numbers are stark: a record points drop in Taiwan, a fall in Nvidia's market value from about $5.8 trillion in early October to about $3.9 trillion, and a 6% fall in the market that makes most of the world's advanced chips. Orbit has covered Nvidia's turmoil since the board probe opened; this article connects that story to the markets, and the next chapter arrives on Tuesday when Nvidia reports. The question is no longer whether the AI boom is real, but whether investors still believe the spending behind it will keep growing.

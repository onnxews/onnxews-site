Alphabet's Google Ventures Held $548 Million Qualcomm Stake Through Startup Deal

A securities filing shows Google Ventures held nearly 3 million Qualcomm shares, a stake that traces back to Qualcomm's purchase of AI startup Modular, not a fresh cash investment.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 7, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Alphabet, the parent company of Google, has quietly built a major stake in Qualcomm worth nearly $548 million, according to a regulatory filing disclosed this week. The filing shows Alphabet held nearly 3 million Qualcomm shares as of September 30, 2026, strengthening the chipmaker's position just as it tries to expand beyond smartphones and into the fast-growing market for data-center processors.

The disclosure landed as demand for AI infrastructure pushes the biggest technology companies to look for alternatives to expensive Nvidia processors. Qualcomm shares gained around 3 percent in premarket trading on Friday, November 6, after the stake became public, a modest but telling reaction to the news that one of the world's largest technology companies is backing its ambitions.

The stake's origin matters. Google Ventures was an investor in Modular, an AI startup Qualcomm agreed to acquire in December 2025 in a deal that closed on July 28. The shares shown in the filing appear to be the ones Google Ventures received as part of that acquisition, rather than a new cash purchase of Qualcomm stock by Alphabet, the parent company of Google. That distinction changes the story from Alphabet actively betting on Qualcomm to a venture arm holding shares it received in a deal.

Alphabet and Qualcomm are longtime partners. Qualcomm processors power many of the smartphones running Google's Android operating system, and the two companies have worked together for years on mobile chips. The stake adds a financial dimension to a relationship that has so far been mostly commercial, and it arrives as Qualcomm tries to reinvent itself as a supplier of custom AI processors for data centers.

Qualcomm's data-center push is already drawing big names. In September, the company reached an agreement allowing Amazon to purchase up to $60 billion worth of Qualcomm AI data-center chips and related products. Microsoft and Meta have also placed orders for Qualcomm's data-center processors, giving the chipmaker a roster of hyperscale customers that would have been unthinkable a few years ago.

The company is targeting $15 billion in data-center revenue, an ambitious goal that would transform a business still best known for phone chips. The Alphabet stake does not by itself guarantee that target will be met, but it signals that Google sees Qualcomm as a serious part of the AI hardware landscape rather than just a mobile supplier.

There are limits to what the filing actually reveals. It does not disclose when Alphabet purchased the Qualcomm shares, or explain the reason behind the investment, so any reading of Google's motives is inference rather than fact. The stake could be a strategic bet, a financial investment, or both, and Alphabet has not commented publicly on the position.

The timing is nonetheless striking. The disclosure came in the same week that AI hardware stocks suffered a brutal selloff, with the Nasdaq falling 5.5 percent on Thursday before partially recovering on Friday. Qualcomm itself had slid earlier in the week after issuing a weak outlook, with handset chip sales down 26 percent, a reminder that its core phone business is under pressure.

That pressure is precisely why the data-center pivot matters. If Qualcomm can convert its relationships with Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and now Alphabet into sustained chip orders, it could offset softness in smartphones and become a genuine second source for AI infrastructure. If it cannot, the stake will look like a vote of confidence that arrived too early.

For now, the filing stands as one more sign of how the AI chip race is widening. Nvidia remains dominant, but the biggest buyers of AI hardware are increasingly spreading their bets, and Qualcomm, with Google now on its shareholder register, is positioning itself to catch some of that spending.

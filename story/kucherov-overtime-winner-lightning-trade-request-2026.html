A Week After Asking for a Trade, Nikita Kucherov Wins It in Overtime for the Lightning

Kucherov scored a power-play goal 3:23 into overtime to lift Tampa Bay past Minnesota 4-3, capping a wild Saturday night around the NHL.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 8, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Nikita Kucherov, one week after requesting a trade, scored a power-play goal 3:23 into overtime to lift the Tampa Bay Lightning to a 4-3 win over the Minnesota Wild on Saturday in Saint Paul. One of the franchise's all-time greats took the puck between the circles and fired in a shot to end a long sequence in the Minnesota end.

The goal was the story of the night in the NHL, and not just for the result. Kucherov's trade request, made public a week ago, has hung over the Lightning ever since, raising questions about the future of one of the most talented players of his generation. Scoring the winner in overtime does not resolve those questions, but it was a reminder of what Tampa Bay would be losing.

The win improved the Lightning to 11-3-2, while Minnesota fell to 12-3-4 despite a strong performance at home. The Wild had pushed Tampa Bay all night, and the game needed overtime to separate two of the best teams in the league. Kucherov's goal, on the power play, was the difference in a game that could easily have gone either way.

Elsewhere on Saturday, Jack Hughes scored 1:18 into overtime for the New Jersey Devils, who overcame a two-goal, third-period deficit to beat the New York Islanders 5-4 in Elmont. The Devils improved to 12-5-0, and Hughes's winner capped a comeback that showed why New Jersey is one of the most resilient teams in the Eastern Conference.

The Carolina Hurricanes delivered the night's most lopsided result, routing the Chicago Blackhawks 9-2 in Raleigh. Carolina improved to 14-3-2, the best record in the league, while Chicago fell to 4-13-1. Jordan Martinook scored the game-winner in the first period, and the Hurricanes never looked back.

The Edmonton Oilers beat the Vancouver Canucks 4-2 at Rogers Place, with Leon Draisaitl scoring the game-winner in the third period. Edmonton improved to 12-3-3, while Vancouver's miserable season continued, the Canucks falling to 3-15-1. The Colorado Avalanche shut out the Detroit Red Wings 4-0 behind a game-winner from Martin Necas, improving to 10-6-2.

The Washington Capitals edged the Florida Panthers 4-3, with Pierre-Luc Dubois scoring the game-winner in the third period. The Pittsburgh Penguins beat the Los Angeles Kings 5-2, and the night also brought a milestone for Sidney Crosby, who moved to sixth all-time in NHL scoring. The Montreal Canadiens beat the Toronto Maple Leafs 4-2, with Chris Kreider scoring the winner.

The New York Rangers held off a furious third-period comeback to beat the Seattle Kraken 4-3, building a 4-0 lead before Seattle scored three unanswered goals. The Nashville Predators beat the St. Louis Blues 3-1, the Ottawa Senators beat the Utah Mammoth 4-2, and the Dallas Stars edged the San Jose Sharks 4-3 on a game-winner from Thomas Harley.

Twelve games produced 81 goals across the league on Saturday night, a reminder of how entertaining the NHL's regular season can be when the schedule lines up. From Kucherov's overtime winner to Carolina's nine-goal outburst to Crosby's climb up the all-time scoring list, the night had something for every kind of hockey fan.

For Tampa Bay, the questions about Kucherov's future will not go away because of one goal. But for one night, the focus was on what he does on the ice rather than what he wants off it. The Lightning are 11-3-2, they are winning games, and their best player is still deciding them in overtime. That is a good problem for a team to have, even if the trade request still hangs in the air.

APPEALS COURT TO TRUMP'S ICE SURGE: “Why Do You Need This?” — Voter Crackdown Stays Blocked

A federal appeals court refused to let Homeland Security restart its pre-election voter-fraud crackdown in nine states, questioning why the surge was rushed just days before the midterms.

ONOrbit News NetworkOct 30, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

A three-judge panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals signaled deep skepticism on Thursday, October 29, toward the Trump administration's emergency bid to resume a Department of Homeland Security enforcement surge targeting alleged illegal voting.

The program, known as the Unlawful Voter Initiative, sent agents into nine states — California, Connecticut, Georgia, Missouri, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Washington, and Wisconsin — to conduct street-level and door-to-door investigations of people accused of voting or registering to vote illegally.

A federal judge in California had already blocked the initiative, and the appeals panel declined to lift that block. The preliminary injunction stays in place through Election Day, leaving the administration unable to resume the field operations in those states in the final days before the November 3 midterms.

During Thursday's hearing, Judge Kim Wardlaw pressed the government on whether the surge was designed to instill fear, while Judge Sidney Thomas asked bluntly why the operation was so urgent now: “Why do you need this? What are you prevented from doing in the next four days? You can't prosecute anybody in four days.”

The judges noted that the government had conceded it relied on faulty data to choose targets and acknowledged specifically targeting naturalized citizens. The panel concluded the government failed to show it would suffer irreparable harm if the program stayed paused through Election Day. The fight may not be over. An anonymous judge on the 9th Circuit requested a vote on whether the case should be reheard by the full court, and the administration could still appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court — both next steps are unconfirmed as of this writing.

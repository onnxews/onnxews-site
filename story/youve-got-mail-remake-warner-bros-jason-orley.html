Warner Bros. Is Remaking 'You've Got Mail' With 'I Want You Back' Director Jason Orley

Jason Orley will write and direct the Warner Bros. remake of the 1998 Nora Ephron classic, with no cast or release date announced yet.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 11, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Warner Bros. is developing a remake of 'You've Got Mail,' the beloved 1998 romantic comedy starring Tom Hanks and Meg Ryan, with filmmaker Jason Orley attached to write and direct. The news was first reported by Deadline on Tuesday, November 10, and confirmed by Variety, setting off an immediate debate among fans of one of the most cherished rom-coms of the 1990s.

Orley is working from an earlier screenplay by Deborah Kaplan and Harry Elfont, the writing team behind 'Can't Hardly Wait' and 'Josie and the Pussycats.' Orley is best known as the director of 'Big Time Adolescence' from 2019 and the romantic comedy 'I Want You Back' from 2022, and as a frequent collaborator of Pete Davidson. Producers Neal Moritz and Tania Landau are developing the new version through Original Film.

The studio has not announced a cast, a filming schedule or a release date, and there has been no announcement about whether Hanks or Ryan will be involved in any way. Details about how the new film will approach the original story also remain under wraps, including whether it will be released in cinemas or go to streaming.

The original film, directed by Nora Ephron, starred Hanks as Joe Fox, a corporate bookstore magnate, and Ryan as Kathleen Kelly, the owner of an independent children's bookshop whose business is threatened by Fox's expansion. The two fall in love anonymously online, neither knowing the other is the rival trying to put them out of business. It was the third on-screen pairing of Hanks and Ryan, following 'Joe Versus the Volcano' and 'Sleepless in Seattle.'

Released on December 18, 1998, the film earned more than 250 million dollars worldwide and remains one of the most recognizable romantic comedies of the decade. Its vision of romance conducted through AOL chatrooms and dial-up email now reads as a time capsule of the early internet, which is exactly the problem the remake must solve.

The story itself is much older than the internet. 'You've Got Mail' was already a remake of the 1940 film 'The Shop Around the Corner,' which was itself an adaptation of the 1937 Hungarian stage play 'Parfumerie' by Miklos Laszlo. The tale of two people who are enemies by day and anonymous pen pals by night has been retold in every era of communication, from letters to telegrams to email.

That history is both the remake's opportunity and its biggest challenge. The 1998 version worked because email was new enough to feel romantic and private. In 2026, anonymous online romance means dating apps, social media, direct messages and AI, and it is far harder to make that world feel innocent. How Orley updates the premise for an era of Hinge, Tinder and catfishing will decide whether the remake charms a new audience or feels like a curiosity.

Orley's own track record suggests a lighter touch. 'I Want You Back' was a well-received romantic comedy that played with modern dating without cynicism, and 'Big Time Adolescence' showed he can handle coming-of-age stories with warmth. But neither film carried the weight of expectation that comes with remaking a Nora Ephron classic starring two of the most beloved actors of their generation.

The remake also lands in a very different theatrical landscape from 1998. Romantic comedies now struggle to draw audiences to cinemas, and many are made for streaming instead. Warner Bros. has not said where the film will land, and that decision will say a lot about how much faith the studio has in the project.

For now, the film exists only as a director, a draft and a title. The casting will be the next test, and the hardest one: finding two leads who can hope to match the chemistry Hanks and Ryan brought to the original is a task that has defeated many remakes before. Fans will be watching, and many of them will be skeptical, which is exactly the reaction Warner Bros. should expect when it touches a classic.

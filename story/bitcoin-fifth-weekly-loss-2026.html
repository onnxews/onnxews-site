Bitcoin Posts Fifth Straight Weekly Loss as ETF Investors Pull $1.28 Billion

Bitcoin closed its fifth straight losing week at $74,754, down 13.6 percent over the run, as spot ETFs bled $1.28 billion.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 10, 2026 · 3 min readFacebookX

Bitcoin has confirmed its fifth consecutive weekly loss, closing the week at $74,754 on Coinbase, down 4.45 percent from Monday's open of $78,232, in the largest weekly fall of a losing run that has now erased 13.6 percent of the cryptocurrency's value.

The selling continued into Monday's Asian session, when bitcoin slipped to $73,755 and traded near $73,870, just above Sunday night's low of $73,477, its lowest price since August 21. Ether and XRP also slid to their lowest levels since August, as the rout spread across the crypto market.

Measured week by week, the decline has been steady rather than sudden: bitcoin fell 3.27 percent in the week from October 5, 2.00 percent from October 12, 2.38 percent from October 19, 2.28 percent from October 26 and 4.45 percent last week. The last weekly gain came in the week from September 28, when the weekly close stood at $86,507.

The steepest drop came on Thursday, when bitcoin fell 5.6 percent as investors reacted to the Democratic sweep in Tuesday's midterm elections and fresh worries about AI spending. The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5 percent that day, its worst session since April 2025, on concerns that Democratic control of Congress could slow the artificial-intelligence data center buildout, and the selling spread from stocks into crypto.

Institutional investors have been heading for the exits. U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded net outflows on four of the five trading days last week, for a weekly total of $1.28 billion, their worst week since late June, according to Farside Investors. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust, the largest of the funds, accounted for much of the selling.

Leveraged traders have paid the price. Liquidations across tracked derivatives venues reached $1.32 billion in the 24 hours to Monday morning, according to the tracker CoinBoss, with more than $1 billion of the wiped-out positions belonging to traders who had bet prices would rise. The Crypto Fear and Greed Index, published by Alternative.me, stood at 25 on Saturday, a level the provider classifies as fear.

Technically, the picture has darkened. Bitcoin closed last week below the 50-week moving average near $75,500 for the first time since early September, and it now sits between its 50-day moving average at $81,800 and its 200-day average at $73,200. Traders have flagged $75,000 as near-term support and $80,000 as a ceiling that has capped recent rallies, with the next levels below at $73,200 and the 200-week exponential moving average near $69,500.

The slide has even caught up with the corporate buyers. Bitcoin is now about 2 percent below the $75,451 average price that Strategy, the largest corporate holder, has paid for its 849,327 coins, as disclosed in its November 2 filing, putting the company's entire position underwater for the first time in the current run.

The next test comes on Tuesday, when the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics releases October consumer price data at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, with a December Federal Reserve rate hike already largely priced in. Crypto got the margin call while stocks got the peace rally on Monday, and whether bitcoin can find a floor will depend on whether the inflation data gives risk assets a reason to recover, or another reason to sell.

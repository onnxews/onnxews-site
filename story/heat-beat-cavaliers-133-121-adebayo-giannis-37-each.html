Adebayo and Giannis Score 37 Each as Heat Beat Cavaliers 133-121

Adebayo and Antetokounmpo combined for 74 points and joined LeBron James and Dwyane Wade as the only Heat duos to each score 35-plus in the same game.

ONOrbit News NetworkNov 14, 2026 · 4 min readFacebookX

Bam Adebayo and Giannis Antetokounmpo put on a show on Friday night, scoring 37 points each as the Miami Heat beat the Cleveland Cavaliers 133-121.

Adebayo shot 13-of-19 from the field and added 14 rebounds, including 18 points in the fourth quarter. He scored Miami's first 10 points of the final period, setting the tone for a closing stretch that decided the game.

Antetokounmpo matched him point for point, scoring 37 on 15-of-20 shooting with nine rebounds. Together, the two stars combined for 74 points, and the duel was the story of the night even before the final score was settled.

The pair made Heat history. They are the only duo in franchise history besides LeBron James and Dwyane Wade to each score at least 35 points in the same game. James and Wade did it twice, on March 14, 2012, and February 26, 2013, and no Miami pair had matched the feat since.

Davion Mitchell was the engine of the Heat offense, scoring 22 points with a career-high-tying 17 assists and just one turnover. Donovan Mitchell led Cleveland with 25 points and seven assists, James Harden added 20 points and eight assists, and Tyrese Proctor scored 22 for the Cavaliers.

Miami opened the fourth quarter on a 13-2 run, turning a close game into a comfortable lead. The Heat's defense tightened, the Cavaliers' shots stopped falling, and the Kaseya Center crowd roared the home team to its third straight win.

The victory was Miami's third straight, and the Heat have now won six of their past eight games. Cleveland has now lost five of its past seven games, a slide that has raised questions about a team expected to contend.

Antetokounmpo is in his first season in Miami after the blockbuster trade that brought him from Milwaukee in exchange for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Kasparas Jakučionis. He faces his former team on November 18, in a game billed as "A Night in Vice City" with a Grand Theft Auto 6 promotion.

Both teams were short-handed. Miami played without Klay Thompson, out with an ankle injury, and Pelle Larsson, out with an illness. Cleveland was missing Evan Mobley with a toe injury and Sam Merrill with an elbow injury.

The win also showed the Heat's new identity taking shape. Miami has always prided itself on toughness and conditioning, and the fourth-quarter run was a classic Heat performance: defense, pace and a star taking over. Adebayo's 18 points in the period were the kind of closing stretch that defines a team's season, and the crowd responded to it.

Cleveland's problems are the mirror image. The Cavaliers have the talent to compete, but five losses in seven games is a pattern, not a blip. The injuries do not help, but the team looked flat in the fourth quarter, and the coaching staff will need answers before the slide gets worse. The East is too competitive to give away games in November.

For Miami, the win was a statement: two stars scoring 37 each, a third straight victory and a team that looks like it is finding its identity around its new superstar. For Cleveland, it was another reminder that the season is slipping, with five losses in seven games and no easy fix in sight. Read the report from Reuters

Why it matters: Two 37-point nights in the same game is rare enough, but the company Adebayo and Giannis joined, LeBron and Wade, is what makes this one historic for Miami. It is also the clearest sign yet that the Giannis trade is working: the Heat have won three straight and look like a team with an identity, while Cleveland's slide to five losses in seven games suggests a contender in trouble. The November 18 reunion with Milwaukee is now circled on every calendar in the league.
